Method · how a Canvas is made

Every Canvas earns its way here.

This is The Operator Economy, where we show you how to use AI to build, own, and operate a sustainable business of one. This page explains what an Operator Canvas is, how one gets made, and how to read the labels you'll see on every material claim.

Five stages, each behind a gate.

Gates, in plain terms: between stages, named reviews check the evidence, the language, and the model, and the owner signs off. A Canvas that fails a gate goes back, not through. A locked Canvas can still change: any material edit creates a new revision and everything downstream is re-checked.

The four evidence classes

Every material claim carries one.

Proof and uncertainty share the same surface. The class tells you what kind of thing a statement is; the receipt tells you where it came from.

Observed

A sourced fact. Someone measured or reported it, and the receipt names them. A vendor-published figure is still observed; the receipt says who published it so you can weigh it.

Parallel

Transferred from an adjacent model. Nobody has run this exact business, but a neighboring one runs on the same mechanics. The statement says what transfers and what doesn't.

A parallel never becomes proof of demand for the exact offer. It earns the right to test, nothing more.

Modeled

An assumption with its arithmetic shown. Every modeled figure travels with its assumption list, and scenarios render side by side, never blended into one confident number.

Modeled figures are never dressed up with reassuring adjectives. If a modeled case fails its own target, the page says so.

Unknown

An open question, kept visible. Unknowns are content, not gaps. Each one is registered with how it resolves, and no diagram or polished figure is allowed to paper over one.

The most useful line on a Canvas is often the unknown its first test is designed to answer.

Model status

The whole model gets its own classification.

Evidence classes label individual statements. Model status classifies the whole Canvas: observed model means this business demonstrably runs today; adjacent synthesis means it's assembled from proven neighboring pieces; frontier hypothesis means the combination itself is untested. The two labels never merge: a Canvas can be an adjacent synthesis built mostly from observed facts.

Economics rules

Equations, scenarios, and honest failures.

  • Economics render as equations plus a low, base, and high case. There's no single headline number, anywhere.
  • Each case carries its own evidence class and assumption list.
  • If the base case fails the livelihood target, the Canvas says so plainly.
  • Every Canvas carries a required disclosure: modeled scenarios aren't observed performance or an earnings forecast.
Versioning and hashes

A changed Canvas field creates a new revision.

When a Canvas locks, its source file gets a SHA-256 fingerprint. The page you read and the PDF you download each carry their own. All three are printed in the provenance panel so you can verify that what you read, what you downloaded, and what was locked are the same system. Old revisions stay published, labeled, and unchanged.

What we don't publish

The claims discipline, in reader terms.

  • No income promises. A livelihood case is a design test the Canvas must pass or admit failing, not a forecast.
  • No figure without a source or its arithmetic. Estimates are marked and never blended into sourced figures.
  • No modeled number upgraded by an adjective.
  • No diagram that resolves an unknown. Visuals inherit the evidence label of what they draw.
  • Held-back material: none. Everything load-bearing is on the page; the PDF adds detail and print utility.
