Step 01 · Opportunity

Is there a real problem worth operating?

Start with the tension, not the tools. OTAs provide valuable discovery; the opportunity appears when the hotel must pay to reacquire a guest it has already served.

What to look at
  • Who already solves discovery well.
  • Where the relationship breaks after the stay.
  • What changes if the second booking becomes direct.

Step 03 · Evidence

What do we know, and what are we assuming?

Evidence earns the right to continue investigating. It does not turn an illustrative model into a property-specific result or prove that every hotel has the same problem.

What to look at
  • The evidence class attached to every figure.
  • The difference between reported facts and arithmetic.
  • The assumptions carried with the $135K model.
Your decision Is the case strong enough to test without claiming it is already proven?

Step 04 · Economics

Where could value move—and what is still unpriced?

The market pressure, hotel model, and operator workload answer different questions. Switch between them; never collapse them into one confident revenue claim.

What to look at
  • Observed commission pressure in the cited report.
  • The assumptions behind the illustrative hotel model.
  • The missing delivery capacity, pricing, and renewal data.
Your decision Is there plausible room for an offer—and which unknown must you price first?

Step 05 · First test

What can you test before making a bigger claim?

The first engagement should reduce uncertainty, not hide it. Establish the baseline, repair the destination, respect permission, and define the result that would justify continuing.

What to look at
  • The baseline required before intervention.
  • The booking and phone path that must work first.
  • The stop conditions that prevent activity theater.
Your decision Can a bounded test produce a credible yes, no, or redesign signal?
Sheet 04 · Guardrails

The first test is bounded. So is the claim.

Start with permission and a baseline.

  1. Measure booking mix, commission, cancellations, missed calls, and repeat stays.
  2. Repair the mobile booking and phone path before running outreach.
  3. Begin with past guests who have permission to hear from the property.
  4. Track direct share before and after; use other metrics to diagnose the path.

Stop or redesign when:

  • The property cannot establish a credible baseline.
  • Guest permission or data access is unclear.
  • The destination cannot complete the promise.
  • Activity rises while direct share does not move.
