# Boundary Ledger motion language

Status: canonical direction, provisional implementation binding.

> **The frame establishes a stable world. Only the accountable change moves.**

Motion is not a way to keep the surface alive. It exposes a business operation, an evidence arrival,
an exception, or a change in ownership. Each event needs a readable before state, operation, after
state, and settle.

## Derived vocabulary

These operations are defined in the semantic core and selected—not reinvented—by Step 3:

1. **Establish** — reveal the current actors and relationship with no active accent.
2. **Trace** — expose an existing dependency, construction, or route.
3. **Route** — move a persistent object, customer, case, value, or claim through the current system.
4. **Interrupt** — stop, break, reject, or expose a failed handoff.
5. **Correct** — retain a legible prior mark while revising its consequence.
6. **Return** — activate the one oxide operator-controlled path.
7. **Pin** — attach evidence and provenance to the object or operation it supports.
8. **Settle** — stop nonessential motion and hold the changed relationship.

Rev D’s `gravity`, `resist`, `activate`, `reverse`, `expand`, and `resolve` remain useful narrative
descriptions only when they map to one of these checkable operations. “Expand” reveals already
understood tangible capabilities; it never just grows a generic node map.

## The restraint conflict

Boundary Ledger does not require slow openings. Start inside the operation when the argument calls
for it: a fee is already accumulating, a key is already moving toward an OTA, a case is already
blocked, or work is already leaving the operator’s control. The frame can be kinetic without losing
hierarchy because only the thesis-bearing action and its immediate consequence move.

Use this cold-open construction:

`current operation → visible consequence → 0.3–0.75s hold → oxide intervention → changed route → settle`

The conflict supplies energy. Camera shake, breathing images, glow, infinite pulse, and frantic cuts
do not.

## What moves

- one persistent customer, case, payment, key, claim, or unit of work;
- the current steel route while it is operating;
- one oxide path when the commitment becomes available;
- a tally only while the represented quantity is actually accumulating;
- real source material moving into the exact parameter it supports;
- a case entering and leaving a human judgment gate;
- a thesis phrase when type is the genuine subject.

The paper field, settled actors, source attribution, inactive branches, completed marks, caption
anchors, and already-landed docket remain still. Maximum concurrency is one meaningful action plus
its immediate causal reaction.

## Authored roughness over time

Do not animate a precise SVG and add turbulence. Use one of four authored methods:

1. reveal pre-drawn irregular stroke groups with uneven masks;
2. use two or three correction passes that preserve a readable abandoned mark;
3. expose short hand-drawn frame sequences made from independently authored marks;
4. move a stable tangible object through a rough model while the drawing itself remains still.

Rough marks can overshoot, stop short, double back, and vary in pressure. They may not jitter after
landing. Roughness is material history, not perpetual instability.

## Starting timing bands at 30fps

| Event | Duration |
| --- | --- |
| State switch or decision mark | 4–6 frames / 133–200ms |
| Label or short mark | 6–10 frames / 200–333ms |
| Rough stroke cluster | 8–14 frames / 267–467ms |
| Route traversal or handoff | 14–24 frames / 467–800ms |
| Model transformation or camera change | 18–36 frames / 600–1200ms |
| Causal settle | 6–10 frames / 200–333ms |
| Pre-reversal stillness | 9–22 frames / 300–750ms |
| Reading hold after a result | 24–45 frames / 800–1500ms |

These are defaults, not a beat grid. Locked words and word timestamps remain authoritative. Use
decisive ease-out entries, ease-in exits, and purpose-built ease-in-out repositioning. No bounce,
elastic overshoot, blur, glow, or parallax as a quality signal.

## Scene archetypes

### Human stake and constraint

A tangible human or owned object sits in the paper world. A steel dependency acts on it by pulling,
tolling, diverting, holding, or transferring. Hold the consequence. Do not imply that a customer
voluntarily chose a harmful route when the actual thesis is structural dependency.

### Working Model build

Establish two to four tangible actors, reveal the current steel path, move one persistent object,
show the toll or handoff, pause, activate the oxide route, move the same object through it, and hold
the complete model.

### Evidence to model

Show the real source with identifying context. Isolate the exact supported value or phrase, carry it
to the affected parameter, then change the model. Provenance stays pinned. An evidence surface is
the subject until extraction is complete.

### Decision gate

A case reaches the gate and stops. The criterion and accountable state become visible. Only the
selected branch moves after human judgment. Inactive branches remain legible and still.

### Thesis type

Typeset text becomes the subject only for a thesis, reversal, warning, exact quote, critical number,
or identity. Reveal by clause or line, mark one phrase, and hold. Do not duplicate the same phrase in
the caption rail at the same time.

### Outcome and earned pullback

The recurring object completes the changed route, a measurable state attaches, and the frame
returns to the human or operator. Pull back only after the viewer has learned the parts. Hold the
finished relationship.

## Docket-derived motion components

Do not paste the full web docket into every frame.

- **Identity docket** — episode or speaker identity; enters once and holds.
- **Evidence receipt** — source, date, claim state, and estimate/reporting caveat.
- **Decision record** — criterion, chosen branch, and human-review state.
- **Chapter rail** — short orientation without resetting the world.
- **Lower third** — name and role only; no badge collection.

These are accountable surfaces. They cut or slide into position, attach to their subject, and stop.
A verified mark resolves once and never pulses indefinitely.

## Transition grammar

The default is a cut. Use a designed continuity seam only when a real property persists:

- the same guest, key, payment, case, or value crosses the cut;
- a sourced value becomes a model parameter;
- a physical action becomes a system operation;
- the oxide route crosses into its consequence;
- a pullback reveals already learned state.

Match carrier identity, axis, direction, and velocity. Cut mid-motion. Use no more than two or three
transition families in a long film and one or two in a short clip. Reject paper tears, scribble
mattes, ink blots, blueprint wipes, page turns, crossfades by default, zoom punches, and decorative
whip pans.

## Aspect-ratio bindings

The rule is relationship equivalence, not geometric equivalence.

- **16:9:** horizontal routes can coexist. The complete static model may sit inside a wider world,
  with an attached evidence or identity surface. Documentary footage may establish Reality World.
- **9:16:** redraw vertically. Usually reveal one object and relationship at a time. Down can express
  dependency; the oxide return may reverse or climb only when that direction matches the operation.
- **1:1:** use a compact loop or diagonal relationship. If model and thesis cannot coexist legibly,
  make them consecutive beats.

Captions overlay the full composition; they do not create a dead reserved band. Avoid placing
critical small text directly under the caption rail, then judge final caption-over-content
legibility visually.

## Runtime boundary

This document defines behavior, not code. Blueprint Cinema applies it to an episode. HyperFrames is
the canonical designed-scene runtime under the current toolchain and must implement deterministic,
finite timelines. Resolve owns final conform, mix, captions, online, and delivery. Runtime presets,
generic house glows, ambient loops, or component libraries never override this language.
