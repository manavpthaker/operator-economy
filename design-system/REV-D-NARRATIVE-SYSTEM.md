# OE Rev D: Human Outcome System

> Working direction, 2026-08-17. This extends Rev C rather than replacing it in production. The hotel OTA-gravity concept is the first reference frame; validate the grammar across at least three episodes before promoting these rules into tokens and Remotion.

**View the system:** [`guidelines/Rev D Human Outcome System.html`](guidelines/Rev%20D%20Human%20Outcome%20System.html) includes a silent, playable 60-second cold-open prototype and cross-channel specimens.

## The change

Rev C made OE credible but treated the episode like a document: evidence objects, drawing sheets, restrained motion, and no faces in thumbnails. That works after someone is interested. It does not reliably create interest.

Rev D begins with a person losing or recovering something valuable. The operating system appears as the force that changes the outcome.

**Creative sentence:** A recognizable operator confronts a system that is pulling value away. OE reveals the counter-system that returns control and produces a tangible human outcome.

The hierarchy is fixed:

1. Human stakes
2. Business conflict
3. Tangible outcome
4. Operating model
5. Tools and evidence

The thumbnail collapses this sentence. The video, website, and posts progressively expand it.

## What survives from Rev C

- Paper `#F5F0E6`, Ink `#1A1A1A`, Schematic Navy `#14263E`, and Ledger Gold.
- Specific claims, source chips, marked estimates, and honest failure modes.
- Fragment Mono for published numbers and citations.
- The blueprint as the detailed implementation layer.
- Calm credibility. Drama comes from the situation, not hype language.

The grid, engineering title block, and working schematic remain useful evidence-layer components. They are no longer the default world for every frame.

| Token | Meaning | Hotel expression |
|---|---|---|
| `role.operator` | Person responsible for the outcome | Innkeeper |
| `role.customer` | Human relationship at stake | Returning guest |
| `role.ownedValue` | Asset the operator should retain | Guest relationship, booking, margin |
| `role.constraint` | System pulling value away | OTA dependency |
| `role.counterSystem` | New operator-controlled path | Direct-booking operation |
| `role.outcomeObject` | Physical proof of success | Room key passed directly |
| `role.evidence` | Defensible proof | Commission, booking, and margin figures |

### Color roles

- `constraint.cobalt`: external control, dependency, extraction. Use on real platforms only when the episode establishes that role.
- `counter.gold`: an OE-designed path, connection, or return flow. Gold is not decoration.
- `world.paper`: human possibility, daylight, physical business surfaces.
- `evidence.navy`: analysis, sources, architecture, and proof.
- `risk.brick`: verified loss or failure, never general drama.

### Spatial roles

- `direction.down`: extraction, dependency, loss of control.
- `direction.across`: the current operating reality.
- `direction.return`: gold motion against the default current.
- `direction.up`: recovered leverage only. Do not send the problem upward.
- `camera.human`: close enough to read a face, hand, and outcome object.
- `camera.system`: pulled back enough to understand flows and capabilities.

### Motion roles

- `motion.gravity`: value moves toward the constraint without a voluntary gesture.
- `motion.resist`: human effort slows the loss but cannot solve it alone.
- `motion.activate`: the gold counter-system switches on.
- `motion.reverse`: an asset or relationship changes direction.
- `motion.expand`: a simple gold path opens into capability nodes and tools.
- `motion.resolve`: the system terminates in a human action and measurable outcome.

## Full-video translation

### Narrative waveform

Every episode moves through five emotional states:

1. **Peril:** Show a specific person losing time, margin, ownership, or opportunity.
2. **Absurdity:** Explain why the default system produces this result. Steelman its useful role first.
3. **Reversal:** State the outcome the operator actually needs.
4. **Build:** Expand the gold counterforce into the operating model, tools, human review, and economics.
5. **Agency:** Return to the person operating a better system. Name the limits and first move.

### Hotel opening, 0:00-1:15

- `0:00-0:02` Start inside the thumbnail moment. Keys and reservation slips begin sliding downhill.
- `0:02-0:04.4` Overlay the OE logotype and universal sting on the moving scene. Do not cut to a separate bumper.
- `0:04.4-0:08` Lock the episode title briefly, then cut to real hotel footage.
- `0:08-0:20` VO names the contradiction over a human action and a recognizable booking surface: the hotel did the work, but the relationship leaves through another company's system.
- `0:20-0:35` Steelman the OTA: it supplied discovery and demand. Then show where useful distribution becomes dependency.
- `0:35-0:50` Freeze on the innkeeper and key. State the outcome: turn an OTA stay into a future direct relationship.
- `0:50-1:15` Activate the gold current. Pull from `camera.human` to `camera.system`; the current expands into capture, permission, message, offer, booking, and retention.

### Episode grammar

Use this sequence instead of treating the whole episode as an animated sheet:

`human scene -> branded market force -> source artifact -> system reveal -> live workflow -> economics -> human resolution`

Rev C components enter when their job begins:

- Citation chips prove claims.
- Schematics explain the expanded counter-system.
- Tool logos label capabilities, not the entire story.
- Gap figures show before/after economics.
- Title blocks package the blueprint at the end.

Alternate `camera.human` and `camera.system`. Never hold the schematic world for more than 25 seconds without returning to a person, place, artifact, or live workflow.

### Preview proof window

The first 30 seconds must also work as a silent credibility trailer. YouTube may play the opening inline, but its separate three-second preview is selected automatically from the first half of the video. We cannot choose that excerpt, so the entire first half should maintain visual credibility rather than concentrating all footage in the opening.

For the opening 30 seconds, require:

- one recognizable human action in the real operating environment;
- one branded market surface or interface when the brand is material to the story;
- one source, receipt, dashboard, or economic artifact;
- one visual glimpse of the operator-controlled counter-system;
- a meaningful composition change every 4-8 seconds.

Use five footage roles: `human_context`, `market_force`, `proof`, `process`, and `outcome`. Stock is acceptable only when it shows the exact place, object, or action being discussed. Generic atmosphere, abstract typing, empty offices, and decorative drone footage remain publish blockers.

## Voiceover

The authority remains `content-os/voice.md` and `studio/config/speech-profile.md`. Rev D changes performance and narrative temperature, not factual standards.

### Register

**Documentary rigor with lived urgency.** The narrator is an operator who notices an absurd system, understands why it exists, and is bothered enough to redesign it.

- Begin with a scene, action, or concrete contradiction. Do not begin with channel positioning.
- Use active verbs: pulls, keeps, routes, pays, returns, catches, loses.
- Let the problem section carry controlled indignation. Aim it at the system, never the customer.
- Move from urgency to curiosity during diagnosis, then to confidence during the build.
- Speak to one operator. Use Manav's observed restarts, questions, asides, and uneven sentence lengths.
- Keep caveats audible and concise. A marked estimate should sound careful, not apologetic.
- Do not sound uniformly calm. Calm is the baseline; contrast creates attention.

### Performance direction

| State | Pace | Pitch/energy | Pause behavior |
|---|---|---|---|
| Peril | 0.98-1.04x | alert, contained | short pauses after concrete losses |
| Absurdity | 0.94-1.00x | incredulous, conversational | pause before the contradiction lands |
| Reversal | 0.90-0.96x | decisive | one clean silence before the thesis |
| Build | 1.00-1.06x | forward, competent | breathe at capability boundaries |
| Agency | 0.92-0.98x | warm, grounded | space around limits and first action |

Avoid movie-trailer growl, breathless urgency, guru certainty, and polished pharmaceutical warmth.

## Music and sound

Retire the assumption that one continuous bed should carry every section. Rev D uses a small score language with silence between states.

### Motifs

- `motif.human`: tactile piano, muted guitar, upright bass, brushed percussion, or warm mallets. Imperfect, close, physical.
- `motif.constraint`: descending two- or three-note figure, low pulse, filtered mechanical texture. Never horror drones.
- `motif.counter`: ascending gold figure that answers the descending constraint motif.
- `motif.build`: dry percussion and a restrained modular pulse as capabilities connect.
- `motif.outcome`: human motif returns with wider harmony and less rhythmic pressure.

### Rules

- Cold-open with location sound and the constraint motif. Overlay the sting on continuing action at roughly `0:02`; never stop for a standalone bumper.
- Use suction, luggage roll, paper movement, key impact, and room tone as story-bearing sound.
- Drop to silence immediately before the reversal line.
- Introduce the gold motif only when the operator-controlled path appears.
- Do not run music continuously by default. Silence separates argument states.
- Avoid corporate inspiration, ukulele optimism, cinematic apocalypse, EDM risers, and constant trailer percussion.
- Preserve intelligibility and the existing final loudness targets; redesign arrangement before changing mastering.

The current `calm | build | silence` contract should evolve to:

`human | constraint | tension | silence | counter | build | resolve`

### Working score direction

**Episode lane A: operatic baroque × Afrobeat.** `Operatic Baroque Afrobeat.mp3` validates this direction. Lean into the contrast rather than muting it. Use Vivaldi-like string motion for ambition and precision, then let a confident Afrobeat rhythm give the operating system physical momentum. Start with low cello and fragments of the ostinato, gather percussion under extraction, cut to silence before the reversal, and enter the build at roughly 104–112 BPM with the full rhythmic and string pattern locked together.

**Episode lane B: ornate motion / downtempo electronic.** `Ornate Motion.mp3` validates the more restrained lane: sub pulse, tactile percussion, and controlled analog movement. It can sit more cleanly under dense explanation. Select between A and B by the episode's dramatic shape; do not force a universal background bed.

**OE logo/title sting — fixed across every episode:** `2.4 seconds`, free-time and tempo-neutral so either score lane can enter beneath its tail. Use one deep organic pulse, a three-note ascending D-minor/Dorian string mnemonic, and a precise metallic key/relay strike on the resolve. The first note introduces the logotype; the third locks the episode title. Keep the tail under `0.6 seconds`. No riser, trailer boom, choir, brass blast, or beat drop.

Generation/composer prompt:

> 2.4-second audio logo, no vocals. A memorable three-note ascending mnemonic in D minor/Dorian, played by intimate baroque strings over one deep organic drum pulse, resolving with a precise metallic key or relay strike and a short warm tail. Premium, intelligent, human plus system, confident but not heroic. Free-time and tempo-neutral so it bridges into Afrobeat, orchestral, or deep-house scores. No riser, boom, choir, brass blast, EDM drop, or melody beyond three notes.

If voice `yUXeTfC1IFOCSjGc96sQ` is owned or otherwise eligible for ElevenLabs remixing, generate a separate derivative at low prompt strength first. Preserve the source voice; do not replace it. Recommended prompt:

Shift from smooth, relaxed narration to a close, grounded documentary operator: more forward consonants, firmer endings, moderate pace, and clear contrast between contained urgency, conversational disbelief, decisive explanation, and warm resolution. ## Website translation

### Homepage

1. **Human outcome hero:** a real operator, customer, and tangible result. Headline names the change, not the publication format.
2. **The force working against them:** a short interactive extraction scene.
3. **The counter-system:** scrolling reverses the flow and expands gold into capabilities.
4. **Proof:** current Rev C source chips, figures, and case evidence.
5. **Blueprint:** the detailed implementation artifact.

### Episode page

Open with the human conflict. As the reader scrolls, transition from `camera.human` to `camera.system`:

`what is being lost -> why the default system does it -> desired outcome -> capability map -> current tools -> economics -> first implementation move`

Tool brands live inside capability details. They do not own the page hero unless the brand itself is the episode subject.

## LinkedIn and post translation

### Single image

Use the human conflict frame with one sentence of context. The post adds evidence; the image does not become a mini-carousel.

### Carousel

1. Human problem
2. System causing it
3. Economic consequence
4. Desired outcome
5. Gold counter-system
6. Capability and tool stack
7. Honest economics and failure mode
8. First operator move

The first four slides dramatize and diagnose. The final four explain and qualify. Keep the no-CTA and evidence rules owned by content-os.

### Short video

Use a single loop: extraction begins, the contradiction lands, gold reverses one object, then cut before the full stack resolves. The long-form episode earns the explanation.

## Production model

Create every episode in two coordinated visual distances:

- **Human plate:** people, place, conflict, outcome object, and branded constraint.
- **System map:** the same scene abstracted into flows, capabilities, tools, evidence, and economics.

The storyboard must explicitly mark the transition between them. Asset planning should collect real business surfaces and human actions before generic stock or diagrams.

For every footage beat, record `role`, `source`, `rights`, `narration_anchor`, and `preview_eligible`. Prefer original capture and licensed archival footage, then specific stock, then generated plates. Every clip must either establish reality, demonstrate a mechanism, prove a claim, or show the outcome; otherwise remove it.

## Validation before Rev D promotion

1. Test this grammar on the hotel episode plus two non-hospitality episodes.
2. Produce at least two thumbnail hypotheses per episode: problem-peril and outcome-control.
3. Run the 120 px test and YouTube Test & Compare where available.
4. Storyboard one 60-second opening with the new VO and score states.
5. Prototype one episode-page scroll from human scene to system map.
6. Confirm the system still supports citations, estimates, accessibility, reduced motion, and current release gates.
7. Promote semantic tokens into CSS, Remotion, and generation schemas only after the cross-category test passes.

## Decision boundary

Rev D is not "more cinematic" as decoration. Every dramatic device must identify one of four things: who is affected, what is being lost, which system causes it, or how control returns. If it does none of those, remove it.
