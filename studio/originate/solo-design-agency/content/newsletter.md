# One Person, $5,000 a Month, No Employees

There's a design agency that reportedly runs on $95 a month in tools, has zero employees, and — depending on which post you read — makes somewhere between $1M and $3.1M a year. There's also a venture-backed competitor selling the identical thing with 850 employees and a $15,000/month minimum. Same category. Three orders of magnitude apart in headcount.

This week's episode is about that gap, and about what's actually true versus what's just been repeated enough times to sound true.

## The setup

The reference case is Designjoy, run solo since 2017 by Brett Williams. It shows up constantly in "how I built a one-person business" content, usually with a single flattering revenue number attached. The problem: there isn't one number. Across Starter Story, Startup Stash, and various secondary blogs, the reported figure ranges from $1M to $3.1M for roughly the same period — and every version traces back to Brett's own posts. No audit exists at any point in that chain.

What is consistent, across every version: zero employees, roughly 35 concurrent clients, a flat $4,995/month price, no client meetings, about 5 hours of work a day.

On the other end sits Superside — same subscription-design model, reported at $44.9M ARR in 2024, ~850 employees, a $15,000/month minimum, and $35M raised. The gap between these two companies is either an efficiency story about what one person can now do, or a warning about what one person genuinely can't cover. The episode doesn't pick a side — it lays out both and lets you weigh it against your own risk tolerance.

We also ran our own small, honest data point: BusyLobby, a build-in-progress targeting hotels. Six cold outreach emails, one callback, zero signed clients as of this writing. One in six looks great against 2026 cold-email benchmarks (~3–5% average). It's also not a sample size — and we say so directly, instead of quietly calling it a 17% response rate.

## The takeaway

Three things worth carrying out of this one, regardless of whether you build this exact business:

1. **The tool stack collapsed, the judgment didn't.** Figma reports ~70% AI adoption among active users. Your prospective clients have access to the same generation tools you do. What they're paying for is the time and judgment to pick the right output — not the ability to generate one.
2. **Self-reported case studies need a confidence flag, not a headline.** The $1M–$3.1M Designjoy range is the only public data point at this solo scale — and it's unaudited. Use it as a directional signal, not a plan.
3. **Distribution is the actual moat, not the pricing page.** Designjoy's tool stack and pricing model are copyable in an afternoon. Its reported growth engine was years of Twitter presence — millions of cumulative impressions. The tools got cheap. The audience that buys from you didn't.
