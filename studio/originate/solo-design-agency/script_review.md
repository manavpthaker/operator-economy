## thesis
- (1) A design subscription agency sells ongoing creative work — logos, decks, landing pages, working sites — for a flat monthly fee instead of billing by the project. Startups and small companies use it because it's cheaper than hiring a designer and faster than posting a job.
  - source: Brainy Papers — productized design services model [vendor]
- (2) The reason one person can now do this is Figma. Its AI features hit seventy percent adoption among active users this year — the same layout and variant generation that used to take a team now runs inside a single file, operated by one person.
  - source: Figma Q1 2026 disclosure — 70% AI adoption [reported]
- (3) Built solo and part-time, this looks like a handful of retainer clients on the side. Built as a full-time operation, the reference case in this category runs roughly thirty-five concurrent clients on one flat price. Both versions run on the same stack — what changes is hours, not tools.
  - source: Medium/Zack Liu — Designjoy operating shape [reported, self-sourced]

## stack
- (1) The stack that shows up again and again in this category is two or three tools deep, not seven. Plan in Figma or Relume, produce in Figma Make, v0, or Lovable, ship in Framer, and generate imagery in Midjourney.
  - source: Guideflow — AI design tool stacking patterns [vendor]
- (2) Figma with Figma Make is the design surface — AI generation happens inside the same file, and it's free to start. Relume handles wireframing and sitemaps at thirty-eight dollars a month solo. v0 and Lovable turn a prompt into working code or a full site, running free to about twenty-five dollars a month at the tier a solo operator needs.
  - source: buildmvpfast — Framer/v0/Lovable pricing comparison [vendor]
- (3) Framer ships the final site for twenty to forty dollars a month. Midjourney covers hero imagery for ten to sixty. Claude handles copy and client-facing writing for twenty to a hundred. Designjoy's own reported tool cost is ninety-five dollars a month total — the client pays for their own software, not you.
  - source: startupfounderstories — Designjoy tool cost [reported ⚠️]

## playbook
- (1) Week one: pick a narrow niche — landing pages for SaaS, or hotel websites, or pitch decks — and build three demo pieces for real companies who haven't asked for them. No client yet. The demo is the pitch.
- (2) The scout-and-score approach I use on BusyLobby applies directly here: build a short list of target companies, score each one on how badly their current site or deck is underperforming, then build the fix before you ever email them.
  - source: First-party — busylobby pipeline methodology
- (3) Week two through four: send that finished demo cold, not a pitch deck about your services. Track who opens it. A tool like PostHog shows you exactly which prospects looked twice — those are your first calls.
  - source: First-party — busylobby PostHog analytics
- (4) Price the first client under market — somewhere in the twenty-five hundred range — to get a working case study fast, then move toward the four to seven thousand band once you have one finished project to show.
  - source: Brainy Papers — $2,500-$7,500 solo pricing band [vendor]
- (5) Month two onward is capacity math: at flat monthly pricing, adding client six is easier than getting client one, because the stack is already built. The harder question is where the ceiling sits before one person can't keep the turnaround promise.
