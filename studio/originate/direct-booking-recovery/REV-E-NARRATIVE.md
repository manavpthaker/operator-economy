# Direct Booking Recovery — Rev E Narrative Blueprint

## Editorial Spine

**Promise:** Show why independent properties lose control of the guest relationship, then install a practical system that shifts repeat and referred demand back to direct booking.

**Position:** This is not an argument for abandoning OTAs. They are powerful discovery engines. The operator outcome is a healthier mix: use OTAs for discovery, then earn the return trip directly.

**Narrative waveform:** `gravity → consequence → recognition → counter-system → installation → recovery → agency`

Target runtime: 9–11 minutes. The opening through the first thesis beat should run 35–50 seconds. The ident is a clean break: no narration underneath it.

## 01. Universal Ident — 0:22–0:25

No VO. A gold path reverses the down-right gravity vector, draws the OE monogram, then resolves to the Operator Economy wordmark. Universal sonic sting lands on the wordmark. The ident should feel like agency being restored, not a channel bumper.

## 02. Episode Thesis — 0:25–1:05

VO: “This is The Operator Economy. This week: direct booking recovery. Not replacing Booking.com or Expedia. Building the system that turns a discovered guest into a returning guest the property can reach directly.”

Visual: episode title, followed by a single guest journey. `DISCOVERED → BOOKED → WELCOMED → REMEMBERED → RETURNED`. The first booking may pass through an OTA; the return path turns gold.

VO: “For a ten-to-forty-room property, that system is rarely one person’s job. It is scattered across the website, the phone, the booking engine, the guest list, and the follow-up nobody had time to send. The opportunity is to operate those pieces as one outcome.”

Visual: five disconnected fragments join into one gold path. Outcome label: **MORE REPEAT AND REFERRED BOOKINGS, DIRECT**.

## 03. Gravity — 1:05–2:20

### Discovery has a price

Cloudbeds, a vendor selling hotel software, reports 63.4% OTA share for independent hotels. Show the vendor attribution prominently. Explain that headline commissions can rise to an all-in 18–30% with visibility and loyalty programs.

### The booking is less durable

Show one comparison only: `OTA cancellation 21.8%` versus `direct cancellation 10.6%`, from the same vendor-published dataset. The OTA column falls apart into cancelled booking tokens.

### Operator recognition

VO position: leaning on OTAs is rational, not negligent. A small property buys global discovery it could not reproduce alone. The problem begins when the property has no mechanism for retaining the relationship after the stay.

Human visual: quaint, residential B&B footage. One operator moves between guest care, housekeeping, and the phone. No office, laptop-at-desk, corporate lobby, or masked footage.

## 04. The Human Gap — 2:20–3:25

Use Coqui Coqui resort footage as memory, not generic luxury decoration.

VO: “At Coqui Coqui, the brand and website were only the beginning. The real work was making social, the site, the call, the booking, and check-in feel like one continued conversation. I could do that with a team of more than fifty people across the operation. A small innkeeper does not have that headcount.”

Visual: sensory resort arrival intercut with a continuous-line journey. Cut to the B&B operator as the line breaks into missed tasks.

Payoff: **The missing product is not another dashboard. It is operating capacity.**

## 05. The Counter-System — 3:25–5:05

Introduce the system as roles attached to a guest journey, not a logo wall.

1. **Discovery keeper** — maintains the property’s Google profile, photos, descriptions, and current offers.
2. **Destination keeper** — ensures the website, rate parity, booking engine, and phone can complete the promise.
3. **Relationship keeper** — records permission and useful guest context, then sends post-stay, review, referral, and re-book messages.
4. **Operator** — reviews exceptions, approves voice and offers, and owns the commercial judgment.

Only after each role is understood should tools appear beneath it: Google Business Profile; Mews, Cloudbeds, SiteMinder, or Little Hotelier; Twilio and a voice agent; Resend or equivalent; Make or n8n. Logos are implementation options, never the story.

End on the operating loop: `FIND → CONVERT → REMEMBER → RETURN → MEASURE`.

## 06. Installation — 5:05–7:15

### Step 1 — Measure the gravity

Pull booking mix, commission, cancellation, missed calls, direct conversion, and repeat bookings. Establish a before state. Never promise savings without this baseline.

### Step 2 — Repair the destination

Check rate parity, mobile booking friction, availability, cancellation language, phone coverage, and whether the property looks and sounds consistent from search result through check-in.

### Step 3 — Recover the known guest

Start with previous guests who have permission to hear from the property. Post-stay thank-you, review request, seasonal reason to return, and referral prompt. No cold automation disguised as hospitality.

### Step 4 — Publish the offer

The reviewed providers quote privately. Treat that opacity as a positioning opportunity, but do not invent a market price. Publish a clear scope and a pricing method tied to the work and the measured commission line.

### Step 5 — Report one outcome

Track direct share before and after. Supporting metrics diagnose the path; direct share is the operator outcome.

## 07. Market Proof and Business Model — 7:15–8:25

Compress high-end market proof into one screen: Mews announced a $300 million Series D at a $2.5 billion valuation in January 2026 and said the capital would support AI-driven hotel operations. Attribute the company-reported scale figures rather than presenting them as independent verification.

Interpretation: capital is flooding into the infrastructure, but a twenty-room property still needs someone to configure the flow, direct the agents, check the output, and prove the result.

Keep the low-end evidence honest. ZipRecruiter’s reported $94,500–$140,000 range is salary data for full-time-equivalent revenue-management work, not a client price and not an income promise. It establishes that the work exists; it does not establish what this service should charge.

## 08. Recovery — 8:25–9:20

Return to the guest from the opening. Their first stay may have arrived through an OTA. After checkout, the property earns permission, remembers the experience, and offers a relevant reason to return. The second booking follows the gold path to the property.

VO: “The win is not eliminating the platforms that introduced the guest. It is no longer paying to meet the same guest from scratch every time.”

Visual outcome stack: `GUEST REMEMBERED → RELEVANT RETURN → DIRECT BOOKING → WARMER WELCOME`. Human arrival footage completes the loop.

## 09. Blueprint and Bridge — 9:20–End

Soft blueprint mention after the recovery payoff: the downloadable blueprint contains the audit, implementation sequence, and monthly scorecard.

Close on agency: “OTAs can keep doing what they do best: discovery at global scale. The operator’s job is to make sure discovery becomes a relationship the property can keep.”

End screen bridges to the missed-call episode as the next mechanism in the same guest journey. No generic subscribe speech.
