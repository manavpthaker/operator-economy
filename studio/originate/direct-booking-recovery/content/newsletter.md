# Hotels Are Paying 18-30% to Book Their Own Rooms

Independent hotels lose about two-thirds of their bookings to Booking.com and Expedia — and pay 18-30% in all-in commission to do it, once you add in loyalty programs like Booking.com's Genius or Expedia's Accelerator. That's according to Cloudbeds' 2026 State of Independent Hotels report, which analyzed roughly 90 million bookings.

Run the arithmetic on a 20-room hotel at a $180 average rate and 70% occupancy — an illustrative model, not a universal number — and that commission comes to roughly $135,000 a year. Money that never touches the hotel's own website.

The capital is already flowing toward fixing this. Mews raised $300 million in January 2026 at a $2.5 billion valuation — the largest funding round hospitality software has ever seen — and now moves $19.7 billion a year across 42 million bookings. Cloudbeds has raised $250 million, including from SoftBank, and serves 27,000 properties. Both are building the infrastructure. Neither is going to install it in your 20-room independent that's never had a revenue manager.

That's the opening this week's episode covers: not the software, but the standing task force that runs it. A booking engine doesn't generate demand — it catches the guest who's already looking. Something has to keep pushing traffic toward it, every day, without a payroll line for a full-time marketing hire.

That something is three narrow AI agents, not one do-everything assistant. One keeps the property's profile — photos, descriptions, pricing — current everywhere a guest might search, starting with a free Google Business Profile most independents let go stale. One runs outreach to past and lookalike guests through Make or n8n and Claude, so it goes out on schedule whether or not anyone opens a laptop. One prompts reviews and referrals after checkout. None of them answers the phone. None of them is a receptionist.

## The takeaway

Direct booking recovery isn't a website project — we spoke with an operator who built exactly this flow at Coqui Coqui with a 50-person team to lean on. A single-operator bed-and-breakfast doesn't have that headcount, and that gap is the opportunity: standing in for manpower a small property was never going to hire.

The honest math, though, cuts both ways. Software runs a few hundred dollars a month by rough estimate — cheap against the $135K it's meant to offset. But none of the vendors most commonly named for this work (TCRM, Revenuenaire, Catala, HotelMinder) publish a price, which means the real market rate is unknown, and the real cost is your labor directing the agents, not the software license. Run this for 3-4 properties and it's realistic supplemental income. Run it for 10-12 and the workload tracks toward the $95K-$140K/year freelance revenue-management range reported by ZipRecruiter — salaried full-time-equivalent pay data, not a promise of what any one hotel will pay you.

The failure mode is real too: a hotel signs, sees one good month, and doesn't renew because nobody proved the number moved. Whether your pricing model survives that conversation is the actual test — and it's the one most operators in this space are avoiding by not publishing a price at all.
