## stack
- (1) The instrument comes first, and it is the one piece you may not own. A certified assessment gives you the engagement and individual reports the whole method runs on. Verify what your license lets you do with that output before you build anything on top of it, because that contract is the foundation and the biggest risk at once.
- (2) The analyst is a Claude project, running twenty to a hundred dollars a month depending on volume. You load it with your scoring logic, your spreadsheets, and one anonymized past engagement, and it turns raw assessment data into draft deliverables. It is the piece that used to be a salaried analyst you could not afford.
  - source: public pricing — Claude Pro/Max ($20–100/mo)
- (3) The methodology lives in a spreadsheet, which costs nothing and is what makes the automation possible in the first place. One standardized intake template. Assessment exports, headcount, tenure, turnover history, org structure. If it is not in the template, you do not accept the engagement, because a clean input is what lets the machine run without you rebuilding it every time.
- (4) The deliverables are four generated outputs, not four hand builds. A client dashboard as one self-contained web page. A ninety day retention plan. A profile per leader. And the deck that presents it. Each one is a prompt plus a template, and all of it renders for free on top of the tools you already pay for.
- (5) All in, the fixed cost lands under a hundred and fifty dollars a month, and a portal only enters the picture in phase two, when a paying client asks to log in and track over time. The margin on a five to seven thousand dollar engagement, priced as an estimate for now, is basically your time. So how do you turn this stack into a first paying client?
  - source: public pricing — stack totals (< $150/mo); engagement price is blueprint estimate

## playbook
- (1) Week one is not sales, it is foundations.
