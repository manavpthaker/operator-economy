Most small businesses that go up for sale never close. Work from the Exit Planning Institute puts the share that do at roughly three in ten. At the same time, in the IBBA and M&A Source Market Pulse survey for Q2 2026, almost nine in ten deals above five million dollars drew at least three offers. Those are advisers reporting their own closings, not an audited database.

Buyers are competing for some businesses while most listings fail. The gap is usually inspection: the business runs through the owner, and a buyer cannot price what he cannot inspect.

Everyone around a small-business sale is paid when it closes. Nobody is paid to make it closeable years earlier. This episode builds that missing job as a one-person, fixed-fee sale-readiness practice: what it does, what it must not do, how the licensing line shapes the price, and the arithmetic.
