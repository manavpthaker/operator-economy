## Short 01 · Tue 10/6

```
The first tool for getting a small business ready to sell fits on one page: a list of what stops if the owner steps away for thirty days.

How to build it:
Ask the owner to walk you through a month without them.
Circle every answer that turns into "I call," "I approve," or "I know."
Beside each one, write who else could act, what they'd need, and what stops if nobody can.

That page is a dependency map. It's a working diagnostic, not a sale or valuation prediction, and thirty days is a test setting rather than a proven threshold.
```

## Short 02 · Wed 10/7

```
Getting a small business ready to sell sounds like M&A work. The work before a deal is closer to operations.

When the owner steps away, a buyer needs to know three things: whether customers still get served, whether decisions still get made, and whether the records can still be found.

An experienced operator can trace those dependencies and write down how the business actually runs. AI can organize the interview material and draft the procedures. It can't decide which gap a buyer will question. That judgment is the job.

Valuation, finding a buyer, and negotiating stay outside it.
```

## Short 03 · Thu 10/8

```
Charge a cut of the sale, and preparing a business to sell can start to look like brokering it.

A fee tied to the sale can trigger brokerage rules, depending on where you operate. One bounded model charges a fixed fee for defined preparation work:
– map the owner dependencies
– organize the records
– document how the business runs
– deliver a readiness plan

That fee is the same whether the sale closes or never happens.

Fixed-fee wording doesn't settle every legal question. It gives you a clear scope to take to local counsel. This is not legal advice; verify the rules where you operate.
```

## Short 04 · Fri 10/9

```
Before pitching owners on sale-readiness work, there's a cheaper first conversation: a business broker.

Ask one question: what happens to a business you can't list because it isn't ready?

If they already fix those for free, the referral channel may be closed.
If they turn them away or send them elsewhere, ask what was missing and who handles it now.

A referral shows access, not demand. Only an owner agreeing to pay starts to prove the business.
```
