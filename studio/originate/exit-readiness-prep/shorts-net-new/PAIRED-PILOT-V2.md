## Pilot 01: Thirty Days, No Owner

### Why it pulls

The viewer gets a test they can perform while listening. The repeated word `I` becomes visible
evidence of owner dependency, and the Short resolves by showing how to turn that evidence into a
map.

### YouTube Short

**Spoken copy**

Explain how your business runs for thirty days without using the word "I."

"I call the customers. I approve the exceptions. I know where the records are." If the answer keeps
falling back to the owner, circle it.

Beside each circle, write who else could act, what they would need and what stops if the owner is not
there.

That is a first working map of where the business still depends on one person.

That map does not prove the business would fail or sell for less. It shows what still lives in one
person's head.

EP007 asks whether clearing that working map can become a fixed-fee business.

**Narrow payoff:** The viewer can draft a working owner-dependency map from the thirty-day answer.

**Larger episode loop:** What the bounded service includes, where it stops and whether an owner will
pay for it.

### LinkedIn Page video

**Working title:** `Profit Can Hide Dependency`

**Spoken copy**

A profitable P and L can still hide how much of the business lives in one person's head.

Ask: "How would this business run for thirty days without the owner?" Then answer without using the
word "I."

Circle every answer that still begins with "I call," "I approve" or "I know where that is."

## Pilot 02: The Spreadsheet and the Stopwatch

### Why it pulls

The first frame contains a contradiction: `$96K IN SALES` still misses the selected target. The
Short then catches two tempting but unsupported spreadsheet repairs and replaces them with two
different tests.

### YouTube Short

**Spoken copy**

This model misses my target. I'm not fixing it.

Eight modeled engagements at twelve thousand produce ninety-six thousand gross. After ten thousand
in modeled costs, eighty-six thousand remains before owner pay and tax. It falls short of my one
hundred twenty thousand dollar target. These are assumptions, not observed performance or a
forecast.

More clients assumes the work fits and the clients can be found. A higher fee assumes someone pays
it.

So I would time one engagement and make one real offer. That gives me one delivery-time observation
and one price response. Neither proves the market.

EP007 adds the channel test and the conditions that would stop me from building it.

**Narrow payoff:** Collect one delivery-time observation and one price-response observation before
changing the spreadsheet.

**Larger episode loop:** The complete offer, operating boundary, channel test and stop conditions.

## Pilot 03: Agreement Is Not Demand

### Why it pulls

The opening exposes a timing mismatch: preparation may be needed before the transaction that funds
the familiar deal roles exists. The payoff is not the metaphorical empty seat. It is a market test
the viewer can use.

### YouTube Short

**Spoken copy**

The work can exist. The budget might not.

Years before a sale, an owner may need operations written down, records cleaned and the work in
their head made visible. But there may be no buyer or deadline.

Near a transaction, brokers, accountants and lawyers have clear roles. That can be fair when the
owner does not want to pay for a sale that may never happen.

So this may be a service. Or work everyone agrees with and nobody funds.

Make one paid offer. If an owner says it matters but will not pay yet, that is agreement. It is not
evidence of demand. The warning is when that keeps happening.

EP007 tests the other risk: brokers may already do enough of this work free.

**Narrow payoff:** Agreement with the problem is not demand evidence; record the response to a paid
offer and look for a repeated pattern.

**Larger episode loop:** The bounded offer and whether brokers already perform enough of the work
free to remove its front door.

**Related Video close:** The final EP007 sentence and native Related Video cue remain gated on the
canonical target and Studio readback.

**Pinned comment draft:** The role map is an incentive model, not a compensation survey. The first
test is paid commitment before a sale deadline exists. EP007 builds the proposed offer and the
second answer that could stop it. Open the Related Video under the channel name.

### LinkedIn Page video

**Working title:** `Agreement Is Not Demand`

**Spoken copy**

A service gap is not proof of demand. It may be work with no budget yet.

Near a business sale, brokers, accountants, lawyers and advisers have clear roles. That can be fair

**OE Page post draft**

Agreement with a problem is not evidence of demand.

In the episode's incentive model, brokers, accountants, lawyers and wealth advisers have clear roles
around a transaction. That timing can be fair to an owner who does not want to pay for a sale that
may never happen.

Preparation can begin years earlier: document operations, clean records and surface owner
dependencies. The timing gap may support a fixed-fee service. It does not prove a budget exists.

The first move is a paid offer before a buyer or deadline exists. If an owner agrees and defers,
record that conversation as no demand evidence. A repeated pattern is the warning, not one polite
conversation.
