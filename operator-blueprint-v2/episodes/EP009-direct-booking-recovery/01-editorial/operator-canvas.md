## 1. Operator

Who could credibly run this business: a marketing-and-operations generalist with hospitality judgment who can read a booking-mix report, work a booking engine and a Google Business Profile, write a guest message an innkeeper would be proud to send, handle consent and email-law hygiene, and hold a money conversation with an owner. `PARALLEL`: research brief, "Required skills or credentials".

Relevant experience, access, or advantage: hospitality work, an innkeeper relationship, or prior hotel-software sales or support. The advantage is being let into the extranet, the property system, and the front desk. `OBSERVED` for the host's own bounded vantage: CLM-016.

What the operator does personally at the beginning: every audit, every ceiling calculation, every direct-path test, the consent-capture design, the voice of every message, the owner conversation.

What the operator should not need to be: a hotel revenue manager, a licensed anything, an agency, a software reseller. No credential is required. `OBSERVED`: research brief.

Not a fit: someone who wants to sell software, run paid advertising, or promise a property it can leave the sites.

## 2. Buyer and beneficiary

Economic buyer: the owner or owner-operator of a US independent lodging property of roughly 20 to 40 rooms with no marketing or revenue hire. `OBSERVED` for the buyer relationship, `UNKNOWN` for band size: CLM-012 (AHLA counts 33,200-plus small-business properties, not a room band).

Day-to-day user: the front desk, which carries the consent capture at check-in.

Ultimate beneficiary: the returning guest, who books direct at a rate the property sets.

Buyer trigger: a visible commission line on the monthly invoice, a 2026 Genius visibility change that asks for a deeper discount, a stretched desk, or the audit's own ceiling number placed in front of the owner. `MODELED`: triggers inferred from CLM-001, CLM-005, CLM-012, not surveyed.

Qualified-buyer rule: owner-run, roughly 20 to 40 rooms, no marketing or revenue hire, most bookings through the sites, no consented guest list.

Unsuitable-buyer rule: under roughly 20 rooms. On base commission with low site share (recoverable a few hundred a month). Already retaining an agency. Chain affiliated. Unwilling to grant extranet, property-system, and front-desk access.

## 3. Costly problem

Job the buyer is trying to accomplish: fill rooms at the lowest sustainable cost of acquisition and keep the guests it has already served.

Current failure or friction: most reservations arrive through the sites with a commission attached. The site hides the guest's email behind an alias and asks partners to keep communication on-platform. The desk has no time to build a first-party list. So the property pays the same introduction fee for the same guest's next stay. `OBSERVED`: CLM-001 (63.4 percent OTA share of independent bookings in 2025, global, vendor), CLM-002 (direct channels 50.9 percent of European overnight stays, HOTREC), CLM-008 (alias policy, primary via amendment 01), CLM-012 (65 percent of surveyed hotels report staffing shortages).

Operational, financial, or strategic cost: a layered commission on most bookings (mechanics primary via CLM-005; base-rate band secondary, roughly 15 percent in most European markets plus Preferred Partner and Visibility Booster). Site bookings cancel at 21.8 percent versus 10.6 percent direct in the vendor dataset. `OBSERVED` (vendor, attributed): CLM-001.

Why the buyer acts now rather than later: OTA share rose in 2025. The 2026 Genius change reduced guaranteed placement for the minimum discount. Free booking links exist and go unconfigured. `OBSERVED`: CLM-001, CLM-005 (secondary for the 2026 change), CLM-009.

Current alternatives and their limits: do nothing. Buy a PMS or booking-engine bundle and assume it produces direct bookings (Little Hotelier about $29 to 39 plus 1 percent, or about $109 flat, CLM-010). Hire a hotel marketing agency from $1,500 a month (CLM-011). Engage an outsourced revenue manager, quote-only (CLM-011). The spend exists. The fit at 20 to 40 rooms is `UNKNOWN`.

What is not claimed: no RevPAR uplift, commission saved, or direct share gained as an observed result. The vendors' uplift claims are excluded (analogy map ANA-009).

## 4. Offer

Plain-English offer: a paid direct-booking audit, then a flat monthly retainer covering three jobs (findable, bookable, remembered), with one number reported monthly, direct share of bookings before and after.

What is included, audit: booking mix, commission line, cancellation by channel, the ceiling on real inputs, a direct-path test as a guest (rate versus the site, mobile booking completion, cancellation-policy readability, phone at a busy hour), and a check of whether the registration card captures the guest's own email. A written recommendation: retainer, audit-only, or not this property.

What is included, retainer: findable (Google Business Profile current, free booking links live through the property's connectivity partner, photos and offers), bookable (direct rate parity, mobile booking flow, missed-call fallback), remembered (consent capture at check-in and on wifi with a stated purpose line, post-stay thank-you, review request, seasonal return offer, referral prompt, every sensitive send read by the operator), and the monthly direct-share scorecard.

What is excluded: pricing strategy and revenue management. Paid advertising and metasearch spend management. Any marketing to masked OTA addresses. Any advice to leave the sites. Software resale.

Engagement shape: audit, then managed service.

Time to first meaningful result: the audit returns the ceiling and the direct-path findings inside the engagement. Direct-share movement, if any, is measured monthly against the audit baseline and is `UNKNOWN` until observed.

Evidence label: `PARALLEL` for the format (ANA-002 outsourced revenue management, ANA-003 agency retainers with direct-share reporting), `MODELED` for the combination. No verified operator sells this bundle at disclosed economics (research brief, "Direct evidence").

## 5. Buyer result

Result being purchased: a documented baseline, a tested and fixed direct path, a consented first-party guest list that grows monthly, and one number the owner can act on.

How the result is measured: direct share of bookings, monthly, against the audit baseline.

Baseline required: the audit's booking mix, commission line, cancellation by channel, and direct-path findings.

Primary acceptance measure: the audit is accepted when the owner holds the four inputs, the ceiling, the direct-path findings, and the written recommendation. The retainer is accepted month by month on the delivered scorecard.

Why one measure could mislead, and why it is used anyway: direct share moves with seasonality and with site promotions. It is still the honest single number because it is observable in the property's own reports and it is what the owner is buying. The scorecard carries cancellations, missed calls, and list size as explanation, never as the outcome.

Leading indicators: consented emails captured per month, direct-path test passing, free booking link live.

Lagging indicators: direct share, repeat-guest direct bookings.

What the operator can influence but not guarantee: whether direct share moves. Commission saved remains a labeled hypothesis until measured. `UNKNOWN`.

## 6. Delivery system

### Before delivery

Qualification and intake: apply the buyer rules. Ask for four numbers. Run the ceiling in the first conversation. Decline unsuitable properties.

Required access, data, permissions, or integrations: extranet reports, property-system reports, Google Business Profile access, the property's booking engine, and a property-side consent process at check-in. All permissioned by the owner, all inside the owner's own accounts.

### Core workflow

1. Pull extranet and property-system reports. Compute the commission line and the ceiling on real inputs. Owner: operator. Output: baseline and ceiling.
2. Test the direct path as a guest on a phone. Owner: operator. Output: findings and fix list.
3. Fix findability: Google Business Profile, free booking links through the connectivity partner, photos, offers. Owner: operator inside the property's accounts. Output: live direct placement.
4. Design consent capture at check-in and on wifi. The registration card asks for the guest's own email with a stated purpose. Owner: operator designs, front desk runs. Output: a growing consented list. `OBSERVED` constraint: CLM-008.
5. Build the post-stay sequence. A language model drafts, an automation tool schedules off checkout dates, the operator approves voice and every sensitive send. Output: thank-you, review request, seasonal return offer, referral prompt.
6. Report direct share monthly against the baseline. Owner: operator. Output: the scorecard.

### Quality and exception handling

Human review: the operator sets the voice, approves offers, decides who receives a message, and reads every sensitive send. No message goes to a masked address.

Failure route: if direct share does not move, the scorecard says so and the operator diagnoses (seasonality, site promotion, broken direct path, low consent capture) rather than decorating the report. If the audit shows base commission and low site share, the recommendation is audit-only or not this property.

Escalation boundary: anything touching rate-parity contract terms, consumer law on rate display, or EU guest data beyond consent-first handling routes to the owner's own counsel.

### After delivery

Handoff, reporting, support, or renewal: the scorecard on the first of each month. Renewal depends on the number moving. The property owns every account, every list, and every message template. Revocation is immediate on request. `MODELED`.

## 12. First 30-day construction and validation plan

Question being tested: will an owner in the band pay for the audit, and then fund a retainer priced under the property's own ceiling?

Business asset or capability constructed before testing: the audit (checklist, ceiling calculator, phone-side direct-path test, consent-capture check), written so it could be handed to someone.

First paid wedge to offer: the audit at $1,200 (modeled).

Smallest credible test inside that construction: three audits, one unpaid for an owner already known, two at the fee. The ceiling and a retainer under it placed in front of each owner. The answer, the objection, and the owner's current approach to the commission line recorded.

Target participants: three properties in the band the operator can visit. Two PMS or channel-manager partner managers, one innkeeper-association contact, two independent-hotel agencies (smallest retained property).

What must be built before testing: the audit and the ceiling calculator.

What must not be built yet: the post-stay sequence, the automation, the scorecard template. Those follow the first yes.

Success signal: one owner pays the audit fee. One owner agrees to a retainer at or below the ceiling. Audit delivery lands inside 15 hours.

Failure signal: no owner funds $600 a month. Agencies already serve the band at prices owners prefer. Audits show recoverable commission systematically below the model.

Decision at day 30: continue building / revise (move the band to 40 to 80 rooms, or sell the audit alone) / return / stop.

Maximum time and cash at risk: roughly 36 audit hours plus calls, and the operator-side tools for one month.

Days 31 to 90: deliver the first retainer. Replace the 8-hour assumption with recorded hours. Put the first scorecard in front of an owner. Rerun the operator's arithmetic on observed inputs. Decide: continue, move the band, or audit-only. Add nothing to the offer until a paying owner and the hours record say it should.
