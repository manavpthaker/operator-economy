## Recommendation

Decision: **eligible, subject to the mandatory owner review at 70** — and Reviewer A recommends the owner treat it as **continue research** until the first validation test is run, because the score sits on the line and the business sits near its own ceiling.

Evidence class: **adjacent synthesis**

Required disclosure: OTA share of independent bookings, cancellation by channel, and the Genius change are **observed but vendor- or OTA-published and attributed** every time; European direct share, the 2024 ECJ ruling, the 2026 Amsterdam filing, and the OTAs' 2025 flows and blended take rates are **observed**; the service format is **transferred** from outsourced revenue management and independent-hotel marketing agencies, which are bought by larger properties; the recovery ceiling, the operator's price, hours, capacity, and contribution are **modeled** — `modeled scenario, not observed performance or an earnings forecast`; that the service raises direct share, and that a 20-40-room owner pays under the ceiling, are **hypotheses**. No RevPAR uplift, commission saved, or direct share gained may be stated as a result. The property should not be told to leave OTAs.

Rationale: This scores 70 and lands on the threshold inside the calibration zone. Compared with the V1 package it is better evidenced where V1 was weakest — the buyer's cost is now anchored in the OTAs' own filings and an association study rather than one vendor report, the salary-as-labor-value and vendor-uplift transfers are removed, and the guest-data constraint is treated as design rather than nuisance. It is also more honest about the thing V1 never computed: the property's commission line bounds the retainer, and at 20 rooms that bound is about a thousand dollars a month on modeled inputs. That honesty costs points in economics and go-to-market, and it should.

Two weaknesses are real and are not argued away. The level of OTA dependence in the **US 20-40-room band** is not independently sourced, so the buyer factor cannot score higher until it is. And the offer parallels are bought by properties that can fund them; whether a 30-room inn funds a $600-1,000 retainer is the whole business and is unknown. Both are first-order tests in the thirty-day plan, and either could kill the candidate or move the band to 40-80 rooms.

One sourcing condition carries to editorial and to the promotion record if the owner promotes: the Booking.com partner pages could not be opened on 2026-09-03. Commission mechanics and the guest-data policy are load-bearing and are currently secondary. Open them, record what they say on the day, and only then lock a script.
