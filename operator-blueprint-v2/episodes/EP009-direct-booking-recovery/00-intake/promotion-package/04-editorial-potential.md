Narrative verdict: **pass**

Failure rule check: this is not a tool list. The tools appear only inside the three jobs, and the story is carried by one guest's two stays and one arithmetic result. The risk named in the candidate brief — collapsing into "ten ways to increase direct bookings" — is real and is controlled by leading with the masked alias and the ceiling rather than the stack.

### Search-volume exception

Exact-query volume is recorded as **not measurable**. A measurement attempt was made on 2026-09-03: Google Trends returned HTTP 429 on two queries (CLM-018), and no licensed exact-volume tool is available to this operation.

The exception is properly invoked. Consequential interest passes on SEC-filed flows and a filed damages claim; triangulation passes on four independent families, none of which sells the service being examined. "The market is new" is not offered — the market is old; what is new is the 2026 visibility rule, the litigation, and the commission-free direct placement.

## Overall editorial verdict

Narrative engine: **pass**

Audience pull: **pass**

Overall editorial potential: **pass**

Story spine: `an innkeeper gives a guest a good stay; the reservation carries a masked alias and the messaging window closes a week after checkout` -> `the guest returns a year later through the same OTA and the property pays to meet them again, while the OTA's 2026 rule asks for a deeper discount to stay visible` -> `discovery is rented per booking, the channel withholds the contact, and nobody at a short-staffed property owns the post-stay path` -> `an audit, a tested direct path, consent captured at the property, known guests re-booked, and one number reported monthly` -> `the retainer must sit under the property's own recoverable commission, which at 20 rooms is roughly a thousand dollars a month on modeled inputs` -> `the viewer can run the ceiling on any property in four inputs and decide build, test, or reject on their own cost of time`

Working audience promise: A small hotel pays to meet the same guest twice, and the booking site keeps the guest's email. Here is the service that gives the relationship back — and the arithmetic that decides whether anyone can afford to sell it.
