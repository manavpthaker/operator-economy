## Attempts to break the candidate

1. **Is this V1 with a smaller room count?** Partly. The business is the same; what changed is that the buyer band is narrowed, the salary and uplift transfers are gone, the guest-data constraint is designed in, and the ceiling is computed. Those are real improvements. But the reframing did not produce a new buyer, a new offer, or new evidence that anyone pays — it produced a sharper statement of why the price is small. A rescripted episode must own that, or it is a re-cut.
2. **Is 63.4% the buyer's problem or Cloudbeds' marketing?** Both. The dataset is large and the company sells the alternative. HOTREC's association study says European direct channels still hold about half of overnight stays. The package keeps both visible, which is correct, and neither describes a 30-room US inn. Until validation step 1 produces three real booking mixes, the band's dependence is an assumption wearing a vendor's number.
3. **Are the commission mechanics verified?** Not at source. Booking.com's partner pages returned HTTP 403 on every attempt. The mechanics in CLM-005 come from two 2026 blogs that describe those pages. That is acceptable for a Step 0 model and not acceptable for a script. Reviewer B treats the factual-blocker gate as conditional on opening them, and would fail the gate at promotion if they remain unopened.
4. **Does the ceiling make the business or kill it?** At 20 rooms it nearly kills it: $1,000-1,400 a month recoverable on modeled inputs, of which the operator can take perhaps $600. At 40 rooms it roughly doubles and the agencies start at $1,500. The viable band may be a 10-room window between "too small to fund it" and "already served by an agency." The package names this; it does not resolve it, and cannot without the owner conversations.
5. **Does the class action prove US demand?** No. It proves 15,000 European hotels believe parity clauses cost them money and that an association will organize them. It is consequential behavior and it is context. The package uses it as timing, which is right, and the audience-pull gate leans on it more than a US-only episode should.
6. **Is the POV synthesis real under Step 0.3?** The ceiling: yes — specific, formula shown, absent from the coverage reviewed. The guest-record expiry: it is a design reframing of a fact that Hospitality Net published on 25 August 2026 and Toedt published in 2015. The package says so. Credit the reframing, not the finding.
7. **Is there an episode or a calculator with a story wrapped around it?** There is an episode if the guest's two stays and the owner's decision under the ceiling carry it. The V1 asset library is a risk here: it was built for "install the stack," and a script that reuses it will drift back to the stack.
8. **Could a generalist do this without hospitality background?** Yes, mechanically. Whether they can write a post-stay message an innkeeper is proud to send is the actual product, and the package is right to make judgment the service. It is also right that this is unmeasured.

- Narrative engine: **pass**.
- Audience pull: **pass** — consequential interest and triangulation are met on hotel-side evidence; the operator audience is unmeasured and the gate does not require it.
- POV: **pass, bounded** — real and honestly limited; the do-not-state phrasing is honoured.
- Factual and source blockers: **pass, conditional** — clear for a blueprint that teaches the ceiling and the consent-first flow; **not clear** for any script that states commission mechanics or the guest-data policy until Booking.com's own pages are opened and recorded.
- Legal, permission, access, and guest blockers: **pass** — manageable within OTA terms, CAN-SPAM, GDPR for EU guests, and property-controlled accounts.

## Decision

Decision: **continue research**

Evidence class: adjacent synthesis

Production promotion authorized: no

Reason: The package supports an honest episode about why a small property pays twice for the same guest and what a recovery service can charge against that. It does not yet support the claim that a 20-40-room owner will pay it. Reviewer B would reopen the candidate on three things, all inside the thirty-day plan: three real booking mixes from properties in the band; one owner's answer to a retainer priced under the ceiling; and Booking.com's partner pages opened and recorded. If the answers are favourable the score moves into the calibration zone on evidence rather than on framing; if the owner says no, the band moves to 40-80 rooms and the candidate is rescored against the agencies.
