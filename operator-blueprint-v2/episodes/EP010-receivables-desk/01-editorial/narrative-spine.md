## Story in one sentence

Software will now chase a small firm's late invoice for free, and the money still doesn't move when the customer answers and nobody at the firm is paid to reply, so the only thing left to sell is owning that list, which one person might sell below the human desks and above the tools, if owners will pay more than the tool costs and the viewer's state allows it.

## Point of view and voice architecture

Short spoken company name: a receivables desk

One-sentence plain definition: It follows up a small firm's late invoices under that firm's name, and a person handles the ones a machine should not touch.

Precise internal operating description: in the thesis, not spoken on first listen.

Opportunity thesis: sending became nearly free and late payment did not go away on the one survey read. Firms without AR staff have nobody who owns the invoice after the reminder. Sellers already charge for that part at $599 and up.

Operator thesis: the tools are open to the buyer too, so the advantage is AR judgment and being trusted to write under another firm's name, in a niche the viewer already knows.

Entry-wedge thesis: one niche, a state check, five offers, one client's list for one paid month with hours logged.

Central thesis to be earned after orientation: the sending is gone as a business. The owning is the business, if owners will pay for it.

Common bad framing: late payment is a sending problem, so the agent that sends solves it.

What a fair skeptic gets right: for many firms a fixed ladder plus one phone call is enough, a wrong PO is a two-minute fix, and asking for the PO at onboarding prevents some of this for free.

Final ruling: BUILD, bounded. If the free sort shows the list is only a bell problem, sell nothing and say buy the tool.

Company-level verdict: BUILD (owner approved, decision 1004)

Target of earned contempt: marketing that implies the job is done once the message is sent, recovery figures quoted by the people selling the recovery, and any pitch that skips the state question.

Governing analogy family: **the doorbell.** Software rings the bell for free, forever. The bell is not the problem.

What moves, what blocks it, who owns the consequence, and what changes: the invoice moves toward paid. A wrong PO, a missing W-9, or a dispute blocks it. The owner carries the consequence. The desk changes who is home when the customer comes to the door.

## Report opening

Concrete cold-open situation: picture an owner with one invoice 47 days late. The software already sent the reminder. It did not cost anything.

Person, transaction, workflow, or consequence carrying it: the owner, the invoice, the customer's AP person, the reply.

What appears normal: the reminder went out, as designed.

What becomes strange, costly, or unresolved: the customer did answer. The PO number is wrong. Fixing it means an awkward note to a good customer, nobody is paid to send it, and the reply has been in the inbox for two weeks.

Specific operator or business payoff teased before the identity sting: the software did its whole job and the money did not move. Something in that gap is worth paying for, and there is a specific price it has to clear.

Mechanism, proof, and final ruling deliberately withheld: the human-layer thesis, the price ladder, the state line, the arithmetic, BUILD.

Question left open before the identity sting: if the machine did everything it was supposed to do, whose job was that reply?

Normal system the context runway must explain: how small firms bill other businesses on terms, what an aging report is (the list of who owes what and how late), who follows up, what the free reminder does, why agencies arrive after about 90 days for a share.

Why reasonable people use that system: the owner knows the customers, a clerk is cheap per hour, an agency costs nothing until it collects, and a free reminder handles anyone who simply forgot.

What changed: sending went to about free in 2026. The overdue share rose from 47% to 59% on one vendor's survey.

Who now feels the consequence: the owner, who gets the reminder for free and still has the reply, and the clerk, whose sending work got cheaper.

Why it matters now: when the sending has no price, only the work around it can be sold.

Central investigation question: when software will chase an invoice for free, what is left for a person to sell, and can one person sell enough of it?

Evidence and explanation required before the thesis can be stated: the QuickBooks figures, the tool prices, and what the sellers themselves list as separate paid work.

How the ending returns to or reinterprets the cold open: the same reply is answered. The PO is corrected and the invoice is resent, and on Friday's report it sits in one known state. It is not shown paid. The change is that it has an owner.

## Causal spine

Protagonist: the viewer who has done AR or bookkeeping and is deciding whether that skill can be sold.

Desired result: about two dozen firms in one niche who pay monthly for someone to own their late invoices.

Constraint: an owner can buy the tool for $99 or nothing, and the human part must clear that gap at under about 1.6 hours per client.

Inciting change or opening: sending became nearly free.

Central question: when software chases for free, what is left to sell, and can one person sell enough?

Proposed mechanism: agents read the list, send, and sort replies; one person clears the queue, fixes documents, makes permitted calls, hands disputes back the same day, returns accounts after 90 days, and writes a weekly report.

Strongest proof: sellers price the human part separately, and named small law firms pay for it, on the seller's own pages. Employers post part-time jobs for it.

Weakest assumption: that owners outside law pay $400 or more to an unknown person when the tool is $99.

Necessary tradeoff or decision: tell some firms to buy the tool. Refuse the $300 fee. Accept a full-time job, not a side income.

Decision or acceptance measure that authorizes the next operating step: two of five take a paid month at $400 or more, four reports with every invoice in a known state, under 1.5 hours a week, no wrong send, and the owner renews.

Resolution: BUILD, bounded to the first month and its stop rule.

First responsible action: read your state's collection law, then mark one aging report by hand.

Causal steps:

1. A small firm bills other businesses on terms and some pay late.
2. The free reminder handles the customer who forgot. That is a real improvement.
3. Sending went to about free, so nobody can be paid for it.
4. But many stalled invoices are stuck on something a reminder cannot fix, and sellers list those items as separate paid work.
5. At a firm with no AR staff, nobody is paid to answer, so the reply sits.
6. People already get paid for this: clerks by the hour, specialist desks by the month, above what a small firm wants to spend.
7. For many firms the tool is enough, and they should buy it.
8. Where it is not, a company can own the list.
9. One person can own it for about 24 firms only because agents do the sending and sorting, unmeasured.
10. The free sort finds out whether a firm needs a person, and the paid month tests whether it will pay for one.
11. At $400 and 24 clients the arithmetic is a full-time job. At $300 it fails.
12. The state may not allow it. That is checked first.

## Tension

What appears true at first: AI solved invoice chasing.

What complicates it: the customer often answers, and the answer needs a person.

What the evidence changes: the sellers themselves split the bill, sending free and the person priced.

What remains uncertain: whether owners outside law pay, how many hours it takes, how often each stuck cause occurs, and whether the viewer's state allows it.

Why the viewer should keep listening after the problem is established: there is one price this has to clear and one stop rule, and nobody selling a tool has an interest in saying either.

## Counterargument and fairness

Fair incumbent case, delivered before any criticism: the reminder works on the customer who forgot, the tools are honest about what they send, and a $99 tool is often the right purchase.

Strongest reasonable objection: a fixed ladder plus one phone call is enough, and the wrong PO is a two-minute fix. In buyer voice, several owners say so (B001), and others say ask for the PO up front (B002).

What the objection gets right: for many firms it is correct, and the free sort must be allowed to end there.

Where the proposed model still holds: the two-minute fix is not two minutes when it needs an awkward note to a good customer and sits behind everything else, and named firms pay a person regardless. Whether this firm would is what the sort and the offer test.

Second objection, ruled: a clerk gives about four times the modeled hours for a similar monthly cost. True. The desk's case rests on agents doing that difference, unmeasured, and on a firm not wanting to hire and manage anyone.

Third objection, ruled: a funded seller will come down-market. It may. Invoice Butler already sells the human layer at an undisclosed price. A disclosed human specialist at $500 or less at comparable scope stops this.

What would invalidate the thesis: fewer than two of five take a paid month at $400 or more; owners buy the tool; hours above about 1.6 at $400; the viewer's state requires a licence they will not obtain; or a seller offers a human specialist at $500 or less at comparable scope.
