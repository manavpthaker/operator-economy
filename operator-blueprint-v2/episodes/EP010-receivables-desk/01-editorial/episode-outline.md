Qualified buyer rule stated without an example industry: invoices other businesses on terms, regularly carries balances past 30 days that are large beside the fee, has nobody whose job is follow-up, and will let an outside person write to customers under the firm's name.

Unsuitable buyer rule: as in the beat sheet.

## Sequence detail

Grouped where one logic governs. Claim IDs, boundaries, and times are in the table.

### S00: the reply

Job: put the viewer inside the unanswered reply, on a list. Enters knowing nothing. Leaves knowing the machine worked and the money didn't move. Thread: introduced. Out of scope: any dollar amount, any count of invoices, sending versus owning.

### S03 and S04: the runway

Job: the normal system stated fairly, then what changed. "Aging report" comes after the list is described. Thread: the reminder from S00 becomes the thing whose price just went to zero. Complication: the survey says invoices are still late, once, attributed. Out of scope: every tool beyond two names. Inherits "the price this has to clear". Hands forward "so it's solved".

### S05: the question

Inherits "so it's solved", and answers with the cold open's reply. Hands forward "what's left".

### S06 and S07: the reversal and the ruling

Job: earn the thesis. S06 returns to the reply, then reads the sellers' own plan lists: free emails on one line, the disputed invoice and the phone call on another. Literal first, then the rang-back image. S07 rules. Thread: the reply stops being one errand and becomes the kind of thing sitting on the list. Counterargument held for S10. Boundary: no share of invoices is stated (U006). Out of scope: prices beyond those already said.

### S08: what's at stake

Inherits "what's it worth". One figure per sentence. "More likely to say" carries the association. Hands forward "who gets paid for this now". Out of scope: any claim that late invoices cause credit-card use.

### S09: the ladder

Job: climb one rung per sentence: clerk, tools, human desks, finance-team agents. Each rung says what it proves. Ends on the plain limit: none of this proves anyone would pay you. Heaviest number load in Act I. Cut order if the read drags: Upflow, then Daylit. Thread: absent by design; returns at once in S10. Out of scope: recovery figures, Invoice Butler's price, any "going rate".

### S10: buy the bell

Job: give the skeptic the floor. The same reply is a two-minute fix. A ladder plus a phone call is enough for many firms. Asking for the PO at onboarding prevents part of it. Then the buyer filter. Then what's different where the tool isn't enough: the reply sits on a list with others, behind an awkward note to a good customer, and it's nobody's job. Ruling: buy the bell if a bell is all it needs. Hands forward "where it isn't". Out of scope: how many firms qualify.

### S11: the company

Inherits "where it isn't". Names the mature company before any first offer: about 24 firms in one niche, each late invoice in one known state each week, one person answerable. States the label is ours and no published example was found. Hands forward "whether that's you".

### S12 and S13: why you, and machine and person

S12 inherits "whether that's you" and answers through the reply: the viewer knows what it's stuck on. Says plainly that someone with no AR background has the weakest case. No claim about the host's experience. S13 places the reply in the queue, sorted "missing document". Agents send and sort. A person locks accounts, fixes documents, calls, hands disputes back the same day, reads every message for two weeks. Out of scope: the fee.

### S14: the free sort

The wedge turn: "You don't start with twenty-four." One list marked by hand, line by line, including the reply's line. The honest answer may be "buy the tool". Three owners show three different decisions, each in a sentence or two. Then one paid month, $400 to $500, no term. Out of scope: hours and income.

### S15: Friday's report

Inherits the yes. The reply becomes one line in one known state. The weekly loop, the 90-day return, the wrong-send route. Money is tracked, never promised, because one client in 30 days can't show cause. Hands forward "who is the first owner".

### S16: the first owner

Past employers, vendors, a bookkeeper who keeps flagging the same list. Bookkeepers may refer or may add the tool and keep the client. No conversion rate. Hands forward "can it pay you".

### S17: your arithmetic

The candid sequence. One operation per sentence, each restated in plain words: 40 hours less 10 is 30. At an hour and a quarter that's 24 clients, not 30. Times $400, less seats and overhead, about $75,900 before your pay and tax. About $39.50 an hour. At $300 it needs 29 or more clients who don't fit. Clerk comparison said out loud. Low case said out loud. About 16 months to fill, so other income first. No humor. Out of scope: the $133,560 high case, any "good living" language.

### S18: the state line

Inherits "can it pay you" through "if you're allowed". None of the seller pages read bring this up. Florida and Washington, each with the non-lawyer reading. The rest grouped, with "not the same as saying you're clear". Not legal advice. Check before anyone's customer is contacted.

### S19: what breaks it

Owners agree and buy the tool. Hours above about 1.6. A wrong send. A funded seller moving down, with Invoice Butler already selling people at a price it doesn't publish. The owner's stop rule, stated once and exactly. The proof ladder: one, then three to five, then up to 24, each with its own buyer decision.

### S20: thirty and ninety days

Five documents, five free sorts, one paid month. Hours log from day one. What not to build yet. About 40 hours and $600, modeled. Days 31 to 90 replace the hours guess with the log.

### S21 to S24: the ending

S21 returns to the reply: PO corrected, invoice resent, one line on Friday's report. Not shown paid. S22 rules. S23 gives the first move. S24 asks once.
