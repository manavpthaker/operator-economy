## Opening contract

Cold-open person, transaction, workflow, or consequence: A "where is my order" message at eleven at night; the bundled AI agent hands it to a human who is not there; the month-end bill.

What appears normal: The store bought an AI agent for this and switched it on.

What becomes strange, costly, or unresolved: The easiest ticket in the business goes to nobody; the store pays for the handful the agent did resolve.

Specific operator or business payoff teased before the silent sting: A job between bought and working; one person could sell it at a fixed price to stores the vendor will not set up by hand.

Why the payoff gives the viewer a strong reason to stay: It names a job, a price shape and a buyer, and promises the arithmetic.

Mechanism, proof, and final thesis deliberately withheld: Why the agent hands back; per-resolution pricing; the $3M line; the job sold above it; the vendor's ability to close the gap; BUILD.

Question left open: Who left the job undone.

## Operator-market selection

Relevant experience or operating judgment the viewer can transfer: Support or product work on a queue; reading policies; running a small merchant.

Trusted relationships or network: Founders of online stores; ecommerce agencies that do not do support.

Earned access or credibility with buyers: A sandbox implementation the operator ran; the audit method published.

Buyer language, workflow, or constraint the operator already understands: "Where is my order"; the return window; the refund that goes wrong.

Decision-makers the operator can realistically reach: Founders and CX leads at brands they know or that their agency contacts serve.

Approved first-market selection rule: Start with the stores you can already reach; qualify with the vendor's own question and the ticket band; do not start with the partner program.

Qualified buyer rule stated without an example industry: as in the beat sheet.

Unsuitable buyer rule: as in the beat sheet.

Three explanatory cases and the distinct customer decision each clarifies: order status (safe); returns (conditional); broken-item refund (never).

Company definition with all example industries removed: as in the beat sheet.

Adjacent budget or costly responsibility: the help desk subscription and per-resolution fees; the vendor's paid onboarding.

Observed operating layers: the vendor; certified agencies and a specialist shop.

Scale-proof pattern: same job across scales; fragmented buyer pool.

Industry used to make the episode concrete: Shopify DTC brands on Gorgias or Zendesk.

Industry status: **approved recommendation** (the thesis selects the market; no product category is named).

Plain line preventing the example industry from becoming universal advice: not needed for the market; for the buyer, "If they give you a person, you're not my buyer."
