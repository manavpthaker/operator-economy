## Listener relationship

- Primary listener: a capable operations, customer-experience, or product generalist who has read a support queue and knows a founder or two.
- What they already understand: what a support inbox is, that small stores buy tools they never fully set up, and that "the AI handles your tickets" is a sales line.
- What they may distrust or misunderstand: that this is a technical business needing an "AI person". That the vendor is a villain. That a BUILD verdict means demand is proven. That the failing base case means the business fails.
- Narrator's role in relation to them: an operator who has put AI on a ticket queue and run customer experience, sitting across a table and working the problem with them. He has not sold this as a service and says so.
- Central episode promise: what the job between bought and working is worth to the store, what it costs one person to do, and the one thing that could make the whole business disappear.
- Final belief or action the narration should make possible: open a ten-dollar sandbox, run the wizard, write down what it leaves undone, and treat that list as the offer or the reason there is none.

## Global performance target

- Baseline energy: alert and specific, seven of ten, as the method-level register sets it. Quiet recognition, not outrage.
- Pace range: unhurried conversational baseline. Slight lift in the cold open and at the S11 turn. Materially slower at the S10 three-million line, through the S17 arithmetic, and across the S18 kill conditions.
- Warmth/restraint: warm toward the founder, the agents, the customer, and the listener. Restrained toward the vendor, which receives its fair case before any criticism. Dry lines are underreactions and are never performed as punchlines.
- Commercial authority: earned, not asserted. Conviction rises only after the vendor's own pages have been read against each other (S10) and the company has been named (S11). The BUILD verdict is a recommendation stated with the case against in the same breath.
- Terms to avoid performing as slogans: "a helpdesk setup practice", "the never list", "the handbook", "engine by the mile", "Draft is cheap. Deciding is the job.", "build".
- Anti-targets: trailer / sales pitch / lecture / synthetic demo / newsreader / motivational speaker / constant sarcasm / a punch-line metronome on short sentences
