## Public framing contract

- Model description: **observed model for the service category; the solo version is synthesized from that category and is not observed at disclosed economics**
- Economics language: **modeled scenario, not observed performance or an earnings forecast**
- Load-bearing assumptions to say plainly:
  1. **The vendor can close the gap.** The business exists between the helpdesk vendor's self-serve setup wizard and its hands-on onboarding for larger brands. A product release or a lower onboarding threshold can shrink or erase the job. The episode must say so.
  2. **The "adopted but not working" finding is enterprise evidence** (MIT NANDA). Its small-brand version is a transferred hypothesis this package has not measured.
  3. **No solo operator was found selling this at disclosed economics.** Certified agencies and the vendor itself sell implementation; the fee, hours, capacity, buyer payback and contribution are all modeled, including the assumed cost per ticket and the hypothesized resolution lift.
  4. **The measure counts containment, not correctness.** A rising verified-resolution rate does not prove good answers; the never-automate list and the wrong-answer log are part of the deliverable for that reason.
- Claims the episode may not make:
  - The legacy $40K a month, 85 to 90 percent margin, $2 to 5K project band, $6K a month freelancer, or $5.9 billion figures, in any form.
  - Accenture's bookings as proof of small-brand demand.
  - Any typical automation rate, "automate 80 percent," or a resolution lift stated as a result.
  - That the vendor's wizard is inadequate (unverified until validation step 1 is run).
  - That Manav has delivered a helpdesk implementation to a client.
  - Any current vendor price without a refresh against the vendor's own page.
- Required source and qualification notes:
  - CLM-013 (the vendor's dedicated-onboarding threshold, which defines the buyer) must be opened at source or confirmed with the vendor in writing before the script states where dedicated onboarding begins.
  - CLM-007 (the per-resolution price) must be confirmed on the vendor's own page before any public price statement.
  - The MIT NANDA finding must be attributed and labeled enterprise.
  - Vendor pricing, tiers, and onboarding menus are time-sensitive; refresh by 2026-12-03.

## Caveats carried forward

- **Pre-lock conditions (Step 1 may draft but may not lock a script until these are recorded):** open CLM-013 and CLM-007 at source; run the vendor's setup wizard end to end on a sandbox store and record what it does and does not configure. The package currently infers the wizard's residue from go-live documentation and a 2024 release note, not from a test.
- **Go-to-market is the weakest factor.** The partner program's application form lists agency types, not individuals; the first-buyer path must be built from the operator's own relationships and stated as such.
- **The buyer's willingness to pay a four-figure fixed fee at this size is the whole business** and is untested. The thirty-day plan (three ticket reviews, one unpaid implementation, two paid offers) is the first construction step the episode recommends, and BUILD at E3I must be argued against that.
- **Data access and consent** are design requirements: scoped, client-controlled admin access, written consent, and no automated action that moves money without per-action sign-off.
- **POV boundary:** builder-side product judgment from facts.md only. No client delivery claim.
