## Story in one sentence

Everyone standing around a small-business sale is paid only if it closes, so nobody is paid to make the business closeable — and that vacancy is a practice one person can run.

## Viewer movement

| From | To |
|---|---|
| Helping owners sell means becoming a broker | Preparation is a separate job, and in most states it needs no licence |
| The problem is finding a buyer | Buyers are plentiful; the problem is being inspectable |
| Success-fee alignment is obviously good for the owner | It also means nobody is paid to tell an owner they are not ready |
| This is an M&A business needing deal experience | It is an operations business needing operating judgement |
| If it were real, someone would be doing it | Someone is — at institutional and platform scale. Nobody at this scale, for structural reasons |

## Point of view and voice architecture

The report is hosted. The host is not a broker and has never sold a business, and the episode says so where it matters. The authority claimed is operational: having been the single point of dependence in a business, and knowing what that looks like from inside.

Register: documentary, dry, unhurried. The narration reasons rather than announces.

## Report opening

**Protagonist:** the capable professional deciding whether to build this practice.

**Goal:** get paid for readiness before a deal exists.

**Constraint:** the payoff lands at an event with no date, and the obvious way to charge for it is regulated.

**Recurring human situation carrying the mechanism:** *the diligence question the owner cannot answer* — "what happens if you are not here for a month?"

It recurs three times and changes each time:
1. **Cold open** — asked by a buyer. The owner starts to answer and stops.
2. **Mid-body** — revealed as the whole mechanism. Everything a buyer discounts is a version of this question.
3. **Ending** — asked again, now answerable, because the answer exists on paper.

No invented identity, metric, quotation, or outcome. The situation is a class description of a standard diligence question, not a case study, and nothing in it is presented as a documented case.

## Scope architecture

Three explanatory situations appear — owner-held relationships, concentrated revenue, unverifiable records. Each clarifies a different failure. **None names an industry.**

A listener can explain the company after every illustrative situation is removed: it makes a small business able to survive a buyer's inspection. The cold open, company definition, prospect denominator and economics contain no example industry.

## Opportunity-to-build architecture

**Act I — believe the opportunity (≈55%).** The normal system and why it exists; what changed; who feels it; the evidence; the fair incumbent case; the missing ownership layer; the mature practice that could own it.

**Act II — believe you can build it (≈45%).** The operator advantage; the AI/human split; the fixed-fee wedge and why the fee is fixed; delivery and acceptance; the adviser-first path; the economics including the failing base case; the hard part; conditional expansion; 30 and 90 days; BUILD.

The turn is explicit and audible: the moment the episode stops arguing that the gap exists and starts building in it.

The mature practice is understood before the single engagement that starts it. The wedge is an entrance, never the ceiling; expansion is conditional and each stage names its required proof.

## Causal spine

1. A small business is sold through a broker paid on success. This exists for good reasons and is fair to the owner with no cash to spend up front.
2. Transition became a volume event and diligence got fast and competitive.
3. Buyers now inspect rather than negotiate. Unprepared businesses are passed over.
4. Value only transfers if the business runs without the person selling it.
5. Undocumented process and owner-held relationships cannot be priced, so they are discounted or refused.
6. Every professional in the room is paid on closing, so nobody is paid to fix step 5.
7. That vacancy is a bounded, fixed-fee engagement.
8. It must be fixed-fee, because a success fee is a regulated activity.
9. One operator can deliver it because AI carries the documentation load.
10. Whether an owner pays before a deal exists is unproven, and that is the thing to test.

## Evidence placement

Evidence enters where it changes understanding, never as a block.

| Evidence | Placed at | Operating implication it lands on |
|---|---|---|
| ~30% of listed businesses sell | after step 3 | Going to market is not the same as selling |
| ~50% of exits are involuntary | after step 4 | You cannot schedule this; the deadline is not yours |
| owners measurably unprepared | after step 5 | The gap is population-wide, not anecdotal |
| deal competition, 87% drawing 3+ offers | after step 3 | Buyers can afford to walk |
| three observed operating layers | before step 7 | The job is real and paid — just not at this scale |
| licensing perimeter | at step 8 | It decides the revenue model, not just compliance |

Each block ends on the implication, not the number.

## Tension

The operator must sell urgency for an event with no date, get paid before the payoff, and stay inside a line that moves by state. The honest version of this business may not clear at the fee the market bears — and the episode says so rather than resolving it.

## Counterargument and fairness

**Fair incumbent case, delivered before any criticism:** brokers are not doing anything wrong. Success-fee pricing lowers the owner's barrier, aligns the broker with a closing, and works well when the business is already sellable.

**Strongest fair counterargument:** *if this were a real business, someone would already be doing it.*

**Explicit ruling:** someone is — at institutional scale in diligence preparation, at platform scale in compliance readiness, at specialist scale in exit planning. Nobody at small-business scale, and the reason is structural rather than an oversight: the fee that works at this scale is too small for a firm to staff, and the pricing model that would make it attractive — a share of the sale — is a regulated activity. The gap is produced by the economics, which is also why it persists.

**Second counterargument, ruled honestly:** *brokers already do this free to win the listing.* The ruling is that free pre-listing work is scoped to what wins a listing, not to what survives diligence — **and this is unproven.** It is named as the first thing to test and as a genuine kill condition. The episode does not pretend to have settled it.

## Governing analogy family

**The inspection before a property changes hands.**

Mapped: the sale depends on someone examining what is actually there; the inspection is bought before and independently of any completion; the agent is paid on completion, the inspector is not; and a property that cannot be inspected does not simply sell for less — it often does not sell.

Developed through callbacks:
- **Introduced** at the opportunity-to-build turn.
- **Callback 1** at the licensing beat — the reason the inspector is not paid on completion is the same reason this practice cannot be.
- **Callback 2** at the ending — the business is now inspectable.

The analogy maps the mechanism and the incentive structure. It carries no factual claim and is never used as evidence.

## Humor and contempt

Register is dry understatement, not jokes. One valid target only: **the phrase "we only get paid when you do," said as though it were purely a virtue.** The observation is that it also means nobody is paid to tell an owner the truth. The target is an incentive structure, never a person or profession, and the fair incumbent case is delivered first. Humor never substitutes for evidence.

## Ending

The opening question returns and is **answered** — not repeated. The payoff teased before the sting is delivered: the paid work in the pause, and what it costs to do honestly. BUILD lands as a recommendation to begin bounded construction with a stated kill condition, not as proof. First construction action: build the checklist, run one diagnostic unpaid, record the hours. Uncertainty is preserved — the base case does not clear, and the viewer is told which number decides it.
