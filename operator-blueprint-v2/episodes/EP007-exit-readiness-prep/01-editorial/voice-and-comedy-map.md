## Hosted-long-form routing record

Hosted long-form report, single narrator, no guest, no dialogue. Register is documentary and dry: the narration reasons in front of the viewer rather than presenting conclusions.

## Company language lock

Spoken name, used consistently: **a sale-readiness practice**

Plain definition, said once early and once at the end: *It makes a small business able to survive a buyer's inspection.*

Never said aloud: the precise internal operating description. It lives in the Canvas.

Banned constructions: "exit readiness solutions", "transition ecosystem", "value acceleration", any phrase stacking three abstract nouns.

## Base relationship

An experienced operator talking to one smart peer across a table, working through a real problem. Not presenting. Not selling. The peer is assumed competent and is never flattered or condescended to.

## Opening voice map

### Cold open
Present tense, plain, no adjectives doing persuasive work. The pause is the beat — the narration stops where the owner stops. No music sting on the line.

### Identity and promise
The brand string is delivered flat and unhurried, exactly as written. The promise that follows is one sentence and makes a specific commitment: what the practice can honestly charge for, and the number that decides whether it works.

### Context runway
The most generous passage in the episode. The incumbent case is made properly — success-fee pricing is genuinely good for an owner with no cash. Any hint of setup-for-a-takedown is a defect here.

### Earned reveal
Quiet, not triumphant. The vacancy is stated as an observation about incentives, not an accusation.

## Report and business plan

### Reporting spine
Evidence is delivered with its limit attached in the same breath: attribute EPI, note it credentials exit planners; note IBBA is advisor-reported closings. Every evidence beat ends on the operating implication, never on the number.

### Business context
Where the abstraction is heavy — the incentive vacancy, the licensing perimeter — the sentence gets shorter, not longer.

### Operator blueprint
Instructional but never breezy. The economics beat is the one place the narration slows and does arithmetic out loud, one line at a time.

## Investment-thesis language map

BUILD is said as a recommendation to begin bounded construction. Never "this works." Never "you should start this today."

### Opportunity-to-build transition
One clean sentence at A8→B1 that marks the turn audibly. The listener should feel the episode change job.

### Mature-company-to-entry-wedge transition
The complete practice is described before the single engagement. The wedge is introduced as where you start, explicitly not as what the business is.

## Governing analogy

**The inspection before a property changes hands.**

- Introduced at the opportunity-to-build turn.
- Callback 1 at the licensing beat: the inspector is not paid on completion, and neither can this practice be.
- Callback 2 at the ending: the business is now inspectable.

Carries no factual claim. Never used as evidence. Never extended past those three appearances.

## Understatement, humor, and contempt

Register is dry understatement. No jokes, no bits, no sarcasm at the viewer's expense.

**One valid target, used once:** the phrase "we only get paid when you do," offered as though it were purely a virtue. The observation is that it also means nobody is paid to tell an owner the truth.

Target is an incentive structure, never a person or a profession. The fair incumbent case is delivered before it. Contempt appears nowhere else, and never substitutes for evidence.

## Manav language map

First person is used only where it is true: the host has run businesses in which he was the single point of dependence, and has been the buyer of professional services rather than the seller of them.

**Never said:** anything implying transaction, brokerage or deal experience. The host has never sold a business, and where the episode brushes against deal mechanics it says so plainly and routes to the people who do it.

## First-listen load plan

Heavy beats get shorter sentences and one idea each: the context runway, the involuntary-exit statistic, the three-layer ladder, the licensing rule, and the arithmetic. The analogy callback at B4 lands only after the rule is already plain.

## Evidence-efficient language plan

Numbers are said once, in the form the viewer needs, and never repeated for emphasis. No number is said without its implication in the following sentence. Prohibited entirely: any multiple uplift from preparation, any typical fee, any implied causation between preparation and a sale.

## Cadence plan

Unhurried baseline with real room between ideas — the room goes between thoughts, not inside them. Pace lifts slightly in the cold open and the turn, and slows for the economics and the licensing rule. Never an announcer, never a pitch.
