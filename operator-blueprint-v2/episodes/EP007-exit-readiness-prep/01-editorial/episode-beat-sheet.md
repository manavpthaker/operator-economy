## Opening ladder

### B00: cold open — 20–40s

A buyer is sitting across from an owner who has run a profitable business for twenty-five years. The buyer asks what happens if the owner is not there for a month. The owner starts to answer, and stops.

Payoff previewed: there is paid work inside that pause, and almost nobody is doing it.

Withheld: why buyers walk, all evidence, the licensing constraint, the thesis.

Ends on an open loop — the unanswered question, not a statement.

Contains: no greeting, no thesis, no evidence, no agenda, no ask.

### B01: silent identity sting — 3–6s

No narration. Approved routine seam exception.

### B02: brand and episode promise — ~20s

Fixed brand string verbatim. Then one episode-specific promise: the business is a sale-readiness practice; one accountable operator can run it; by the end the viewer will know what it can honestly charge for and the one number that decides whether it works.

No table of contents.

### B03: context runway — 60–120s

How a small business is actually sold today: broker engaged when the owner is ready, paid on success, running a marketed process. **Why that exists and why it is fair** — no cash up front, alignment with a closing, works well when the business is already sellable. What changed: transition became a volume event and diligence got fast and competitive. Who feels it: the owner, whose net worth is inside the asset.

### B04: central question — ~10s

Asked only now, once the vocabulary exists:

> If every professional around a sale is paid only when it closes, who is paid to make it closeable?

### B05: earned thesis — ~30s

Placed after the context and the first evidence, never as the first explanation: nobody is. That vacancy is structural, not an oversight, and it is a business.

## Orientation test
