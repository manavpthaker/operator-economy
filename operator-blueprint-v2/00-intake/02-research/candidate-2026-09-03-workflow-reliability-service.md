## Executive finding

- Opportunity: A fixed-fee sprint that makes one cross-tool client handoff in a small professional-service firm reliable — mapped, built on the client's existing platform, tested against exceptions, alerted, documented and monitored for thirty days — sold on the reliability, not the connection.
- Evidence class: **observed model**
- Provisional verdict: **plausible with bounded assumptions**
- Main reason: The business is observed at category level. Zapier states it has 700+ Solution Partners delivering hands-on automation services and its directory shows individual partners with 400+ buyer reviews; n8n defines its expert partners as firms whose main revenue is automation services with at least three active customers, and community agencies report dozens of active clients. The failure that makes the reliability job sellable has primary, dated 2026 documentation: Zapier's own notice that workflows on Pipedrive's V1 API "will stop working" after 31 July 2026. Independent US federal survey data shows small-firm AI adoption running far ahead of integration.
- Largest unresolved question: **What a small professional-service firm will pay an unknown independent for reliability work, and how often a handoff failure actually costs it money.** No independent price observation exists; every 2026 range found is seller-authored, and one says so in its own text.
- Required public framing: The service category, the partner ecosystems, the delivery shape and the platform failure mechanics are **observed**. The adoption-integration gap is **observed in adjacent surveys** (US federal, German public bank, UK government), not measured for this buyer. The bounded sprint scope, the fixed-fee price, the hours, the capacity and the contribution are **modeled**. The monitoring retainer is a **hypothesis**. The historical EP003 pricing bands, income ranges and "few hours a week" framing are **not usable** and may not reappear.

## Buyer reality

- Buyer: Owner or operations lead of a 5–50-person professional-service firm — law, accounting, agency, consultancy, design studio — running a CRM, a scheduling tool and a billing or invoicing tool.
- End customer or beneficiary: The coordinator or assistant doing the re-keying and chasing; the client whose intake, appointment or invoice stalls.
- Recurring problem: Client state is split across tools that do not share it. A person is the integration. Home-made automations, where they exist, were built for the happy path and are not watched.
- Consequence and frequency: **Shown, not measured.** A vendor notice establishes that a whole class of workflows stops on a published date (CLM-009). An agency-published case describes a ten-person law firm whose three intake automations silently filtered out every incoming deal after a platform changed its stage identifiers, undetected until June 2026 (CLM-010; seller-published, outcome unverified). Adjacent surveys establish that administrative and compliance work consumes about 32 hours a month in the average German SME (CLM-002) and that US small firms adopt AI faster than they integrate it (CLM-001). None of these measures the dollar cost of a failed handoff for this buyer.
- Existing alternatives or budget: Staff time; a two-step Zap the owner built; a one-off freelancer; a listed Zapier or n8n partner for firms that find one. Budget for hands-on automation services demonstrably exists at category level (CLM-005, CLM-006, CLM-007). Its price is opaque.
- Why now: Adoption without integration (CLM-001, CLM-022), platform change on a calendar (CLM-009, CLM-012), and a market that is teaching thousands of new entrants to sell the build (CLM-017) while the durable job is the upkeep.

## Evidence landscape

### Direct evidence

- **Zapier Solution Partner Program** (CLM-005). Zapier describes partners as providing "hands-on services to build smarter systems, unify their tools, and move from ideas to outcomes quickly," with tiers that "recognize partners for their proven expertise, quality of delivery, and ability to scale impact for customers." Its own program page invites readers to "Join 700+ Solution Partners." This is the exact professional-service category, described by the vendor that channels demand to it.
- **Zapier Solution Partner directory** (CLM-006). On 2026-09-03 the directory showed 29 featured Platinum partners with pagination beyond. Listed partners carry buyer review counts in the hundreds (Flow Digital 473 at 5.0; MercOlogy 438 at 5.0; Connex Digital 412 at 4.9) and describe services as "quick fixes, complex integrations, full system builds, and ongoing partnership support." Reviews are a proxy for completed engagements, not audited revenue.
- **n8n Expert Partners** (CLM-007). n8n's criteria — partners that "are already implementing services on top of n8n," "offer automation & AI services as their main revenue stream," and "have at least three customers actively using n8n" — are a vendor's definition of the exact business. The program remains a closed pilot with a waitlist.
- **n8n community agencies** (CLM-008). In a February 2026 community thread, one agency owner reports "over 2 dozen active clients for whom we build workflows on n8n" over 24+ months; another reports 12+ months deploying n8n into businesses. Self-reported, unaudited, but first-person and current.
- **Zapier partner case study** (CLM-011). A Solution Partner mapped and implemented a Calendly → Zapier Tables → GoHighLevel → Slack lead workflow and delivered "SOPs and documentation for every workflow," "training videos," and "90 days of post-implementation support." The delivery shape is exactly the proposed sprint. The reported outcomes (60% revenue growth) are vendor-curated and are not used.
- **The failure event, from the vendor** (CLM-009). Zapier's help centre, updated 29 May 2026: Pipedrive V1 API steps across 12 trigger, 7 search and 11 action types are deprecated on 31 July 2026, and "after this date, workflows using deprecated steps will stop working." Users must identify affected Zaps, migrate steps, remap fields, test and reactivate. This is the reliability job, dated, in the platform's own words.
- **The failure event, from an agency** (CLM-010). Connex Digital, 22 July 2026: a Chicago bankruptcy firm of ten staff had three intake automations (welcome letters, mailers, scheduling sheets) that had been "silently filtering out all incoming deals" because Pipedrive "updated its internal stage-reference structure, making those numeric IDs stale." Discovered June 2026; rebuilt in "under an hour"; estimated recovery "2–3 hours/week." Published by the agency that did the work and sells the service; used for the failure mechanism, not for its outcome numbers.

### Adjacent business evidence

- **US Federal Reserve Small Business Credit Survey, 2026 report on employer firms** (CLM-001). Fielded 3 Sep – 14 Nov 2025, 6,525 responses from firms with 1–499 employees. 46% of firms use AI; among users about half are experimenting, 44% have "partially integrated AI into their business processes," and 7% report full integration. Among prospective adopters, the top challenges are finding suitable tools (54%) and implementation time (37%). This is the adoption-integration gap in a primary, current, US, large-sample source. It is about AI tools generally, not cross-tool workflow automation specifically.
- **KfW Research, SME Panel** (CLM-002). 25 April 2025: German SMEs devote around 7% of working time to compliance, "32 hours per month on average per business," median 15 hours or less, at roughly 3.9% of annual personnel costs. Adjacent: administrative burden is broader than software handoffs and the geography is Germany.
- **UK Department for Business and Trade** (CLM-003). 31 July 2025 mixed-method research: SMEs "value reliable, personalised support and advice" through a five-stage adoption journey with information gaps at each stage. Supports the consulting job; says nothing about price.
- **Intuit QuickBooks 2026 Business Owner Report** (CLM-004). Commissioned survey, December 2025, 1,305 US owners with 0–250 employees: bookkeeping and taxes are the top task owners would hand to AI (17%); 40% worry about falling behind without AI adoption. Vendor-commissioned; used as a buyer-attitude signal only.
- **U.S. Chamber of Commerce / Teneo** (CLM-022). 18 August 2025: 58% of small businesses self-report generative AI use (40% in 2024, 23% in 2023); 84% plan to increase use of technology platforms. Association-sponsored survey; adoption context only.

## Proposed Operator Blueprint

- Offer: One workflow-reliability sprint on the client-intake → scheduling → billing handoff: discovery interviews, current-state map, baseline manual-touch count, access plan, one production workflow on the client's existing platform, representative and exception test suite, alerts and retry/stop behaviour, named human exception owner, runbook, training, thirty days of monitored operation, written maintain / expand / retire recommendation.
- Buyer outcome: The named handoff runs, logs, alerts and has an owner. The buyer holds the map, the implementation, the test record, the access inventory and the before-and-after count.
- Delivery workflow:
  1. Score candidate workflows on frequency, rule stability, consequence, exception complexity, data sensitivity, measurable baseline and owner availability; pick one.
  2. Watch the work being done; map inputs, states, decisions, systems, credentials, exceptions and what happens when each fails.
  3. Decide what stays human; define the minimum safe automation.
  4. Build in a client-controlled account with least-privilege access and test data.
  5. Test ordinary, missing, duplicate, delayed, malformed, stale-identifier and downstream-unavailable cases, plus the platform's own stop conditions (task cap, credit exhaustion, log expiry).
  6. Add logs, alerts, retry or stop behaviour and a named exception owner.
  7. Train, hand over documentation and ownership, start the measurement window, monitor for thirty days.
  8. Recommend maintain, expand, redesign or retire, with evidence.
- Technology and human stack: Interviewing and state mapping first; the client's systems and permissions second; the workflow runner the client already pays for third (Zapier, Make or n8n — chosen for fit, not preference); logs, alerts, secrets handling, documentation and measurement around it. Judgement owns ambiguous, sensitive and material decisions.
- Required skills or credentials: Process discovery, basic data and API reasoning, tool implementation, test design, documentation, plain-language client communication, privacy hygiene, and the discipline to refuse a workflow that cannot be bounded. No licence. Regulated data (health, legal-privileged, financial) requires additional review and may disqualify a workflow.
- Go-to-market path: One reachable segment first (professional-service firms the operator already knows or can be introduced to). A bounded paid diagnostic around one visible failure — "why did this stop working" is a live buyer question in 2026 (CLM-009) — credited toward the sprint. Vendor directories are a secondary path: n8n's is a closed pilot; Zapier's tiers reward proven delivery an entrant does not yet have.
- Pricing model: **Fixed fee, bounded hypothesis.** Not transferred from any published range. Monitoring offered separately only when the thirty-day window documents a recurring reliability obligation.
- Defensibility: Buyer and workflow fluency, exception design, tests, observability, documentation and trust. Not tool certification; not "boring."

## Modeled economics

Every number in this section is an internal Step 0 input until it is approved under `content-os/facts.md` for public use.

| Input | Working value or range | Basis | Status | Sensitivity or caveat |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pricing unit | $3,500 per sprint | Falsifiable paid-pilot hypothesis; not a market rate | modeled | **No observed fee exists.** Every published range is seller-authored (CLM-014). Reduce scope before claiming a price |
| Client capacity | 2 sprints per month | 56 delivery hours plus sales, admin and monitoring reserve | modeled | Falls with access delays, dirty data, stakeholder count and exception complexity |
| Delivery hours | 28 per sprint | Discovery 5; map and scope 4; build 7; test and exceptions 5; documentation and training 4; monitoring allocation 3 | modeled | Stress case 36 hours. Owner and staff availability is the least compressible input |
| Loaded labor cost | $60 per hour imputed | Internal opportunity-cost assumption | modeled | Not a wage claim or a recommended rate |
| Software and contractors | $100 per sprint | Client owns production tooling; allowance for operator test utilities | modeled | Hosting, specialist review or paid connectors can be materially higher |
| Acquisition and overhead | $400 per client | Allowance for outreach, calls, proposal, insurance and admin | modeled | Must be replaced by tracked cost in the pilot |

Capacity case formula: `$3,500 x 2 sprints x 1 month = $7,000 gross revenue`

Contribution case formula: `$7,000 - $0 delivery labor (owner-delivered) - $200 software - $0 contractors - $800 acquisition and overhead = $6,000 contribution before owner compensation and tax`

Imputed-labor check: `$6,000 - (56 hours x $60) = $2,640` at 28 hours per sprint; `$6,000 - (72 hours x $60) = $1,680` at the 36-hour stress case.

Price sensitivity: at `$2,500` per sprint the imputed-labor check falls to `$5,000 - $1,000 - $3,360 = $640`; at `$5,000` it rises to `$10,000 - $1,000 - $3,360 = $5,640`. The model is more sensitive to price than to hours, and price is the one input with no observation behind it.

A monitoring retainer is **excluded from the base case**. It enters only when the pilot records incidents, a response obligation and a buyer willing to pay for it.

Disclosure: `Modeled scenario, not observed performance or an earnings forecast.`

## Narrative and audience hypothesis

- Operator protagonist: An operations-minded generalist deciding whether the automation skill everyone is being sold can be turned into a responsible service someone will pay for.
- Starting state: A new client's form lands in the CRM. The calendar does not know. The invoice is never raised. A coordinator notices — or does not.
- Inciting change: A vendor publishes a date. After it, "workflows using deprecated steps will stop working." Or a field is renamed and the automation someone built two years ago starts silently dropping every new client.
- Stakes: Missed intake, stalled appointments, unbilled work, a person doing invisible integration by hand, and an owner who cannot see where the process failed.
- Causal mechanism: Each tool holds part of the client's state; the connections were built for the happy path; identifiers go stale; usage caps and credit pauses stop scenarios; logs expire in seven days; nobody is watching.
- Build transformation: One client record followed through three systems; every state and exception mapped; only the stable path automated; alerts, a named owner, tests against the platform's own stop conditions; a manual-touch count before and after.
- Decisions and tradeoffs: What stays human; client-owned versus operator-owned infrastructure; fixed fee versus the obligation a retainer creates; coverage versus speed; scoped access versus convenience.
- End state and payoff: A handoff with fewer manual touches and an owned exception path — or a failed test that says do not sell this yet. The viewer can score a workflow, scope a sprint, price the hours and run the pilot.
- Visual evidence classes: one client record across three interfaces; the state map; the copy-paste; the execution log; the stale ID; the vendor's deprecation notice with the date; the alert; the test matrix; the before-and-after counter; the runbook.
- Working audience promise: Everyone is selling you the automation agency. Here is the job the buyer actually pays for — keeping one workflow alive when the platform changes — and what it costs to deliver.
- Honest packaging territory: The date on the deprecation notice; the two-year silent failure; "Who Fixes It When It Breaks?"; "The Business Between the Apps." No income figure, no valuation, no "boring goldmine."

## Risks and constraints

- Factual uncertainty: No independent price, utilisation, margin, churn or income data. The failure-cost example is agency-published. Consequence for the bounded buyer is unquantified.
- Analogy-transfer uncertainty: Vendor-listed partners have reviews, tiers and referral flow an entrant lacks. Directory reviews prove engagements, not price or margin.
- Legal or regulatory boundary: Client data in CRM, calendar and billing tools; privileged or regulated data in law, accounting and health-adjacent firms. Data-processing terms, contract and insurance vary; high-impact automated decisions are excluded from a generalist sprint.
- Permissions, privacy, or guest dependency: Written client consent, least-privilege scoped credentials, client-owned accounts, test data, an access inventory and revocation plan, and permission before any client appears on screen. No guest is required for the episode.
- Platform or vendor dependency: Task caps, credit exhaustion, overage pricing, AI-step repricing, API deprecations, connector changes and log retention can each break delivery or contribution — which is the premise, and also the risk.
- Ethical failure mode: Automating an unclear or harmful process; hiding judgement inside an AI step; building without an exception owner; retaining unnecessary client data; creating operator lock-in; selling a retainer for a job that does not exist.
- Claims that must not be made: the historical EP003 setup-fee, retainer, two-client income, year-one income or "few hours a week" figures; any typical market price; "90% margins"; that funding, valuation or builder counts prove service demand; that an AI step makes a workflow valuable; that "boring" is a moat; that Manav has run an agency or delivered client outcomes; the Lovingly florist-data anecdote, which is not in facts.md.

## Thirty-day validation plan

1. Ten conversations with owners or operations leads of professional-service firms in one reachable segment, each with a screen-share of the intake → scheduling → billing handoff. Record frequency, manual touches, failure cases in the last twelve months, consequence, workaround, tool ownership and access constraints.
2. Three current-state maps scored on frequency, rule stability, consequence, exception complexity, data sensitivity, measurable baseline and owner availability. Reject any workflow touching privileged or regulated data without review.
3. Two paid diagnostics at a pre-declared price and one paid sprint at $3,500 with the scope above. Track outreach, calls, objections, discovery hours, build hours, test failures, incidents in the monitoring window, support load and the agreed before-and-after measure.

Success signal: At least three owners describe a recurring handoff failure with a consequence they can name; two pay for a diagnostic; one pays for the sprint; the workflow passes its exception tests and the thirty-day window records the manual-touch reduction without an unresolved access or privacy blocker.

Kill or redesign condition: After fifteen targeted conversations no owner can name a recurring costly handoff, or none will pay for a diagnostic; or the only workflows available depend on regulated data, inaccessible systems, unbounded exceptions, or a support obligation that breaks the capacity model; or the sprint takes more than 36 hours twice.
