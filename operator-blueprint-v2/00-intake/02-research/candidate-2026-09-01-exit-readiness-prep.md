## Executive finding

- Opportunity: A fixed-fee readiness practice that makes an owner-dependent business survivable in diligence, delivered by a generalist and deliberately scoped outside the brokerage perimeter.
- Evidence class: **adjacent synthesis**
- Provisional verdict: **plausible with bounded assumptions**
- Main reason: The consequence is quantified from two independent directions — most listed businesses never sell, and roughly half of all exits arrive without warning. The pricing environment is anchored by quarterly closed-transaction data rather than seller asking prices. The operator-entry barrier is genuinely low in most states.
- Largest unresolved question: **Whether an owner pays now for a payoff at an uncertain future event.** The parallel readiness businesses (SOC 2, ISO) sell against a scheduled examination; this does not have one.
- Required public framing: Transition scale, involuntary-exit share, listing-to-sale rate and transaction multiples are **observed**. The service format is **transferred** from diligence preparation and compliance readiness. Every price, hour and capacity figure is **modeled**. The specific prepared-versus-unprepared multiple gap in circulation is **seller-published and is not used as evidence**. Nothing here is legal advice.

## Buyer reality

- Buyer: The owner of a small owner-operated business, typically 55+, intending to exit within one to five years.
- End customer or beneficiary: The owner; secondarily the acquirer receiving a diligence-ready business.
- Recurring problem: Owners arrive at sale with owner-dependent operations, undocumented process and thin records, and are repriced or passed over by buyers running fast, competitive diligence.
- Consequence and frequency: **Quantified from two directions.** Only about 30% of listed small businesses successfully sell (CLM-001) — the modal outcome of going to market is not transacting. And roughly half of exits are involuntary, driven by death, disability, divorce, disagreement or distress (CLM-001), so preparation cannot be reliably deferred to a date the owner chooses.
- Existing alternatives or budget: Brokers, engaged late and paid on success; CPAs and attorneys at transaction time; CEPA-credentialed advisers, typically inside wealth or brokerage practices. Owners already fund transaction advice. Readiness is the underserved earlier stage.
- Why now: A large ownership-transition wave (CLM-005) meets consolidator demand for diligence-ready targets (CLM-006), against measured owner unpreparedness (CLM-002).

## Evidence landscape

### Direct evidence

Thin, and stated as such. Exit planning exists as a credentialed discipline (CEPA, Exit Planning Institute), but no independent source was found disclosing fees, delivery hours or outcomes for a standalone fixed-fee readiness practice run by an unlicensed generalist. That specific business is the synthesis, not an observed model.

### Adjacent business evidence

Two established practices share the operating structure. **Diligence preparation** assembles a defensible package for a scrutinising counterparty. **Compliance readiness consulting** (SOC 2, ISO) takes an organisation through an external examination it would otherwise fail, sold as fixed-scope remediation. Both are bought by non-specialists, delivered by generalists with a framework, and priced as projects. The limit is stated in the analogy map: those examinations are **scheduled**, and an exit is not.

### Component feasibility evidence

The documentation burden — SOP drafting, process capture, financial narrative assembly, data-room organisation — is exactly the work current AI tooling reduces most reliably. This is capability, not demand.

### Scaled relevance

Roughly six million US small and medium businesses face ownership transition by 2035 (CLM-005). Consolidators are actively buying fragmented owner-operated sectors — HVAC, plumbing, roofing, pest control, dental, veterinary, electrical, auto repair, IT services, accounting, home health — on an arbitrage between small-business and platform multiples (CLM-006). Buyer-side competition is measurable: 87% of deals over $5M attracted at least three offers in Q2 2026, and 33% attracted ten or more (CLM-003).

### Demand and competition

- Exact target queries: `exit planning consultant`, `how to prepare a business for sale`, `business readiness assessment`, `do I need a licence to be a business broker`
- Adjacent query or question families: how to start an exit-planning practice; what exit planning pays; selling an owner-dependent business
- Volume source, attempted measurement, and date: **attempted, not measurable, 2026-09-01.** No exact-volume tool is available to this operation; the constraint recorded in the V1 intake of 2026-08-14 is unchanged.
- Other demand signals: (1) transaction-based quarterly market data showing active deal flow and rising competition (CLM-003); (2) a credentialing body and formal discipline with published national research (CLM-001, CLM-002); (3) documented consolidator demand across named fragmented sectors (CLM-006); (4) transition-wave scale (CLM-005).
- Independent signal count: **three independent families** — professional-association transaction data (IBBA/M&A Source), a research-publishing credentialing institute (EPI), and PE/M&A market analysis.
- Strongest existing coverage: broker and wealth-adviser content addressed to **owners** — how to prepare *your* business. Extensive and competent.
- Information gap: **Almost nothing addresses the operator who wants to build the practice**, and the licensing boundary is treated as a footnote rather than as the constraint that determines the revenue model. See "Original synthesis" below.

## Proposed synthesis

- Parallel A and supported component: Diligence preparation → assembling a defensible package for a scrutinising counterparty
- Parallel B and supported component: SOC 2 / ISO readiness consulting → fixed-scope remediation against an external examination
- Parallel C and supported component: Technical or operational audit → diagnostic sold ahead of, and separately from, implementation
- New combination: Compliance-style readiness discipline applied to owner transition, delivered by a generalist with AI carrying the documentation load, and priced as a fixed fee **because** success fees trigger brokerage licensing.
- Primary transfer risk: **The missing deadline.** SOC 2 and ISO readiness sell against a scheduled audit. An exit has no date. The mitigation is CLM-001's involuntary-exit finding, which converts "no deadline" into "an unpredictable one" — a weaker but real urgency.
- Analogy map: `../03-validation/candidate-2026-09-01-exit-readiness-prep-analogy-map.md`

## Proposed Operator Blueprint

- Offer: A fixed-fee readiness engagement — records and reporting hygiene, process documentation, owner-dependence reduction, customer-concentration and key-person analysis, assembled diligence package, prioritised remediation plan.
- Buyer outcome: A business that survives diligence, and a written record of what was fixed and what remains open.
- Delivery workflow: Readiness diagnostic against a framework → bounded remediation sprint → package assembly → handoff, including a named referral to a licensed broker or adviser where a transaction follows.
- Technology and human stack: Jobs first — diagnosis, prioritisation, dependence mapping, and owner conversation. AI carries SOP drafting from interview recordings, financial narrative assembly, and data-room organisation. None of it supplies the judgement about what a buyer will actually discount.
- Required skills or credentials: Operational and finance-adjacent literacy, documentation discipline, and the ability to hold a difficult conversation with a founder about their own indispensability. **No licence is required for preparation work in the 34 states plus DC that do not regulate brokerage** (CLM-004).
- Go-to-market path: Referral from the professionals who meet owners years before a sale — CPAs, insurance and wealth advisers, and brokers who decline unready listings. A broker who cannot list a business today has a direct interest in it becoming listable.
- Pricing model: **Bounded hypothesis, fixed fee.** Not success-based, by legal design.
- Defensibility: The boundary itself. A practice scoped precisely inside the preparation perimeter, with a documented handoff, is a harder thing to assemble than it looks and is what most adjacent advisers get wrong.

## Narrative and audience hypothesis

- Operator protagonist: A capable generalist deciding whether to build a practice serving owners years before a payday.
- Starting state: An owner has run a profitable business for 25 years and intends to sell "in a couple of years."
- Inciting change: A buyer runs diligence, finds that the business *is* the owner, and reprices or walks.
- Stakes: Roughly 80% of the owner's net worth sits inside an asset that most of the time does not sell.
- Causal mechanism: Value transfers only if the business runs without the person selling it. Undocumented operations and owner-held relationships cannot be priced, so buyers discount or decline them.
- Build transformation: A readiness practice that converts an owner-dependent business into a transferable one, on a fixed fee, without touching the transaction.
- Decisions and tradeoffs: Getting paid years before the payoff; staying on the legal side of a line that moves by state; selling urgency for an event with no date.
- End state and payoff: The viewer knows whether to build it, what they may legally sell, and the condition under which it fails.
- Visual evidence classes: the diligence checklist against the filing cabinet; an org chart where every line terminates at one person; the state licensing map; a data room before and after; the listing-to-sale rate.
- Working audience promise: The people who help owners sell only get paid if the deal closes. Here is the business that gets paid before it — and the legal line that decides whether you can run it.
- Honest packaging territory: The transition wave against the 30% that actually sell, and the licence question underneath it. No earnings claim.

## Risks and constraints

- Factual uncertainty: No observed readiness-engagement fee, hours, or client outcome. The circulating prepared-versus-unprepared multiple gap is seller-published and excluded (CLM-007).
- Analogy-transfer uncertainty: The missing scheduled examination. Partially but not fully mitigated by the involuntary-exit finding.
- **Legal or regulatory boundary: the central constraint.** 16 states regulate business brokerage and Illinois requires registration; California requires a broker or law licence to advise on a transaction at all; Florida requires a real estate licence (CLM-004). Non-compliance can forfeit fees entirely. The offer must be scoped inside preparation, the revenue model must be fixed-fee rather than success-based, and the episode must direct the viewer to verify their own state rather than ruling on it.
- Permissions, privacy, or guest dependency: None required. Owner financials are confidential and handling standards belong in the Canvas.
- Platform or vendor dependency: None material.
- Ethical failure mode: Drifting across the brokerage line for a share of a deal, or selling readiness on an implied valuation uplift the evidence does not support.
- Claims that must not be made: any specific multiple uplift from preparation; any claim that preparation causes a sale; any figure presented as a typical readiness fee; anything resembling legal advice on licensing.

## Thirty-day validation plan

1. Run one full readiness diagnostic, unpaid, on a real owner-operated business. Record actual hours, and which findings the owner had not already seen.
2. Ask three brokers what they do today with listings they decline as unready, and whether they would refer that work out.
3. Verify the brokerage perimeter in the operator's own state in writing, and define the exact scope line the practice will not cross.

Success signal: An owner recognises findings they had not seen and asks what remediation would cost; at least one broker will refer declined listings.

Kill or redesign condition: Owners see nothing new in the diagnostic, or brokers already do this free to win listings, or the operator's state defines the perimeter so tightly that fixed-fee preparation cannot be sold without a licence.

## Unresolved questions

- **Will an owner pay before there is a deadline?** Owner. Resolved only by validation step 1 — does a real owner, shown real findings, ask what remediation costs.
- **Do brokers already do this free to win listings?** Owner. Resolved by validation step 2. If they do, the referral channel and the paid offer both collapse.
- **What is the exact brokerage perimeter in the operator's own state?** Requires written verification and, before any script lock, legal review. This is the single item most likely to reshape the offer.
- **What does a readiness engagement actually cost to deliver?** Unmeasured. Owner-interview time is the component least reducible by tooling.
