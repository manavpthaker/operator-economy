## Executive finding

- Opportunity: A one-person, agent-run monthly desk that handles an aging parent's bills and watches
  their accounts daily, for the adult child who cannot do it from where they live.
- Evidence class: observed model
- Provisional verdict: plausible with bounded assumptions
- Main reason: Families describe the problem in their own words (CLM-001, CLM-017), and remote,
  monthly human services already sell the job (CLM-002, CLM-003).
- Correction, 2026-11-16: the first pass said the agent change is in the watching. Daily account
  watching already sells as software for $7.49 to $26.99 a month (CLM-016). Watching is not what
  the desk adds. What is left for the desk is the mail, the checking of each bill, the calls, and
  the record, which is the part whose hours are untested.
- Boundary, 2026-11-16: two federal sources set the desk's shape. A money manager that takes
  client money into its own account and pays the bills is a money transmitter (CLM-010). A person
  the parent gives a login or card to can move money the parent is then liable for (CLM-012). The
  defensible desk never holds money and never pays with a shared login: it prepares, and the
  parent or their legal agent pays.
- Revision, 2026-11-19: both remote sellers pay from the parent's own account, not their own
  (CLM-019, CLM-020), so the observed model sits outside the FinCEN ruling's facts. Texas and
  California define money transmission as selling payment instruments or stored value, or
  receiving money for transmission (CLM-021, CLM-022). A desk that never receives the money and
  pays only from the parent's account, under authority the bank recognises (not a shared login),
  does not meet either definition on its face. That is an inference; no regulator opinion on this
  model was found. The desk may therefore pay, but only that way.
- Price pressure, 2026-11-19: SilverBills is listed "from $50 per month" (CLM-020). The $250 floor
  is no longer the only anchor; the modeled price range is widened downward below.
- Price correction, 2026-11-23: SilverBills' domain now redirects to Paytrust, which says it
  relaunched as a concierge bill pay service in 2025 and starts "at $500 a month" (CLM-025). The
  "from $50" figure (CLM-020) came from an older review and no longer describes the live offer.
  The two remote seller floors are now $250 and $500. Paytrust also invites clients to "share your
  account logins" (CLM-025), the practice CLM-012 shows can leave the account holder liable.
- Coverage check, 2026-11-23: two mainstream caregiver guides advise against using a parent's
  login (consent reason only) and to keep records, but neither names the money transmitter line,
  the Regulation E liability, or mandatory reporting (CLM-026, CLM-027). "Never log in as them" is
  common advice; the reasons behind it, and the other two limits, were not found in that coverage.
- Largest unresolved question: Will a family trust a remote desk with a parent's accounts, and how
  many hours does each household really take once calls and exceptions are counted? New second
  question (revised 2026-11-19): with a concierge bill-pay seller listed from $50 a month, what
  does a family pay for the checking, calls, and record on top of payment? (2026-11-23: the $50
  listing is stale; the live floors are $250 and $500, so this question is weaker than it was.)
- Required public framing: The remote service and its $250 floor are seller statements, not
  evidence that families pay it. Household counts, hours, and income are a modeled scenario. Trust
  and price are the viewer's first test.

## Buyer reality

- Buyer: An adult child, often in another state, of a parent who lives alone.
- End customer or beneficiary: The parent.
- Recurring problem: Mail the parent can no longer sort; bills missed or paid twice; premiums
  lapsing; scam charges noticed late (CLM-001; scout ledger items carried, CLM-009).
- Consequence and frequency: Not quantified from a primary source this run. Forum anecdotes only.
- Existing alternatives or budget: The child does it at night with online access, autopay, and
  mail forwarding (CLM-001 replies); local daily money managers by the hour (CLM-006, CLM-009);
  remote bill-paying services (CLM-002, CLM-003); alert apps where the family still does the work
  (CLM-004); general bill-paying services (CLM-005).
- Why now: Agents can read scanned mail and statements and check accounts daily, so watching costs
  far less per household than an hourly visit.

### Legal and access boundary (added 2026-11-16)

- CLM-010: FinCEN ruled that a company selling daily money management, where "the customer
  deposits money into the Company's account" and the company pays the customer's bills by company
  check, "is indeed a money transmitter" under the Bank Secrecy Act (FIN-2012-R004). The ruling
  does not address a manager who only has authority on the client's own account. Fact (federal
  ruling on one company's facts). Allowed inference: a desk that pools or holds client money takes
  on registration and state licensing; this brief's desk must not.
- CLM-011: Texas's banking regulator lists "Third-Party Bill Paying" among the money services it
  regulates and says a licence is generally needed for money transmission unless an exemption
  applies. Fact (regulator FAQ). It does not say a desk that never takes possession is exempt;
  that is unverified.
- CLM-012: Under Regulation E, a transfer is not "unauthorized" when made "by a person who was
  furnished the access device to the consumer's account by the consumer", and if that person
  "exceeds the authority given, the consumer is fully liable for the transfers" until the bank is
  told (12 CFR 1005.2(m)(1); comment 2(m)-2). An access device includes a "code". Fact (federal
  rule). Allowed inference: if a parent hands the desk a login or card and the desk overpays or
  steals, the parent may have no federal claim against the bank. This gives the forum's "never
  delegate" warning (CLM-001) a legal footing.
- CLM-013: Read-only access for a third party is something regulators have suggested banks offer,
  not something banks must offer. The CFPB's 2016 advisory, "voluntary best practices", says banks
  can "offer view-only access for authorized third parties". The 2024 interagency statement says a
  trusted contact "typically would not have authority to view account information or execute
  transactions" unless separately authorized. Fact. How many banks offer view-only access was not
  measured; a desk depends on each parent's bank.
- CLM-014: In Texas "a person having cause to believe that an elderly person ... is in the state
  of abuse, neglect, or exploitation shall report ... immediately" (Human Resources Code
  48.051(a)). In Florida "Any person ... who knows, or has reasonable cause to suspect, that a
  vulnerable adult has been or is being abused, neglected, or exploited must immediately report"
  (Fla. Stat. 415.1034(1)(a), 2026). Fact for two states. Allowed inference: in these states the
  desk operator must report suspected exploitation, including by a family member who is the
  paying customer. California's rule was read only in secondary summaries (it names bank staff)
  and is not used.
- CLM-019 (2026-11-19): Parent Bill Pay says "Bills are paid from their accounts straight to the
  company that sent the bill, and every payment shows on their statement", that "How payments are
  released, and who approves them, is agreed in writing at the start", and that a capable parent
  "can sign the forms that let a bookkeeper see statements and pay bills on their behalf". Seller
  copy. It shows the seller does not hold client money.
- CLM-020 (2026-11-19): A Bills.com review dated 2026-11-01 says SilverBills will "set you up to
  authorize having bill payments made out of your checking account", that "You do not have to keep
  money on deposit with SilverBills", that with permission it notifies the bank of payee and
  amount, and lists the service "from $50 per month". Third-party review read through WebFetch (a
  model's reading of the page); the account mechanics are not on SilverBills' own pages. Updates
  CLM-003's "costs are not listed".
- CLM-021 (2026-11-19): Texas Finance Code 152.003, as enacted by S.B. 895 (88th Leg., eff.
  2023-09-01): "Money transmission" means "(i) selling or issuing payment instruments ... (ii)
  selling or issuing stored value ... or (iii) receiving money for money transmission services",
  and "includes payroll processing services". Fact (enrolled bill text). Note: the chapter was
  enacted by S.B. 895; CLM-011's note that the regulator FAQ cites H.B. 1666 is left as recorded.
- CLM-022 (2026-11-19): California's regulator, quoting Financial Code 2003(q) and (u), states that
  money transmission includes "(3) receiving money for transmission", defined as "receiving money
  or monetary value in the United States for transmission within or outside the United States by
  electronic or other means" (Department of Business Oversight interpretive opinion, 2019-11-26).
  Fact as quoted by the regulator; the letter itself is about a payee's agent, not a household
  desk, and its holding is not used.
- Allowed inference from CLM-021 and CLM-022: a desk that never receives or holds the parent's
  money is outside both definitions on their face. Not shown: any regulator ruling on a desk that
  initiates payments from the parent's own account. The episode may not say "no licence needed".
- CLM-023 (2026-11-19): Florida's power of attorney law says "An agent is a fiduciary", must "keep
  a record of all receipts, disbursements, and transactions made on behalf of the principal", and
  "May not delegate authority to a third person except as authorized" (Fla. Stat. 709.2114(1),
  2026). Fact for one state. Allowed inference: a family member acting under a POA can hire help
  but cannot hand the desk their authority, and the desk's weekly record is the record that agent
  is already required to keep. This sharpens the candidate finding: the product is the record.
- CLM-024 (2026-11-19): Wells Fargo lets a customer create a Guest User with "view-only account
  access"; guests "are not able to ... perform transactions including Transfers and Payments", and
  the customer must "create and manage" the guest's username and password. Fact for one bank (its
  own help page). Two other banks' pages were tried: Huntington returned 403; a Wells Fargo
  caregiving page returned 404; Amboy Bank's senior page offers only a trusted contact. One of
  three banks confirmed.
- CLM-025 (2026-11-23): silverbills.com redirects to paytrust.com. Paytrust's pages say its CEO
  "conceived and supervised its re-launch as a concierge bill pay service in 2025"; pricing is
  "Starting at $500 a month. No long-term contract. No setup fee."; clients "Send your bills to us
  once, or share your account logins"; the concierge "reviews each bill before money moves" and
  keeps "meticulous records"; rules include "approve each payment". A testimonial names
  "Paytrust/Silverbills". Seller copy. It shows an observed remote seller at a $500 floor that
  accepts shared logins. It does not show buyers pay that price, and the site is not aimed only
  at aging parents (it also courts financial advisers).
- Not read: any power of attorney statute outside Florida; the CFPB "Managing Someone Else's
  Money" guides (landing page only, updated 2026-06-25); bond and E&O prices.

## Proposed synthesis

- Parallel A and supported component: remote bill-paying services -> offer, remote delivery,
  monthly floor.
- Parallel B and supported component: local daily money managers -> scope, ethics, insurance norm,
  hourly price.
- Parallel C and supported component: EP010 receivables desk -> agents do the reading, the person
  owns exceptions.
- New combination (revised 2026-11-16): agents do the reading, matching, and drafting inside a
  human service that prepares and records but never holds or moves the money. Daily watching is
  bought in (CLM-016), not built.
- Primary transfer risk: trust and per-household call time. Added: whether "prepare, don't pay"
  is still the job families want done.

## Proposed Operator Blueprint

- Offer: a monthly money desk per household; backlog clean-up priced separately.
- Buyer outcome: no missed or double-paid bills, no lapsed policies, odd charges flagged in a day,
  one shared weekly record.
- Delivery workflow: intake call and written household rules; mail redirect or scan; e-bill routing;
  agent reads and sorts each item; bills checked against the last; payments prepared; family or
  parent approves new or unusual items; daily account watch; weekly one-pager; same-day call on an
  alert.
- Technology and human stack: mail scanning, read-only account views where the parent's bank
  offers them (CLM-013), document-reading agents, a payment step the parent or their legal agent
  performs in their own bank; the operator owns rules, calls, and the record.
- Hard limits (2026-11-16, revised 2026-11-19): no client money in the operator's account
  (CLM-010, CLM-021, CLM-022); no payments made with a parent's shared login or card (CLM-012);
  payments only from the parent's own account under an authority the bank recognises, agreed in
  writing (CLM-019, CLM-020); the POA agent keeps their authority and the desk keeps their record
  (CLM-023); report suspected exploitation where state law requires it (CLM-014).
- Required skills or credentials: bookkeeping or household administration; no license found for
  daily money management (scout, context); bonding and E&O insurance are customary (CLM-006).
- Go-to-market path: referrals from elder-law attorneys, geriatric care managers, and local
  aging services; AgingCare-style forums are where the buyers ask.
- Pricing model: modeled from the $250 seller floor (CLM-002) and hourly DMM rates (CLM-009).
- Defensibility: the record and the approval discipline, plus a named insured person; not the
  software.

## Narrative and audience hypothesis

- Operator protagonist: a former bookkeeper or bank operations worker, or someone who has done this
  for their own parent.
- Starting state: the child finds a double payment and an unknown charity on a Sunday night.
- Inciting change: a shut-off notice or a lapsed premium.
- Stakes: money, coverage, the parent's control, sibling trust, the operator's liability.
- Causal mechanism: nobody near the parent owns the mail; autopay hides errors.
- Build transformation: rules, route, sort, watch, approve, record.
- Decisions and tradeoffs: what the desk may pay without asking; read-only versus payment access;
  how many households before quality slips.
- End state and payoff: a synthetic household's month of records and the viewer's stop rule.
- Visual evidence classes: documents, flows, state changes, comparisons.
- Working audience promise: How one person can watch a parent's money every day, and why the record
  matters more than the payment.
- Honest packaging territory: "The job families are told never to hand off."

## Risks and constraints

- Factual uncertainty: no primary count of the problem; remote prices beyond one floor unknown.
- Analogy-transfer uncertainty: trust from in-person to remote; call time per household.
- Legal or regulatory boundary: money transmission if the desk holds or pools client funds
  (CLM-010, CLM-011); the parent's loss of Regulation E protection if the desk uses a shared login
  (CLM-012); read-only access depends on the bank (CLM-013); mandatory reporting in at least
  Texas and Florida (CLM-014); POA statutes not yet read; no investment, tax, legal, or Medicaid
  advice. None of this is legal advice and the episode may not say "no licence needed".
- Permissions, privacy, or guest dependency: synthetic household only; no real family's documents.
- Platform or vendor dependency: banks' read-only access and mail-scanning vendors.
- Ethical failure mode: the operator becomes the abuse risk the forum warns about; a sibling uses
  the desk against the parent's wishes.
- Claims that must not be made: that families pay remote desks at any price; any scam-loss or
  prevalence number without a primary source; any income figure as a forecast; that the IC3
  figure measures missed bills or what a desk would prevent; that a desk paying from the parent's
  own account is outside money transmitter law (2026-11-19: the definitions read support it on
  their face, but no ruling was found; still not to be said).

## Thirty-day validation plan

These are the viewer's first tests, not owner field work.

1. Write one household's rules and run a synthetic month of mail through the agent workflow; time
   every step, including the calls.
2. Offer the desk to five families through one referral source at a stated monthly price.
3. Ask each family what they would need to see before granting access, and record it.
4. Tell each family plainly that the desk prepares every payment and they or the parent press
   pay; record whether that changes their answer.

Success signal: at least two of five families agree to a paid first month, and the timed workflow
stays under 3 hours per household.

Kill or redesign condition: fewer than two of five agree, or families will only pay for an
in-person visit, or the timed month exceeds 4 hours per household.
