## Executive finding

- Opportunity: A fixed-scope engagement ending in a SKU-level landed-cost record and a written
  keep / reprice / resource / drop decision for a small US importer of record.
- Evidence class: adjacent synthesis
- Provisional verdict: **uncertain**, trending not viable
- Main reason: The work divides into a regulated half and an automated half, and the operator can
  have neither. Classification and valuation for another party for compensation are customs business
  requiring a broker's license, and the definition also reaches "activities relating to the
  preparation" of documents intended to be filed with CBP (CLM-002); the license exceptions do not
  include a paid third-party adviser (CLM-004). What remains after the regulated work is removed —
  the landed-cost calculation — is already sold as automated software at $2 per order plus 10% of
  duties and taxes (CLM-005). No source establishes that anyone pays an independent operator for the
  residual decision layer.
- Largest unresolved question: Is there a lawful unlicensed scope at all, and if there is, does it
  contain anything a buyer would pay for that software and the buyer's own accountant do not already
  cover?
- Required public framing: The rule change is verified fact. The legal boundary reading is an
  unreviewed non-lawyer reading of the regulation text and must be stated as such. The buyer's
  distress, the price ranges, and the market response are seller-described and must not be presented
  as measured demand. No episode may tell a viewer that this service is lawful for them to sell.

## Buyer reality

- Buyer: Owner or finance lead of a small US business that is the importer of record on its own
  inbound goods.
- End customer or beneficiary: The buyer's own retail and marketplace customers.
- Recurring problem: Per-parcel duty and entry cost is now a function of HTS code and country of
  origin on goods that previously entered duty-free, and existing codes were approximate.
- Consequence and frequency: Per shipment, continuously. The fixed per-entry cost falls hardest on
  the smallest shipments. Magnitude is not established by any source accessed on this run; the
  circulating percentage figures are seller content and are recorded as not usable (CLM-008).
- Existing alternatives or budget: Licensed customs brokers; freight forwarders that give
  classification advisory away as an acquisition device; cross-border duty software (CLM-005); the
  buyer's own spreadsheet.
- Why now: De minimis duty-free treatment suspended effective 2025-08-29; CBP modernised low-value
  shipment processing published 2026-06-24 (CLM-001).

## Evidence landscape

### Direct evidence

None. No source accessed on this run shows an independent unlicensed operator selling a bounded
import cost-and-decision engagement, at any price, to any buyer.

### Adjacent business evidence

- Licensed customs brokerage and trade-compliance advisory perform the whole of this workflow,
  including the parts the proposed offer must exclude. Publicly advertised adjacent prices exist
  (CLM-007) but are seller content-marketing pages and are **not usable** as market prices or as
  evidence of completed sales. Shared structure: same buyer, same job, same artifacts. Limit: the
  incumbent is licensed and the operator is not, which is the entire question.
- Fractional ecommerce-finance and merchandising-margin consulting is the closest lawful parallel for
  the residual decision layer. No source was accessed on this run that prices or scopes it against
  this specific job; recorded as a research direction, not evidence.

### Component feasibility evidence

- The landed-cost calculation component demonstrably works: it is productised, integrated at
  checkout, and priced per order (CLM-005). This proves the component and simultaneously undermines
  the offer, because the component is the part the operator would be selling as labor.

### Scaled relevance

The underlying change is government-wide and permanent until further rulemaking (CLM-001). Scale of
the *affected small-importer population* was not established: no primary dataset on affected filer
counts or parcel volumes was accessible on this run (CLM-008, CLM-009 not usable).

## Proposed synthesis

- Parallel A and supported component: fractional ecommerce-finance consulting -> the decision layer
  and fee model.
- Parallel B and supported component: licensed customs brokerage -> workflow shape and the exclusion
  boundary.
- Parallel C and supported component: cross-border duty software -> the calculation component.
- New combination: selling the merchant's own margin decision separated from the regulated
  determination.
- Primary transfer risk: Parallel C is more plausibly a substitute than a component. If the
  calculation is automated and the determination is licensed, the "new combination" is a decision
  memo, and no evidence shows it is purchased.
- Analogy map: not created. A scored map would imply the transfers are close enough to compare; on
  this evidence they are not.

## Proposed Operator Blueprint

- Offer: Fixed-scope import cost-and-decision engagement over a bounded SKU set.
- Buyer outcome: SKU-level landed-cost record per shipping mode, supplier origin-evidence file,
  consolidation comparison, and a written keep / reprice / resource / drop decision per SKU and
  channel.
- Delivery workflow: SKU and supplier inventory; origin documentation collection; classification
  taken as an input from the buyer's licensed broker; landed-cost build; consolidation model;
  channel margin recomputation; decision record.
- Technology and human stack: The operator's judgment and the buyer's existing broker and duty tool.
- Required skills or credentials: Import operations or ecommerce finance. Any version of this offer
  that includes determining classification or valuation requires a customs broker's license.
- Go-to-market path: Unknown. Not researched, because the scope question precedes it.
- Pricing model: **No price is issued.** No observed, transferred, or defensibly modeled input
  exists for this bounded offer.
- Defensibility: Claimed defensibility is accountability for the merchant's own decision. Unproven,
  and thin against an accountant or the buyer doing it themselves.

## Narrative and audience hypothesis

- Operator protagonist: an import-operations or ecommerce-finance operator testing whether a lawful
  business exists inside a regulated workflow.
- Starting state: a small importer prices from invoice plus shipping and never reads a tariff
  schedule.
- Inciting change: the threshold is gone and every parcel carries duty and an entry cost.
- Stakes: real per-parcel money, real penalty exposure, and a service that the law may compress into
  something nobody buys.
- Causal mechanism: duty is a function of code and origin; the importer of record holds the
  liability regardless of who supplied the code.
- Build transformation: a SKU-level cost-and-decision system with a hard boundary at the regulated
  line.
- Decisions and tradeoffs: which determinations to refuse, and whether the remainder is saleable.
- End state and payoff: an honest answer about whether the residual business exists.
- Visual evidence classes: regulation text, tariff lookups, supplier origin documents, a per-SKU cost
  build, before/after channel margin, a broker handoff.
- Working audience promise: the cheapest answer to a duty question is free, and the person who takes
  it is the one who pays when it is wrong.
- Honest packaging territory: the boundary of a regulated job, not a tariff-savings promise.

## Risks and constraints

- Factual uncertainty: affected-population scale, buyer distress magnitude, and real substitution
  rate are all unestablished.
- Analogy-transfer uncertainty: duty software is more likely a substitute than a component.
- Legal or regulatory boundary: **primary risk.** 19 CFR 111.1 / 111.2(a)(2) / 111.4 (CLM-002,
  CLM-004, CLM-003). An episode that encouraged viewers into this work without a qualified review
  could route them into penalty exposure.
- Permissions, privacy, or guest dependency: a licensed broker or customs attorney would likely need
  to be quoted or to appear.
- Platform or vendor dependency: the interim rules and marketplace duty policies are both changing.
- Ethical failure mode: teaching an audience to sell a regulated service.
- Claims that must not be made: that this service is lawful for an unlicensed operator; that any
  advertised broker or software price is a market price or a completed sale; any duty-saving,
  customs-outcome, compliance, or penalty-avoidance promise; any statement of the affected market's
  size; any of the circulating parcel-volume or landed-cost percentage figures (CLM-008).

## Thirty-day validation plan

1. Qualified review by a customs attorney or licensed broker: does a lawful unlicensed scope exist,
   and does taking classification as a broker-supplied input keep the operator outside "activities
   relating to the preparation" of a CBP filing?
2. If a lawful scope exists, one explicit paid ask to a target importer for that scope alone, with
   the regulated tasks visibly removed from the statement of work.
3. Three target-importer interviews on what their broker, forwarder, duty software, and accountant
   already do, and who currently makes the keep/drop decision.

Success signal: a qualified reviewer confirms a lawful scope **and** one target buyer accepts a paid
engagement for that scope with the regulated tasks excluded.

Kill or redesign condition: a qualified reviewer finds no lawful unlicensed scope, or three
interviews show the margin decision is already made by the buyer or the buyer's accountant at no
incremental cost.
