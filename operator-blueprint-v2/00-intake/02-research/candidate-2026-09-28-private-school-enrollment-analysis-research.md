## Executive finding

- Opportunity: Fixed-scope admissions and re-enrollment analysis for small private and religious
  schools, or for the diocese or network that runs them, with every admissions, tuition, and aid
  decision left to the school.
- Evidence class: adjacent synthesis
- Provisional verdict: uncertain
- Main reason: The timing change is verified from primary sources: Texas TEFA pays up to $10,474 per
  private-school student in 2026-27 (CLM-001) with more than 102,000 awards (CLM-002), and the
  federal tax-credit scholarship starts 2027-01-01 (CLM-007). Buyer-side documents show dioceses,
  parishes, and one school paying a planning firm to analyze enrollment (CLM-016 to CLM-019), but
  none gives a price, and the work is regional planning, not admissions-funnel analysis. The main
  substitute is strong: admissions software already reports conversion, selectivity, and yield over
  five years (CLM-013), and dioceses and foundations do similar work in-house or subsidized
  (CLM-022, CLM-024).
- Largest unresolved question: Is there any price a small school or diocese pays for this work,
  and does anything remain to sell once the school's own software report and diocesan help are
  counted?
- Required public framing: TEFA and federal-credit facts are observed. Paid diocesan enrollment
  studies are observed without prices. The small-school funnel-analysis service, its price, hours,
  and demand are hypotheses. No claim that small schools "need" or "buy" this.

## Buyer reality

- Buyer: Head of school or board of a private or religious school under about 300 students, or a
  diocesan schools office buying for several schools. Evidence so far points to the diocese or
  parish as the payer (CLM-016, CLM-017, CLM-019), not the single small school.
- End customer or beneficiary: School leadership; indirectly, applicant families.
- Recurring problem: Reconciling inquiry, application, enrollment, re-enrollment, and aid records
  across years and systems to see where families drop out and why.
- Consequence and frequency: Per-student value for a TEFA family is up to $10,474 in 2026-27
  (CLM-001). Fort Worth diocese reported 400 to 600 new students, far above its own expectation of
  "one to two kids per campus" (CLM-004). 40.0% of U.S. private schools had fewer than 50 students
  and 27.2% had 50 to 149 in 2021-22 (CLM-011); Catholic schools are larger (38.6% at 150 to 299).
- Existing alternatives or budget: built-in funnel reports (CLM-013, CLM-014), low-cost small-school
  systems from $75 a month (CLM-015), diocesan enrollment staff (CLM-022), foundation coaching
  (CLM-024), paid planning studies (CLM-016 to CLM-019), enrollment consulting firms (CLM-026).
- Why now: TEFA first year (CLM-001 to CLM-003); federal credit in 27 opted-in states including
  Texas as of 2026-06-08 (CLM-008); falling births and a projected 13% drop in high-school graduates
  from 2025 to 2041 (CLM-009, CLM-010).

### Buyer side considered: family side

The owner's words ("private school admissions analysis") could mean helping parents get a child
admitted. The family side was checked and not adopted. Evidence there is seller price pages from
admissions consultants, which are not buyer-paid evidence, and the market is crowded. TEFA widens
the family pool but the program's money goes to approved schools and providers; no evidence was
found that TEFA families pay for admissions help. The school side has at least buyer-side documents
of paid enrollment studies (CLM-016 to CLM-019) and a clear operator job. Reopen the family side
separately only if the owner meant it.

### Component feasibility evidence

- CLM-013: Finalsite Enrollment's Admissions Report shows "Admissions funnel data with student
  counts, conversion, selectivity, and yield rates across years", current term plus four prior
  years. This is the substitute, and it also shows the data exists in structured form where a school
  uses the system.
- CLM-014: Blackbaud EMS advertises funnel dashboards (search summary; not opened).
- CLM-015: SchoolCues, a small-school system, "From $75/month", with admissions reports.
- What AI would do (hypothesis, not tested): de-duplicate families across years when names, emails,
  and household IDs differ; classify free-text "how did you hear about us" and withdrawal reasons;
  match each year's re-enrollment list to the prior roster and flag TEFA or scholarship status;
  draft the findings memo from computed tables for the operator to rewrite. Before AI, this was an
  admissions director's spare-hours spreadsheet work, an on-site consultant visit, or not done.
  Hours saved are unknown.

- Exact target queries: "private school enrollment analysis", "enrollment audit private school",

- Adjacent query or question families: TEFA parent questions; Catholic school closure coverage.
- Volume source, attempted measurement, and date: not measured this run.
- Other demand signals: paid diocesan studies (CLM-016 to CLM-019); a diocese hiring an Enrollment

## Proposed synthesis

- Parallel A and supported component: Meitler studies → buyer (diocese, parish, school), scope
  (enrollment trends and demographics). Price unobserved.
- Parallel B and supported component: public-district recruitment contracts → a budget category
  exists when choice programs move students. Different buyer.
- Parallel C and supported component: diocesan data role and foundation coaching → the job is
  recognized; often in-house or subsidized.
- New combination: AI cleaning and matching makes a school-level funnel analysis small enough to
  sell at a fixed fee below a planning study.
- Primary transfer risk: diocesan planning studies are bought for big decisions (new schools,
  mergers); a funnel analysis for one small school is a smaller decision that the software report
  may already answer.
- Analogy map: not created this run.

## Proposed Operator Blueprint

- Offer: Fixed-fee enrollment analysis, school-level or diocesan package.
- Buyer outcome: cleaned multi-year dataset, readable funnel, re-enrollment risk list, TEFA-family
  comparison, three changes to test.
- Delivery workflow: scope and data agreement → exports from SIS or spreadsheets → AI de-duplication
  and matching → operator check of every match → funnel and cohort tables → written findings →
  school decides.
- Technology and human stack: school's own system access or de-identified extracts; spreadsheet or
  notebook; AI for matching, classification, and draft text; operator verification.
- Required skills or credentials: data cleaning, basic cohort analysis, clear writing for a head and
  board, data-handling discipline. No licence identified.
- Go-to-market path: diocesan schools offices, state private-school associations, TEFA-participating
  school lists (finder tool). Unverified.
- Pricing model: fixed fee; no observed anchor.
- Defensibility: weak against the school's own report; stronger where data sits in spreadsheets or
  several systems, or where a diocese wants the same analysis across many schools.

## Delivery boundary

- FERPA: the Department of Education states FERPA "does not apply to private schools at the
  elementary and secondary levels because they do not generally receive such funds" (CLM-006).
  Texas SB 2 §29.368(a) says a provider receiving TEFA money "is not a recipient of federal financial
  assistance" (CLM-005). A school that does take Department funds is covered; check per school.
- Texas TEFA: §29.369(c) requires a provider that obtains information about a participating child to
  follow state and federal confidentiality law and bars it from selling "or otherwise distribute"
  that information (CLM-005). Whether sharing records with a contracted analyst is "distribution" is
  unresolved; the safe design is working inside the school's own accounts or on de-identified
  extracts until a qualified reading exists.
- §29.368(b)(2)(B) protects the school's freedom in "determining admissions and enrollment practices,
  policies, and standards" (CLM-005). The operator advises; the school decides.
- Other states' voucher and scholarship-organization rules and state student-privacy laws: not
  checked.

## Narrative and audience hypothesis

- Operator protagonist: A former admissions or business-office person, or a data analyst, deciding
  whether to sell this.
- Starting state: A small school with five years of records in two systems and a spreadsheet.
- Inciting change: TEFA brings new applicants; births keep falling.
- Stakes: tuition revenue per family; family data handled outside the school.
- Causal mechanism: no one has time to reconcile the records.
- Build transformation: fixed-scope analysis with AI matching and a written hand-back.
- Decisions and tradeoffs: school vs diocese as buyer; inside-account work vs de-identified extract;
  whether to sell against the built-in report or only to schools without one.
- End state and payoff: synthetic school rebuilt on screen; viewer compares it with a built-in
  funnel report and decides.
- Visual evidence classes: program documents, NCES size table, messy vs clean export, funnel chart.
- Working audience promise: "School-choice money is changing who applies. Here is what small
  schools already pay for, what their software already does, and what is left to sell."
- Honest packaging territory: the $10,474 student and the school that cannot see where families go.

## Risks and constraints

- Factual uncertainty: school counts conflict (CLM-027); Fort Worth figure is a news report
  (CLM-004); the birth figures come from search summaries of NCHS reports (CLM-010).
- Analogy-transfer uncertainty: planning studies for dioceses vs funnel analysis for one school.
- Legal or regulatory boundary: §29.369(c) distribution bar; FERPA where a school takes Department
  funds; other states' program rules unchecked.
- Permissions, privacy, or guest dependency: none for the episode; high sensitivity for the business
  (children's records, family income).
- Platform or vendor dependency: school systems and their export quality.
- Ethical failure mode: analysis used to screen out families by income or disability; data leaving
  the school.
- Claims that must not be made: that schools lose "most" families or cannot read their funnel; the
  pool's "13% private-school enrollment decline" (it is WICHE's high-school-graduate projection,
  CLM-009); any price or earnings figure; seller-reported outcomes such as "55% increase in
  applications" (CLM-026); that TEFA makes schools grow.

## Thirty-day validation plan

This is the viewer's first test, not an owner condition.

1. Desk (week 1): pull the TEFA school finder list for one region; mark schools under about 150
   students and which admissions system each names on its site; find the diocesan schools office
   for each.
2. Rehearsal (week 1-2): build a synthetic three-year export with duplicate families, free-text
   sources, and a re-enrollment roster; run AI matching with full human check; log hours; compare
   the output with what a built-in funnel report gives.
3. Field (week 2-4): offer a fixed-scope analysis at a stated price to ten small schools and two
   diocesan offices; ask what they use today and what they paid for any enrollment study.

Success signal: one paid engagement or signed scope with a price, plus at least three buyers naming
a need the built-in report does not meet.

Kill or redesign condition: buyers uniformly say their software report or diocesan office covers it,
or no one will share records under a data agreement. Redesign toward diocesan packages if single
schools decline but dioceses engage.
