## Executive finding

- Opportunity: A bounded, research-designed diagnostic that tells a brand how it is represented in AI-assisted answers, which sources those answers cite, and which gaps are genuinely controllable.
- Evidence class: **adjacent synthesis**
- Provisional verdict: **plausible with bounded assumptions**
- Main reason: The buyer behaviour is now well evidenced by independent research (Pew, n=5,119), the category has institutional validation (Profound, $1B valuation), and the adjacent audit-and-monitoring sale is established. What is *not* evidenced is that the intervention reliably works — and the strongest independent academic review says plainly that it does not yet.
- Largest unresolved question: **Whether a defensible diagnostic can be delivered at a sellable price.** The independent evidence on measurement instability implies far more sampling than the obvious service design assumes, which raises delivery hours against a fee the market appears to anchor low.
- Required public framing: Consumer behaviour change is **observed**. Category investment is **observed**. The service format is **transferred** from SEO audits and brand monitoring. Every price, capacity and margin figure is **modeled**. The claim that optimisation reliably improves organic discoverability is **not supported** and must not be made.

## Buyer reality

- Buyer: A marketing, content, or organic-growth leader responsible for digital discovery at a consumer-facing brand.
- End customer or beneficiary: The consumer using an AI assistant to research or compare options.
- Recurring problem: Conventional search reporting can read healthy while AI-assisted answers omit, misdescribe, or unfavourably compare the brand — drawn from a source mix the brand neither owns nor audits.
- Consequence and frequency: **Unquantified.** No independent source found that links AI-answer absence to measurable revenue loss. This is a genuine gap, not a research shortfall, and the blueprint must say so.
- Existing alternatives or budget: SEO audits, competitive analysis, brand monitoring and agency retainers are established purchases with existing budget owners. Vendor tools now sell prompt and mention tracking directly.
- Why now: Independent survey evidence shows AI-assisted information seeking is now mainstream in the US (CLM-001), while buyer-side measurement practice remains immature and the independent evidence base for intervention is weak (CLM-004).

## Evidence landscape

### Direct evidence

Sparse, and honestly so. No independent evidence was found for a standalone paid AI-visibility *diagnostic* practice with disclosed pricing, delivery hours, or client outcomes. Published GEO pricing is almost entirely agency self-published (CLM-005), which under `EVIDENCE-STANDARD.md` may verify a firm's own published rates but cannot carry market demand or industry-wide economics.

### Adjacent business evidence

The SEO and digital-marketing market already sells fixed-scope diagnostics ahead of, and separately from, implementation, and sells recurring reporting on channels the brand does not control. The *format* of the sale — bounded audit, prioritised actions, optional retainer — is well established. The limit: SEO audits are bought partly because the metric is trusted and the remedy is known. Neither yet holds here.

### Component feasibility evidence

Commercial platforms demonstrably sample prompts and log mentions, competitors and citations at scale — Profound reports processing over 100 million AI search queries monthly across 700+ enterprise customers (CLM-003). This evidences **capability**, not buyer value or service demand. Academic work also establishes that citation visibility is measurable and manipulable under controlled conditions (CLM-006).

### Scaled relevance

Independent, current, methodologically disclosed survey evidence establishes the underlying behaviour change at national scale (CLM-001). Category investment establishes institutional belief and buyer budgets at the enterprise tier (CLM-002).

### Demand and competition

- Exact target queries: `ai visibility audit`, `geo agency`, `how to appear in chatgpt answers`, `generative engine optimization cost`
- Adjacent query or question families: whether a brand appears in AI answers; whether GEO is real or repackaged SEO; how to measure AI citations
- Volume source, attempted measurement, and date: **attempted, not measurable, 2026-09-01.** No exact-volume tool is available to this operation — the V1 intake recorded that the Ahrefs plan carries no API access, and that constraint is unchanged. Vendor-published growth percentages were found but are marketing claims, not measurement, and are not used.
- Other demand signals: (1) independent national survey evidence of the underlying behaviour, CLM-001; (2) category funding and enterprise customer count, CLM-002/CLM-003; (3) commercial supply-side proliferation — a dense field of agencies publishing GEO pricing pages, CLM-005, which evidences seller belief and competitive intensity rather than buyer demand; (4) sustained independent academic attention across 2023–2026, CLM-004/CLM-006.
- Independent signal count: **three independent families** (academic, national survey, venture/enterprise adoption), plus one supply-side signal that is explicitly not treated as demand.
- Strongest existing coverage: agency pricing guides and vendor category explainers. Uniformly written by parties selling the service.
- Information gap: **No neutral account of whether the business works.** No published source found that states the measurement-reliability problem and prices the honest version of the service against it. That is the episode.

## Proposed synthesis

- Parallel A and supported component: SEO audit → fixed-scope paid diagnostic sold before implementation
- Parallel B and supported component: Brand and competitive monitoring → recurring observation of representation in unowned channels
- Parallel C and supported component: AI-visibility tooling → prompt sampling, mention logging, citation capture
- New combination: Research-designed sampling with an explicit repetition protocol, plus a citation-source map, plus a controllability classification — sold as bounded professional judgement rather than a dashboard subscription or a ranking promise.
- Primary transfer risk: **The analogy transfers the format of the sale without the reason for it.** An SEO audit is bought because the metric is trusted and the fix is known; here the metric is unstable (CLM-004) and the fix is unproven (CLM-006, CLM-007). The diagnostic may be honest and still unsellable.
- Analogy map: `../03-validation/candidate-2026-09-01-ai-visibility-diagnostic-analogy-map.md`

## Proposed Operator Blueprint

- Offer: A fixed-scope AI discovery diagnostic — one brand, one market, a buyer-approved question set, multiple answer surfaces, **with a disclosed repetition protocol per question**.
- Buyer outcome: A decision-grade baseline: where the brand appears, how it is described, what is cited, what is factually wrong, what is controllable, what to test, and how and when the sample repeats.
- Delivery workflow: Scope and decision question → question design with buyer approval → repeated capture at recorded times and configurations → log presence, description, competitors, citations, **and run-to-run variance** → accuracy review against approved brand documents → controllability classification → prioritised actions → repeat-measurement plan.
- Technology and human stack: Jobs first — sample design, source validation, variance interpretation, factual review, prioritisation, and buyer communication. Tools serve capture and logging; none of them supply the judgement.
- Required skills or credentials: Research design, source validation, uncertainty communication, and marketing-buyer fluency. Explicitly not: model training, algorithmic influence, or guaranteed placement.
- Go-to-market path: Practitioners with an existing organic-discovery client relationship, sold as an addition to work already trusted. No large audience assumed.
- Pricing model: **Bounded hypothesis.** Published agency one-time audit pricing spans roughly $1,500–$5,000 at the snapshot tier and $8,000–$25,000 at the engagement tier (CLM-005), but every one of those figures is seller-published. Treated as a range the market *asks*, not a range it *pays*.
- Defensibility: The repetition protocol and the controllability split. Anyone can resell a dashboard reading; the defensible product is a sample a buyer can trust and an honest statement of what cannot be influenced.

## Narrative and audience hypothesis

- Operator protagonist: An experienced organic-discovery practitioner whose existing service line is being devalued by a shift they cannot yet measure.
- Starting state: The search dashboard is green. Rankings hold. The discovery problem looks solved.
- Inciting change: The category question is asked in an AI assistant and the brand is absent — or described in a competitor's framing, citing a source the brand has never seen.
- Stakes: The practitioner can sell certainty and be dishonest, sell only uncertainty and not get paid, or find the bounded thing that is both true and sellable.
- Causal mechanism: AI answers assemble from a source mix the brand does not own, so the controlled channel and the actual representation have come apart — and the measurement itself is unstable run to run.
- Build transformation: A diagnostic with a disclosed repetition protocol, a citation-source map, and an explicit controllable/uncontrollable split.
- Decisions and tradeoffs: How many repetitions before the sample is defensible; what to refuse to promise; whether to price the honest version or decline the work.
- End state and payoff: The viewer can decide whether to build it, and knows the exact condition under which it fails.
- Visual evidence classes: the green dashboard against the absent answer; one question across two surfaces returning different sources; run-to-run variance; a citation map; the controllability split; delivery hours against price.
- Working audience promise: Everyone is selling AI visibility. Almost nobody has priced the honest version — here is what it can truthfully promise and what it costs to deliver.
- Honest packaging territory: The measurement problem underneath a category that has already produced a unicorn. No earnings claim, no guaranteed-visibility premise.

## Risks and constraints

- Factual uncertainty: No quantified financial consequence of AI-answer absence. Delivery hours and price are modeled, not observed.
- Analogy-transfer uncertainty: The SEO-audit parallel may transfer the sale format without the reason for the sale.
- Legal or regulatory boundary: None identified. No scraping of protected surfaces is required; capture is of publicly returned answers.
- Permissions, privacy, or guest dependency: None. No guest, no named client.
- Platform or vendor dependency: Answer surfaces change without notice; a captured baseline may not be reproducible later. This is a property of the product, not a research gap, and belongs in the blueprint.
- Ethical failure mode: **Selling certainty.** The commercial field's central failure, named directly by the independent survey — "commercial claims about GEO have advanced faster than the evidence" (CLM-004).
- Claims that must not be made: that optimisation reliably improves organic discoverability; that the 40% benchmark figure describes real-world results; that absence from AI answers has a known revenue cost; that any price, capacity, or margin figure is typical.

## Thirty-day validation plan

1. Run the diagnostic once, unpaid, against a real consumer brand with an existing relationship. Record actual hours at the repetition count the evidence requires.
2. Present the finished baseline to a real marketing buyer and ask what they would pay, before naming a price.
3. Recapture the same question set 14 days later and measure run-to-run and week-to-week variance directly, rather than inheriting the published figures.

Success signal: A buyer names a figure at or above the modeled floor after seeing an honest deliverable that promises no placement; measured variance is low enough that the baseline is defensible.

Kill or redesign condition: Actual delivery hours at a defensible repetition count exceed what the named price supports, **or** measured variance is high enough that no bounded sample can produce a trustworthy baseline. Either outcome kills the service as specified — and is still a publishable episode.

## Unresolved questions

- **Will a buyer pay a professional fee for a measurement with no quantified business consequence?** Owner. Resolved only by validation step 2 — presenting a real deliverable and asking before pricing.
- **What is the true delivery hour count at a defensible repetition level?** Owner. Resolved by validation step 1, measured rather than estimated.
- **Is run-to-run variance in practice as high as CLM-004 reports?** Owner. Resolved by validation step 3. If it is, the diagnostic needs more sampling than any low-thousands fee supports, and the honest answer may be that the service does not clear.
- **Does any seller-published price correspond to a real transaction?** Unresolved and possibly unresolvable from public sources. Carried as a disclosed limit on all pricing language.
