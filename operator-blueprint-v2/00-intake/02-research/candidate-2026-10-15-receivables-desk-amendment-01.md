## What this changes

1. **The bottom of the price ladder is $0, not $99.** A QuickBooks-connected tier sends follow-up emails for free. Sending has no price left to defend.
2. **A funded seller now sells the human layer too.** The human part is no longer unsold territory. The operator can win only where this seller does not go, on disclosed price and on smaller firms. CLM-019 (CollBox) and CLM-017 (Chaser Care) already showed human desks; this one bundles the same judgment work the desk would sell.
3. **The human-layer list gets more concrete.** The seller's own list (the disputed line, the customer who says they paid, the account too important to nudge twice, W-9s, portals) is a usable description of the job. It is seller copy and shows what is sold, not that buyers value it.

## What this does not change

- **Park condition: not met.** The 2026-10-22 condition is an agent desk bundling a human specialist at or below $300–$500 a month. Invoice Butler discloses no price, requires an annual contract paid in advance, and compares itself to a $60–100K hire. That suggests a larger buyer than the desk's. It does not prove the price is higher than $500, so the comparison stays an inference.
- Bounded recency check 2026-11-02 (one Exa news search since 2026-10-26): no new seller of a human AR specialist at a disclosed price at or below $500 a month. Ignition's Parkes (press release 2026-10-26) chases invoices for accounting firms' own billing, read from headline and summary only; it is not recorded as a claim. One search is not proof of absence.
- The six bound hashes, score (68/100), evidence class, and hard-gate results are unchanged. No score was revisited.
