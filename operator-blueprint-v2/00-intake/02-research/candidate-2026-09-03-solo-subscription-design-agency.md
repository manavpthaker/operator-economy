## Executive finding

- Opportunity: One designer-operator sells seed-stage B2B software startups a month-to-month marketing-design subscription — one active request at a time, 48-hour first draft, flat fee — with AI tooling on production and the operator's judgment on the price.
- Evidence class: **observed model** — the exact offer contract is published by a solo operator and by a category of roughly fifty vendors. What is *not* observed is any audited solo economics.
- Provisional verdict: **plausible with bounded assumptions**, and materially weaker than the legacy screen's 72-82 band suggested.
- Main reason: The business exists and its offer, price ladder and delivery contract are verifiable from the sellers' own pages. But the three things that decide whether a *new* solo operator succeeds — that this buyer pays above the commodity floor, that they stay past month one, and that an unknown operator can acquire them without an audience — have no independent evidence. Retention has none at all. Meanwhile the category's two most visible scaled operators retreated in the last fifteen months (CLM-006, CLM-004) and the buyer is being sold the same production tools (CLM-009).
- Largest unresolved question: **Will a seed-stage startup that already has Figma Make pay one unknown designer $2,500-$5,000 a month, and renew?**
- Required public framing: The offer contract, the category price ladder, Design Pickle's retreat from "unlimited", Figma's adoption figures and BLS labor data are **observed**. Buyer behavior is **transferred** from a founder survey about contract talent generally, not design specifically. Every solo revenue, client-count, hours and churn figure is **modeled** — the only public solo figures are one founder's self-reports, told at five different revenue levels, and are **excluded**. Retention is a **hypothesis**.

## Buyer reality

- Buyer: Founder or first marketing hire at a seed-stage B2B software startup, roughly 2-20 people, no designer on the team.
- End customer or beneficiary: The startup's prospects and investors.
- Recurring problem: A continuous need for marketing assets — a landing page per campaign, a deck per raise, creative per ad test — with no one to make them. Seed-stage companies now average 6.2 equity-holding employees, down from 10.3 in 2021 (CLM-014), and 61% of surveyed early-stage companies describe themselves as reliant on contract talent, a third of them explicitly to avoid or delay full-time hires (CLM-013).
- Consequence and frequency: **Not quantified in dollars by any source found.** The consequence is delay and credibility — a launch that ships unfinished or waits on a project quote. The package must carry this as an unquantified cost, not manufacture one.
- Existing alternatives or budget: DIY with AI builders; project freelancers; subscription vendors from $699 (ManyPixels) and $995 (Penji) through $4,995 (Designjoy) to $15,000 monthly minimum plus $1,000 software fee (Superside) (CLM-001, CLM-003, CLM-007). A category directory compares 50 vendors, 45 with published prices from about $39 to $6,990 and a typical entry point near $990 (CLM-008). Budget exists as a category. Whether this buyer pays a *solo* operator above the floor is untested.
- Why now: Production compressed — Figma Make weekly actives grew over 70% quarter-over-quarter in Q4 2025 and about 60% of Figma's $100K+ customers use it weekly (CLM-009). The same tools reach the buyer directly. BLS now projects graphic-designer employment to *decline* 2% through 2035 and names AI tools as the reason (CLM-011). And the category's incumbents moved: Design Pickle ended "unlimited" on 2025-06-02 (CLM-006); Superside cut roughly 60 people on 2026-03-05 (CLM-004).

The exact solution is not novel. The underlying buyer behavior — early-stage reliance on contract talent — has usable adjacent evidence. Design-specific willingness to pay a solo operator does not.

## Evidence landscape

### Direct evidence

- **Designjoy's published contract** (CLM-001): $4,995/month (shown as a discount from $5,995), one request at a time, average 48-hour delivery, up to two users, Webflow development included, pause or cancel any time, 75% refund if not satisfied in week one, and the statement that the service is "run entirely by Brett" with no hired designers or outsourcing. This is the exact solo offer, verifiable as the seller's own current terms.
- **Designjoy's economics** (CLM-002): **not usable.** Revenue is relayed at $1M, $1.5M, $1.7M ARR ($145K MRR), "$2M+" and "$3.1M in 2024" across outlets; client count at "about 20" in a June 2025 podcast and "about 35" in secondary write-ups. Every figure traces to the founder's own statements. Under `EVIDENCE-STANDARD.md` a self-published figure cannot establish industry economics, and here the variance across tellings is itself the finding.
- **The category price ladder** (CLM-007, CLM-008): ManyPixels $699-$2,599; Penji $995-$4,500+; a 50-vendor directory with a typical entry near $990. These are vendors' own prices and verify only that the prices are published — not that any given vendor is profitable or retains clients.
- **Design Pickle's retreat** (CLM-006): a primary vendor statement, dated 2025-06-24, that the company ended "unlimited" on 2025-06-02 because clients could request endlessly while delivery capacity was finite — "It wasn't a lie. But it wasn't the whole truth either." This is direct evidence about the model's central promise from an operator that ran it at scale.

### Adjacent business evidence

- **Superside** (CLM-003, CLM-004, CLM-005): the venture-scale version of the same subscription. Its published minimum is $15,000/month on an annual term plus $1,000/month software; its ARR is only available as a third-party estimate ($44.9M for 2024) and is not used; its March 2026 restructuring (about 60 people; 9% or 15% of staff depending on the aggregator) is reported only by layoff trackers with no company statement found, so it is carried as low-confidence context.
- **Early-stage contractor reliance** (CLM-013, CLM-014): a 1,500-founder survey and Carta headcount data support the buyer's behavior — lean teams that buy capacity rather than hire. Neither isolates design spend.
- **Graphic-design labor market** (CLM-011): BLS median pay $62,960 (May 2025), 253,100 jobs, 20% self-employed, projected -2% to 2035 with AI named as the driver. Supports the labor-cost anchor and the why-now; it does not support demand for the offer.

### Component feasibility evidence

- **Tooling** (CLM-009, CLM-012, CLM-018): Figma Professional Full seat $16/month with Figma Make included; Framer Pro $30/month; Relume from $18-$40/month. Figma's own 2,500-user survey (April 2025) found 78% agree AI significantly enhances efficiency but only 32% say they can rely on the output, and only 31% of designers use AI in core design work. Read honestly: production is faster; selection and finishing remain human. Tool capability is not demand.
- **Acquisition mechanics** (CLM-015, CLM-021): cold-email reply benchmarks conflict by an order of magnitude — 3.43% average on one platform's data, 0.45% on another's 7.5 million emails — and the legacy brief's "5.8% for lists under 50" could not be found on the page it was attributed to. BusyLobby, the first-party demo-first loop, stands at 6 emails, 1 callback, 0 clients. Acquisition is a component with a process and no proof.

### Scaled relevance

The problem is scaled by the buyer population (Carta seed cohort, Mercury's 1,500 respondents) and by the category's supply (50+ vendors, Superside's $15K minimum with hundreds of reported clients). The category's incumbents restructuring (CLM-004, CLM-006) is scaled relevance of a different kind: evidence that the model's promise is under pressure exactly where it is largest. None of this establishes that a new solo operator will win a client.

### Demand and competition

- Exact target queries: `design subscription business`, `how to start a design subscription agency`, `Designjoy model`, `productized design service`, `unlimited design subscription`.
- Adjacent query or question families: `how many clients can one designer handle`, `design subscription vs freelancer`, `best design subscription for startups`, `design pickle unlimited`.
- Volume source, attempted measurement, and date: **attempted, not measurable, 2026-09-03.** Google Trends returned HTTP 403 (CLM-019); no licensed exact-volume tool is available to this operation.
- Other demand signals: (1) supply-side — 50 vendors in one directory with published prices, ~27 designer listings on Dribbble's services search for "monthly creative subscription" from $1,000 to $13,500 (CLM-008); (2) buyer behavior — 61% early-stage reliance on contract talent (CLM-013); (3) category stress as tension — Design Pickle's retraction and Superside's cuts (CLM-004, CLM-006); (4) creator coverage of the reference operator across Starter Story, podcasts and YouTube, though the one view count captured was modest (4,054 views on a June 2025 podcast episode) (CLM-020).
- Independent signal count: **three usable families** — vendor-published category supply, an independent founder survey, and a public-company filing plus BLS for the why-now. Creator coverage is context.
- Strongest existing coverage: vendor listicles ("10 best design subscription services 2026"), founder-story pieces repeating one self-reported revenue figure (Starter Story, Medium, Startup Stash, YesPress), and Brett Williams's own essays and podcast appearances.
- Information gap: none of that coverage treats the request-cadence contract as the actual product, excludes the self-reported economics, names the variance across tellings, or connects the incumbents' retreat from "unlimited" to what a one-person version can honestly promise. See "Original synthesis" in the POV section.

## Proposed synthesis

- Parallel A and supported component: Designjoy's published contract → offer, delivery cadence, price ceiling for a solo operator.
- Parallel B and supported component: Commodity and mid-market subscription vendors → price floor, accepted deliverable scope, competitive density.
- Parallel C and supported component: Design Pickle's end of "unlimited" (and Superside's restructuring as weaker corroboration) → the capacity constraint, transferred as the thing a solo operator must publish rather than hide.
- New combination: None claimed. This is an observed model bounded to one buyer, one cadence and one test, with honest economics.
- Primary transfer risk: Designjoy's price ceiling may be inseparable from its founder's audience on X. A new operator with no audience may only be able to sell at the floor, where the model is a job rather than a business.

## Proposed Operator Blueprint

- Offer: Month-to-month marketing-design subscription — landing pages, sales and pitch decks, ad and social creative. One active request at a time, unlimited queue, 48-hour first draft, unlimited revisions, source files owned by the client, pause or cancel any time.
- Buyer outcome: A queue of shipped marketing assets per month, each within the published turnaround.
- Delivery workflow: Request lands on a shared board → operator scopes it against the contract (in scope / out of scope / split) → AI-assisted draft (Figma Make, v0 or Lovable for layout and variants; Midjourney for imagery; Claude for copy alternatives) → operator selects, edits, finishes → client review → ship → next request.
- Technology and human stack: Jobs first — scoping, selection, taste, brand consistency, client communication. Tools produce options; the operator picks and finishes. Figma's own survey says only a third of users can rely on AI output (CLM-012); that gap is the job.
- Required skills or credentials: **Real design ability with a portfolio.** Marketing-design fluency, typographic and layout judgment, Figma proficiency, Framer or Webflow build skill, and the discipline to hold a 48-hour promise. No credential; but no shortcut — a non-designer cannot run this on tools.
- Go-to-market path: Demo-first outreach to a named list — build the prospect's landing page before contacting them, send the demo, track who views it, call the viewers. The channel that built the reference operator (an audience on X) is not assumed.
- Pricing model: **Bounded hypothesis, anchored to observed prices.** Introductory $2,500-$3,500/month, between the commodity floor and the solo ceiling; move toward $4,995 only with a case study. Charged in advance.
- Defensibility: Taste and turnaround consistency, a small roster the operator knows deeply, and the fact that the buyer's own Figma Make button produces options but not decisions. It is thin, and the episode must say so.

Capacity case formula: `$3,000 x 5 clients x 12 months = $180,000 gross revenue per year`

Contribution case formula (monthly): `$15,000 - $0 delivery labor (owner) - $250 software - $0 contractors - $500 acquisition and overhead = $14,250 contribution before owner compensation and tax`

Fully loaded check after imputed owner labor: `$14,250 - (40 hours/week x 4.33 x $45) = $14,250 - $7,794 = $6,456 per month`

Stress case (6 hours per request, 4 clients at $2,500): `$10,000 - $250 - $500 = $9,250 cash; minus 40 hours x 4.33 x $45 = $1,456 after imputed labor`

Disclosure: `Modeled scenario, not observed performance or an earnings forecast.`

**Load-bearing economic note.** At the modeled point this is a well-paid job for one designer, not the legacy episode's seven-figure story. The only public figures above this level are one founder's self-reports and are excluded. The model's two unknowns — retention and the price an unknown operator can actually clear — are each large enough to move the result from "good freelance income" to "below freelance rates," and neither has evidence.

## Narrative and audience hypothesis

- Operator protagonist: A designer deciding whether to turn freelance project work into a subscription with a published cadence — and a non-designer learning that they cannot.
- Starting state: A seed-stage founder needs a landing page by Tuesday; there is no designer; the last freelancer quote took a week.
- Inciting change: AI tools make the first draft nearly free, for the founder as much as any vendor, and the category's biggest operators respond by retreating from "unlimited."
- Stakes: Five clients is the roster; one leaving is a fifth of revenue; a sixth breaks the 48-hour promise; the buyer's own tools get better every quarter.
- Causal mechanism: Subscription design sells capacity, not design. "Unlimited" breaks when many clients queue against a shared bench (CLM-006). One operator with one request at a time is the only version in which the promise and the capacity are the same number.
- Build transformation: A published cadence contract, a request queue the operator can actually clear, and a demo-first outreach loop with a kill condition.
- Decisions and tradeoffs: Price at the floor or the ceiling; scope creep versus the contract; when to say no to a sixth client; what to do the month a client cancels.
- End state and payoff: The viewer knows whether they are a designer who can sell this, what to charge, how many clients one person can honestly keep, and the day-30 renewal question that decides it.
- Visual evidence classes: pricing pages side by side ($699 → $4,995 → $15,000); the request board and a 48-hour clock; Design Pickle's retraction in its own words; Figma Make's adoption line against BLS's employment decline; a demo redesign beside the prospect's live page; the outreach tracker with its zero.
- Working audience promise: The biggest design-subscription companies stopped promising "unlimited." The one-person version never had to. Here is what that business actually sells, what it can charge, and how many clients one person can keep.
- Honest packaging territory: "Unlimited" is over; one request at a time is the business. No revenue figure in the title.

## Risks and constraints

- Factual uncertainty: No audited solo economics; no category retention data; buyer consequence unquantified; cold-outreach benchmarks conflict by an order of magnitude; Superside's restructuring is aggregator-reported only.
- Analogy-transfer uncertainty: Designjoy's price may depend on its founder's audience; Design Pickle's capacity problem at scale may not describe a five-client roster (the transfer is that the *constraint* is the same, not the outcome).
- Legal or regulatory boundary: Subscription cancellation. The FTC's click-to-cancel rule was vacated by the Eighth Circuit in July 2025; the FTC restarted rulemaking on 2026-03-11; ROSCA and Section 5 enforcement continue (CLM-017). The Canvas must specify clear terms, express consent and a simple cancellation path. IP assignment and stock-asset licensing belong in the client agreement. Not legal advice.
- Permissions, privacy, or guest dependency: Unsolicited demo redesigns use a prospect's public brand; the demo must be private, non-published and clearly labeled as unsolicited. No guest required. Any first-party client shown on screen requires written permission.
- Platform or vendor dependency: Figma, Framer and AI-tool pricing and capability change quarterly; the buyer-side improvement of those tools is the central disintermediation risk (CLM-009).
- Ethical failure mode: Selling "unlimited"; presenting AI drafts as finished design; presenting the reference operator's self-reported revenue as typical; implying a non-designer can run this on tools.
- Claims that must not be made: any Designjoy revenue or client-count figure as fact; "70% of Figma's active users use AI" (CLM-010, not found in primary); "5.8% reply rate for small lists" (CLM-015, not found at source); any churn or retention rate; any income range as typical; that BusyLobby has clients or revenue; that Manav has run a design business.

## Thirty-day validation plan

1. **Build the list and the demos (days 1-10).** Select 30 seed-stage B2B software startups that announced a round in the last 12 months, show 2-20 people and no designer on their team page, and have a visibly weak marketing page. Build three unsolicited landing-page demo redesigns for three of them. Log hours per demo.
2. **Run demo-first outreach (days 8-20).** Send the demo, not a pitch, to all 30. Track demo views, replies and calls. Predeclared price: $2,500-$3,500/month. Predeclared success: at least 3 replies and 1 paid month.
3. **Deliver and ask (days 11-30).** For the first paying client, log every request, hours per request, revisions, and turnaround hit rate. On day 30, ask for renewal and record the answer and the stated reason.

Success signal: One paid month at or above $2,500; delivery under 6 hours per request on average; the client renews or names a reason unrelated to "we can do this ourselves with AI."

Kill or redesign condition: Fewer than 3 replies from 30 and no paid month; or average delivery above 8 hours per request at the predeclared price; or the first client declines renewal because the tools now cover the need. Any of these means the bounded business does not clear, and the episode must not be produced as a build recommendation.
