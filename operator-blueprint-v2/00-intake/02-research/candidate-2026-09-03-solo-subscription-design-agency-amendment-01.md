## Evidence pass

**(a) Retention.** No source found gives churn, tenure, or renewal for productized or subscription design with a stated method. The searches covered open-startup and payment-verified dashboards (Baremetrics Open Startups, TrustMRR), vendor pages and blogs (Design Pickle, ManyPixels, Penji, ManyRequests), founder posts (Designjoy AMA, Pentaclay), public filings (none exist for these vendors; Superside is private), and agency surveys (Promethean, SoDA, AMI). The closest thing with any method is Promethean's 165-agency survey (A01-001), which states only a sample size and covers digital agencies generally. Every design-specific churn statement found is opinion or arithmetic (A01-003, A01-004). **Retention stays a hypothesis. Re-entry item 2 is not met.**

**(b) Acquisition.** New buyer-side evidence exists but is weak. Contra buyers posted ongoing monthly design budgets of $1,200-$2,000 for graphic work, $3,000-$5,000 for a lead Webflow designer, and $5,000-$10,000 for a senior web and brand designer (A01-010). That shows buyers do post monthly budgets for ongoing design, mostly at or below the brief's $2,500-$3,500 working price. But those posts are 15 months old, the buyers are anonymous, and none is identified as a seed-stage software company. Upwork has many recent SaaS landing-page posts, but the pages could not be read (A01-011). On the seller side, the observable channels are founder communities and showcases (A01-008, A01-009), marketplace listings (A01-012), and tool-vendor expert directories (A01-013). Both founder accounts credit existing reputation or community presence; one reports cold email to 500-1,000 leads produced no clients. That is one anecdote, but it points the same way as the 2026-09-03 finding: the reference operators grew on audience, not cold outreach. **Acquisition remains unproven for an operator without an audience.**

**(c) Price ladder.** Asking prices held steady for four of five vendors checked on 2026-09-03. Two vendors were added (Delesign at $649, Endless at $4,999-$7,999). The ladder now runs from $649 to $7,999 per month for self-serve subscriptions, then $15,000 minimum annual for Superside. Two changes matter: Penji now states capacity in daily hours per plan, and Design Pickle no longer shows a price or month-to-month billing. Both fit the capacity mechanism the package is built on (CLM-006): vendors are publishing or contracting around capacity rather than "unlimited". Dribbble asking prices are unchanged in range ($1,000-$13,500).

**(d) The AI change.** Verified and dated at the tool level. Figma reports over half of its $10K+ ARR customers using its built-in agent weekly by 2026-07-31 and over 80% consuming AI credits weekly (A01-016). Anthropic's Claude Design (2026-04-17) produces first versions of decks, one-pagers and prototypes, applies a brand's design system, and exports to PPTX, PDF and HTML (A01-017). These are this offer's core deliverables, and they are now in the buyer's own tools. Two peer-reviewed studies find that after image-generating AI, platform job posts for image creation fell about 17% and exposed freelancers lost contracts and earnings, while the remaining posts were more complex and paid more (A01-018, A01-019). **What was not found:** any independent measurement of how much more output one marketing designer produces with these tools. "One designer now does a team's work" remains a hypothesis; the only numbers are vendor testimonials.

## Adversarial pass

This pass was run by the same agent that did the evidence pass. It is not independent.

1. **Do the Contra budgets fix Reviewer B's objection that a seller's price is not a buyer's spend?** Partly. They are buyer-posted, which is the right kind of evidence. But six posts, 15 months old, from unnamed and mostly e-commerce buyers, do not show that seed-stage software startups post these budgets now. They move the budget pillar from "inferred" to "observed in an adjacent buyer", not further.
2. **Does the new AI evidence help or hurt?** It hurts the commodity end more than it helps. The buyer now has Claude Design and the Figma agent. The academic evidence says platform demand for image work fell after image AI. The one reading that helps is that the remaining freelance work is harder and pays more. That supports an operator selling judgment above the floor, but it is a reading, not a finding about this buyer.
3. **Did the desk search for retention try hard enough?** It covered every source family the re-entry item named. The gap is structural: the vendors are private, founders publish revenue but not churn, and the one founder asked directly about churn in public did not answer (A01-008). More searching is unlikely to change this. A viewer can answer it only by running the business.
4. **Is the price ladder being used as demand?** No. It is labelled as asking prices throughout. The one inference drawn from it (vendors contracting around capacity) is about how sellers describe the offer, not about buyers.
5. **Could Design Pickle's hidden price be read as "the category is failing"?** No. It shows one vendor moved to sales-led, quarterly-or-annual billing. The reason is not stated. It may be stated only as a change in how the vendor sells.
6. **Does the coaching standard let this candidate move forward now?** Only its re-entry wording changes. Rule 5 holds the scores: field-only gaps stay scored as gaps. Retention and acquisition were the gaps; desk work did not close either.
7. **Is anything excluded being let back in?** No. No new primary source was found for Designjoy's economics, the Figma 70% figure, the 5.8% reply rate, Superside's cuts, or agency churn tables. Two new unverified figures (A01-005, A01-006) are added to the excluded list.

## Viewer's first-test plan

This replaces the owner outreach test. It is what a viewer who can design must run before investing. It is not a condition on the owner or on this package.

1. **Days 1-5: check the buyer before building anything.** Pull 20 current buyer-posted jobs for ongoing marketing or landing-page design from Upwork, Contra, or startup job boards, posted within the last 60 days by software companies. Record budget, duration, and whether "ongoing" is stated. Success: at least 5 posts at $2,000/month or more (or an hourly rate × hours that reaches it). Kill or redesign: fewer than 3, which means the viewer should price at the floor or pick a different buyer.
2. **Days 1-10: build the list and three demos.** 30 seed-stage B2B software startups that raised in the last 12 months, show 2-20 people and no designer. Build three unsolicited landing-page demos, kept private and labelled unsolicited. Log hours per demo.
3. **Days 8-20: send demos, not pitches.** Predeclare the price ($2,500-$3,500/month) before sending. Track views, replies, and calls. Success: at least 3 replies and 1 paid month. Kill: fewer than 3 replies from 30 and no paid month.
4. **Days 11-30: deliver and log.** For the first client, log every request, hours per request, revisions, and whether the 48-hour promise held. Kill: average above 8 hours per request at the predeclared price.
5. **Day 30: the renewal question.** Ask for month two and record the stated reason either way. Kill: the client declines because "we can do this ourselves with Claude Design / Figma" (A01-016, A01-017). That answer is the disintermediation risk showing up in one account.

What the viewer should expect going in: no public data says how long clients stay, the reference operators grew on an audience, and the buyer has the same tools.

## Recommendation

**Park.** Not an owner promotion review, and a re-score is not recommended.

Reasons:

- Both reviewers scored below 70 (A 66, B 57). Owner promotion review is not the right next step: A sits in the 65-69 band where promotion needs an explicit owner override, and both reviewers recommended against one.
- The desk work Reviewer B said could not help has now been done. It found no retention evidence and only weak new acquisition evidence. Removing the owner test does not change any score (coaching standard rule 5).
- A re-score would not change the outcome. The factors new evidence touches, and the most they could plausibly move:
  - *Buyer and costly problem:* +0 to +1, from buyer-posted monthly budgets (A01-010). Offset by the evidence of falling platform demand for image work (A01-018, A01-019).
  - *Audience demand and timing:* +0 to +1, from dated AI-tool evidence (A01-016, A01-017). The same evidence sharpens the disintermediation risk.
  - *Go-to-market plausibility:* +0 to +1, from observed channels (A01-008, A01-009, A01-013). These confirm the reliance on audience and community rather than supporting cold outreach.
  - *Economics and capacity clarity:* 0. Retention is still unknown and the price ladder is unchanged at the points the model uses.
  - Best case: A about 69, B about 60. Both stay below 70.
- The candidate still has teaching value. The capacity story (Penji now selling hours, Design Pickle leaving month-to-month billing, a solo operator still at one request at a time) and the viewer's first test are sound. It is parked because the scores do not clear, not because the research is wrong.
