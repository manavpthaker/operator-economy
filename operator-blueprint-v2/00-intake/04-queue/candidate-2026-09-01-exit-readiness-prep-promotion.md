## Public framing contract

- Model description: **synthesized from parallels**
- Economics language: **modeled scenario, not observed performance or an earnings forecast**
- Load-bearing assumptions to say plainly:
  1. **This asks an owner to pay now for a payoff at an event with no date.** The businesses it is modelled on — SOC 2 and ISO readiness — sell against a scheduled audit. This does not. The honest counter, that roughly half of exits are involuntary and most listings never sell, converts a deadline into a probability and is a weaker sale.
  2. **The revenue model is a legal consequence, not a preference.** The fee is fixed rather than success-based because success fees on a transaction trigger brokerage licensing.
  3. **No operator has been observed running this practice** at disclosed economics. Every fee, hour and capacity figure is modeled.
  4. **The go-to-market rests on one assumption** — that brokers refer unready listings rather than doing the work free to win them.
- Claims the episode may not make:
  - Any multiple uplift attributed to preparation. The circulating 4.5–5.5× versus 7.5–9.0× figures are published by firms selling preparation services and are **excluded by name** from the evidence base.
  - That preparation causes a sale.
  - Any figure presented as a typical readiness fee.
  - Anything constituting legal advice on brokerage licensing.
- Required source and qualification notes:
  - EPI figures (CLM-001, CLM-002) must be attributed, and EPI's institutional interest in owner unpreparedness noted where they carry weight.
  - IBBA/M&A Source data (CLM-003) is advisor-reported closings, not an audited deal database.
  - Licensing (CLM-004) must be framed as varying by state and requiring the viewer's own verification.
  - CLM-005 and CLM-006 are advisory self-published and usable for context only.

## Caveats carried forward

- **Sourcing standard carried to editorial:** CLM-004 rests on an industry licensing reference rather than primary law. It is sufficient to establish that a brokerage perimeter exists and that it forces a fixed-fee rather than success-fee revenue model, which is the episode's actual point. It is **not** sufficient for the script to name specific states and tell a viewer what they may do there. Either verify any named state against primary sources before lock, or describe the pattern without naming states. Standard not-legal-advice framing applies: compliance is the operator's own responsibility, and the episode directs the viewer to verify their state.
- The offer parallel is structurally valid but **urgency-poor**. Editorial must not manufacture a deadline the evidence does not support.
- Go-to-market has a single point of failure and it is testable in thirty days.
- No transaction POV exists. Manav has never sold a business and the script may not imply deal experience.
- Owner-interview time is the delivery component least reducible by AI tooling, and the economics are most sensitive to it.
