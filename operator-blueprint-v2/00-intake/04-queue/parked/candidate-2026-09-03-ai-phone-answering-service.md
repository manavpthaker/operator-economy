## Why parked

A business-fit failure with a specific, checkable cause, not a timing decision.

The premise EP002 published in July 2026 assumed an independent operator could sell an AI receptionist to home-services contractors. Between then and the 2026-09-03 review the field-service platforms those contractors already use absorbed the product: Jobber ships it inside its plan, Housecall Pro and ServiceTitan sell it as an add-on, and the build itself is listed on freelance marketplaces for tens of dollars. What remains for an operator is escalation design, disclosure and consent compliance, and answer-rate reporting. No evidence exists that any contractor pays for that residual layer, and the legal surface forces the buyer to own the platform account, which removes the operator's margin.

The statistic that carried the original episode, that most calls to small businesses go unanswered, traces to a 2016 SEO-agency post about 85 businesses. It is excluded and must not return in any form.

## Unblock condition

Either of these. Both require evidence that does not exist today.

1. **One paid managed engagement** on a contractor's own platform account, with a 60-day call log showing the answer rate moved and the fee clearing the operator's imputed labor.
2. **A segment the platforms have not absorbed**: current evidence of a home-services or adjacent vertical where the incumbent software does not ship a receptionist and buyers still pay a third party for one.

## Review trigger
