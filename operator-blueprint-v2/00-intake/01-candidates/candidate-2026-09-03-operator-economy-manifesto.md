## Opportunity in one sentence

There is no operator business in this premise: EP005 argues that the minimum viable size of a software business collapsed, that the count of small durable software firms is rising while the median attempt gets worse, and that a builder should evaluate an idea by two questions (would anyone with capital bother reproducing it; does distribution survive a platform's mood) — a channel thesis about how to judge businesses, not a business for one operator to build, sell, deliver and test.

## Viewer outcome

- Viewer promise (historical): The viewer understands why "too small for venture" and "too small to defend" are different things and can run the two-question evaluation on an idea before building.
- Operator decision: Evaluate or reject an idea of their own. The episode does not support a build / test / adapt decision about any specific business, because it proposes none.
- Practical capability: Run the reproduction-cost arithmetic and the distribution question against their own idea. That is an evaluation skill, not a delivery capability.
- Expected Operator Canvas: **None can be filled.** There is no operator fit, buyer, trigger, offer, delivery workflow, capacity, pricing arithmetic or kill condition for a business, because the episode is the evaluation lens that other candidates are judged with.

## People

- Viewer/operator: Experienced operators deciding what to build outside employment (the published `blueprint_summary.who_its_for`). This is an audience, not an operator role.
- Buyer: **None.** Nobody pays for anything in this premise. The viewer's future customers belong to whatever business the viewer picks.
- End customer or beneficiary: The viewer, as a thinker.
- Guest or outside participant required: no

## Problem

- Costly problem: Builders grade small software businesses on the venture scoreboard and either do not build things that would work at small scale, or build things that cannot be defended once build cost falls for everyone.
- Why it matters: The published figures (all in `content-os/facts.md`, still subject to refresh) frame both tails: 7,857 US software firms with fewer than five employees in 2022 (+55% since 2017, Census SUSB) against a 400x gap between the top 5% and bottom quartile of new subscription apps (RevenueCat) and ~70% of micro-SaaS under $1,000 MRR (Freemius). This is the cost of choosing wrong, not the cost of an unserved buyer's problem.
- Why now: AI-assisted building lowered the team size a software business needs; the toolmakers (Cursor, Replit, Lovable — reported run-rates) monetized that collapse before the builders did. Current check on 2026-09-03: RevenueCat's 2026 State of Subscription Apps report still carries the 400x-vs-200x figure (vendor-published, own platform data, usable only for its own measurement). The Census SUSB 2022 tables (released April 2025) remain the latest annual release found; no 2023 release was located, so the count figures are current but approaching a refresh check.
- Existing alternatives and budget: The alternative is other people's evaluation frameworks (venture pattern-matching, indie-hacker "validate with a landing page" advice). No one budgets for this; it is an editorial position, not a category of spend.

## Narrative potential

- Starting state: Everyone narrates the one-person billion-dollar company; nobody counts the 7,857 small firms.
- Inciting change: The minimum viable team size for software fell.
- Causal mechanism: Build cost dropped; distribution and attention did not, so the count rose and the median fell at the same time.
- Operator build: **None.** The "build" section of the published episode is repurposed as the moat argument (`stack` covers Toast's fintech revenue and Constellation's purchasing of vertical knowledge). There is no operator constructing a system.
- Stakes and tradeoffs: Real for a thinker (choosing the wrong scoreboard), absent for an operator (no build, no client, no delivery failure).
- End state: The viewer has two questions to ask. No measurable business state change.
- Visual evidence: Census charts, the 400x spread, the Toast revenue split, the outsider failure/success pairs. Documentary evidence cards; no operator, place, interface or workflow that changes state.

The Editorial Potential gate requires a causal operator journey through starting system, inciting change, mechanism, build, tradeoff or test, and measurable end state. The premise has the first three and lacks the last three at the operator level. That is by design: it is a thesis episode.
