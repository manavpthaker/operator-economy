## Opportunity in one sentence

One operator running AI agents sells a monthly money desk to the adult child of a parent who lives
alone: the parent's mail and statements reach the desk, agents read and sort every item, each bill
is checked against the last, payments are made only from the parent's own account under an
authority the bank recognises and the family agreed in writing, and the family gets a weekly record
that is the record a power-of-attorney agent is already told to keep.

## What changed from issue 01

- Offer: from "payments prepared for the family's approval" to "prepare, check, pay from the
  parent's own account, record". The desk may press pay, but only that way (research brief,
  revision 2026-11-19; CLM-019, CLM-021 to CLM-023).
- Watching: removed as the agent change. Daily account monitoring already sells as software for
  $7.49 to $26.99 a month (CLM-016); the desk buys it in per household.
- Agent claim: agents read, sort, and match the mail and statements, and draft calls and letters.
  This is untested (ANA-007) and is the viewer's timed first test.
- Price band: remote seller floors are $250 (Parent Bill Pay, CLM-002) and $500 (Paytrust,
  formerly SilverBills, CLM-025). Modeled price $200 to $400 per household per month. `Modeled
  scenario, not observed performance or an earnings forecast.`
- Existing alternatives: the child does it themselves, including with power of attorney and online
  access (CLM-001, CLM-029); local daily money managers by the hour (CLM-006); remote sellers
  (CLM-002, CLM-025); monitoring apps (CLM-004, CLM-016); general bill pay at $49 a month (CLM-005).

## Three hard limits

1. Never hold the money. No client money passes through the operator's account. A daily money
   manager that takes deposits and pays bills from its own account is a money transmitter
   (FinCEN FIN-2012-R004, CLM-010).
2. Never pay with a shared login or card. A person given the parent's access device makes
   "authorized" transfers, and the parent is liable if that person exceeds the authority
   (Regulation E, CLM-012). Paytrust accepts shared logins (CLM-025); this desk does not.
3. Report suspected exploitation where state law requires it, including by the family member who
   pays the desk (Texas and Florida: any person must report, CLM-014).

These are a non-lawyer's reading of federal rules and two or three states. The viewer checks their
own state. The episode may not say "no licence needed".

## Most important unproven assumption

That an adult child will pay a remote stranger more than the price of monitoring software to check,
call, pay, and record for a parent, when caregiver guides tell them to do it themselves and keep
the record (CLM-026 to CLM-028).

## Discovery and POV (revised)

- Operator Economy POV: candidate synthesis only. The separation rule, signing as agent, and the
  record duty are common federal-regulator coverage (CFPB agent guide, CLM-028), so they are not
  original. What was not found in the three guides read: why a shared login leaves the parent
  without a claim (CLM-012), where money handling becomes money transmission (CLM-010), and that
  the desk may be a mandatory reporter of its own customer (CLM-014).
- POV boundary: no experience managing anyone's money is claimed or inferred; no family has been
  shown to pay a remote desk.
