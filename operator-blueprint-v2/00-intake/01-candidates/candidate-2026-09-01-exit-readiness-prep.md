## Opportunity in one sentence

A capable generalist sells retiring small-business owners a fixed-fee preparation engagement that makes the business sellable — clean records, documented operations, reduced owner dependence, and an assembled diligence package — explicitly stopping short of brokering the sale.

## Viewer outcome

- Viewer promise: The viewer understands whether they can build a preparation practice for retiring owners without a brokerage licence, what it can legally include, and what it has to charge to work.
- Operator decision: Build the practice, test it on one owner, fold readiness prep into existing consulting, or reject it.
- Practical capability: Distinguish preparation from brokerage under their own state's law, scope a readiness engagement, and price fixed-fee work against a deal outcome they are not paid on.
- Expected Operator Canvas: Operator fit, buyer and trigger, the legal boundary, offer scope, delivery workflow, judgment points, capacity, pricing arithmetic, and disqualifiers.

## People

- Viewer/operator: A capable generalist — operations, finance-adjacent, consulting or systems background. **No licence or sector credential required for preparation work in most states**, which is the point.
- Buyer: The owner of a small business, typically 55+, intending to exit within one to five years.
- End customer or beneficiary: The owner, and secondarily the acquirer receiving a diligence-ready business.
- Guest or outside participant required: no

## Problem

- Costly problem: Owners reach the point of sale with owner-dependent operations, undocumented processes and messy records, and are repriced or passed over by buyers running fast diligence.
- Why it matters: **The consequence is measurable in multiples.** Prepared sellers are reported to transact materially higher than unprepared ones, and roughly 80% of the average owner's net worth sits inside the business.
- Why now: A demographic transition wave meets consolidator demand. Millions of owner-operated businesses face transition this decade, buyers are running deeper diligence, and most owners have no documented exit plan.
- Existing alternatives and budget: Brokers (paid on success, engaged late), CPAs and attorneys (transaction-time, not readiness), CEPA-credentialed advisers (exists, typically attached to wealth or brokerage practices). Owners already spend on transaction advice; readiness work is the underserved earlier stage.

## Proposed business

- Offer: A fixed-fee readiness engagement — records and reporting hygiene, process documentation, owner-dependence reduction, customer-concentration and key-person analysis, and an assembled diligence package with a prioritised remediation plan.
- Customer outcome: A business that survives diligence, plus a written record of what was fixed and what remains.
- Delivery hypothesis: Diagnostic against a readiness framework, then a bounded remediation sprint, then package assembly. AI does the documentation load — SOP drafting from interviews and recordings, financial narrative assembly, data-room organisation.
- Revenue hypothesis: **Fixed fee, deliberately not success-based.** Success fees on a transaction are what trigger brokerage licensing.
- Most important unproven assumption: **That an owner will pay real money years before a sale, for work whose payoff arrives at an exit that may not happen.** This is the mirror of the AI-visibility failure and must not be assumed away.

## Initial synthesis hypothesis

- Parallel A: The SEO/technical audit → fixed-scope diagnostic sold before implementation.
- Parallel B: Due-diligence preparation in M&A → assembling a defensible package for a scrutinising counterparty.
- Parallel C: Quality-systems and process documentation consulting (ISO, SOC 2 readiness) → getting an organisation through an external examination it will fail unprepared.
- New combination: Compliance-style readiness discipline applied to owner transition, delivered by a generalist with AI leverage on the documentation burden, priced as a fixed fee outside the brokerage perimeter.
- Suspected transfer risk: SOC 2 and ISO readiness sell because the examination is **scheduled and mandatory**. An owner's exit is neither. The urgency that makes those businesses work may not transfer.

## Narrative potential

- Starting state: An owner has run a profitable business for 25 years and intends to sell "in a couple of years."
- Inciting change: A buyer runs diligence, finds the business is the owner, and reprices — or walks.
- Causal mechanism: Value transfers only if the business runs without the person selling it. Undocumented operations and owner-held relationships are unpriceable, so buyers discount them.
- Operator build: A readiness practice that converts an owner-dependent business into a transferable one, on a fixed fee, without touching the transaction.
- Stakes and tradeoffs: Get paid years before the payoff; stay on the legal side of a line that moves by state; sell urgency for an event with no deadline.
- End state: The viewer can decide whether to build it, and knows the condition under which it fails.
- Visual evidence: The diligence checklist against the actual filing cabinet; an org chart where every line ends at one person; a multiple comparison; the state licensing map; a data room before and after.

## Audience pull

- Exact or adjacent viewer questions: how to help owners sell, whether a licence is needed, what exit planning pays, how to start an M&A-adjacent practice without a finance background.
- Initial interest signals: **To be established in research.** Transition-wave scale, transaction data, and the existence of a credentialing body are candidate proxies.
- Timely tension: The largest ownership transition in modern small business meets the fact that most owners are not ready, and the people who could help are paid only if a deal closes.
- Coverage gap: Existing coverage is written by brokers and wealth advisers for **owners**. Almost nothing addresses the operator who wants to *build the practice*, and the licensing boundary is treated as a footnote rather than the design constraint it is.
- Honest working premise: The people who help owners sell only get paid when a deal closes. Here is the business that gets paid before it — and the legal line that decides whether you can run it.

## Known blockers

1. **Licensing.** 16 states plus an Illinois registration regulate business brokerage; California requires a broker or law licence to advise on a transaction at all. The offer must be scoped inside the preparation perimeter and the boundary verified per state. **This shapes the business rather than blocking it, but it is not optional.**
2. **The prepared-versus-unprepared multiple gap** currently traces to advisory-firm publications. Under `EVIDENCE-STANDARD.md` that is seller-published for a service those firms sell, and needs corroboration or downgrading.
3. **No deadline.** Unlike SOC 2 or ISO readiness, an exit has no scheduled examination. The urgency that makes the parallel businesses work may not transfer.
4. **Long sales cycle and deferred payoff.** The owner pays now for value realised at an uncertain future event.
5. **No transaction POV.** Manav has not sold a business; the channel cannot speak from deal experience.

## Intake decision

Decision: research

Reason: The consequence is quantified in a way the parked AI-visibility candidate never was, the pricing anchor comes from closed-transaction data rather than seller asking prices, and the operator-entry barrier is genuinely low for a generalist — which is what the audience needs. The two real risks are the unverified multiple gap and the absence of a deadline, and both are researchable. Proceed to the research brief.
