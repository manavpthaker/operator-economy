## Opportunity in one sentence

An operator sells a small private or religious school (or the diocese or network that runs several)
a fixed-scope analysis of its own admissions and re-enrollment records — cleaned multi-year data,
where families drop out, how new school-choice families behave, and three changes to test next
cycle — while the school keeps every admissions, tuition, and aid decision.

## Viewer outcome

- Viewer promise: Understand why new state and federal school-choice money changes who applies to
  small private schools, what schools and dioceses already pay outside firms to study enrollment, and
  why the admissions software many schools own may already do the easy half of the job.
- Operator decision: Test, adapt, or reject a fixed-fee enrollment analysis after seeing the buyer
  evidence (paid enrollment studies with no public price), the software substitute, the subsidized
  diocesan and foundation substitutes, and the Texas student-data rule.
- Practical capability: Scope one analysis; list the exports needed; name what AI does in the
  cleaning and matching step; separate analysis from decisions; price against the value of one
  retained family.
- Expected Operator Canvas: Buyer (school head vs diocesan schools office), costly job, bounded
  offer, data boundary, substitute map (SIS reports, diocesan staff, foundations, planning firms),
  price hypothesis, first-test plan.

## People

- Viewer/operator: Someone with admissions, school business-office, or data-analysis experience who
  can work with messy spreadsheets and explain findings to a head of school or a school board.
- Buyer: Head of school or board of a small private or religious school (under about 300 students),
  or a diocesan schools office or school network buying for several schools. Unknown which pays.
- End customer or beneficiary: The school's leadership; indirectly, applicant families.
- Guest or outside participant required: no. The episode can use a synthetic admissions export and
  public program documents.

## Problem

- Costly problem: Small schools lose families between inquiry, application, enrollment, and
  re-enrollment and often cannot say where or why. A trade article described student-information
  systems distorting funnel counts (CLM-025, dated 2016).
- Why it matters: For a TEFA family, one lost student is up to $10,474 of state-funded tuition for
  2026-27 (CLM-001). One Texas diocese reported 400 to 600 new students (CLM-004).
- Why now: Texas TEFA opened for 2026-27 with more than 102,000 awards and about 145,000 on the
  waitlist (CLM-002); the federal tax-credit scholarship starts 2027-01-01 in states that opt in,
  27 of them as of 2026-06-08 (CLM-007, CLM-008); U.S. births fell from 4.32 million (2007) to
  3.63 million (2024) (CLM-010) and high-school graduates are projected to fall 13% from 2025 to 2041
  (CLM-009).
- Existing alternatives and budget: built-in funnel reports in admissions software (CLM-013,
  CLM-014, CLM-015); diocesan enrollment staff (CLM-022); foundation coaching (CLM-024); planning
  firms paid by dioceses and parishes for enrollment studies (CLM-016 to CLM-019); enrollment
  consulting firms (CLM-026, seller pages only).

## Proposed business

- Offer: Fixed-fee enrollment analysis: intake of admissions, enrollment, re-enrollment, and aid
  exports for three to five years; AI-assisted cleaning and record matching; funnel by stage, grade,
  and source; re-enrollment risk list; TEFA/scholarship-family comparison; short written findings
  with three changes to test.
- Customer outcome: A cleaned dataset the school owns, a funnel the head can read, and a written
  list of changes; measured next cycle by conversion and re-enrollment rates.
- Delivery hypothesis: The school exports from its system or spreadsheets; the operator works inside
  the school's accounts or on de-identified extracts; AI de-duplicates families across years,
  standardizes free-text sources and withdrawal reasons, and matches rosters; the operator checks
  every match and writes the findings.
- Revenue hypothesis: Fixed fee per analysis, or a diocesan package covering several schools.
  Price unknown; no public price for comparable work was found.
- Most important unproven assumption: A small school or diocese will pay an outside operator for
  analysis that its admissions software, diocesan staff, or a subsidized foundation program already
  partly provides.

## Initial synthesis hypothesis

- Parallel A: Catholic planning firm (Meitler) hired by dioceses, parishes, and schools to analyze
  enrollment potential (CLM-016 to CLM-019) → buyer type, budget holder, scope. No price observed.
- Parallel B: Public districts paying an outside firm for enrollment recruitment with "market and
  trend analysis" (CLM-020, CLM-021) → budget category exists when choice programs pull students
  away. Different buyer and offer.
- Parallel C: Diocesan enrollment data role (CLM-022) and foundation enrollment coaching (CLM-024)
  → the job is recognized and funded, but often in-house or subsidized.
- New combination: AI cleaning and matching makes a small, fixed-scope funnel analysis cheap enough
  for a school too small for a planning firm or on-site consultant.
- Suspected transfer risk: The paid parallels are diocesan planning studies (new schools, mergers),
  not admissions-funnel analysis for one small school; the free substitutes may cover the rest.

These are research directions, not evidence. The completed research brief and analogy map decide
whether the transfers are valid.

## Narrative potential

- Starting state: A 150-student school with one part-time admissions person and five years of
  inquiries in two systems and a spreadsheet.
- Inciting change: State voucher money brings a new group of applicants; births keep falling.
- Causal mechanism: Nobody has time to reconcile the records, so the school cannot see where
  families leave.
- Operator build: A fixed-scope analysis: export, AI matching, human check, funnel, three changes.
- Stakes and tradeoffs: Family data handled outside the school; findings that tell a head what they
  do not want to hear; a school that could have pressed "report" in its own software.
- End state: A synthetic school's funnel rebuilt on screen and the viewer deciding whether the
  work beats the built-in report.
- Visual evidence: TEFA amounts and award counts, NCES school-size table, a synthetic messy export
  and its cleaned version, a Finalsite-style funnel report for comparison.

- Honest working premise: "Texas just put $10,474 behind each private-school student. Here is the

  left for a one-person operator to test."

## Known blockers

- No observed price for enrollment analysis bought by a small school or diocese.
- Strong substitute: admissions software already reports conversion, selectivity, and yield over
  five years (CLM-013); diocesan staff and foundations do similar work free to the school.
- Texas SB 2 §29.369(c): a school may not "sell or otherwise distribute" information about a TEFA
  child. Whether an operator working under contract counts as distribution is unresolved.
- Unclear buyer: dioceses hire the paid studies; individual small schools may not have the budget.

## Intake decision

Decision: research

Reason: Owner-admitted lead with a real timing change and adjacent evidence that dioceses, parishes,
and schools pay outside firms for enrollment studies. Research must find a price anchor and test
whether the software and free substitutes leave a sellable residual.
