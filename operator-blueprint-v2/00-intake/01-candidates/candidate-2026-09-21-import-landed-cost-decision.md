## Opportunity in one sentence

An operator with import-operations or ecommerce-finance experience sells a fixed-scope engagement to
a small US importer that ends in a SKU-level landed-cost record and a documented keep / reprice /
resource / drop decision per product and channel, while every classification determination and every
CBP filing stays with the buyer's licensed customs broker.

## Viewer outcome

- Viewer promise: Understand where the legal boundary of this work actually sits, what the licensed
  and automated incumbents already cover, and what — if anything — is left for an independent
  operator to sell.
- Operator decision: Test, adapt, or reject the service after seeing the licensing boundary, the
  automated substitute, and the absence of buyer-paid evidence.
- Practical capability: Outline the lawful scope line, build a landed-cost and margin model from a
  broker-supplied classification, and name the determinations that must never be the operator's.
- Expected Operator Canvas: Buyer, costly job, bounded offer, professional boundary, substitution
  map, acquisition path, fixed-fee economics, and falsification threshold.

## People

- Viewer/operator: Someone with real import-operations, ecommerce-finance, or merchandising-margin
  experience. Not safe for a generic automation novice: the adjacent work is a licensed profession.
- Buyer: The owner or finance lead of a small US business that is the importer of record on its own
  inbound goods, imports below the volume that justifies a full-time trade-compliance hire, and has
  never needed precise classification because shipments used to land under the $800 threshold.
- End customer or beneficiary: The buyer's own retail and marketplace customers, who absorb the
  repricing decision.
- Guest or outside participant required: unknown. A licensed customs broker or a customs attorney
  would likely have to appear or be quoted to make the boundary claim responsibly.

## Problem

- Costly problem: Duty and entry cost is now a per-parcel function of HTS code and country of origin
  on goods that previously entered duty-free, and the buyer's codes were approximate because nothing
  depended on them. The buyer remains the importer of record when a code or origin claim is wrong.
- Why it matters: The cost is already on this quarter's books, and the fixed per-entry cost falls
  hardest on the smallest shipments — where it can exceed the duty itself.
- Why now: De minimis duty-free treatment was suspended effective 2025-08-29 and CBP published
  modernised low-value shipment processing on 2026-06-24 (CLM-001).
- Existing alternatives and budget: A licensed customs broker, a freight forwarder that gives
  classification advisory away to win the freight, cross-border duty software priced per order
  (CLM-005), the buyer's own spreadsheet, or nothing.

## Proposed business

- Offer: A fixed-scope import cost-and-decision engagement over a bounded SKU set.
- Customer outcome: A SKU-level landed-cost record per shipping mode, a supplier origin-evidence
  file, a consolidation-versus-direct-parcel comparison, and a written keep / reprice / resource /
  drop decision per SKU and channel.
- Delivery hypothesis: Inventory SKUs and suppliers; collect supplier-supplied origin documentation;
  take classification as an input from the buyer's licensed broker rather than producing it; build
  landed cost per unit per mode; model consolidation; recompute channel margin; write the decision
  record. The operator does not classify, does not value merchandise, does not prepare or assist in
  preparing anything intended to be filed with CBP, and promises no customs or duty outcome.
- Revenue hypothesis: A fixed project fee, unknown, with a possible annual re-run.
- Most important unproven assumption: That once every regulated task is removed, a buyer will still
  pay an independent operator for the landed-cost model and the margin decision — work that duty
  software already automates per order and that the buyer's accountant or the buyer may simply do.

## Initial synthesis hypothesis

- Parallel A: Fractional ecommerce-finance and merchandising-margin consulting -> the decision layer
  and the fee model.
- Parallel B: Licensed customs brokerage and trade-compliance advisory -> the workflow shape and the
  boundary the operator must not cross.
- Parallel C: Cross-border duty and landed-cost software -> proof the calculation component works,
  and the reason it may not be sellable as labor.
- New combination: Separating the merchant's own margin decision from the regulated determination,
  and selling only the former.
- Suspected transfer risk: Parallel C may not be a supporting component at all. It may be the
  substitute that removes the offer.

## Narrative potential

- Starting state: A small importer prices from an invoice and a shipping quote, and never looks at a
  tariff schedule.
- Inciting change: The threshold is gone; every parcel now carries duty and an entry cost.
- Causal mechanism: Duty is a function of code and origin, and the importer of record carries the
  liability regardless of who supplied the code.
- Operator build: A SKU-level cost-and-decision system with a hard boundary at the regulated line.
- Stakes and tradeoffs: Real money per parcel, real penalty exposure, and a service that may be
  legally constrained into something nobody buys.
- End state: A decision record showing which products and channels still clear margin.
- Visual evidence: Tariff schedule lookups, supplier origin documents, a per-SKU cost build, a
  before/after channel margin comparison, the regulation text itself, and a broker handoff.

## Audience pull

- Exact or adjacent viewer questions: How do I find the right HS/HTS code; who pays the duty now;
  should I keep importing this product.
- Initial interest signals: Recurring search formulations on HS/HTS code selection (recurrence only,
  no volume claim); a heavily commented r/smallbusiness thread visible only as a search-result
  rendering (CLM-010, not usable as evidence); a large volume of vendor and forwarder merchant
  guidance published since the suspension.
- Timely tension: The cost has already changed; the answer most merchants are using is a free tool
  or a chatbot, while the liability stays with them.
- Coverage gap: Public coverage explains the rule or sells the tool. No observed source establishes
  whether an unlicensed operator can lawfully sell any part of this, or whether the residual is
  worth buying.
- Honest working premise: The tariff is not the story. The story is that the person who is told the
  answer for free is still the one who pays when it is wrong — and that the law may not let an
  operator sell the answer at all.

## Discovery and POV

- Search-volume status: attempted but not measurable. Only relative direction and recurring
  formulations were observed.
- Operator Economy POV: Original synthesis, provisional. The specific finding is that the customs-
  business definition sweeps in "activities relating to the preparation" of documents intended to be
  filed with CBP, and that its compliance carve-out is limited to a *related* business entity
  (CLM-002) — so the "assemble the evidence, let the broker file" framing that the surrounding
  merchant guidance implies may itself sit inside the licensed zone.
- POV evidence: CLM-002, CLM-003, CLM-004, read from the regulation text on 2026-09-21.
- POV boundary: This is a reading of the regulation by a non-lawyer. It is not a legal opinion, it
  has not been reviewed by counsel or a licensed broker, and the channel cannot state where the line
  falls until it has been. No first-party operating experience in importing exists.

## Initial evidence status

- Buyer-problem evidence: usable adjacent. The cost change is primary-sourced; the buyer's distress
  is described only by sellers.
- Budget or current-alternative evidence: lead found. Broker and software prices are published by
  the sellers themselves; no observed buyer spend on the proposed offer.
- Offer and delivery parallel: missing for the bounded offer. The unbounded version is a licensed
  profession.
- Economics or capacity inputs: missing. No defensible price, hours, or capacity input exists.
- Audience-interest signals: one signal, weak. Recurrence of a question is not demand.
- Narrative engine: strong.

## Known blockers

1. The lawful scope of an unlicensed paid adviser under 19 CFR 111 is unresolved and requires a
   qualified review (CLM-002, CLM-003, CLM-004).
2. The calculation output is already automated and priced per order by incumbents (CLM-005).
3. No buyer-paid evidence exists for the residual decision layer.
4. The lead's "why now" stack mixes two different buyers: the de minimis suspension binds the US
   importer of record, while the Etsy DDP requirement binds non-US sellers shipping into the US
   (CLM-006). One buyer must be chosen; this candidate chooses the US importer of record.

## Intake decision

Decision: research

Reason: The cost change is real, primary-sourced, current, showable, and has no duplicate anywhere
in the repository. The business premise is unresolved on the one question that decides everything —
what an unlicensed operator may lawfully sell — and that question is worth answering in the record
even if the answer closes the opportunity.
