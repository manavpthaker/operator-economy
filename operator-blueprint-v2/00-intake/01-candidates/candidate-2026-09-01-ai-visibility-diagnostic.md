## Opportunity in one sentence

An experienced organic-discovery practitioner sells brands a fixed-scope diagnostic that establishes, for a defined set of buyer-approved questions, whether and how the brand appears in AI-assisted answers, which sources those answers cite, and which gaps are actually controllable.

## Viewer outcome

- Viewer promise: The viewer understands whether a bounded, research-led diagnostic service is a business they could build, what it can honestly promise, and where its economics break.
- Operator decision: Build a paid diagnostic, test it once against a real brand, adapt it into an existing service line, or reject it as unsellable at the price it requires.
- Practical capability: Design a defensible question sample, distinguish controllable from uncontrollable gaps, price a fixed-scope engagement against real delivery hours, and state uncertainty to a buyer without losing the sale.
- Expected Operator Canvas: Operator fit, buyer and first segment, offer boundary, delivery workflow, the human-judgment points, capacity ceiling, pricing arithmetic, and the disqualifiers.

## People

- Viewer/operator: An SEO, content, digital-strategy, or competitive-intelligence practitioner who can design a sample, validate sources, and hold a marketing conversation. Not a beginner reselling a dashboard.
- Buyer: A marketing, content, or organic-growth leader responsible for digital discovery at a consumer-facing brand.
- End customer or beneficiary: The consumer using AI-assisted research to compare options.
- Guest or outside participant required: no

## Problem

- Costly problem: Conventional search reporting can look healthy while AI-assisted answers omit, misdescribe, or unfavourably compare the brand, drawing on a source mix the brand does not own and has never audited.
- Why it matters: The buyer has no repeatable baseline for which questions matter, how the brand is described, which sources are cited, or which gaps they can actually influence. **The financial consequence is unquantified and this candidate must not claim a recoverable revenue loss.**
- Why now: AI-assisted research is displacing part of the discovery path that conventional SEO reporting was built to measure, and the category has attracted venture funding at scale, but buyer-side measurement practice is immature.
- Existing alternatives and budget: SEO audits, competitive analysis, brand monitoring, and agency retainers are established purchases with existing budget owners. Vendor tools now sell prompt and mention tracking directly.

## Proposed business

- Offer: A fixed-scope AI discovery diagnostic for one brand, one market, ten buyer-approved questions, two answer surfaces.
- Customer outcome: A decision-grade baseline — where the brand appears, how it is described, what is cited, what is factually wrong, what is controllable, what to test next, and how the sample will be repeated.
- Delivery hypothesis: Scope and question design with the buyer, capture answers at a recorded time and configuration, log presence/description/competitors/citations/variance, review accuracy against approved brand documents, classify controllability, prioritise actions.
- Revenue hypothesis: A fixed diagnostic fee, with any monitoring or implementation scoped separately afterwards.
- Most important unproven assumption: **That a buyer will pay a fixed professional fee for a measurement whose business consequence cannot yet be quantified.** Everything else is secondary to this.

## Initial synthesis hypothesis

- Parallel A: The SEO audit — a paid, fixed-scope diagnostic sold before and independently of implementation.
- Parallel B: Brand and competitive monitoring — recurring observation of how a brand is represented in channels it does not own.
- Parallel C: AI-visibility tooling — existing products that sample prompts and log mentions, competitors, and citations.
- New combination: Research-designed question sampling plus source-citation analysis plus a controllability classification, sold as a bounded professional judgment rather than a dashboard subscription.
- Suspected transfer risk: SEO audits are bought partly because the remedy is known and the metric is trusted. Neither holds here yet. The analogy may transfer the *format* of the sale without transferring the *reason* for it.

## Narrative potential

- Starting state: A brand's search reporting is green. Rankings hold, traffic is stable, the dashboard says the discovery problem is solved.
- Inciting change: Someone asks the category question in an AI assistant and the brand is simply absent, or is described using a competitor's framing and a source the brand has never seen.
- Causal mechanism: AI answers assemble from a source mix that is not the brand's owned surface, so the brand's controlled channel and its actual representation have come apart.
- Operator build: A bounded diagnostic that makes the invisible legible — a defensible sample, a citation map, and an honest split between what the brand can influence and what it cannot.
- Stakes and tradeoffs: Sell certainty and you are lying; sell only uncertainty and nobody buys. The episode's real tension is whether an honest version of this service can be priced.
- End state: The viewer can decide whether to build it, and knows the specific condition under which it fails.
- Visual evidence: The green dashboard against the absent answer; the same question across two surfaces returning different sources; a citation map; a controllability split; the pricing arithmetic against delivery hours.

## Audience pull

- Exact or adjacent viewer questions: whether a brand appears in AI answers, how to measure or influence it, whether "GEO" is a real service or repackaged SEO, and whether it can be sold.
- Initial interest signals: **To be established in research.** Category funding, tool launches, agency service-page proliferation, and practitioner debate are candidate proxies. Vendor-claimed search growth is marketing, not measurement.
- Timely tension: The practitioner's existing service line is being devalued by a shift they cannot yet measure, while vendors sell certainty they have not demonstrated.
- Coverage gap: Page-one coverage is written by parties selling the service. No neutral account of whether the *business* works.
- Honest working premise: A category everyone is selling into and almost nobody has priced honestly — here is what the diagnostic can truthfully promise, and what it costs to deliver.

## Discovery and POV

- Search-volume status: attempted but not measurable — the V1 intake recorded no Ahrefs API access. Step 0.2 permits proxy signals plus a documented attempt.
- Operator Economy POV: Manav is running a channel that is itself invisible to AI-assisted discovery, and has been the buyer of organic-discovery work rather than its vendor.
- POV evidence: First-party experience of the channel's own discovery problem, and product/AI-implementation experience relevant to judging tool claims.
- POV boundary: **This is experience being lived, not a completed engagement.** The channel has not sold this diagnostic, has no client outcome, and cannot claim one. The V1 intake scored POV 10/15 for exactly this reason.

## Initial evidence status

- Buyer-problem evidence: lead found — survey evidence on AI-assisted research behaviour requires current verification.
- Budget or current-alternative evidence: lead found — adjacent SEO audit and retainer pricing is established; GEO-specific pricing is largely agency self-published.
- Offer and delivery parallel: lead found — audit, monitoring, and answer-source analysis exist separately; the combination is unproven.
- Economics or capacity inputs: modelable — price and capacity must be presented as scenario arithmetic, never as typical performance.
- Audience-interest signals: one signal — needs triangulation.
- Narrative engine: strong — the green-dashboard contradiction is a genuine causal story with visual evidence.

## Known blockers

1. **Low-end pricing evidence is largely agency self-published.** Under `EVIDENCE-STANDARD.md` such sources may verify their own published prices but require corroboration for market demand or industry-wide economics. The V1 intake flagged this as the same source class EP002 had to hedge aloud.
2. **No quantified financial consequence.** No credible published figure links AI-answer absence to lost revenue. The blueprint must state this rather than imply a recoverable loss.
3. **Search demand is unmeasured**, not zero. Requires a documented measurement attempt plus independent proxy signals.
4. **POV is lived, not completed.** No client engagement exists to draw on.
5. **Fixture material is not evidence.** The Step 1 GEO fixture and the Step 2 AI Visibility script are test artifacts explicitly excluded from publication authority. They may direct research; they may not be cited as findings.

## Intake decision

Decision: research

Reason: The buyer, the problem, and the narrative engine are strong, and the honest-pricing tension is a genuine Operator Economy story rather than a category explainer. Three of the five blockers are evidence-quality questions that research can resolve or bound. Proceed to the research brief, with the unquantified financial consequence carried as a disclosure rather than treated as a defect to be argued away.
