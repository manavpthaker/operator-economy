## Executive finding

- Opportunity: An operations-minded independent could sell a fixed-scope workflow-reliability sprint that maps, implements, tests, documents, and monitors one costly cross-tool handoff for a small service business.
- Evidence class: observed model
- Provisional verdict: viable as an evidence-bounded blueprint; commercial performance remains unproven.
- Main reason: Zapier and n8n both recognize consultants, agencies, and systems integrators delivering automation services to active customers, while independent government and public-development-bank research supports the underlying small-business adoption and administrative-work problem.
- Largest unresolved question: Will a reachable small-business owner pay an unknown independent for the proposed reliability sprint, at a price that works after discovery, exception testing, documentation, support, and acquisition time?
- Required public framing: The service category and buyer problem are observed. The proposed scope, price, capacity, acquisition path, and contribution are a modeled pilot design, not observed performance or an earnings forecast.

## Buyer reality

- Buyer: Owner, COO, head of operations, RevOps lead, or agency operations lead at a roughly 10-100-person service business using several SaaS systems.
- End customer or beneficiary: Employees performing the handoff and, when the workflow touches lead or service delivery, the customer waiting for the next action.
- Recurring problem: Important state is divided across forms, calendars, CRMs, spreadsheets, inboxes, project tools, and billing systems. People manually re-enter, reconcile, chase, or remember the next action.
- Consequence and frequency: KfW's representative SME research establishes that administrative work consumes meaningful time and personnel cost, although it does not isolate cross-tool automation. A Zapier partner case documents a direct workflow example in which missed or canceled meetings lacked follow-up and teams relied on manual reminders and ad hoc notes. The case is vendor-published, so its performance claims remain qualified.
- Existing alternatives or budget: Manual staff work, native integrations, DIY automation, general IT or operations consulting, internal developers, virtual assistants, and specialist automation partners. Current exact service pricing remains opaque and is not inferred from salary, funding, or software price.
- Why now: UK government research finds that SMEs are adopting productivity software but face information gaps and value reliable, personalized support. Meanwhile, Zapier and n8n are explicitly expanding partner ecosystems for hands-on implementation, and current YouTube attention shows substantial operator interest alongside crowded, opportunity-led coverage.

The exact service scope is new to this package. The underlying problem, implementation category, and exact professional-service model are observed; the commercial proposition still requires a paid pilot.

## Evidence landscape

### Direct evidence

- Zapier's Solution Partner Program is for partners delivering hands-on services that help businesses unify tools and move from ideas to outcomes. Zapier describes the program as a route for partners to attract leads and grow service businesses. This directly establishes the professional-service category, not its average economics.
- n8n's Expert Partner pilot is limited to agencies and systems integrators already implementing on n8n, earning their main revenue from automation and AI services, and serving at least three active n8n customers. This is strong evidence that exact operating businesses exist, but it is selected vendor-program evidence rather than a representative market study.
- A Zapier-published case shows a Solution Partner mapping and implementing a cross-tool lead workflow, plus documentation, training material, and post-implementation support. This supports the proposed delivery shape. The reported growth and time-saving results are vendor-curated and cannot be generalized.
- Current Upwork profiles show n8n and workflow-automation specialists with completed and in-progress work histories and predefined project offerings. This supports a reachable procurement channel and a freelance floor. Individual profiles do not establish market-average price, margin, quality, or durable demand.

### Adjacent business evidence

- UK Department for Business and Trade research on SME digital adoption describes a staged adoption journey, information gaps, and demand for reliable personalized support. This supports the consulting and implementation job, but not the exact workflow-reliability offer or price.
- KfW's representative SME panel quantifies administrative burden. This supports a meaningful class of underlying work, but bureaucracy is broader than disconnected SaaS handoffs and German SME results are not automatically transferable to a US service-business buyer.
- Operations consulting, systems integration, and managed IT provide adjacent models for diagnosis, scoped implementation, documentation, ownership, and ongoing support. No pricing transfer is made without a source and fit test.

### Component feasibility evidence

- Zapier's current paid plans support multi-step workflows, webhooks, shared connections, alerts, and related production features, with entry prices published by the vendor. Tool price proves accessibility, not project price or buyer demand.
- Make's current model offers a visual workflow builder, routers, filters, webhooks, error handlers, and usage-based credits. Its documentation makes an important operating constraint visible: scenarios can pause when credits run out, while queues and notifications govern recovery. This supports the need for monitoring and capacity planning.
- n8n supports visual workflows, code, API requests, webhooks, retries, execution logs, and error workflows. These components make the proposed implementation and observability workflow technically plausible. Tool availability does not establish that an operator can configure it safely for every client.

### Scaled relevance

- KfW's 2025 representative SME panel found an average of 32 hours per month spent on bureaucratic processes and estimated those costs at 3.9% of annual personnel costs. This is an adjacent administrative-burden signal, not a savings promise for automation.
- UK government mixed-method research reports high digital adoption among SMEs and a need for reliable personalized support and advice.
- Two independent tool ecosystems now operate or pilot partner channels around automation implementation. That is stronger evidence for the service category than the old episode's infrastructure valuations.

Funding, software valuation, and broad iPaaS market size receive no promotion credit because they do not establish a small buyer, service price, operator capacity, or customer outcome.

## Proposed Operator Blueprint

- Offer: One workflow reliability sprint: discovery, current-state map, baseline, access plan, one production workflow, representative test suite, exception route, alerts, runbook, client training, and thirty days of monitored operation.
- Buyer outcome: A named workflow has fewer undocumented manual handoffs and a visible owner, log, alert, and recovery path. The buyer receives the current-state map, production implementation, test record, runbook, access inventory, and before-and-after measurement.
- Delivery workflow:
  1. Select one high-frequency, rule-stable workflow with an identifiable owner and measurable baseline.
  2. Observe the work and map inputs, states, decisions, systems, credentials, exceptions, and failure consequences.
  3. Decide which steps remain human and define the minimum safe automation.
  4. Build in a client-controlled environment where possible; use least-privilege access and non-production test data.
  5. Test ordinary, missing, duplicate, delayed, malformed, and downstream-unavailable cases.
  6. Add logs, alerts, retry or stop behavior, and a named human exception owner.
  7. Train the team, transfer documentation and ownership, establish the measurement window, and monitor for thirty days.
  8. Recommend maintain, expand, redesign, or retire based on evidence.
- Technology and human stack: Process interview and state mapping first; client systems and permissions second; workflow runner third; logs, alerts, secrets handling, documentation, and measurement around it. Human judgment owns ambiguous, sensitive, and material decisions.
- Required skills or credentials: Process discovery, basic data and API reasoning, tool implementation, test design, documentation, client communication, privacy hygiene, and the judgment to refuse unsafe or poorly defined workflows. Regulated processes require additional domain and legal review.
- Go-to-market path: Start with one segment the operator can already reach, such as small agencies or professional-service firms. Offer a bounded paid diagnostic around one visible failure, then credit part of the diagnostic fee toward a fixed-scope sprint. Use a before-and-after process map and client-approved result as the next proof. Partner directories and freelance marketplaces are secondary acquisition paths, not assumptions.
- Pricing model: Fixed project fee. The test model uses a price hypothesis rather than the old episode's unsupported market range. Monitoring is a separate offer only when a recurring reliability job and response expectation are documented.
- Defensibility: Buyer and workflow fluency, reliable exception design, documentation, measurement, and trust. Tool certification alone is not a moat.

## Narrative and audience hypothesis

- Operator protagonist: An operations-minded professional deciding whether their implementation skill can become a responsible small service business.
- Starting state: A customer or lead completes an action in one system, but the next system and person never receive a trustworthy state change.
- Inciting change: Small firms add more software and AI-capable tools, increasing both automation potential and the number of brittle handoffs.
- Stakes: Staff perform invisible integration work, follow-up slips, data duplicates, and the owner cannot see where the process failed.
- Causal mechanism: Each tool sees only part of the transaction; humans bridge the gaps; simple happy-path automations hide missing data, duplicates, access failures, usage limits, and ambiguous decisions.
- Build transformation: The operator follows one transaction, maps every state and exception, automates only the stable path, adds observability and human ownership, and measures the result.
- Decisions and tradeoffs: Which workflow is safe and valuable; what stays human; client-owned versus operator-owned infrastructure; speed versus test coverage; fixed handoff versus ongoing support; whether to expand or stop.
- End state and payoff: One workflow either shows a documented reduction in manual touches, cycle time, missed handoffs, or error exposure, or fails its test and produces a clear reject/redesign decision.
- Visual evidence classes: Customer token, process map, app states, inbox and spreadsheet handoffs, event log, duplicate and missing-data cases, alert and retry path, access ledger, before-and-after measures, and transferred runbook.
- Working audience promise: Learn how to turn one broken business handoff into a tested service without selling an “AI agency” fantasy.
- Honest packaging territory: `The Business Between the Apps` / `Who Fixes the Workflow When It Breaks?` / `Build One Automation a Business Can Trust`.

## Risks and constraints

- Factual uncertainty: No representative service-price, utilization, margin, churn, or solo-income dataset was found. Vendor outcomes are not independent audits.
- Analogy-transfer uncertainty: Selected partners may have credentials, referrals, teams, or existing customers that a new operator lacks.
- Legal or regulatory boundary: Data-protection, contract, industry, and automated-decision rules vary. High-impact decisions in health, employment, credit, insurance, legal, or safety workflows are excluded from a generalist pilot without qualified review.
- Permissions, privacy, or guest dependency: Client consent and scoped credentials are required. Use least privilege, client-controlled accounts where practical, test data, a secrets inventory, an access revocation plan, and permission before discussing a client publicly. A guest is not required for the episode.
- Platform or vendor dependency: Pricing, rate limits, task or credit exhaustion, API changes, connector failures, and platform terms can break delivery or contribution.
- Ethical failure mode: Automating an unclear or harmful process, hiding human judgment inside an AI step, building without exception ownership, retaining unnecessary data, or creating operator lock-in.
- Claims that must not be made:
  - that three or four retainers take only a few hours per week;
  - that the business has 90% margins;
  - that $2,000-$6,000 per month is a realistic year-one outcome;
  - that an AI step automatically makes a workflow valuable;
  - that software valuation or funding proves service demand;
  - that the proposed price, capacity, customer result, or retainer is observed;
  - that “boring” alone creates a moat.

## Thirty-day validation plan

1. Interview ten owners or operations leads in one reachable service-business segment. Require an actual screen-share or process walk-through; collect frequency, manual touches, failure cases, consequence, current workaround, system ownership, and access constraints.
2. Produce three current-state workflow maps and score them for frequency, rule stability, consequence, exception complexity, data sensitivity, measurable baseline, and buyer ownership. Reject regulated or materially ambiguous workflows.
3. Offer two bounded paid diagnostics and seek one paid implementation sprint at a predeclared price and scope. Track outreach, calls, objections, discovery time, delivery time, test failures, support load, buyer usage, and the agreed before-and-after measure.

Success signal: At least three qualified buyers reveal a recurring consequential workflow, two authorize a diagnostic, one pays for a bounded pilot, and the implemented workflow can be tested and measured without an unresolved permission or safety blocker.

Kill or redesign condition: After fifteen targeted conversations or outreaches, no qualified buyer exposes a recurring costly handoff or no buyer will pay for a bounded diagnostic or pilot; or the only available workflows depend on high-risk judgment, inaccessible systems, unmanageable exceptions, or support obligations that break the capacity model.
