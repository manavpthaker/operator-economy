## Recommendation

Decision: **eligible, pending calibration-zone owner review** — with the recommendation that the owner treat it as **continue research** until the two verifications and validation step 1 are complete (see rationale)

Evidence class: **observed model** for the service category; solo economics modeled

Required disclosure: Small-firm AI adoption rates, the enterprise "no measurable value" finding, vendor prices and the vendor's own setup requirements and onboarding menu are **observed**. The "adopted but not working" problem is **transferred** from enterprise research to small brands and must be said as such. The service category is observed — certified agencies and the vendor sell implementation and optimization — but **no solo operator was found selling it at disclosed economics**, and every fee, hour, capacity, payback and contribution figure is **modeled**, including the assumed cost per ticket and the hypothesized resolution lift. The measure counts containment, not correctness. **The vendor can close the gap.** The legacy figures — $40K a month, 85–90% margins, $2–5K bands, $5.9 billion — are **excluded and may not appear**.

Rationale: This scores 71 — one point over the threshold and squarely inside the calibration zone — which is an honest reflection of a premise that has been made real but not yet proven. What changed from the legacy episode is the bounding: the buyer is now defined by the vendor's ticket tiers and onboarding threshold, the deliverable by the vendor's go-live checklist, and the measure by the vendor's billing meter. Those are concrete in a way "businesses drowning in AI" never was, and the offer is observed in the market in exactly that form.

What has not changed is the absence of any observed small-brand transaction with a solo operator. The buyer's spend on the tool is verified; the buyer's spend on setup at this size is a hypothesis; the enterprise failure rate is one hop from the buyer; and the platform that creates the gap can close it. The score is above 70 because the mechanism, the offer and the measure are sourced; it is not higher because the demand for the solo version rests entirely on the thirty-day plan.

Three items should be settled before the owner promotes: open CLM-013 at source (it defines the buyer); confirm the Gorgias per-resolution price on the vendor's own page; and run validation step 1 on a sandbox so the episode can say what the wizard does rather than infer it. All three are days of work, not weeks. Given that, the reviewer's own recommendation to the owner is to hold at **continue research** for those three items rather than promote on a one-point margin — while recording that the package as it stands passes the score and every hard gate.
