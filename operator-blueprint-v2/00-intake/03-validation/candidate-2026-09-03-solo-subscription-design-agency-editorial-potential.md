## Narrative engine

| Check | Pass condition | Result | Evidence or proposed story function | Failure or caveat |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Operator protagonist | A specific prospective operator has a decision, capability gap, or opportunity. | **pass** | A working designer deciding whether to turn project work into a published-cadence subscription — and, as a deliberate second protagonist, the non-designer who learns from the episode that they cannot run this on tools | The disqualifier is part of the story, not a footnote. The channel's generalist viewer must be told early that this one requires design ability |
| Inciting change | A sourced change or persistent failure makes the old way inadequate now. | **pass** | CLM-009 — Figma Make weekly actives up over 70% in a quarter; CLM-006 — Design Pickle ends "unlimited" on 2025-06-02; CLM-011 — BLS projects designer employment to decline with AI named as the cause | Superside's restructuring (CLM-004) is aggregator-reported and may be mentioned only as "reported", with no percentage |
| Stakes | The viewer can see what is lost, gained, delayed, or put at risk. | **pass** | At five clients, one leaving is a fifth of revenue and ~24 hours of unpaid demo work to replace; a sixth client breaks the 48-hour promise; the buyer's own tool improves every quarter | Stakes are operator-side and structural. The buyer's cost of not having design is **unquantified** and the episode may not invent it |
| Causal mechanism | The episode can explain why the problem happens. | **pass** | Subscription design sells capacity, not design. "Unlimited" fails when many clients queue against a shared bench (CLM-006, in the vendor's own words); one operator with one request at a time is the only version where the promise and the capacity are the same number | Genuinely mechanistic and stated by an operator that ran the model at scale |
| Build transformation | The business changes the starting system through understandable operating steps. | **pass** | Request board → scope against the contract → AI draft → operator selects and finishes → review → ship; plus the demo-first outreach loop that fills the roster | Every step is showable; the "select and finish" step is where the human judgment lives and must be shown, not asserted |
| Decisions and tradeoffs | Meaningful choices with constraints, alternatives, or failure modes. | **pass** | Price at the $699-$995 floor or toward $4,995; hold the contract against scope creep; refuse the sixth client; what to do the month a client cancels; whether to build the audience the reference operator had | The pricing decision is real because the ladder is observed (CLM-007, CLM-008) rather than asserted |
| Payoff | A usable blueprint and a more informed build / test / reject decision. | **pass** | A cadence contract, a capacity calculator, a pricing ladder, a 30-day demo-first test with a kill condition, and a clear "not for you" if the viewer cannot design | The payoff may be "reject" for most of the audience. That is a legitimate Operator Economy ending |
| Visual evidence potential | The causal story is showable. | **pass** | Pricing pages side by side ($699 → $4,995 → $15,000); the request board and a 48-hour clock; Design Pickle's retraction in its own words; the Figma Make line against the BLS decline; a demo redesign beside the prospect's live page; the outreach tracker with its zero | Strong. The "unlimited" retraction and the three pricing pages are single images that carry an argument |

Narrative verdict: **pass**

## Audience pull

| Check | Pass condition | Result | Evidence | Failure or caveat |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Must be packaged so the non-designer still gets a decision |
| Consequential interest | At least one usable direct or adjacent source shows problem behavior, spending, adoption, risk, or active buyer attention. | **pass, narrowly** | CLM-013 — 61% of 1,500 surveyed early-stage companies reliant on contract talent, 32% to delay hires; CLM-006 — a scaled operator's own statement that clients requested beyond capacity; CLM-007/008 — a 50-vendor category with published prices | The survey is about contract talent generally, not design; the vendor statement is about capacity, not spend. Consequential *design* spend by *this* buyer is inferred |
| Demand triangulation | At least two independent signals support interest. | **pass** | Three families: vendor-published supply (CLM-007, CLM-008); independent founder survey plus Carta data (CLM-013, CLM-014); public-company filing and government data for the why-now (CLM-009, CLM-011). | Timely tension | A current change, contradiction, threat, or opening. | **pass** | The tools that let one person do the work are being sold to the client (CLM-009), the category's incumbents retreated from "unlimited" (CLM-006) and restructured (CLM-004, reported), and the solo reference operator kept its contract and price (CLM-001) — all inside fifteen months | The contradiction is structural and dated. It is the strongest editorial asset in the package |
| Differentiated promise | Existing coverage leaves room for a synthesis, decision tool, or blueprint. | **pass** | Existing coverage is vendor listicles and founder-story pieces that repeat one self-reported revenue figure. If packaging leans on "$2M a year, one person," the differentiation collapses into the coverage it critiques |
| Honest packaging | A compelling premise without unsupported outcome, fear claim, or earnings promise. | **pass** | "The biggest design-subscription companies stopped promising 'unlimited.' The one-person version never had to." No revenue in the title; no "you can make"; no AI-doom claim | The legacy EP004 title carried "$5,000 a month" in a working title; the V2 packaging must not carry a dollar figure that is either a vendor's price or a self-report |

Audience-pull verdict: **pass**

### Search-volume exception

Exact-query volume is recorded as **not measurable**. A measurement attempt was made on 2026-09-03: Google Trends returned HTTP 403 (CLM-019), and no licensed exact-volume tool is available to this operation.

The exception is invoked with caution. Consequential interest passes narrowly on an adjacent survey and a vendor's capacity statement; triangulation passes on three families, none of which measures buyer demand for a *solo* subscription directly. "The category is crowded" is offered as evidence of supply, not demand, and the gate records that distinction.

## Overall editorial verdict

Narrative engine: **pass**

Audience pull: **pass**

Overall editorial potential: **pass**

Story spine: `a seed-stage founder needs a landing page by Tuesday and has no designer` -> `AI makes the first draft nearly free for everyone, and the category's biggest operators respond by ending "unlimited"` -> `subscription design sells capacity, not design, and one person with one request at a time is the only version where the promise and the capacity are the same number` -> `a published cadence contract, a request queue one person can clear, and a demo-first outreach loop` -> `price at the floor or the ceiling, refuse the sixth client, survive the month one leaves, and watch the buyer's own tool improve` -> `the viewer knows whether they can design well enough to sell this, what to charge, how many clients one person can keep, and the day-30 renewal answer that decides it`

Working audience promise: The biggest design-subscription companies stopped promising "unlimited." The one-person version never had to. Here is what that business actually sells, what it can charge, and how many clients one person can keep.
