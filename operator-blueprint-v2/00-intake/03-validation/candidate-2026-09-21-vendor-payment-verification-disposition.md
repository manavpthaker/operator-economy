## Reason for stopping

- Failed or incomplete requirement: evidence, business fit, economics, truth, and permission
- Plain-English finding: A current rule, a material problem, adjacent paid work, public seller offers, and a showable delivery mechanism make the candidate worth researching. They do not show that the target small business will pay an independent operator for this narrow preventive install. Price, acquisition, delivery hours, provider qualification, insurance, and liability remain unverified. Completing the remaining promotion artifacts would turn modeled assumptions into false precision.
- Claims or assumptions that remain unusable:
  - That “all small businesses” need this service. The Nacha rule covers non-consumer ACH Originators and related covered parties, not every business.
  - That a call-back or this exact service is required by Nacha. The rule is risk-based and technology-neutral.
  - That any public seller price is a proven market price or completed sale.
  - That general fraud-loss statistics establish small-business purchase intent.
  - That the owner or an unqualified operator can safely deliver the work.
  - Any promise of fraud prevention, account-ownership verification, cybersecurity assurance, legal compliance, or Nacha certification.
  - The modeled $1,500-$3,000 price, 16-32 delivery hours, two-client monthly capacity, or contribution margin as observed economics.

## Adversarial editorial finding

The strongest competing explanation is that no independent operator business exists at useful scale. The buyer's bank, bookkeeper, CPA, MSP, insurer, AP consultant, or a $99-per-month software product may cover the same control more cheaply and with more trust. The 2026 rule can create implementation work without creating a new category or a durable audience-worthy business.

The strongest editorial risk is overstating a timely compliance change as a buyer-demand signal. The episode is showable, but no script should be started until a real buyer decision and a safe provider boundary exist.
