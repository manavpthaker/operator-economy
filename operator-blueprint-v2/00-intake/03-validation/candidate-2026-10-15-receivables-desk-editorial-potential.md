This gate asks whether the opportunity can become a compelling Operator Economy episode. It is separate from business plausibility. A useful business idea can still fail editorially if it produces only a tool list, generic explainer, or static pitch deck.

## Narrative engine

The operator is the narrative center. A guest or conventional character arc is optional. The story may follow a person, business system, transaction, customer journey, or operating decision, but it must change causally over time.

| Check | Pass condition | Result | Evidence or proposed story function | Failure or caveat |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Operator protagonist | A specific prospective operator has a decision, capability gap, or opportunity to pursue. | pass | A former bookkeeper or AR clerk deciding whether to sell a desk or just resell a tool | Composite viewer, not a real person |
| Inciting change | A sourced change or persistent failure makes the old way inadequate now. | pass | CLM-001 (47% to 59% overdue in a year); CLM-022 (Ramp AR launch) | One vendor's survey |
| Stakes | The viewer can see what is lost, gained, delayed, or put at risk. | pass | CLM-002, CLM-003 ($17,700 owed; credit-card reliance); customer relationships | Mean, not median; association, not cause |
| Causal mechanism | The episode can explain why the problem happens, not merely assert that it exists. | pass | Follow-up is unowned and awkward; the free reminder sends the first nudge, not the third, and cannot fix a wrong PO (CLM-006, CLM-019 case study: "the spreadsheet would look the same the following Tuesday") | Mechanism shown in seller and forum material, not a study |
| Build transformation | The proposed business changes the starting system through understandable operating steps. | pass | Aging report to schedule to agent sends to reply sorting to dispute hand-off to weekly report | Synthetic client only |
| Decisions and tradeoffs | The operator must make meaningful choices with constraints, alternatives, or failure modes. | pass | Price above $99–$299 software but below the $599–$729 human desk; flat fee versus share; which accounts to lock; which states to serve; partner with bookkeepers or compete with them | |
| Payoff | The ending delivers a usable blueprint and a more informed build / test / reject decision. | pass | Canvas plus the five-offer, one-client, state-check first test with kill condition | Results are the viewer's to find |
| Visual evidence potential | The causal story can be shown through people, places, interfaces, documents, flows, comparisons, or measurable state changes. | pass | Aging report, follow-up ladder, agent log, dispute sort, price ladder ($99 / $299 / $729 / $1,199), state map, unit-economics table | Interfaces are synthetic or vendor screenshots needing permission |

Narrative verdict: pass

Failure rule: A chronology, list of tools, market overview, or ten-slide taxonomy is not a narrative engine unless the elements form a causal operating journey.

## Audience pull

Search volume is one signal, not the definition of interest. An emerging topic can pass without measurable exact-query volume when the research documents current proxy demand.

| Check | Pass condition | Result | Evidence | Failure or caveat |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Named audience job | The intended viewer recognizes a decision or problem they may plausibly face. | pass | Bookkeepers and AR workers asked "can you help us actually collect this?" (CLM-021 frames the same question) | Seller framing |
| Consequential interest | At least one usable direct or adjacent source shows problem behavior, spending, adoption, risk, or active buyer attention. | pass | CLM-001 to CLM-004, CLM-019 | |
| Demand triangulation | At least two independent signals support interest. Signals may include exact or adjacent search demand, buyer behavior, transactions, adoption, recurring questions, community demand, or credible category research. | pass | Vendor survey (CLM-001); employer postings (CLM-004); forum threads (CLM-006); credit-insurer survey and vendor launches (CLM-022) | Exact search not measurable (Trends relative, low volume) |
| Timely tension | The topic contains a current change, contradiction, threat, or opening rather than generic evergreen advice. | pass | Overdue share up while AR agents launch for finance teams and now small firms; the tool that makes the desk cheap also threatens it | |
| Differentiated promise | Existing coverage leaves room for an Operator Economy synthesis, decision tool, or blueprint. | pass (narrow) | Coverage is vendor guides and vendor pitches; idealab shows the idea pitched as a 2–20 person venture (`crowded`). None read covers the one-person version, the price ladder, or the state licensing line | The angle must be the squeeze and the line, not "AI chases invoices" |
| Honest packaging | A compelling title-and-thumbnail premise can be stated without an unsupported outcome, fear claim, or earnings promise. | pass | Working premise: "AI already sells the invoice chaser for $99. Here is the part it can't do, and the legal line to check first." | No collection rates, no income, no "no licence needed" |

Audience-pull verdict: pass

### Search-volume exception

Exact-query volume is marked `not measurable`: Google Trends US (2026-10-13 scout) gives only
relative interest, with "unpaid invoices" at a five-year high and "accounts receivable outsourcing"
near zero. Consequential interest and demand triangulation pass on other signals.
