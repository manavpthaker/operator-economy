## Recommendation

Decision: **eligible**

Evidence class: **adjacent synthesis**

Required disclosure: Transition scale, involuntary-exit share, listing-to-sale rate and transaction multiples are **observed**. The service format is **transferred** from due-diligence preparation and compliance-readiness consulting, which share its structure but not its urgency — those sell against a scheduled examination and an exit has no date. Every operator-side figure — fee, hours, capacity, contribution — is **modeled**, and no observed readiness-engagement fee was found. The circulating claim that preparation lifts multiples from 4.5–5.5× to 7.5–9.0× is **seller-published and excluded**; no multiple uplift may be attributed to preparation. The revenue model is fixed-fee as a **legal consequence**, not a preference. Nothing regarding licensing is legal advice, and the perimeter varies by state.

Rationale: This scores 79 and is eligible outside the calibration zone. It is materially better evidenced than the candidate parked earlier the same day, and the improvement is concentrated exactly where that one failed. The buyer relationship is direct rather than adjacent. The consequence is quantified from two independent directions instead of being unquantifiable. The pricing environment is anchored by quarterly closed-transaction data from a professional association rather than by seller asking prices — and the one figure that *would* have been seller-published, the prepared-versus-unprepared multiple gap, is excluded by name so it cannot leak into the script.

The operator-entry barrier is the other reason this fits: no licence or sector credential is required for preparation work in 34 states plus DC, which makes it addressable by the generalist audience the channel serves rather than only by someone who has already worked in the sector.

Two weaknesses are real and are not argued away. The offer parallel is structurally sound but **urgency-poor** — SOC 2 and ISO readiness sell against a dated audit, and this does not. The partial answer, that about half of exits are involuntary, converts a deadline into a probability and is a weaker sale that the episode must present honestly. And go-to-market rests on a single assumption: that brokers refer unready listings rather than doing the work free to win them. Both are first-order tests in the thirty-day plan, and either could kill the business.

One item is escalated rather than resolved: **the brokerage perimeter requires written legal review before script lock.** It determines whether the practice is legal as designed, it varies by state, and the channel must not be the thing that rules on it.
