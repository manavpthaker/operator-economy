## Attempts to break the candidate

1. **Is this a duplicate of the promoted AI-implementation candidate?** In structure, largely yes. The promoted candidate is "an operations-minded generalist sells small DTC brands a fixed-fee implementation" of a tool they already pay for, retainer excluded until a recurring job is observed, with the vendor able to close the gap and the same Lovingly POV. This candidate is the same template on a different tool and buyer. What it has that the promoted one lacks is the commodity-fix lesson and the substitute arithmetic. What the promoted one has that this lacks is AI at the centre. Two episodes on the same spine for the same would-be-operator audience spend the channel's differentiation twice.
2. **Is it "a Zapier freelancer with a longer checklist"?** On the current evidence, yes, and the package now says so itself (amendment 02: without the retainer "the business is cheap per-fix and small-build labour"; the evidence "leans toward" this objection). Buyers write reliability into build budgets; nobody was found buying it separately. The longer checklist may win a bid; it has not been shown to raise the price.
3. **The commodity fix market ($40–$150).** Single-fix offers of $40, $125, €150, $149 and $150 in September 2026 with no visible hire; Fiverr floors from $5; Make repair quotes clustered $125–$300. Rung 1 competes in that pool with no edge but the checklist. The ladder's entry rung sits above every observed audit ask ($95–$400) and is priced off a build budget. The package is honest that this is transferred, not observed.
4. **The unevidenced retainer.** No buyer price exists; the one seller asking $750–$1,500/month shows no buyer; ANA-008 is rejected as evidence. Yet amendment 02 shows the business only clears the imputed labour line with the retainer. A blueprint whose viability hinges on one unobserved purchase can be taught, but it cannot score well on economics, and it should not be packaged as "the reliability business."
5. **The tool-plus-fix substitute.** $29/month alerts (CLM-033) or free Zapier and Make failure emails (CLM-029) plus a $150 fix beats a $300–$500 retainer below about 1.8–3.1 incidents a month. Nothing in the package suggests a small firm's single handoff breaks that often; the platform auto-off rules and notices even do part of the retainer's alerting job for free. The retainer's remaining case — one accountable person, catching deprecations early — is real but soft, and it is the pitch every $150 seller can also make.
6. **Does AI change who can deliver this (the owner's AI-change test)?** No, on the package's own evidence. Delivery steps 1–8 contain no job AI does; AI appears only as why-now context (CLM-001 adoption without integration) and as AI-step repricing on the platforms. The business would work the same way without AI. Reviewer inference, not sourced: to the extent AI coding help lowers the skill needed to fix a workflow, it lowers it for every $40 seller too, which deepens the commodity problem rather than opening a new job. The AI-change test is a scout rule (owner decision 2026-09-25, `oe-candidate-scout.md` test 9), not a locked Step 0 gate, so it does not change a score or a hard-gate result here. It is still an owner thesis rule, and a lead screened today with this description would be rejected on it. That weighs heavily in the recommendation.
7. **Audience pull.** The audience signal that grew (`automation agency`) is seller-side and has fallen about 80% from its May 2026 plateau; buyer-problem queries are unmeasurable. Consequential interest improved (buyer budgets), but that is buyer evidence, not viewer pull. The audience gate still passes on triangulation; the score does not support more.
8. **Is the episode worth making if the retainer claim is removed?** Only as a "do not build this as described" episode. Strip the retainer and the honest payoff is: the fix is a commodity, reliable builds are a crowded bid market at $1,500–$2,500, and the operator is a small-build freelancer (amendment 02's own kill branch). That is a legitimate reject decision under the payoff check, but it is a segment, not a blueprint, and its lesson (implementation is commodity; the recurring job must be earned) is already the spine of the promoted AI-implementation episode. As a standalone Operator Economy blueprint, no.
9. **Is the coaching standard being used to inflate anything?** Not in the amendments; both say field-only gaps stay scored as gaps, and neither changed a score. This review holds the same line (see next section).
10. **Is the package internally consistent?** No. The candidate brief, analogy map (ANA-006 and its disclosure), Canvas gate (disclosure item 1: "its $3,500 fee, its 28 hours") and editorial review still describe the retired sprint. Disposition addendum item 3 asked for a revised research brief as a new version and for the substitute to be added to the Canvas; amendment 02 is an amendment record that does the substance of the price reset but neither re-issues the brief nor re-reviews the Canvas. This is a record defect, not a failed gate, but no promotion record could bind these hashes as they stand.

## What the coaching standard legitimately changes, and what it must not

**Changes, legitimately:**

- Owner interviews, pilot asks and outreach are no longer re-entry conditions. The 2026-09-03 re-entry item 1 is correctly retired.
- Field-only unknowns (incident frequency, retainer take-up, audit-and-fix conversion, win rate against bidders) become the viewer's first test. Amendment 02's plan (desk week, ten conversations, sell the entry, offer the retainer beside the written-out substitute, with success and kill conditions) is a fair teaching device and satisfies the Canvas "first validation test" check.
- Those unknowns are nonfatal to the "no unresolved factual or source blocker" gate as long as they are labelled and taught as unknowns.

**Must not change, and did not here:**

- Scores. The retainer's missing price, the unmeasured consequence and the unknown win rate stay deducted in economics, buyer and go-to-market. No factor rose because field work was removed.
- Hard gates, the evidence floor, the analogy test, the evidence class (observed model at category level; every operator-side number modeled) and the need for owner promotion.
- Public framing. The episode may not claim the business works, that buyers pay for reliability, or any earnings outcome. The modeled retainer may not be presented as an anchor.
- Editorial judgement. A viewer test cannot fix a thesis-fit failure (AI-change) or a duplication problem; those are not evidence gaps.
- One adversarial caution on the teaching device: the viewer's test is fair only when the outcome is genuinely open. Here the desk evidence already leans toward the kill branch for the retainer and the audit-and-fix rung. Teaching a test the package predicts will fail is honest only if the episode says so.

## Decision

Decision: **park**

Evidence class: **observed model** at category level; the ladder prices are transferred from seller asks and buyer build budgets; the retainer and every operator-side figure are modeled (`modeled scenario, not observed performance or an earnings forecast`)

Production promotion authorized: no. This review does not create a promotion record, does not touch the queue and does not promote.

Recommendation among promote review / continue research / park: **park.**

Reason: The re-score lands at 62, below 65, which is the condition the disposition addendum and both amendments set for park. More importantly, no remaining gap is desk-closable in a way that could reach 70: what is missing (incident frequency, retainer take-up, win rate) is field-only and belongs to the viewer, and under the coaching standard those gaps stay scored as gaps. The price reset was done honestly and it answered the central question the wrong way: the fix is a commodity, reliability is bought only inside crowded build budgets, and the business clears its labour cost only on an unpriced retainer that must beat a $29 tool plus a $150 fix. Two further reasons sit outside the score: the candidate fails the owner's AI-change test (AI does no part of the delivery), and its spine duplicates the already-promoted AI-implementation episode for the same audience. The best of this package (the commodity-fix price ladder, the substitute break-even, the platform stop-condition checklist) is worth carrying as a segment or Canvas panel inside the promoted episode, if the owner wants it, rather than as a separate blueprint.

Re-entry, if ever: a new reviewed package with a re-issued research brief, analogy map and Canvas gate built on the ladder; a demonstrated AI-change role in delivery; and a differentiated spine from the promoted AI-implementation episode. Not on the current evidence.
