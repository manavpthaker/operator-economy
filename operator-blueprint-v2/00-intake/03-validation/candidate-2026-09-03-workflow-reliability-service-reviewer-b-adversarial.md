## Attempts to break the candidate

1. **Is this just the old EP003 with better footnotes?** Partly, and the package admits it. The underlying business is the same. What changed is that the buyer is bounded, the retainer is demoted to a hypothesis, the seller-authored bands are excluded by name, and the valuation ladder is rejected as evidence. That is a different episode. It is not yet a different evidence base for price.
2. **Is "reliability" a real product or a positioning word?** It is a real scope — tests, exception ownership, alerts, documentation, monitoring — but nothing in the package shows a small firm paying for that scope separately from the connection. The Zapier partner case (CLM-011) shows SOPs, training and 90 days of support bundled into an engagement; it does not show they were priced. If buyers only pay for "make it work," the differentiation collapses into a Zapier freelancer with a longer checklist.
3. **Does the federal survey prove the buyer problem?** No. It proves that AI adoption outruns integration in US small firms, which is the right *shape*. It does not mention workflow automation, cross-tool handoffs, professional-service firms, or a cost. The package uses it correctly as adjacent evidence; the scorecard should not let it carry more than that.
4. **Is the failure evidence a market fact or a marketing asset?** The Pipedrive V1 deprecation (CLM-009) is a fact with a date. The Connex Digital case (CLM-010) is a marketing asset — a competent, plausible one, written by the firm that sold the fix, with an unverifiable "2–3 hours/week." The package attributes it properly. Reviewer B holds that one vendor notice for one app pair does not establish that platform change is frequent enough to fund a retainer, and the package agrees by making the retainer a hypothesis.
5. **Are the directory reviews buyer demand?** They are evidence that small firms have bought this service from the partners Zapier features. They say nothing about firms that could not find or afford one, and they are displayed by the vendor that benefits from the ecosystem looking busy. They pass consequential interest; they do not support a high audience score.
6. **Is the price defensible at all?** The package never claims it is. $3,500 is chosen to be falsifiable. But the sensitivity table shows the whole contribution hinges on the one input with nothing behind it, and the two channels that could have supplied an asking-price band (Upwork, Fiverr) were inaccessible on the research date. Until a firm accepts or declines a proposal, economics cannot score above the bottom half.
7. **Can an unknown independent reach this buyer?** Unproven. The directories are the observed channel and the entrant is not in them. Warm-network diagnostic entry is a reasonable hypothesis and is properly the first validation step. It is not evidence.
8. **Is the POV real?** Yes, and it is small. Facts.md supports "I have automated my own team's work and learned what breaks." It does not support "I have sold this," and the package says so. The synthesis claim is over-stated in the candidate brief ("original synthesis, partial") relative to what the research demonstrates: a good reframing of a mechanism that agency blogs already describe. Under Step 0.3 that is worth 3, not 4, and it is worth saying that the lowered floor is functioning — a candidate with builder-side experience and a partial synthesis scores 3, not the old default of 4.
9. **Is there an episode?** Yes — one client record, one dated break, one retainer decision. The risk is the format: this subject is where the channel's tutorial reflex is strongest. The editorial gate names the risk; it cannot prevent it.
10. **Is the null own-channel result being used as cover?** No. The package records EP003's one view, invokes the channel's own "n too small" rule, and draws no inference. That is correct. Reviewer B notes only that a real candidate re-entering a premise the channel already published owes the owner a plain statement: the earlier publication contributed no audience evidence, positive or negative.

## Decision

Decision: **continue research**

Evidence class: **observed model** (category level); the reliability scope and every operator-side number are modeled

Production promotion authorized: no — this review does not create a promotion record

Reason: The package supports an honest episode about what small firms are actually buying when they pay for automation, and about how to scope, test and price a reliability sprint. It does not yet support a claim that the reliability scope is purchasable at a price that covers the hours, because no buyer has been observed paying for it and no independent price exists. That is a research gap, not a structural fault, and it is cheap to close: ten screen-share interviews and a marketplace asking-price sample. Reviewer B would not recommend an owner override at 63; the honest position is that the number is not yet earned.
