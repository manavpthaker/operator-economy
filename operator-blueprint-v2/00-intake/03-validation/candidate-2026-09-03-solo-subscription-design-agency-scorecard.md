## Owner override

Used: **no**

Reason: Not requested by this review. The score sits at 66, inside the 65-69 band where an override *could* be recorded alongside passing hard gates. This reviewer recommends against using one until the 30-day test has produced a paid month, because the four points between 66 and 70 are exactly the points that a paid month, logged hours and a renewal answer would supply.

What remains incomplete: a first-party paid month at a predeclared price; logged hours per request; a day-30 renewal answer; any independent retention evidence for the category.

## Recommendation

Decision: **continue research**

Evidence class: **observed model** (offer verified; solo economics not)

Required disclosure: The offer contract, the category price ladder, Design Pickle's end of "unlimited", Figma's adoption figures and BLS labor data are **observed**. Buyer behavior is **transferred** from a founder survey about contract talent generally. Every operator-side figure — price point, hours, capacity, acquisition cost, contribution — is **modeled**, and at the modeled point the business is a well-paid job for one designer. The only public solo revenue and client-count figures are one founder's self-reports, told at five revenue levels and two client counts, and are **excluded**. Retention is a **hypothesis**. The buyer has the same tools. This business requires design ability.

Rationale: The legacy screen put this premise at 72-82 because an exact observed model exists. It does, and that is why the hard gates pass: the contract is published, the price ladder is verifiable from fifty sellers' own pages, the capacity constraint is stated by an operator that ran the model at scale, and the bounded 30-day test is real. But the screen's band was carried by the reference operator's economics, and under `EVIDENCE-STANDARD.md` those economics are a single founder's self-report and cannot be used. Remove them and what is left is honest and thin: a buyer who demonstrably buys contract capacity but has not been shown to buy *design* from a *solo operator* above the floor; a channel the reference operator built on an audience the viewer does not have; and a retention question with no evidence at all.

The research also moved the premise. The story is no longer "one person, seven figures." It is that the category's biggest operators stopped promising what the model is named for, in the same window that AI made production cheapest, and the one-person version is the only one whose promise and capacity are the same number. That is a better episode than the one that was published — and it is still an episode about a business whose two decisive questions have not been answered by anyone.

Continue research means one specific thing: run the 30-day plan. A paid month, logged hours and a renewal answer would move Buyer, Economics and Go-to-market by enough to clear 70 on evidence rather than by override. If the test fails on its kill conditions, the honest outcome is **parked**, and the episode should not be produced as a build recommendation.
