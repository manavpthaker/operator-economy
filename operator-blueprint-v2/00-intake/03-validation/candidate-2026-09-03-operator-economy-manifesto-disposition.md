## Reason for stopping

- Failed or incomplete requirement: **business fit** (structural, non-waivable). Secondary: Canvas feasibility and the narrative-engine gate would fail for the same reason, and no research brief could change that.
- Plain-English finding: EP005 is the channel's argument about how to judge a small software business — the floor collapsed, not the ceiling; ask whether anyone with capital would bother reproducing it and whether distribution survives a platform's mood. It names no buyer, sells nothing, delivers nothing, has no unit economics, no go-to-market path and no thirty-day test. Step 0 exists to validate one bounded operator business; this premise is the lens Step 0 candidates are judged with, so putting it in the queue would have the rubric score itself. The candidate brief tried five extractions to find a bounded business inside the premise without changing what the episode is: a paid evaluation diagnostic (contradicts the episode's own "no product, no course" promise and has no spend evidence), a "too small to bother" vertical micro-SaaS (a category with a selection rule, not a business), the Toast software-wedge-payments-business pattern (not operator scale), a Lakewood-derived local compliance tool (deliberately omitted from the episode; would be its own intake), and buying-and-operating a sub-$10K-MRR software business (a real, bounded business — but a different one). Writing the research brief, analogy map, gates, scorecard and adversarial pass would have produced seven artifacts documenting the absence of a buyer.
- Claims or assumptions that remain unusable:
  - Any restatement of the thesis as an operator opportunity with a revenue mechanism. The published episode's CTA is the free evaluation sheet; no product, course or service may be attached by inference.
  - The 2026-08-21 legacy-screen band (8-20) as a score. It was a screen, not a scorecard, and this record assigns no score.
  - For the editorial track, the do-not-state entries in `content-os/facts.md` carry forward unchanged: the micro-SaaS "$15B to $60B by 2030" figure; Portes / Wilson / Zhou as support for fluency-as-moat; "illegibility as a moat" as an original coinage; any single AI-productivity multiplier.
  - Marketplace and guide-published micro-acquisition pricing found on 2026-09-03 (Acquire.com self-reported deal counts; guide claims of roughly $30K-$200K listing prices at 2.5-4x SDE for sub-$10K-MRR businesses). Leads only, uncorroborated, and belonging to a different candidate.
