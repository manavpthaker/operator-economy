## Reason for stopping

- Failed or incomplete requirement: economics (no price, no hours) and budget/current-alternative
  (paid studies found, but no amounts and not the same job).
- Plain-English finding: The timing change is real and primary-sourced (TEFA $10,474 per student,
  102,000+ awards; federal credit from 2027-01-01). The pool's hold condition — a school, diocese, or
  network paying an outside provider for enrollment analysis, with scope — is partly met: dioceses,
  parishes, and one school paid a planning firm to study enrollment (CLM-016 to CLM-019). None states
  a price, and all are regional planning studies, not funnel analysis for one small school. The main
  substitute is strong: admissions software already reports conversion, selectivity, and yield over
  five years (CLM-013), and diocesan staff and foundations do related work free to the school.
  Desk sources that could supply a price have not been searched yet, so research continues; scoring
  now would invent the economics.

  - "13% private-school enrollment decline" (it is a high-school-graduate projection, CLM-009).
  - A single count of TEFA-participating private schools (CLM-027).
  - Seller-reported outcomes (CLM-026) and the unverified $50,000 enrollment-director salary

  - Any statement that an operator may receive TEFA-child records without restriction (CLM-005).

## Adversarial editorial finding

Performed by the same agent as the evidence pass; not an independent review.

- Strongest alternative explanation: small schools do not lack analysis; they lack applicants and
  staff time. The software they already pay for draws the funnel, the diocese has an enrollment
  office, and a foundation may coach them for free. What dioceses do buy from outside is big-decision
  planning (open, merge, close), which a solo operator with AI is not obviously better placed to sell.
- Strongest causal weakness: TEFA adds demand. A school with a waitlist has less reason to study
  where families drop out. The funnel problem may bite hardest in states without vouchers.
- Hidden labor: data agreements, getting clean exports from small schools, board presentations,
  diocesan sales cycles.
- Recourse and liability: children's records and family income leaving the school; the Texas
  distribution bar (CLM-005).
- If the most attractive claim (voucher money) is removed: what remains is enrollment consulting, a
  crowded seller market with no public prices and a software substitute. Weak.
- If the buyer is reframed to the diocesan schools office: stronger — the paid evidence is there, and
  one analysis template across many schools fits an AI-enabled operator. This reframe should be
  tested in the next desk pass.
- Family side: considered in the research brief and not adopted; seller price pages only.
- Duplication: none found (per pool deduplication).
