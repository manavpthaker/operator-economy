## Attempts to break the candidate

1. **Is the "managed coverage layer" a business or a consolation prize?** The package is honest that it is the residue after absorption. Reviewer B's finding is that it has not been shown to be a product at all. The evidence that escalation and compliance are *jobs* (CLM-005, CLM-006, CLM-019, CLM-021, CLM-026) is good. The evidence that anyone *buys* them separately from the platform is zero. The distinction is the whole score.
2. **Do the vendor answer-rate datasets pass the evidence standard?** As adjacent evidence that calls ring out in this buyer class, yes. As a magnitude, no — they disagree by definition and both sellers profit from contractors worrying about missed calls. The package uses them correctly as a range and defers magnitude to the buyer's own log, which is the right treatment and a weak position.
3. **Is ANA-004 a label-only comparison?** Yes. "Configure, monitor, document, report" describes managed IT, workflow reliability, and also a person who sets up a thermostat. The transfer needs a shared reason to buy, and the reason managed IT is bought — costly, complex failure — is exactly what the package says may be absent. Reviewer B fails the offer-and-delivery pillar on this ground.
4. **Does the legal layer create the business?** It creates a job and a liability, not a buyer. The platforms' "isn't required" and "up to you" stances (CLM-005, CLM-006) are documented gaps, but a contractor who does not know Maine has a statute is not a contractor who will pay to comply with it. The compliance work is real; it is also a greeting sentence and a state lookup that the platform could ship as a default in a release note.
5. **Is the buyer-owns-the-account design a strength or an admission?** Both. It is correct under the Ambriz capability theory (CLM-021) and it removes the only margin the old business had. The package says so plainly. Reviewer B notes that a business whose correct legal design deletes its margin is telling you something.
6. **Is the vendor-sourced pricing being passed off as market evidence?** No. This is the package's best discipline. Every price is a vendor's own published price used to verify only itself, and the buyer's alternatives are shown before the operator's offer. The legacy bands, the 62% figure, the "handles 80%" claim and the income range are excluded by name.
7. **Is there invented POV?** No. The experience claims stop at facts.md: Director of Customer Experience at four boutique properties, AI Product Manager at a florist platform. The synthesis findings are sourced. Reviewer B checked the "Do not state" list and found the package honours "two years in boutique hospitality" and does not claim phone AI at Lovingly.
8. **Is the audience pull real?** Barely. The gate passes on adoption of the *platforms* and on content aimed at *operators*. Both are real; neither is a contractor wanting an independent. The consumer surveys — interested, but corroborated in direction by independent reporting — say a third of callers hang up on the greeting the operator would be selling. Reviewer B passes the gate because the standard is met on paper and records that it is met by interested parties only.
9. **Is there an episode?** There is a very good episode about absorption. There is not, on this evidence, a very good episode about a business. The narrative engine passes; the blueprint underneath it is the thinnest of any candidate reviewed under V2.

## Shadow gate verdicts

- Evidence and analogy floor: **fail** — the offer-and-delivery pillar rests on a label-only comparison (ANA-004) after the observed parallel (ANA-003) is correctly rejected as the operator's offer. Reviewer B does not accept "bounded first-party test" as a substitute for an operating parallel when the test has not been run. This is a disagreement with Reviewer A's "pass, narrowly."
- Operator Canvas feasibility: **fail** — synthesis coherence fails for the same reason, and economics fails as a business while passing as a model. Reviewer B accepts that this is a strict reading; the gate's own wording ("at least three valid parallels jointly support the required evidence pillars") is met on count and not on the offer pillar.
- Narrative engine: pass
- Audience pull: pass, on interested signals only
- POV: pass, bounded
- Factual and source blockers: clear for an honest episode about absorption; blocked for any claim of a typical fee, margin, income, missed-call rate above the vendor range, or platform revenue lift
- Legal, permission, access, and guest blockers: manageable inbound-only, buyer-owned account, disclosure and recording greeting, informational texts only, no outbound; not legal advice; contractor consent required for log access

## Decision

Decision: **parked**

Evidence class: **adjacent synthesis** (Reviewer B would accept **frontier hypothesis** for the residual layer specifically, since it has no operating precedent)

Production promotion authorized: no

Reason: The package proves, better than the original episode did, that the business the original episode described no longer exists for an independent. What it proposes in its place is a job without a demonstrated buyer. Parked rather than archived because the thirty-day test is cheap, bounded and decisive, and because the absorption story has editorial value regardless of the test's result — but that value belongs to an editorial thesis about absorbed categories, not to the Operator Blueprint queue, unless a contractor pays.

Re-entry condition, stated by Reviewer B: one paid managed engagement at a fee that clears imputed labor, on the buyer's own account, with a 60-day log showing the answer rate moved — or current evidence of a buyer segment where the vertical software has not shipped a receptionist. Reviewer B adds that the second condition is likely to close within a quarter of being found.
