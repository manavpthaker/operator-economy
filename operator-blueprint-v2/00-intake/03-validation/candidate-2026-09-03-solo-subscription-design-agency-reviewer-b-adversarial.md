## Attempts to break the candidate

1. **Is this an "observed model" or a label?** It is observed. Designjoy's contract is live on its own page, fifty vendors sell the shape, and the buyer type is named by the sellers themselves. But the observed thing is the *offer*, not the *solo economics*. The package says so. The label survives; the reader must not hear "observed" and infer "proven."
2. **Are the pricing pages being passed off as market evidence?** Partly. The analogy map calls ANA-001 and ANA-002 "direct," which is accurate for the offer and price ladder. But the coverage test uses them to pass "budget or current alternative," and a seller's price is not a buyer's spend. The pillar passes because Superside's $15,000 minimum and fifty competing vendors make it implausible that nobody pays — but that is an inference, and the scorecard should not have given 12 on Buyer while calling the consequence unquantified. Ten is the honest number.
3. **Is Design Pickle's retraction evidence of anything beyond marketing?** It is a vendor's own account of ending its own promise, framed as authenticity. It does not disclose margins, churn or the client behavior that forced the change. It supports one thing: an operator that ran "unlimited" at scale says the promise and the capacity did not match. That is enough for the mechanism and nothing else. The package uses it correctly; editorial must not inflate it into "the category is collapsing."
4. **Is Superside's restructuring a fact?** Not yet. Two layoff aggregators, two different percentages, no company statement, no reputable outlet found. The package carries it as "reported" context with no percentage. That is the ceiling. If editorial wants it on screen, someone must find the primary.
5. **Does the timely tension prove demand, or the opposite?** The opposite is at least as plausible. BLS says AI reduces the need for designers (CLM-011). Figma's buyer-side adoption is verified (CLM-009). The Mercury survey shows AI-heavy startups relying *more* on contractors, which the package reads as opportunity — but those contractors may be engineers, not designers, and the same survey found design hiring among the lowest-invested functions. The tension is real; its direction is unknown. That is why the 30-day test exists, and why Buyer cannot score above half.
6. **Is the POV invented?** No. Nothing exceeds `facts.md`. But it is thin: buyer-side judgment inferred from roles, a synthesis that is a pattern-read, and a pipeline with zero closes. Step 0.3 lowered the floor for candidates with no demonstrable source; this candidate has demonstrable sources that are each weak. Three is the honest score, and it is not a hard-gate failure because nothing is fabricated and every limit is stated.
7. **Can the channel's generalist viewer build this?** Mostly no, and the package says so. This is the right honesty and a real editorial cost: an Operator Economy episode whose Canvas opens with "not for you unless you can design" is serving a narrower audience than the channel's promise. That does not fail a gate. It should temper enthusiasm for producing the episode ahead of candidates that serve the whole audience.
8. **Is there an operator journey, or a category critique?** As written, it is closer to a critique with a test attached. The narrative gate passes because the journey is *designed* — the request board, the sixth client, the cancellation month — but none of it has happened. The fixture's rule applies: one operator, one client, one request cycle must carry the episode, or it becomes a well-sourced essay about Designjoy and Design Pickle.
9. **Should the legacy episode have been published as it was?** Not under this standard. Its economics section, hook and two Shorts carried the self-reported revenue range with hedges; its thesis carried a Figma statistic that is not in Figma's filings; its outreach beat carried a benchmark that is not at its cited source. The V2 package excludes all three by ID. That is the correct disposition of the old material, and it is also the reason the fresh score is 9-16 points below the legacy screen's band.

## Shadow gate verdicts

- Evidence and analogy floor: **pass, narrowly** — the buyer pillar rests on an adjacent survey that does not isolate design plus the inference that a fifty-vendor category has buyers. A stricter reader could fail it; this reviewer passes it because the inference is one hop and the validation plan tests it directly.
- Operator Canvas feasibility: **pass** — all checks pass as a *test* blueprint; go to market and costly problem are the weak checks and are named.
- Narrative engine: **pass** — conditional on the episode following one operator and one client cycle, not the category.
- Audience pull: **pass, narrowly** — three proxy families, none measuring buyer demand for a solo subscription; the timely tension carries it.
- POV: **pass, bounded and thin** — nothing invented; every limit stated.
- Factual and source blockers: **clear for a test blueprint**; **blocked** for any statement of solo revenue, client count, churn, reply rate, or a Superside layoff percentage.
- Legal, permission, access, and guest blockers: **manageable** — cancellation terms must follow the current FTC/ROSCA posture; unsolicited demos stay private; client footage needs permission.

## Decision

Decision: **continue research** — conditional. If the 30-day test in the research brief is not actually run before the 2026-12-03 refresh date, this reviewer's disposition becomes **parked**: the package cannot improve from the desk, and the channel has candidates that serve its whole audience.

Evidence class: **observed model** (offer verified; solo economics excluded)

Production promotion authorized: no

Reason: The package supports an honest episode about what subscription design actually sells and how to test whether one person can sell it. It does not support the episode that was published, and it does not yet support a build recommendation. Fifty-seven is the score of a real business with a real test and no results.
