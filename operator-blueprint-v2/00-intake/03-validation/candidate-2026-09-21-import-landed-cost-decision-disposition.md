## Reason for stopping

- Failed or incomplete requirement: business fit, permission and legal boundary, economics, evidence
- Plain-English finding: The work splits cleanly in two, and the operator can hold neither half.
  Classification and valuation for another party for compensation are customs business requiring a
  broker's license, and the definition also reaches activities *relating to* the preparation of
  documents intended to be filed with CBP; the license exceptions do not include a paid third-party
  adviser. What is left after the regulated work is removed is the landed-cost calculation, which is
  already productised and priced per order. No accessed source shows anyone paying an independent
  operator for the residual decision layer. Completing the analogy map, scorecard, Canvas gate, and
  editorial-potential artifacts would spend four more artifacts scoring a business whose scope is
  undefined, and would produce a number that looks like a judgment.
- Claims or assumptions that remain unusable:
  - That an unlicensed operator may lawfully sell any part of this. Unresolved, and the regulation
    text points the other way.
  - That "assemble the classification evidence and let the broker file" is outside customs business.
    This is the framing the surrounding merchant guidance implies, and it is exactly what the
    definition's "activities relating to the preparation" language may capture.
  - That the corporate-compliance carve-out helps. It is defined for a **related** business entity.
  - That any advertised broker or software price is a market price or a completed sale.
  - The circulating parcel-volume and landed-cost percentage figures. No primary attribution was
    located; they must not appear in any script.
  - That the Etsy DDP requirement is a why-now signal for this buyer. It binds non-US sellers
    shipping into the US, not the US importer of record.
  - Any duty-saving, customs-outcome, compliance, or penalty-avoidance promise.

## Adversarial editorial finding

The strongest alternative explanation is that this is not a business at all, but a regulated task
with a free marketing front end. Forwarders give classification advisory away to win the freight,
duty software calculates the landed cost at checkout for $2 plus 10% of duties, and the buyer's
accountant already owns the margin question. The rule change created a great deal of work and put
none of it within reach of an unlicensed operator.

The structural failure is the same one recorded against the AI phone-answering candidate on
2026-09-03: incumbents absorb the product, the residual is a thin decision layer, and no evidence
exists that anyone pays for the residual.

The strongest editorial risk is specific to this candidate and worse than usual. An episode that
made this look like an available operator business could route viewers into penalty exposure under
19 U.S.C. 1641. The topic is only publishable as an examination of where the boundary sits, and only
with a qualified reviewer on the record.

What survives if the most attractive claim is removed: strip the "new operator business" claim and
the episode still has a verified rule change, a real merchant decision, and an unusually clean
visual story about who is allowed to answer a question. That is a strong explainer. It is not a
blueprint, and the channel's format is the blueprint.
