# Step 0 evidence and transfer standard

Status: approved V2 Step 0.2 rule; locked 2026-08-21.

Template version: `operator-blueprint-v2-step0.2`

This standard allows Operator Economy to examine new business models without pretending that novelty is proof. An exact precedent is useful but not required. The package may synthesize a business from verified neighboring models when every transfer is explicit, limited, and testable.

## Two separate questions

Every evidence record answers both questions:

1. **Is the source-supported fact true?**
2. **How directly does that fact support the proposed business?**

A source can verify a fact about SEO agencies, for example, without proving that a new AI-visibility agency will have the same demand, price, margin, or outcome.

## Claim status

Every material claim is labeled as one of:

- `verified` - directly supported by a suitable source.
- `qualified model` - calculated from stated inputs and presented as a scenario, not an observed result or forecast.
- `hypothesis` - a proposition to test, not a fact to state.
- `not usable` - missing, contradictory, inaccessible, too weak, or materially out of date.

`Not usable` evidence cannot pass a hard gate. A hypothesis can support a validation plan, but cannot be stated publicly as an observed fact.

## Evidence relationship

Record one relationship between each verified fact and the proposed business:

- `direct` - evidence about the same buyer, problem, offer, and delivery model.
- `adjacent` - evidence about a neighboring model with a substantially similar buyer, job, budget, or workflow.
- `component` - evidence that one required part works, such as measurement technology, audit delivery, pricing mechanics, or a sales channel.
- `context only` - useful background that does not support feasibility.

Evidence does not become stronger because several weak articles repeat the same unsupported number.

## Analogy transfer test

Every adjacent or component analogy used to support feasibility must record:

- the exact business component it supports;
- the verified source claim IDs behind it;
- the shared structural feature, such as buyer, job, budget, workflow, or outcome;
- the adaptation required for the proposed model;
- what the analogy does not prove;
- the condition that would break the transfer;
- transfer confidence: high / medium / low;
- verdict: valid / weak / rejected.

A valid analogy supports one named bridge. It does not prove the entire proposed business. A load-bearing conclusion may not depend on more than one untested inference hop.

## Minimum evidence floor

A novel or composite candidate can pass without an exact precedent only when all of the following are true:

- At least one usable direct or adjacent source establishes a real buyer problem, existing spend, or consequential behavior.
- At least three valid analogy records come from at least two independent source families.
- Valid evidence covers the buyer/problem, offer/delivery, and economics/capacity pillars, plus at least one of budget/current alternative, go to market, or outcome measurement.
- Any uncovered pillar is explicitly modeled or hypothetical and can be tested through the bounded validation plan before substantial investment.
- No essential conclusion relies on a label-only comparison, a distant analogy, or a chain of speculative transfers.

Passing this floor means the synthesis is reasonable enough to teach as a clearly labeled blueprint. It does not mean the business, revenue, or customer outcome is proven.

## Source record

Each load-bearing claim records:

- a stable claim ID;
- source title, publisher, and URL or repository path;
- publication date and access date;
- exact page, table, section, paragraph, or timestamp locator;
- local snapshot or archive path and SHA-256 when permitted and practical;
- source type, confidence, and evidence relationship;
- caveat, contradiction, and refresh date when time-sensitive.

## Buyer problem and willingness to pay

The exact proposed solution may be new. The underlying problem cannot be invented. Evidence must identify a buyer with a recurring job, cost, risk, missed opportunity, or existing category of spend.

Market size, funding, attention, and product launches may demonstrate context or why-now conditions. Alone, they do not establish willingness to pay for the proposed offer.

## Operator feasibility

Evidence that an individual or small team could deliver the proposed work may be direct, adjacent, or component-level. It can include:

- a disclosed engagement or bounded first-party result;
- a comparable service workflow;
- verified tool capability and cost;
- observed marketplace pricing with clear limits;
- a transparent time-and-capacity model;
- required expertise that a defined operator already has or can realistically acquire.

Salary is not service pricing. Revenue is not profit or take-home income. Tool capability is not customer demand. A platform's suggested earnings are not operator results.

## Scaled relevance

Scaled relevance can be established through the underlying problem, buyer behavior, category spending, transactions, adoption, or a durable market change. It does not require a scaled company selling the exact composite offer.

Funding and valuation may establish investor interest or infrastructure scale. They do not prove customer demand, unit economics, or small-operator feasibility.

## Modeled economics and earnings language

A modeled business case must show its formula and assumptions. At minimum, record:

- assumed price and pricing unit;
- client or transaction capacity;
- utilization or retention assumption when relevant;
- delivery hours and loaded labor cost;
- software, contractor, acquisition, and material overhead assumptions;
- gross revenue and contribution margin before owner compensation and tax;
- which inputs are observed, transferred, modeled, or unknown.

Prefer capacity statements such as: “At four clients paying $2,500 per month, the model produces $120,000 in annual gross revenue before labor, software, acquisition costs, taxes, and churn.”

Every such statement must be labeled `modeled scenario, not observed performance or an earnings forecast`. Do not use “you will make,” “expected income,” or a scenario presented as a likely outcome.

Before public release, every stated number still requires entry and approval under `content-os/facts.md`. Step 0 calculations do not create release authority.

## Source hierarchy and caveats

Prefer primary documents, official datasets, filings, direct disclosures, and first-party artifacts that actually support the claim. High-quality secondary reporting may be used with attribution. Vendor or self-published sources can verify their own current features or published prices, but require corroboration when used for market demand, customer outcomes, or industry-wide economics.

Conflicting credible sources remain visible. Do not silently select the more attractive number.

## Currentness

Prices, search volumes, product capabilities, platform policies, affiliate terms, funding, customer counts, and regulations are time-sensitive. Record a refresh date appropriate to the claim. A passed refresh date makes the promotion package stale until the affected claims are rechecked.

## Non-waivable boundary

An owner override cannot turn missing, misleading, inaccessible, fabricated, contradicted, or structurally invalid evidence into usable evidence. It also cannot turn a modeled scenario into observed performance.
