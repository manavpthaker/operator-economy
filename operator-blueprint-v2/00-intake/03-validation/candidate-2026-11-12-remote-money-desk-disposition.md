## Reason for stopping

This record does not stop the candidate. It records why the next artifacts wait for one more desk
pass.

- Failed or incomplete requirement: evidence (delivery boundary and scale), not business fit.
- Plain-English finding: The lead's claim that no remote monthly service exists was wrong. Parent
  Bill Pay sells the job remotely from $250 a month and SilverBills sells concierge bill pay. That
  makes this an observed model, not a gap in the market, and moves the episode's question from "is
  this possible" to "what do agents add, and will families trust a remote desk". The analogy map
  and Canvas would be premature until the state authority rules, bank access rules, and a primary
  prevalence source are read.
- Claims or assumptions that remain unusable: every scout-ledger item not re-read (CLM-009); any
  prevalence or scam-loss number; household capacity of 60–80; any statement that families pay a
  remote desk.

Re-entry items from 2026-11-12:

1. State rules: partly done. Abuse reporting read for Texas and Florida; in both, any person must
   report (CLM-014). No power of attorney statute read. California official text blocked.
2. Bank access: done at the federal level. View-only access is a voluntary bank feature
   (CLM-013). A shared login makes the desk's transfers "authorized" and the parent liable
   (CLM-012). Which banks offer view-only access is not measured.
3. Primary prevalence: done for scams (FBI IC3 2025, CLM-015). No primary count of missed bills.
4. CLM-009 re-read: blocked. AgingCare returned 403 and Exa refused for lack of credits. Open.

Findings that change the candidate:

- A daily money manager that takes client money into its own account is a money transmitter
  (FinCEN FIN-2012-R004, CLM-010). The desk must never hold or pool client money.
- The desk must not pay with a parent's login or card (CLM-012). The offer becomes "prepare,
  check, call, record", with the parent or their legal agent making each payment.
- Daily account watching already sells as software for $7.49 to $26.99 a month (CLM-016). The
  first pass's "the agent change is in the watching" is withdrawn.
- New buyer voice: an out-of-town child asks what security checks a hired household manager
  would have; another family praises a bonded agency's quarterly accounting to everyone
  (CLM-017, CLM-018). Anecdote only.

Evidence pass (same agent as the adversarial pass; not independent): seven new claims from federal
and state primary sources, one seller page, two Reddit anecdotes. Texas statute read through a
mirror. Nothing here shows that families pay a remote desk.

Adversarial editorial pass: with the watching bought for $20 and the payment left to the family,
what remains is sorting mail, checking bills, and phone calls. That is the labor agents shrink
least, and it may not be worth $250 a month to a family. The episode is only worth making if the
legal boundary itself is the lesson ("why you must not be the one who pays"). If Parent Bill Pay
and SilverBills pay from their own accounts, the observed model sits inside the FinCEN ruling and
the episode cannot hold it up as the pattern. Two desk episodes in a row (after EP010) remains a
repetition risk.

Next bench desk items, in order: (1) how Parent Bill Pay and SilverBills move the money; (2)
whether paying from the parent's own account under a POA is outside state money transmitter law,
two state sources; (3) one POA statute; (4) three banks' view-only access pages; (5) CLM-009
re-read when AgingCare is readable; then the analogy map. If (1) and (2) show the only lawful
desk is "prepare, don't pay" and no seller offers that at a monthly price, the bench should
consider parking with that as the stated reason.
