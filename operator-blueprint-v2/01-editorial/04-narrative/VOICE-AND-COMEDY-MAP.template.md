## Failure-mode review

- [ ] The cold open is an ordinary situation, not an abstract thesis.
- [ ] The narrator begins curious and earns authority through the report.
- [ ] Silent identity sting, fixed brand string, and episode promise are distinct.
- [ ] The short spoken company name and one-sentence definition are clear when heard once.
- [ ] The precise internal definition does not become a repeated public category label.
- [ ] A proposed editorial category name is not spoken as proof of established market demand.
- [ ] The viewer receives the normal system and change before the main ruling.
- [ ] The primary calibration is reporting plus plainspoken curiosity, not a comedy impression.
- [ ] Modern MBA contributes business context without controlling information density.
- [ ] Last Week Tonight contributes steelman, reveal, and callback without controlling joke rate.
- [ ] Manav's patterns feel natural rather than inserted.
- [ ] The governing analogy maps cleanly and develops.
- [ ] Humor follows comprehension.
- [ ] Contempt targets a system or incentive rather than a person.
- [ ] Evidence boundaries do not become a running apology.
- [ ] The script has conviction after context rather than a verdict before the case.
- [ ] The opportunity case and operator blueprint receive roughly equal narrative attention.
- [ ] The complete company is named before the first offer narrows the scope.
- [ ] The opportunity-to-build and mature-company-to-wedge transitions sound conversational rather than administrative.
- [ ] The first 30 days begin construction rather than another round of general consideration.
- [ ] The final episode ruling is BUILD, while client-level no-build or refusal decisions remain available where responsible.
- [ ] Short lines do not create a punch-line metronome.
- [ ] Removing episode-specific nouns would break the plan.
- [ ] The ending resolves or reinterprets the opening.
