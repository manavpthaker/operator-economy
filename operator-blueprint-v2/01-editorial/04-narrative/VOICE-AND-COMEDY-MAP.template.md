## Failure-mode review

- [ ] The cold open is an ordinary situation that teases a specific operator or business payoff, not an abstract or fully argued thesis.
- [ ] The narrator begins curious and earns authority through the report.
- [ ] Silent identity sting, fixed brand string, and episode promise are distinct.
- [ ] The pre-sting payoff is clear enough to create a reason to stay while the mechanism, proof, and final ruling remain unspent.
- [ ] The short spoken company name and one-sentence definition are clear when heard once.
- [ ] The precise internal definition does not become a repeated public category label.
- [ ] A proposed editorial category name is not spoken as proof of established market demand.
- [ ] The viewer receives the normal system and change before the main ruling.
- [ ] The primary calibration is reporting plus plainspoken curiosity, not a comedy impression.
- [ ] Business context explains the operating system without controlling information density.
- [ ] Steelman, reveal, and callback functions strengthen the argument without controlling joke rate.
- [ ] Manav's patterns feel natural rather than inserted.
- [ ] The positive hosted-voice plan covers opening, opportunity case, operator build, economics, and ending with episode-specific lexical jobs.
- [ ] No row relies on zero banned words, clean punctuation, smooth cadence, or lack of imitation as proof of voice identity.
- [ ] The plan uses observed functions without forcing catchphrases, contractions, self-repairs, jokes, or borrowed mannerisms.
- [ ] The governing analogy maps cleanly and develops.
- [ ] Humor follows comprehension.
- [ ] Contempt targets a system or incentive rather than a person.
- [ ] Evidence boundaries do not become a running apology.
- [ ] Material evidence uses conversational VO, on-screen source-receipt, and full show-note layers without turning the narration into a bibliography.
- [ ] The viewer is told to select a first market from relevant experience, relationships, earned access, buyer understanding, and reachability; the episode's example industry is not universalized.
- [ ] The script has conviction after context rather than a verdict before the case.
- [ ] The opportunity pitch receives 52 to 58 percent of the spoken script unless an approved exception is recorded.
- [ ] The scale argument moves through costly behavior, adjacent budget, comparable operating layers, and operator synthesis without turning funding or company revenue into demand proof.
- [ ] A horizontal opportunity has a precise qualified-buyer rule and no more than three explanatory cases.
- [ ] No example industry defines the cold open, company, total market, prospect denominator, or economics.
- [ ] The complete company is named before the first offer narrows the scope.
- [ ] The opportunity-to-build and mature-company-to-wedge transitions sound conversational rather than administrative.
- [ ] The first 30 days begin construction rather than another round of general consideration.
- [ ] The final episode ruling is BUILD, while client-level no-build or refusal decisions remain available where responsible.
- [ ] Short lines do not create a punch-line metronome.
- [ ] The same discourse marker does not open multiple scenes.
- [ ] Every adjacent narrated sequence inherits and advances a specific noun, question, decision, image, or consequence rather than resetting like a deck heading.
- [ ] Removing episode-specific nouns would break the plan.
- [ ] The ending resolves or reinterprets the opening and delivers the payoff teased before the sting.
- [ ] One natural like-and-subscribe sentence is the final spoken beat and follows the BUILD verdict and first construction action without stacking asks.
- [ ] The live hosted-long-form Content OS routing conflict is recorded as unresolved or linked to an authorized resolution.
