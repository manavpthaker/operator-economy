# Editorial contract: workflow-reliability service

Status: approved for dry-run testing only

Episode: unassigned fixture

## Viewer

Primary viewer: An experienced operations, product, RevOps, implementation, or systems generalist considering whether their workflow skill can become a bounded client service.

Starting state: The viewer thinks the product is automation—connecting two apps and charging for the build.

Ending capability: The viewer can identify one valuable handoff, map its states and exceptions, decide what remains human, define a reliable service scope, model the labor, and run a paid validation test.

## Episode job

Working promise: Show how to turn one broken business handoff into a tested workflow service—including what not to automate and how to know whether the work is worth selling.

Central argument: The product is not an automation. It is a reliable operating state with a baseline, owner, test record, exception path, documentation, and measurable handoff.

Narrative question: Can an operations-minded independent take one recurring client-onboarding failure inside a small agency and turn it into a safe, measurable service rather than a fragile demo?

Why now: Smaller firms are adopting more productivity software while partner ecosystems formalize hands-on automation services. More tools increase both opportunity and handoff risk.

First responsible action: Interview owners in one reachable service segment and observe the workflow before choosing a tool or offering implementation.

## Point of view

Common coverage mistake: Selling tool fluency, generic “AI agency” positioning, and attractive recurring revenue before establishing one valuable production workflow.

Operator Economy view: Automation is useful only when it reduces real operating work, exposes failure, preserves human judgment, and can be handed back to the client.

What would change the recommendation: No recurring consequential handoff, no buyer willingness to authorize and fund discovery, unsafe or inaccessible systems, unmanageable exceptions, or support obligations that break the capacity model.

## Scope

Must answer: operator fit, buyer, workflow selection, current state, offer, delivery, human controls, stack by capability, first-buyer path, economics, access and privacy, maintenance boundary, measures, and 30-day test.

Out of scope: a Zapier or n8n tutorial, broad “AI agency” setup, historical EP003 economics, final packaging, scene direction, or tool-specific implementation instructions.

## Truth contract

The service category exists. This particular sprint, buyer segment, onboarding workflow, price, hours, capacity, contribution, and buyer result are modeled and unproven.
