## Base relationship and point of view

- Base relationship: a seasoned operations strategist shows one capable operator how to turn automation theater into a responsible reliability service.
- Central thesis: the normal flow is the demo; failure handling is the product.
- Common bad framing: the operator sells connections between apps and adds a retainer because recurring revenue sounds attractive.
- Steelman: workflow tools make stable multi-step automation accessible, and many teams genuinely benefit from them.
- Ruling: the operator earns a fee only by owning discovery, exception design, testing, observability, documentation, recovery, and handoff.
- Disputable line: if you only tested the happy path, you did not build an automation; you recorded a screen demo.
- Operator decision: sell discovery before architecture, build only after a pass decision, and refuse a retainer without a recurring duty.

## Craft functions

- First-principles reasoning: a handoff moves client state from one owner to the next; reliability requires visible states, bounded rules, exception ownership, recovery, and buyer acceptance.
- Observational delivery: a proposal is signed while sales, operations, finance, project delivery, and the client each believe a different state is true.
- Escalation: separate tools, separate signals, manual translation, hidden failure, client waiting, nobody owning recovery.
- Opinionated spine: happy-path automation and fees without responsibility are the targets.
- Research-and-comedy engine: administrative burden establishes the environment, vendor programs establish the service category, the “group project” analogy exposes fragmented ownership, and the operator receives a diagnostic, build gate, failure matrix, economic stress test, and kill conditions.

## Governing analogy

Analogy family: group project.

| Mapping | Analogy | Business mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| What moves | the shared assignment | client information and responsibility moving across systems |
| What blocks it | separate files, unclear owner, missing handoff | fragmented state, access, rules, and exception ownership |
| Who owns the consequence | the person waiting for the final submission | the client and buyer waiting for acknowledged kickoff ownership |
| What changes | one shared definition of done and a named next owner | visible state, acceptance event, recovery owner, and runbook |

- First use: every tool completes its own slide while nobody assembles the deck.
- Callback 1: the diagnostic finds the missing shared assignment and names the owner.
- Callback 2: failure testing checks what happens when a teammate is absent, submits twice, or loses access.
- Final resolution: monitoring is paid only when someone remains responsible for keeping the project working.
- Excluded metaphors: plumbing, relay races, toll roads, kitchens, and generic software metaphors.

- Target of contempt: software theater, happy-path demos presented as systems, and retainers without continuing responsibility.
- Protected people: staff manually holding the process together, buyers with messy systems, clients waiting for service, and under-resourced teams.
- Humor tools: precise operational absurdity, group-project callback, broken pattern, undercut, escalation, and deadpan verdict.
- Calm passage: KfW, UK government, Zapier, and n8n evidence.
