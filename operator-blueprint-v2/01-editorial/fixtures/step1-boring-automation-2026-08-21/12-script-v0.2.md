# Script v0.2: workflow-reliability service

Status: integrated dry-run candidate; production lock prohibited

Episode: unassigned fixture

## S01: The client signed. Then nothing happened.

### Narration

A client signs a proposal with a small agency.

Sales marks the deal complete. Operations waits for an intake form. Finance has not created the invoice. The project manager does not know the kickoff needs to be scheduled.

The client thinks the work has started.

Inside the agency, five people are waiting for five different signals.

This is a hypothetical workflow. It is not a measured agency case.

But it shows the business clearly.

The service is not connecting two apps.

It is following one important handoff, exposing every missing state, deciding whether the process should be automated, and—only if it should—building something the client can test, observe, and own.

The product is not the automation.

The product is a reliable handoff.

## S02: Humans are carrying the state

### Narration

Smaller firms keep adding forms, calendars, CRMs, project tools, spreadsheets, inboxes, and billing systems.

Each tool knows one part of the client.

People carry the rest.

They copy the name. Re-enter the date. Notice the missing field. Remember that this account needs a different approval. Send the message that makes the next tool matter.

KfW reported that German SMEs spend an average of thirty-two hours a month on bureaucratic processes, at a cost equal to 3.9 percent of annual personnel costs.

That is broad administrative burden in Germany. It is not a measure of disconnected software, and none of those hours can be claimed as recoverable here.

UK government research separately found information gaps among small businesses adopting productivity technology, along with value in reliable, personalized support.

The opening is not missing software.

It is missing operating logic between the tools.

## S03: The category exists. The new operator is unproven.

### Narration

Exact service businesses and procurement paths exist.

Zapier operates a partner program for hands-on automation services.

n8n's expert-partner pilot describes selected agencies and systems integrators that earn their main revenue from automation and AI services and already serve at least three active customers.

A Zapier-published case also shows the delivery shape: cross-tool implementation, documentation, training, and support.

That proves the category is real.

It does not prove representative demand. It does not prove a new independent will win the work. And it says nothing reliable about the price, margin, or client result in this model.

So the first offer should not be a broad automation retainer.

It should be a paid diagnostic around one handoff.

## S04: Earn the right to build

### Narration

For our hypothetical agency, the diagnostic begins when the proposal is signed and follows the client until someone clearly owns the kickoff.

Observe the people doing the work.

Map the inputs, states, decisions, systems, credentials, exceptions, and consequences.

Choose one primary acceptance measure: time from signed proposal to acknowledged kickoff ownership.

Then ask whether the process deserves automation.

Are the rules stable? Is the handoff frequent enough to matter? Can access be bounded? Are exceptions understandable? Can the buyer measure the starting state? Does a named person own recovery?

If the answer is no, the diagnostic ends with a map and a recommendation not to build.

That is not a failed sale.

It is the first proof that the operator is selling judgment instead of software.

## S05: Build the minimum safe path

### Narration

If the handoff passes, build only the stable path.

Keep ambiguous, sensitive, and material decisions human. Use client-controlled accounts where practical, least-privilege access, and test data.

Then bring the signed client back through the system.

What happens when intake information is missing?

What happens when the same acceptance event arrives twice?

What happens when billing or the project system is unavailable?

The full test matrix belongs in the Canvas. The principle is simple: normal flow is not enough. Failure needs a visible state and a human owner.

Add logs and alerts. Decide when the system retries, when it stops, and who recovers it.

Train the team. Transfer the runbook and access inventory. Monitor for thirty days.

At the end, recommend maintain, expand, redesign, or retire.

## S06: The economics are a stress test

### Narration

Now the model.

It assumes a three-thousand-dollar fixed sprint, two new sprints per month, and twenty-four delivery hours for each one.

The cash view comes first.

Six thousand dollars of modeled gross revenue, less two hundred dollars of software and six hundred dollars of acquisition and listed overhead, leaves five thousand two hundred dollars before owner compensation and tax.

Then the economic view.

Assign forty-eight hours of operator labor at a modeled sixty dollars per hour. The modeled contribution becomes two thousand three hundred twenty dollars.

Now stress it.

If each sprint takes thirty-two hours, modeled contribution falls to one thousand three hundred sixty dollars.

Modeled scenario. Not observed performance. Not an earnings forecast.

The model survives only if the buyer pays for discovery, exception design, testing, documentation, and handoff—not merely a connection between tools.

## S07: Do not invent a retainer

### Narration

The attractive version of this business adds monthly revenue to every project.

The responsible version asks whether an ongoing job exists.

Did incidents occur during monitoring? Who responds? How quickly? Which vendor failures are included? What happens when the client changes a field, replaces a tool, or revokes access?

If the responsibility is real and the buyer will fund it, monitoring can become a separate offer.

If it is not, transfer ownership and stop billing.

Recurring revenue is not a reason to invent recurring responsibility.

And some processes should never enter a generalist pilot: health, employment, credit, insurance, legal, safety, or any workflow where an automated mistake creates a high-impact decision without qualified review.

## S08: Sell discovery before architecture

### Narration

The first thirty days are not for building an agency website.

Interview ten owners or operations leads in one reachable service segment.

Require a real process walk-through. Map three handoffs. Reject unstable rules, high-risk judgment, inaccessible systems, missing ownership, and anything without a measurable baseline.

Offer two paid diagnostics.

Seek one paid sprint at the predeclared price and scope only after the diagnostic says the build is responsible.

Track outreach, discovery, proposal, delivery, tests, failures, support, and buyer acceptance.

Continue only if buyers expose a recurring consequential handoff, authorize safe discovery, and one funds the full reliability job.

Stop or narrow if no one pays, the process cannot be bounded, or support breaks the model.

The first deliverable is not an automation.

It is evidence that one handoff deserves to be automated—and that someone will pay to make it reliable.
