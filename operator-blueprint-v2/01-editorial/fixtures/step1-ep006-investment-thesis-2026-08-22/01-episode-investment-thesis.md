## Show thesis

AI has lowered the cost of building and operating a small company. An experienced professional no longer needs a large team or a large share of the market to create useful owned work. The opportunity is to apply judgment and domain experience to one costly problem, use AI to reduce the setup and repetitive labor, and build a company that can support one accountable operator.

## Episode Investment Thesis

### Opportunity thesis

Independent hotels need OTAs for discovery, but too often they keep paying the platform to reach a guest the property has already served. The hotel technology market has invested heavily in booking infrastructure, while responsibility for the relationship after the stay remains fragmented. That creates an opening for a direct-booking recovery company that owns the path from first stay to permissioned direct return.

### Operator thesis

An experienced hospitality, revenue, growth, customer-experience, or operations professional can enter this market without building another hotel platform. AI and automation can reduce the labor involved in monitoring, drafting, organizing, and following up. The operator still owns consent, judgment, the guest experience, exception handling, measurement, and the relationship with the hotel.

### Build verdict

**BUILD.** The opportunity is large enough to support a focused company, the buyer and economic pain are identifiable, the high-end infrastructure is established, and one hotel provides a bounded entry point. Build the company around direct-booking recovery. Enter through one measurable post-stay return loop, prove that the work changes a buyer-relevant result, then expand only into responsibilities the operator can continue to own.

The missing public price is a design assignment for the first paid pilot. It is not a reason to shrink the company back into an audit.

This is a recommendation to build the company, not a claim that a particular income, conversion lift, client count, or payback period is established.

## The company being pitched

Working category: direct-booking recovery company

Primary operator: an experienced hospitality, revenue, growth, customer-experience, or operations professional who can sell and manage the initial service personally

Primary buyer: owner or general manager of an independent hotel

End customer: a guest who first found the property through an OTA and has a reason to return

Owned outcome: a larger share of eligible returning guests reserving directly through a permissioned, measurable relationship the hotel controls

Company promise:

> We help independent hotels turn a first stay acquired through an OTA into a guest relationship the property can responsibly earn back and operate directly.

The company is not:

- another booking engine;
- a promise to eliminate Booking or Expedia;
- generic hotel marketing;
- an automated guest-message factory;
- a promise of direct-booking growth; or
- a one-time software installation presented as a company.

## Why the opportunity exists

### The useful incumbent

OTAs provide discovery, comparison, translated listings, paid marketing, and checkout reach that many independent properties could not recreate alone. The first booking can be useful paid acquisition.

### The structural change

Hotel systems can now handle more of the booking, communication, data movement, monitoring, and drafting that previously required more staff or a broader agency. The tools reduce the operating burden. They do not assign responsibility for earning the next direct reservation.

### The unowned gap

At a small property, the website, front desk, booking system, guest list, reviews, offers, missed calls, and post-stay communication are often handled as separate jobs. The guest experiences one hotel. The hotel operates several disconnected handoffs. No one consistently owns the full return path or the measurement of whether it worked.

### The economic opening

The operator does not need to replace the OTA or capture the hotel software market. The company needs a reachable portfolio of independent properties for which repeat direct reservations are valuable enough to justify accountable outside ownership of the recovery system.

## Big case to small capture

### What the high end proves

- Hotel booking and operating infrastructure is valuable.
- Independent-hotel demand moves through large platforms and software systems.
- Buyers already spend money around reservations, revenue management, distribution, and guest operations.

### What the high end does not ask the operator to do

- Build another property-management system.
- Compete with Mews, Cloudbeds, Booking, or Expedia.
- Raise venture capital.
- Win a large percentage of the hotel market.

### What the business of one captures

- The responsibility between installed systems.
- The judgment required to make the direct path trustworthy.
- The permissioned return relationship after checkout.
- The measurement and recovery work a small hotel does not consistently own.

## Aspirational company and entry wedge

### Aspirational business of one

A portfolio-based outsourced retention function for independent hotels. The company operates the direct-return system across the guest journey, maintains the responsibilities the hotel has delegated, reports the agreed outcome, and earns ongoing fees only where an ongoing duty exists.

### Entry wedge

One independent hotel, one defined eligible returning-guest segment, and one post-stay recovery loop:

```text
completed stay
→ valid permission and usable guest record
→ relevant post-stay communication
→ reason to return
→ direct booking path
→ attributable direct reservation or documented non-conversion
```

The first purchase is a paid baseline and recovery-loop installation. Continued operation is a separate decision based on measurable responsibility and buyer value.

### Expansion ladder

1. Post-stay return loop.
2. Missed-call and abandoned-direct-path recovery.
3. Direct-channel measurement and ongoing offer calendar.
4. Referral, review, and repeat-guest programs.
5. Portfolio-level outsourced direct-booking and retention operation.

Each step requires proof that the prior responsibility works, can be measured, respects permission, and can be supported by one accountable operator.

## Operator advantage

The operator's advantage is not access to software. It is knowing what a good guest experience, credible offer, safe permission boundary, useful hotel report, and recoverable handoff look like.

AI may assist with:

- organizing discovery notes and guest-journey evidence;
- drafting property-specific messages for human approval;
- classifying exceptions and missing information;
- preparing test cases and reports;
- monitoring agreed workflow states; and
- reducing repetitive coordination across a small portfolio.

The operator retains:

- consent and permission decisions;
- property access and data boundaries;
- final message and offer approval;
- exception and recovery judgment;
- attribution and economic interpretation;
- hotel-owner communication; and
- accountability for the service result.

## Investment case

### What the operator commits to build

- A clear company category and buyer promise.
- A baseline audit tied to one hotel decision.
- A working post-stay return loop.
- A direct path tested as a guest would experience it.
- A scorecard that separates activity from buyer-relevant outcomes.
- A repeatable implementation and support boundary.
- A first-customer acquisition motion grounded in reachable independent hotels.

### First 30 days, modeled build phase

These are operating targets, not observed market outcomes:

1. Select one reachable independent-hotel segment.
2. Hold ten owner or general-manager discovery conversations.
3. Complete two evidence-based baseline audits.
4. Sell one paid recovery-loop pilot with client-controlled access and an agreed acceptance measure.
5. Build only the minimum system required to operate and measure that loop.

### First 90 days, modeled proof phase

1. Complete the paid pilot and document its baseline, actions, exceptions, and measured result.
2. Decide whether ongoing operation buys the hotel a real continuing responsibility.
3. Convert the pilot into an ongoing service only if the buyer accepts the measured value and support boundary.
4. Add a second property only after the first delivery can be repeated without hiding owner labor.
5. Use actual delivery time, acquisition effort, tool costs, and support load to replace the provisional economics.

### Required-share logic

Exact client count cannot be claimed from the current evidence because public per-property pricing is absent and adjacent salary data is not client revenue. The business thesis remains credible because the buyer problem is measurable, the market infrastructure is established, and the entry loop can be sold and tested independently. The first build phase must determine the price, delivery capacity, and number of properties required to support the operator before the company claims sustainable economics.

## The hard part

The hard part is not connecting tools. It is earning permission, winning hotel-owner trust, proving contribution through seasonality and channel noise, and continuing to own the exceptions after the first installation looks complete.

If the operator cannot establish a baseline, secure permission, define attribution, reach owners, or support failures without consuming the economics, the company has not yet earned expansion.
