## Plain-English verdict

The gate rejected the right thing.

It did not reject a large ambition. It rejected a weak explanation of that ambition.

The failed version tried to preserve scope by discussing every buyer, use case, offer, tool class, commercial model, risk, and expansion stage. That made it complete like a pitch deck but inert as a story.

The repaired version starts with one signed-client handoff, proves the diagnostic and build decision, and then asks what evidence would earn the next workflow, a managed-reliability duty, and eventually a productized layer. The full operations-reliability company remains intact and appears as the final aspirational case.

## Rule gap exposed

Proposed v0.2 required causality and a recurring thread, so it could reject the flat draft. It did not explicitly protect the distinction between:

- Narrowing the episode's entry point.
- Narrowing the business's long-term scope.

Without that distinction, a future editor could either flatten the story to preserve every possibility or overcorrect by deleting the larger ambition.

## Proposed v0.3 amendments

- Added aspirational business, entry wedge, scope invariant, expansion ladder, and expansion proof to the Operator Canvas.
- Added entry-wedge and aspirational-destination fields to the editorial contract.
- Required Gate E3 to preserve the full business scope conditionally when it exceeds the initial offer.
- Required Gate E4 to make the wedge causal without mistaking it for the ceiling.
- Added a narrative check that future offers appear as earned stages rather than one simultaneous package.
- Added script and lock checks that retain aspiration without claiming inevitable expansion.
- Explicitly positioned the larger business as a strong final case once the first mechanism is understood.
