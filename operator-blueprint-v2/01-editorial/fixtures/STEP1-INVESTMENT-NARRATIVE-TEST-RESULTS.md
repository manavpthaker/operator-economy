## Owner-approved v1.4 naming calibration

The owner accepted the investment-narrative recovery in both controls and locked a shorter public naming system:

| Control | Display label | Public and spoken company name | Internal operating description | Plain explanation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| GEO | AI Visibility | AI visibility company | AI visibility-intelligence function | Helps brands understand how they appear in AI answers and decide what deserves action. |

The display label is for titles, navigation, and compact visual references. The company name is the only category label intended for future public or spoken use. The longer term is permitted only when an internal document needs a precise operating description. The six V0.6 files below remain frozen as the exact language tested before this naming decision. Their uses of `AI visibility-intelligence company` and `business-reliability company` are historical test language, not approved future public or spoken terminology. Future editorial work must apply the v1.4 names without rewriting the frozen evidence.

## Owner-directed failure

V0.5 solved the earlier opening, orientation, brand, context, first-listen, and voice failures. It still framed each episode too close to the first offer:

- GEO became an episode about selling a fixed diagnostic.
- Boring automation became an episode about fixing one handoff through a diagnostic and sprint.

Both are credible entrances. Neither is large enough to carry the whole Operator Economy promise.

The missing layer was an Episode Investment Thesis that answers, in order:

1. What changed in the market?
2. Why is the problem valuable enough to build around?
3. What complete company belongs in the gap?
4. Why can one experienced operator enter now using AI?
5. What is the first inspectable purchase?
6. How does that purchase grow into the mature business of one?
7. What must the first paid work prove about distribution, labor, price, capacity, and support?
8. Is the editorial verdict to build?

The episode should spend approximately half its time earning belief in the opportunity and half showing how the viewer begins building the company.

## Control hypothesis

A successful recovery must preserve five distinct claims:

| Layer | Required job |
|---|---|
| Show thesis | A capable professional can use AI to build, own, and operate a sustainable business of one without needing a large market share. |
| Opportunity thesis | Name the market change, costly gap, and complete company worth building. |
| Operator thesis | Explain why the viewer's experience plus AI-enabled labor reduction creates a credible way in. |
| Entry-wedge thesis | Define the first purchase without shrinking the mature company to that purchase. |
| Build verdict | Recommend construction affirmatively while preserving evidence, safety, and economics limits. |

`BUILD` is an editorial recommendation, not permission to publish a weak candidate. Production eligibility, claim integrity, and owner approval remain separate gates.

## What the test proves

- The same investment-narrative model works on two materially different opportunity types.
- A larger company thesis can coexist with a bounded and responsible first offer.
- The opportunity half can make the market feel meaningful without importing venture-scale goals into a business of one.
- The blueprint half can cover buyer, delivery, distribution, economics, expansion, risk, and immediate construction without turning the episode into a checklist.
- A confident build recommendation can preserve modeled economics, unknown demand, truth gates, and no-promise language.
- GEO and boring automation no longer peak at their diagnostic or sprint.

## What the test does not prove

- It does not prove either company has demand, accepted pricing, repeat purchase, or sustainable economics.
- It does not approve either fixture for production.
- It does not assign an episode number or create a V2 episode.
- It does not authorize a complete script, narration, visual translation, production, or publication.
- It establishes `AI visibility company` and `workflow operations company` as the owner-approved public and spoken names for future Step 1 work. It does not establish either phrase as an externally validated market category.
- The frozen regression remains evidence for the owner-approved Step 1 v1.4 calibration; it is not production authority.
- It does not make the separate EP006 exercise an authoritative test case.

## Owner decision

The owner accepted four substantive findings from the six frozen V0.6 artifacts:

1. Each first half makes the larger opportunity feel worth committing to before the offer appears.
2. Each mature company feels aspirational enough while remaining operable as a business of one.
3. Each diagnostic or sprint is a credible entrance rather than a replacement thesis.
4. Each second half provides enough operating detail to make `BUILD` useful rather than merely motivational.

The owner rejected only the length of the provisional company names, then approved the v1.4 naming calibration above. `AI visibility-intelligence company` and `business-reliability company` remain visible only inside the frozen V0.6 artifacts as historical test language.
