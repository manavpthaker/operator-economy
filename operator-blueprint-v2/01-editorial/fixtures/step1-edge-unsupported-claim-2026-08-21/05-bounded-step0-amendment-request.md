## Request R-E5-001: annual onboarding cost

Proposed public claim: “Across small agencies, broken onboarding quietly costs more than one hundred thousand dollars a year.”

Missing evidence: A defined small-agency population, onboarding-failure definition, annual cost components, measurement method, sample, geography, time period, and support for the stated threshold.

Narrow research question: Is there current, credible evidence that a defined class of small marketing or creative agencies incurs more than $100,000 in annual cost specifically from broken client onboarding?

Minimum acceptable support: Direct study or transparent primary dataset with relevant population and cost methodology. Broad administrative burden, vendor marketing, or an isolated case is insufficient.

Decision if evidence is absent: Remove the claim.

## Request R-E5-002: majority willingness to pay and price floor

Proposed public claim: “Most small agencies will pay at least three thousand dollars for a reliable handoff.”

Missing evidence: Representative buyer sample, exact offer, purchase context, willingness-to-pay method, agency definition, and support for both majority prevalence and $3,000 floor.

Narrow research question: Does representative evidence show that more than half of a defined small-agency segment will pay at least $3,000 for the bounded reliability sprint described in the Canvas?

Minimum acceptable support: Relevant primary buyer research or observed transaction data matching the offer. Partner directories, listed prices, vendor cases, or interviews without purchase behavior are insufficient.

Decision if evidence is absent: Remove the claim and retain willingness to pay as a validation question.

## Request R-E5-003: market price and initial sales pace

Proposed public claim: “A three-thousand-dollar sprint is a conservative market price, and two new sprints per month is a realistic starting pace.”

Missing evidence: Comparable scoped transactions, price distribution, new-operator acquisition data, sales-cycle definition, conversion basis, and delivery-capacity context.

Narrow research question: Are $3,000 and two new monthly sprints supported as conservative or typical for a new independent selling this exact reliability offer?

Minimum acceptable support: Comparable observed transactions and acquisition evidence. Adjacent service pricing may inform a model but cannot establish typicality.

Decision if evidence is absent: Restore the current modeled-scenario language.
