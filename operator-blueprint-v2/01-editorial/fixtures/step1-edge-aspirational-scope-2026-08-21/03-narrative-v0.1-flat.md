# Narrative v0.1: the automation operating company

Status: deliberately broad Gate E4 candidate

## Story in one sentence

Small businesses use too many disconnected systems, creating an opportunity for an operator to sell diagnostics, automation, monitoring, governance, and eventually a software-enabled operating layer.

## Viewer movement

Starting belief: Automation agencies connect software.

Ending understanding: A more ambitious agency can provide an end-to-end operating layer across a small business.

Decision the viewer should be equipped to make: Whether to build an operations-automation company.

## Broad narrative arc

### S01: The fragmented small business

Establish administrative burden, productivity-tool adoption, and the number of disconnected systems inside a typical service company.

### S02: The market opportunity

Explain partner ecosystems, automation consultants, systems integrators, implementation providers, marketplaces, and the growing accessibility of AI and workflow tools.

### S03: The offer portfolio

Describe process diagnostics, workflow builds, documentation, staff training, monitoring, support retainers, governance reviews, and eventually reusable software.

### S04: Buyers and use cases

Cover marketing agencies, professional services, accounting firms, recruiting firms, hospitality businesses, healthcare-adjacent administration, and other tool-heavy companies. Give examples across sales, onboarding, project delivery, billing, reporting, and customer service.

### S05: Delivery model

Walk through discovery, process mapping, architecture, implementation, testing, deployment, monitoring, training, and optimization.

### S06: Technology stack

Explain workflow engines, APIs, AI models, databases, forms, CRMs, project systems, observability tools, access controls, and documentation platforms.

### S07: Go to market

Cover referrals, marketplaces, platform partner programs, outbound prospecting, vertical specialization, audits, free workflow maps, paid diagnostics, and implementation proposals.

### S08: Economics

Model diagnostics, fixed-scope projects, recurring monitoring, team leverage, capacity, software costs, acquisition costs, and a future product layer.

### S09: Risks

Cover security, permissions, exceptions, vendor dependence, tool changes, support obligations, unstable processes, regulation, and commoditization.

### S10: Company roadmap

Show the path from solo consultant to specialized agency, managed reliability provider, and software-and-service company.
