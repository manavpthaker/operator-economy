## Base relationship and point of view

- Base relationship: a commercially experienced strategist tells one search or content operator where the business is and where the hype begins.
- Central thesis: do not sell control over AI answers; sell evidence about what happened and a decision about what deserves action.
- Common bad framing: GEO is another ranking game with a new dashboard.
- Steelman: search reporting and AI-visibility tools collect useful signals.
- Ruling: signals without a buyer decision are activity dressed as a product.
- Disputable line: optimization before diagnosis is confidence with an invoice.
- Operator decision: sell a bounded diagnostic first and refuse placement, traffic, lead, or revenue guarantees.

## Craft functions

- First-principles reasoning: an answer is assembled from changing sources, so the operator can sample, trace, compare, and classify it but cannot promise permanent control.
- Observational delivery: one travel brand has clean search reporting while an AI answer is missing, stale, or sourced from an unreviewed comparison page.
- Escalation: missing brand, stale description, unknown source, green dashboard, no accountable decision.
- Opinionated spine: fake certainty is the target; the diagnostic must end in a ruling, not another dashboard.
- Research-and-comedy engine: survey evidence establishes the discovery surface, source research reveals the mechanism, the “committee without minutes” analogy exposes the reporting gap, and the operator receives a three-pass service and a kill test.

## Governing analogy

Analogy family: committee meeting.

| Mapping | Analogy | Business mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| What moves | the recommendation discussed in the meeting | brand information entering an AI answer |
| What blocks it | missing, stale, or louder votes | source gaps, conflicting facts, and platform behavior |
| Who owns the consequence | the marketing leader outside the room | the buyer making content, reputation, source, or measurement decisions |
| What changes | reconstruct the minutes and assign an action | record the answer, trace sources, classify the gap, and make a decision |

- First use: AI discovery behaves like a committee where several sources vote and marketing does not receive the minutes.
- Callback 1: the diagnostic reconstructs the meeting rather than pretending to own it.
- Callback 2: monitoring is justified only when a later meeting changes a real decision.
- Final resolution: give the buyer the minutes before selling influence over the room.
- Excluded metaphors: plumbing, road tolls, kitchens, and generic maps.

## Humor, contempt, and cadence

- Target of contempt: fake certainty, dashboard activity presented as a business outcome, and optimization sold before diagnosis.
- Protected people: marketing staff, buyers, customers, search operators, and small teams dealing with a new surface.
- Humor tools: precise absurdity, committee callback, dry verdict, undercut, and escalation.
- Calm passage: the McKinsey, Gartner, and academic evidence boundary.
- Rhythmic waveform: longer situation, short question, direct answer, three concrete beats, blunt verdict, breath.
- Manav mechanics: direct address, ask-answer, negate-replace, triplets, double-tap conclusions, and one natural aside.
- Phrase control: do not use “hypothetical,” “does not prove,” “not real,” “here's the thing,” or “okay” as recurring supports.

## Evidence-efficient treatment

- Consumer behavior evidence: attribute the two surveys and establish only the changing discovery surface.
- Source behavior: attribute McKinsey and academic work, then explain the service boundary without repeating a disclaimer.
- Travel brand: archetypal illustration, recorded in metadata; no identity, metric, quotation, or result, and no spoken disclosure required.
- Offer: label once as the service to test, then speak decisively about its designed mechanism.
- Economics: introduce the entire section once as a model, state assumptions and sensitivity, and do not repeat the scenario warning.
- Demand: unknown becomes the buyer-interview and paid-diagnostic test.
