# Narrative spine: AI visibility diagnostic

Status: approved for dry-run testing only

## Story in one sentence

A search strategist realizes the dashboard they trust cannot show how a buyer's brand appears inside AI-assisted research, builds a bounded source-and-answer diagnostic, confronts what cannot be controlled, and ends with a paid test that may prove the service should not exist.

## Viewer movement

Starting belief: GEO is either the next SEO gold rush or another tool category.

Ending understanding: The plausible business is a narrow evidence service with a measurable deliverable, substantial human judgment, explicit uncertainty, and a willingness-to-pay test.

## Causal spine

- Protagonist: Experienced search or content operator considering a new service.
- Goal: Give a marketing buyer decision-grade visibility and source intelligence.
- Constraint: Answer surfaces vary, causal business impact is unproven, and the operator cannot control placement.
- Opening: Consumer research behavior and measurement tools create a new observable surface.
- Mechanism: Question set -> answer sample -> citation map -> fact check -> controllability classification -> prioritized action -> repeated sample.
- Proof: Consumer studies, adjacent SEO-audit work, product capabilities, and technical measurement research.
- Decision: Whether the evidence changes a buyer decision enough to support a paid diagnostic.
- Resolution: Five interviews, one manual baseline, one paid-condition test, and explicit stop conditions.

## Tension and fairness

The optimistic case is that brands need a new evidence layer. The skeptical case is that this belongs inside existing SEO, varies too much to be valuable, or cannot be connected to business outcomes. The episode earns its conclusion by keeping that skeptical case alive through the final test.

## Sequence arc

1. The dashboard says the brand is fine, but it cannot answer a buyer's AI question.
2. The blind spot exists because answer surfaces assemble information differently.
3. The business is not ranking manipulation; it is a diagnostic.
4. Build the question, answer, citation, and accuracy baseline.
5. Separate what the buyer controls from what they merely observe.
6. Turn the workflow into an offer and first-buyer path.
7. Model the labor and economics without pretending the price is real.
8. Run the 30-day test and accept the possibility of rejection.
