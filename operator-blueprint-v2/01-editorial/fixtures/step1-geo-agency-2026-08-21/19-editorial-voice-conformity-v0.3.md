“Ask the marketing lead what they might do differently after seeing the evidence. If they can't answer that, stop. An interesting screenshot is not a business.”

### Observed pattern 1: ask, then answer

“So what are you recording? Not one permanent ranking. You're recording a sample.”

The construction resolves the viewer's immediate question and replaces the wrong frame.

### Observed pattern 2: negate, then replace

“Not an agency promising rankings. A diagnostic that helps a buyer decide what, if anything, is worth fixing.”

The line removes the inflated offer before naming the responsible business.

### Concrete mechanism

“Pass one, capture the answer. ... Pass two, trace the sources. ... Pass three, classify the gap.”

### Natural evidence and uncertainty

“Does that prove your travel brand has a problem? No. Does it prove somebody will pay you to solve it? Also no.”

The qualification remains explicit without sounding like a legal disclaimer.

### Conversational transition and landing

“All right. How do you deliver it?” and “The tool gathers the pieces. The operator decides what they mean.”

Both move the argument forward in the observed register without adding empty style.
