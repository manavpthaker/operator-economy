# Script v0.1: AI visibility diagnostic

Status: immutable review draft; not approved

Episode: unassigned fixture

## S01: The dashboard cannot answer the question

### Narration

A consumer travel brand can look healthy in search and still have no idea what happens when a customer asks an AI assistant which company to choose.

That is the opening for this business.

Not an agency that promises to rank a brand inside ChatGPT. Nobody controls that outcome.

A diagnostic that shows a marketing leader which questions matter, where the brand appears, how it is described, which sources shape the answer, and which gaps are actually worth working on.

McKinsey reported that half of the consumers in its 2025 U.S. survey intentionally used AI-powered search. Gartner separately found meaningful changes in research habits.

Those studies do not prove a buyer will pay for this service. They do establish that the discovery surface is real enough to measure.

And the business question is simple: can that measurement change a decision the buyer values?

## S02: Why the blind spot exists

### Narration

Traditional search reporting gives you rankings, traffic, pages, and conversions. Useful information.

But an AI answer can assemble its response from the brand's site, publishers, review sites, forums, comparison pages, and sources the brand has never seen.

McKinsey's source analysis found that the mix changes by model and category. Academic GEO research also suggests visibility can be measured, with intervention results varying by domain.

So this is not a new ranking report.

The operator is sampling a changing information environment.

That distinction matters because the buyer may control a product page, influence a publisher, correct a factual error, or do absolutely nothing about a particular source.

The diagnostic has to separate those states. Otherwise it is just a screenshot with a confident conclusion attached.

## S03: The offer

### Narration

The neighboring SEO market already sells audits, competitive analysis, implementation projects, and recurring reporting.

Current AI-visibility products can capture prompts, mentions, competitors, citations, and source patterns.

Put those pieces together and you get a service worth testing.

One brand. One market. Ten buyer-approved questions. Two answer surfaces.

The operator records the answers, maps the cited sources, checks important statements against the brand's approved facts, compares competitors, and classifies every gap as controllable, influenceable, or currently outside the buyer's control.

The buyer receives evidence, priorities, and a repeat-measurement plan.

Not placement. Not traffic. Not leads. Not revenue.

The product is a better decision about what to change and what to leave alone.

## S04: How the work gets done

### Narration

Start with the buyer's decision.

If the buyer cannot explain what they would do differently after seeing the evidence, stop. A dashboard without a decision is not a service.

Then define the ten questions. Include category questions, comparisons, and questions where factual accuracy matters.

Capture the same questions across two surfaces. Record date, context, answer, brand presence, competitors, citations, and visible variation.

Trace the important sources. Compare claims against client-approved documents. Classify the gaps. Recommend only the changes the evidence supports. Then repeat the sample after an agreed interval.

The tools handle collection and comparison.

The operator owns research design, factual judgment, source interpretation, permissions, and the sentence every client eventually wants to hear: we can measure this, but we cannot promise what the platform will do next.

## S05: Who buys first

### Narration

The first test segment in this model is consumer travel brands with an existing SEO or content responsibility.

That is a choice for the validation test, not a proven best market.

Build a list of twenty-five brands. Use public information to prepare a three-question screen. Show one meaningful discrepancy, explain the sampling limits, and ask the marketing owner whether the finding changes a real planning, content, reputation, or measurement decision.

If it does, offer the full fixed-scope diagnostic.

If it does not, do not force a sale with fear about becoming invisible.

The buyer has to value the decision, not merely find the screenshot interesting.

## S06: The model

### Narration

Now the economics, with the important warning first: none of these service numbers are observed performance.

The validation case assumes a two-thousand-dollar diagnostic, sixteen delivery hours, a sixty-dollar hourly labor allowance, ninety-nine dollars of measurement software, and three hundred dollars of acquisition and listed overhead.

That leaves six hundred forty-one dollars before tax and costs the model does not include.

The working case assumes two three-thousand-dollar diagnostics and twenty-four hours of delivery per client. After the same labor allowance, software, and listed overhead, the modeled contribution is two thousand three hundred twenty-two dollars.

This is a scenario, not an earnings forecast.

Its value is not the total. Its value is showing what breaks it: longer delivery, more tools, more sales effort, refunds, specialist review, or a buyer who will not pay without a result guarantee.

## S07: The reasons not to build it

### Narration

There are at least four.

First, the buyer may see this as ordinary SEO work and refuse a separate budget.

Second, the sample may vary too much to support a responsible before-and-after comparison.

Third, the tools may make data collection easy enough that expert interpretation is not valuable.

Fourth, the buyer may care only if the operator promises traffic or revenue, which is exactly the promise this service cannot make.

The service also depends on changing platform behavior, pricing, terms, and coverage. Regulated categories and comparative claims create additional review needs.

Those are not footnotes. They are the business model.

## S08: Test the decision before the agency

### Narration

So do not begin by buying every GEO tool and announcing a new agency.

Interview five marketing leaders in one segment.

Run one manual ten-question baseline with a consenting brand. Track every hour. Produce the evidence package without selling optimization.

Then offer the scope at a fixed validation price.

Continue only if buyers can name the decision this evidence changes, one of them accepts a paid diagnostic or gives a specific budget-backed purchase condition, and the work can be repeated responsibly within twenty-four hours.

Stop or fold it into existing SEO work if the buyer sees no material value, the measure is unstable, the sale requires a guarantee, or the price cannot cover responsible delivery.

That is the blueprint.

The first product is not visibility optimization.

It is evidence about whether a useful business exists.
