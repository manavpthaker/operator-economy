## Opening and orientation

### Concrete cold open

The viewer first meets a marketing lead with a green dashboard and a missing or stale AI answer. The question “which thing is wrong: the answer, the company, or the report?” leaves the case unresolved.

This is a situation, not the thesis. The business offer, market research, and committee analogy are withheld.

Decision: pass

### Show identity

The script preserves a separate no-narration identity beat and then uses the fixed line exactly:

> This is The Operator Economy, where we show you how to build, own, and operate a sustainable business of one using AI.

The next sentence names this episode's business, one operator, and the condition that the work would have to support a business of one.

Decision: pass

### Context runway

The script explains the familiar sequence first: a page ranks, a customer clicks, analytics records the visit, and perhaps the customer buys. It then adds the second doorway, the consumer-use evidence, the changing source mix, and the buyer's reporting gap.

The earlier v0.4 line “This is not a contradiction. It is a measurement failure” is gone. Its function is now explained in ordinary language:

> The old report may be answering the question it was designed to answer while the customer has started asking a different question somewhere else.

Decision: pass

### Earned thesis

Only after the normal system and changed discovery path are clear does the narrator rule that a fixed diagnostic is more responsible than a promise to control rankings.

Decision: pass

## Business-of-one clarity

- AI and tools collect and compare answer samples, citations, and source patterns.
- The operator retains question design, fact-checking, permissions, source judgment, and the recommendation.
- Travel remains a reachable test bench, not a claim to own a broad market.
- The economics section now says directly that the model does not yet show one operator can live on it.
- Sustainability is attached to assumptions that must improve, not promised as an outcome.

Decision: pass

## Plainspoken reporting and voice

### Reporting spine

The green report and the customer's second doorway carry the opening, context, mechanism, and ending. The script now investigates the gap before selling the solution.

Decision: pass

### Operator-advisor relationship

The narrator gives a clear commercial ruling: start with the decision, run a fixed diagnostic, ask whether the evidence changes the buyer's work, and refuse an outcome guarantee.

### First-listen clarity

The main term follows the picture. Evidence is introduced after the traditional search-report chain. Offer, delivery, buyer, economics, failure, and first test occupy separate sequences. The committee analogy enters after multiple source types are explained literally.

The densest necessary passage is the McKinsey and academic source explanation. It remains understandable but should be the first passage tested in an actual owner read.

Decision: pass recommendation; owner ear pending

### Conviction

And later:

The opinions are not hedged. The evidence limits remain intact.

Decision: pass

## Challenges

- Written-not-spoken: no sentence fails outright. The McKinsey source paragraph remains the most likely owner rewrite point because of fact density.
- Verdict-before-case: corrected. The offer thesis begins only after the context runway.
- Density: the episode string is long but still one coherent promise. Test its breath in Step 2 only after owner word approval.
- Analogy: committee-and-minutes remains one mapped family and does not spread into unrelated metaphors.
- Non-imitation: the script uses ordinary setup, patient reporting, understatement, and callback as functions. It does not reproduce a creator's recognizable surface.
