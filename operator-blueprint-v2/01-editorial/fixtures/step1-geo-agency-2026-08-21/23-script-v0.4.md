## S01: Search looks fine. The answer does not.

Claim IDs: C001, C007

### Narration

A travel brand can look healthy in search and still show up wrong in an AI answer.

That is not a contradiction.

It is a measurement failure.

Picture the marketing lead. The pages rank. Traffic looks steady. The dashboard is green, which is apparently the official color of “please stop asking questions.”

Then a customer asks an AI assistant which travel company fits their needs.

The brand is missing.

Or the description is stale. Or the answer leans on a comparison page nobody on the team has reviewed.

So which report explains that?

None of the usual ones.

Here is my view. The useful business is not AI ranking theater. It is a diagnostic that shows the buyer what happened, where the answer came from, and what actually deserves action.

Why?

Because AI discovery works less like one permanent ranking and more like a committee meeting. Your website gets a vote. Publishers get a vote. Review sites, forums, and comparison pages get a vote.

And marketing does not get the minutes.

McKinsey surveyed 1,927 U.S. consumers in August 2025. Half said they intentionally used search tools with generative AI. Gartner separately reported changes in how surveyed consumers research products.

That establishes the new discovery surface. It does not establish demand for this exact service.

Good.

Demand is the first thing you test.

## S02: Reconstruct the meeting

Claim IDs: C002, C005, C006

### Narration

Traditional search reporting tells you where a page ranks, which query sent the traffic, and what happened after the click.

Useful.

But an AI answer may assemble its response from the brand's site, publishers, review sites, forums, and comparison pages.

McKinsey's source analysis found that the mix can change by model and category. Academic GEO research suggests visibility can be measured, with results varying by domain.

So you are not measuring one permanent rank.

You are recording a sample of the meeting.

What did the answer say? Which sources shaped it? Which facts conflict? And does any of this change a decision the buyer already owns?

Some gaps belong to the brand. Some can only be influenced. Some sit outside its control.

That boundary is not the timid part of the service.

It is the intelligent part.

You are not selling ownership of the room. You are reconstructing the minutes well enough for the buyer to decide what to do next.

## S03: The buyer is purchasing a ruling

Claim IDs: C004, C005, C007

### Narration

Now ask the question that clears out most bad offers.

What does the buyer actually purchase?

The neighboring SEO market already sells audits, competitive analysis, projects, and recurring reporting. Current AI-visibility products can collect prompts, mentions, competitors, citations, and source patterns.

Those are useful components.

They are not the product.

The product is a ruling.

One brand. One market. Ten buyer-approved questions. Two answer surfaces.

Then three outputs.

A recorded baseline of what the answers said.

A source and fact-gap analysis showing what shaped them.

And a decision: correct this owned fact, strengthen this source, investigate this outside gap, monitor the result, or leave it alone because action is not justified.

That last option matters. If every diagnostic ends with “buy more optimization,” you did not build a diagnostic. You built a sales funnel wearing reading glasses.

A screenshot is evidence.

A decision is the product.

## S04: Run three passes

Claim IDs: C002, C005, C006, C007

### Narration

How do you deliver it?

Start with the decision.

Ask the marketing lead what they might change after seeing the evidence. If they cannot name a real content, reputation, source, or measurement decision, stop. An interesting screenshot is not a business.

Then run three passes.

First, record the meeting.

Agree on the questions. Capture the answer surface, date, response, brand presence, competitors, and important claims.

Second, check the minutes.

Log the important citations. Compare the important statements with documents the client has approved as factual.

Third, rule on the gap.

Is this an owned fact the brand can correct? A source relationship it may be able to influence? Or a result it should watch without pretending to control it?

Tools can collect and compare some of this. The operator still owns the research design, fact check, source judgment, permissions, and limits.

The tool gathers the pieces.

The operator decides what they mean.

## S05: Pick one buyer, not the entire internet

Claim IDs: C007, C009

### Narration

Who do you test this with first?

Not every company with a website. That is not a segment. That is the internet.

Pick one group where you can reach the buyer and digital discovery already matters to the category.

Use consumer travel brands as the starting segment. The research does not establish travel as the right first segment, so do not pretend it does. You are choosing a reachable test, not announcing a market winner.

Build a list of twenty-five brands with someone responsible for SEO or content.

Prepare a three-question screen from public information and show the sampling limits.

Then ask one question: does this evidence change a decision?

If yes, offer the fixed-scope diagnostic.

If no, do not manufacture urgency by telling the buyer their brand is disappearing into the machine.

No changed decision, no useful diagnostic.

## S06: Let the math make you nervous

Claim IDs: C008

### Narration

Here is the model. Every number in this section is a test assumption, not a market benchmark or an income forecast.

Start with a two-thousand-dollar validation price and sixteen delivery hours.

Assign nine hundred sixty dollars to labor, ninety-nine dollars to measurement software, and three hundred dollars to acquisition and listed overhead.

That leaves six hundred forty-one dollars before tax and before anything the model missed.

Now the working case.

Two diagnostics at three thousand dollars each. Twenty-four delivery hours for each client.

After the labor allowance, software, and listed overhead, modeled contribution is two thousand three hundred twenty-two dollars.

And that number should make you a little nervous.

More delivery time, another tool, harder sales, refunds, specialist review, or one buyer demanding an outcome guarantee can erase it quickly.

The model is doing its job when it exposes the weakness.

The point is not to admire the revenue.

The point is to find out whether responsible delivery leaves a business.

## S07: Kill the fake version early

Claim IDs: C002, C005, C007, C008

### Narration

Four things kill this offer.

First, the buyer sees ordinary SEO and will not fund it separately.

Second, the same questions do not produce a baseline you can repeat responsibly.

Third, the tool already gives the buyer everything needed, so your interpretation adds no value.

Fourth, the buyer only cares if you promise traffic or revenue.

If that is the sale, walk away. Optimization before diagnosis is confidence with an invoice.

Platform behavior, terms, coverage, and pricing will keep moving. Comparative or regulated claims may need specialist review.

These are not warnings to whisper after the pitch.

They decide whether the pitch deserves to exist.

## S08: Sell the minutes before influence

Claim IDs: C007, C008, C009

### Narration

So do not start with a new tool stack and an agency website.

Talk to five marketing leaders in one segment you can reach.

With one consenting brand, run the manual ten-question baseline across two answer surfaces. Track every hour. Build the evidence package without selling optimization.

Then offer the same scope at a fixed validation price.

Continue only if three things happen.

Buyers can name the decision the evidence changes.

At least one accepts a paid diagnostic or gives you a specific, budget-backed purchase condition.

And you can repeat the work responsibly within twenty-four hours.

Stop, redesign, or fold it into existing SEO work when buyers see no material value, the sample is unstable, the sale needs a guarantee, or the price cannot cover responsible delivery.

If the diagnostic repeats, then you can test monitoring. If monitoring repeatedly changes a real decision, then you can earn a recurring service.

Not before.

The first business is not control over AI answers.

It is giving the buyer the minutes before anyone sells them influence over the meeting.

That is useful. That is testable. And unlike ranking theater, it is a promise an operator can actually keep.
