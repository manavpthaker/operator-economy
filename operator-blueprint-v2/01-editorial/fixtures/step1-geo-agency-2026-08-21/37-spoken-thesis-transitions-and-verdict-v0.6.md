## 1. Show identity and episode promise

Placement: after the concrete cold open and silent identity boundary

### Proposed narration

This is The Operator Economy, where we show you how to build, own, and operate a sustainable business of one using AI.

Today, we're building an AI visibility-intelligence company.

The customer has started using a second doorway to research what to buy. The marketing team has reports for the first doorway. For the second one, they may have screenshots, alerts, a few uneasy meetings, and somebody asking why the brand has apparently been described by a committee that nobody invited.

The opportunity is to turn that confusion into evidence a buyer can use. The diagnostic is where you enter. The larger company owns the intelligence that comes after it.

## 2. Opportunity thesis

Placement: after the familiar search system, consumer evidence, source variation, product capabilities, academic benchmark, and adjacent SEO purchase form have been explained

### Proposed narration

So we have a real change in behavior, a new set of things that can be measured, and buyers who already understand the idea of paying for diagnostic work.

What we do not have is proof that companies are waiting to buy this exact service. A dashboard cannot provide that proof. A research paper cannot provide it either.

But they do show us the opening.

AI answers can describe a company using its own site, reviews, publishers, forums, comparison pages, or some changing mix of those sources. The marketing lead needs to know what the answer said, where the important claims appear to have come from, which facts are wrong, and whether any of it should change a decision.

That is the company.

An AI visibility-intelligence business records the evidence, explains the source and fact gaps, separates what the client can control from what it can only observe, and gives the buyer a responsible next move.

You are not selling control of the answer. You are selling a better decision about it.

## 3. Act I to Act II transition

Placement: immediately after the opportunity thesis

### Proposed narration

Now, the fact that software companies built the measuring tools does not mean your job is to build another platform.

You do not need every consumer brand. You need a focused group of buyers with the same unanswered question and enough reason to pay for expert judgment.

That is where experience becomes the advantage.

If you understand search, content, digital strategy, or competitive intelligence, you already know how to design a fair sample, trace a source, check a claim, and explain why an interesting result may still be useless. AI can collect and compare more of the material. It cannot decide which question matters to the buyer, which source deserves trust, or whether the evidence is strong enough to recommend action.

That judgment is your way into the market.

## 4. Aspirational company to entry wedge

Placement: after the mature company has been described

### Proposed narration

Built out, this becomes the outsourced AI discovery-intelligence function for a focused portfolio of brands.

You maintain the questions that matter. You record how the answers and sources change. You check important statements against facts the client has approved. You explain competitor and category context. Then you connect the evidence to a planning, content, reputation, or measurement decision.

But you do not start by asking a buyer to hand you that whole responsibility.

You start with one brand, one market, ten agreed questions, and two answer surfaces. You record the baseline, trace the important sources, check the material facts, sort the gaps by what the client can control, and recommend what deserves action.

That diagnostic is not the company in miniature. It is the first part of the company the buyer can inspect, purchase, and trust.

## 5. Investment-case transition

Placement: before modeled economics and required-share logic

### Proposed narration

Now we have to find out whether the company can support the person building it.

The existing planning model starts with a two-thousand-dollar diagnostic. After the labor allowance, measurement software, acquisition, and the listed overhead, six hundred forty-one dollars remains before tax and the costs the model has not included.

The working case uses two diagnostics at three thousand dollars each. After forty-eight delivery hours, the labor allowance, software, and listed overhead, the modeled remainder is two thousand three hundred twenty-two dollars before tax and unlisted costs.

That is a modeled scenario, not observed performance or an earnings forecast.

And it does not yet support the claim that one operator can live on this business. Its job is to show you what must be learned: what buyers will pay, how long responsible delivery takes, what acquisition costs, how often clients need the work, and how many engagements one person can handle without turning research judgment into copy and paste.

You are looking for the smallest reachable client base that supports the company after all of that work is counted.

## 6. Hard-part transition

Placement: after the modeled economics

### Proposed narration

The difficult part is not collecting more screenshots.

It is getting a marketing leader to pay for responsible judgment when you cannot promise placement, traffic, or revenue. The evidence has to change a real decision. The sample has to be repeatable enough to compare. And your interpretation has to add something the client's SEO report or software account does not already provide.

If the buyer only cares when you promise the result, walk away. If the tool already gives them the full answer, walk away. If every engagement expands until one person is doing research, content, public relations, legal review, and software support for the price of a report, stop calling it a business of one and fix the scope.

Those are the parts of the company you design before you add recurring work.

## 7. Thirty-day build and ninety-day proof

Placement: immediately before the final verdict

### Proposed narration

For the first thirty days, choose one consumer-travel segment you can reach. Build a list of twenty-five brands. Talk to five marketing or organic-growth leaders and ask what decision this evidence could change.

With one consenting brand, run the manual ten-question diagnostic across two answer surfaces. Track every hour. Deliver the baseline, the source-and-fact review, and the recommended actions. Then offer the same scope at the modeled validation price.

Continue only if the buyers can name a consequential decision, at least one accepts a paid diagnostic or gives you a specific budget-backed condition, and you can repeat the work responsibly within twenty-four hours.

The next sixty days are the proof phase. Deliver the paid scope. Record the real acquisition time, delivery time, tool cost, and client load. Repeat the approved sample only if the buyer has a decision to revisit. Offer monitoring only if that second look changes something useful. Add a second paying brand only when the first scope can repeat without hiding your time.

Those are modeled build targets, not observed outcomes. They are also the work of building the company, not thirty more days of watching the category from a safe distance.

## 8. Affirmative build verdict and opening callback

Placement: final editorial resolution

### Proposed narration

Go back to the green report at the beginning.

It was not necessarily wrong. It was looking at one doorway while the customer used another.

The company worth building sits between that missing view and the decision the marketing lead has to make. It records what the answer said, traces the important sources, checks the facts, explains what the buyer can influence, and says when the evidence is too weak to deserve action.

That responsibility is larger than a one-time audit and focused enough for one experienced operator to enter.

So build the AI visibility-intelligence company. Start with one brand, one market, and one paid diagnostic. Prove that the evidence changes a real decision. Repeat it. Earn the monitoring work. Then grow the client portfolio only when the judgment remains useful and the delivery model can support the owner.

The old report can keep measuring the first doorway. Build the company that helps the buyer understand the second one.

## Spoken-language checks

- The company appears before the entry wedge.
- The opportunity thesis describes a business, not an offer slogan.
- Market evidence establishes a changed and measurable surface without claiming demand for the exact company.
- The adjacent SEO market establishes a familiar purchase form without transferring its demand or pricing.
- The operator's experience supplies the advantage; AI and current tools reduce collection and comparison labor.
- The investment transition names price, owner labor, listed costs, capacity, and the unresolved required-share question.
- The hard part is treated as company design, not a closing disclaimer.
- The 30-day phase begins construction; the modeled 90-day phase earns expansion.
- The ending uses an affirmative build verb and resolves the two-doorway opening.
- No placement, traffic, lead, revenue, client-count, or income outcome is promised.
- No public URL is present.
- No em dash is present in proposed narration.
