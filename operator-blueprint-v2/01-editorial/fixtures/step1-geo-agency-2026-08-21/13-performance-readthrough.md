# AI visibility diagnostic

> Dry-run performance read-through derived from `12-script-v0.2.md`. Test fixture only. This file is not independently editable.

## The dashboard does not fully show the answer

Imagine a travel brand with healthy search traffic.

Its pages rank. Its analytics look normal. The marketing team thinks discovery is covered.

Then a customer asks an AI assistant which travel company fits their needs.

The brand is missing from the answer. Or it appears with an outdated description. Or the answer relies on a comparison page the marketing team has never reviewed.

That example is hypothetical. The underlying shift is not.

In McKinsey's August 2025 survey of 1,927 U.S. consumers, half said they intentionally used AI-powered search. Gartner separately reported changes in how surveyed consumers research products.

Those studies do not prove this travel brand has a problem. They do not prove anyone will pay to solve it.

They establish a discovery surface that conventional reporting does not fully show.

And that creates a business worth testing: not an agency that promises rankings, but a diagnostic that helps a buyer decide what is worth fixing.

## The source problem

Traditional search reporting tells the team where pages rank, which queries send traffic, and what visitors do next.

Useful information.

But the AI answer may be assembled from the brand's site, publishers, review sites, forums, and comparison pages.

McKinsey's source analysis found that the mix can change by model and category. Academic GEO research also suggests visibility can be measured, with results varying by domain.

So the operator is not discovering one permanent ranking.

The operator is recording a sample.

For our hypothetical travel brand, the useful question is not, “How do we control the answer?”

It is, “What does the answer say, where did that information come from, and which part gives the buyer a responsible decision?”

Some gaps are controllable. Some can only be influenced. Some are simply outside the brand's control.

That boundary is the product.

## What the buyer purchases

The neighboring SEO market already sells audits, competitive analysis, projects, and recurring reporting.

Current AI-visibility products can collect prompts, mentions, competitors, citations, and source patterns.

The proposed service combines those components into one bounded diagnostic.

One brand. One market. Ten buyer-approved questions. Two answer surfaces.

The buyer receives three things.

First, a recorded baseline of what the selected answers actually said.

Second, a map of the sources and factual gaps shaping those answers.

Third, a prioritized decision: correct this fact, strengthen this owned source, investigate this third-party gap, or leave this result alone because the evidence does not justify action.

The buyer is purchasing evidence they can use.

Not placement. Not traffic. Not leads. Not revenue.

## Build the diagnostic in three passes

The work begins with the buyer's decision.

If the marketing lead cannot explain what they might do differently after seeing the evidence, stop. An interesting screenshot is not a service.

Then run three passes.

Pass one: capture the answer.

Agree on the questions. Record the surface, date, answer, brand presence, and competitors.

Pass two: trace the sources.

Log the important citations. Compare material statements with documents the client has approved as factual.

Pass three: classify the gap.

Is the issue an owned fact the brand can correct? A source relationship it may be able to influence? Or a result it should observe without pretending to control?

Tools can help collect and compare the evidence.

The operator owns the research design, the fact check, the source judgment, the permissions, and the limits.

## Test one buyer segment

For the validation test, choose one segment based on two practical advantages: the operator can reach the buyer, and digital discovery already matters to the category.

This model uses consumer travel brands as an example. The research does not prove that segment is best.

Build a list of twenty-five brands with an existing SEO or content responsibility.

Prepare a three-question public-information screen. Make the sampling limits obvious.

Then ask one question: does this evidence change a real content, reputation, source, or measurement decision?

If the answer is yes, offer the complete fixed-scope diagnostic.

If the answer is no, do not manufacture urgency by telling the buyer their brand is becoming invisible.

The service has value only when the evidence changes a decision.

## Use the model to expose fragility

Now the economics.

These are test assumptions. They are not observed service results.

The validation case starts with a two-thousand-dollar price and sixteen hours of delivery.

The model assigns nine hundred sixty dollars to labor, ninety-nine dollars to measurement software, and three hundred dollars to acquisition and listed overhead.

Six hundred forty-one dollars remain before tax and before costs the model has not included.

The working case assumes two three-thousand-dollar diagnostics, with twenty-four delivery hours for each client.

After the labor allowance, software, and listed overhead, the modeled contribution is two thousand three hundred twenty-two dollars.

Modeled scenario. Not observed performance. Not an earnings forecast.

The number matters because it is fragile.

Longer delivery, additional tools, more sales effort, refunds, specialist review, or a buyer who demands an outcome guarantee can erase it quickly.

## Four ways the decision fails

The same control boundary that defines the offer can also kill it.

If the buyer sees the work as ordinary SEO, there may be no separate budget.

If the same questions produce evidence that cannot be repeated responsibly, there may be no trustworthy baseline.

If the tool already gives the buyer everything they need, there may be no value in expert interpretation.

And if the buyer only cares when someone promises traffic or revenue, the responsible version of the service may be impossible to sell.

Platform behavior, terms, coverage, and pricing will keep moving. Comparative or regulated claims may require specialist review.

These are not disclaimers added after the business model.

They decide whether the business model works.

## Test the decision before building the agency

So do not begin with a tool stack and a new agency website.

Interview five marketing leaders in one reachable segment.

With one consenting brand, run the manual ten-question baseline across two answer surfaces. Track every hour. Produce the evidence package without selling optimization.

Then offer the same scope at a fixed validation price.

Continue only if buyers can name the decision the evidence changes, at least one accepts a paid diagnostic or gives a specific budget-backed purchase condition, and the work can be repeated responsibly within twenty-four hours.

Stop, redesign, or fold it into existing SEO work if buyers see no material value, the sample is unstable, the sale requires a guarantee, or the price cannot cover responsible delivery.

The first product is not AI visibility optimization.

It is evidence about whether a useful service should exist at all.
