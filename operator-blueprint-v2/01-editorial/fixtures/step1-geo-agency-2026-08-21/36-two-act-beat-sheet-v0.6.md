## Episode promise

Show why AI-assisted discovery creates an intelligence company worth building, why one experienced operator can enter that market using AI and current measurement tools, and how to begin with one paid diagnostic before earning an ongoing visibility-intelligence function.

## Episode Investment Thesis

Opportunity thesis:

> Some consumers now use AI-assisted answers while researching products, but the reports many marketing teams rely on were built around another discovery path. The behavior is measurable, yet the buyer still needs someone to turn answer samples, citations, factual gaps, and uncertainty into a decision. That creates an opening for an AI visibility-intelligence company.

Operator thesis:

> An experienced SEO, content, digital-strategy, or competitive-intelligence professional can enter without training a model or claiming control over an answer surface. Tools collect and compare evidence. The operator owns the question design, fact-checking, permissions, source judgment, controllability ruling, and final recommendation.

Build verdict:

> Build the company around decision-grade AI discovery intelligence. Enter through one fixed-scope paid diagnostic, prove that the evidence changes a real buyer decision, and earn recurring monitoring only when repeated intelligence remains useful.

Fixture boundary: `BUILD` is a narrative verdict for this control. Gate E1 remains failed, and no episode or production work is authorized.

## Narrative handoffs

### B03 to B04: familiar system to changed behavior

The old report is useful. That is why the new gap matters. Once the viewer understands what the report measures, introduce the customer behavior it may not contain.

### B06 to B07: measurement components to commercial parallel

Products show that collection is possible. The neighboring SEO market shows that buyers understand diagnostic work. Neither proves demand for this exact company.

### B08 to B09: opportunity to operator

After naming the company, change the viewer's question from “does this opening exist?” to “why could someone with my experience own it responsibly?”

### B10 to B11: company to entrance

Show the mature intelligence function first. Then narrow to one brand and one diagnostic so the first offer feels like an entrance rather than a reduced ambition.

### B14 to B15: economics to hard part

Do not let revenue figures settle the investment case. Move directly into buyer value, repeatability, acquisition, and expert labor because those determine whether the modeled contribution survives.

### B16 to B17: plan to commitment

The 30-day test is the first month of building. The 90-day phase replaces assumptions and earns the next responsibility. End with an affirmative company verdict, not an invitation to keep watching the category.

## First-listen controls

- Explain the normal search chain before introducing AI visibility as a category.
- Attribute McKinsey and Gartner and keep the survey scope audible.
- Keep the approximate 30-CMO sample visibly small.
- Separate product capability from buyer value.
- Separate adjacent SEO purchase behavior from demand for the proposed company.
- State the opportunity thesis once in plain language before introducing the diagnostic.
- Do not introduce tools before the buyer's decision and operator's responsibility are understood.
- Treat the high end as proof that the surface can be measured, not as a demand or outcome claim.
- Keep the mature company visible after introducing the entry wedge.
- Mark every price, count, timeline, and capacity figure as observed, adjacent, unknown, or modeled.
- End on commitment to build the company, not on a promise to control an answer.
