# Incomplete Canvas: AI operating layer for small businesses

Status: Gate E3 candidate; intentionally plausible but unresolved

Overall evidence class: frontier hypothesis presented with observed component parallels

## 1. Operator

An automation specialist or AI generalist who can connect business software and create agent workflows.

Unresolved: Required process-discovery, security, support, data-governance, and incident-response competence.

## 2. Buyer and beneficiary

Buyer: Small and midsize businesses with too many tools.

Beneficiary: Employees and customers affected by broken processes.

Unresolved: Company size spans 5-250 employees, industry is open, economic buyer could be owner, operations, IT, finance, or department leadership, and the first reachable segment is not selected.

## 3. Costly problem

Businesses lose time and create errors when information does not move cleanly between systems.

Unresolved: No first workflow, measured baseline, buyer trigger, problem frequency, consequence, or current alternative has been selected.

## 4. Offer

An AI operating layer that maps processes, connects tools, automates work, monitors failures, handles exceptions, and recommends improvements.

Commercial shape: A setup project followed by a monthly subscription.

Unresolved: Diagnostic, implementation, software access, monitoring, recovery, governance, and optimization are combined. It is unclear what the buyer purchases first, when the operator may recommend no build, and which ongoing duties justify the subscription.

## 5. Buyer result

Fewer manual tasks, faster operations, fewer errors, and a business that runs more smoothly.

Unresolved: No primary acceptance measure, baseline, controllable result, test duration, buyer acceptance event, or non-guaranteed boundary.

## 6. Delivery system

1. Review the client's software.
2. Connect the relevant tools.
3. Add an AI agent to monitor and make decisions.
4. Launch and optimize.

Unresolved: No process observation, stable-rule test, human-decision boundary, least-privilege design, test matrix, logs, retry and stop rules, incident owner, documentation, revocation, or handoff. The proposed agent may make material decisions without a defined review boundary.

## 7. Capabilities and stack

Potential stack: n8n or Zapier, an AI-model provider, a vector database, CRM integrations, project-management integrations, billing integrations, and an operator-owned monitoring dashboard.

Unresolved: Tool names replace capability design. Credentials, client ownership, data retention, provider changes, manual fallback, security review, and operator-owned infrastructure risk are unanswered.

## 8. Go-to-market path

Use content, referrals, platform marketplaces, cold outreach, and partnerships. Offer a free automation audit and sell the complete system.

Unresolved: No first 25 prospects, buyer trigger, diagnostic question, credibility artifact, qualification rule, outreach volume, paid test, objection, or kill condition.

## 9. Entry wedge and expansion ladder

Entry: Set up the AI operating layer for the client.

Expansion: Add more workflows, monitoring, strategic advice, and eventually a reusable platform.

Unresolved: The “entry” is the full aspiration. There is no bounded first transaction, scope invariant, proof between stages, stop condition, or distinction between service learning and product demand.

## 10. Economics

Modeled price: $5,000 setup plus $1,500 per month.

Illustrative revenue: Four new setups and ten subscriptions would produce $35,000 in a month.

Unresolved: The prices have no buyer or delivery rationale. Labor, support, infrastructure, model usage, security, acquisition, refunds, tax, and owner compensation are absent. Delivery hours, incident volume, customer capacity, working-capital timing, and the effect of one serious failure are unknown. Revenue is shown without an operating model.

## 11. Risks and failure modes

Security, unreliable AI output, broken integrations, vendor changes, and client resistance may create problems.

Unresolved: No early warnings, mitigation, recovery owner, liability boundary, high-impact exclusion, or kill condition.

## 12. First 30-day validation test

Build a demo, launch a website, publish content, and contact potential customers.

Unresolved: The test builds architecture before selecting a buyer or workflow. It has no participant target, paid decision, measurable success signal, failure signal, maximum risk, or day-30 decision.

## 13. Blocking unknowns

| ID | Unknown | Why it blocks lock |
|---|---|---|
| U001 | First buyer and workflow | Offer, evidence, access, and outreach cannot be scoped. |
| U002 | First paid transaction | Buyer cannot know what is purchased or accepted. |
| U003 | Human and automated decision boundary | Delivery may create unsafe or unauthorized actions. |
| U004 | Access, ownership, and recovery | The operator cannot define safe implementation or handoff. |
| U005 | Acceptance measure | Result cannot be tested or priced responsibly. |
| U006 | Labor, support, and capacity | Revenue scenario cannot establish economic viability. |
| U007 | Expansion proof | Service learning is being treated as proof of a platform. |
