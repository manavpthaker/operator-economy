## Plain-English verdict

The story was good. The business underneath it was not ready.

The story had a hypothetical client, a clear failure, an operating insight, a meaningful turn, and an ambitious destination. But the first Canvas still could not tell an operator who pays first, what the buyer purchases, how delivery works safely, how the result is accepted, who recovers failures, or whether the revenue model survives labor and support.

Gate E3 stopped the workflow before narrative approval. It preserved the story as exploratory material but refused to let the story answer business questions it had not actually resolved.

## What the repair decided

- First buyer: Owner or operations lead at a reachable 10-100-person marketing or creative agency.
- First workflow: Signed proposal to acknowledged kickoff ownership.
- First transaction: Paid reliability diagnostic.
- Build decision: Separate sprint offered only after stable rules, safe access, understood exceptions, baseline, owner, and acceptance criteria pass.
- Primary measure: Time from signed proposal to acknowledged kickoff ownership.
- Safety: Client-controlled accounts, least privilege, human-only material decisions, test cases, stop rules, manual fallback, revocation, documentation, and handoff.
- First-customer test: Twenty-five prospects, ten interviews, three maps, two paid diagnostic offers, and one conditional sprint.
- Expansion: Adjacent workflows, managed reliability, and productization each require new evidence.

## Economics discrimination

The failed Canvas used revenue without an operating model.

The repaired fixture models a $750 diagnostic and a $3,000 sprint, exposes labor and direct costs, and sets a 60-hour monthly delivery ceiling. Two diagnostics plus two 24-hour sprints fit exactly. If each sprint takes 32 hours, the same mix requires 76 hours and fails.

These are test assumptions, not observed performance or an earnings forecast. Their purpose is to expose the capacity decision, not advertise an income outcome.

## Story status

The early story remains useful but unapproved.

- Its opening, client thread, conceptual turn, and aspiration may be reconsidered after Canvas lock.
- It cannot supply missing evidence or override the repaired operating model.
- The E3 pass does not approve it retroactively.
- Gate E4 must evaluate a narrative rebuilt or reconciled against the locked Canvas.

## Rule gap exposed

Proposed v0.3 already said that an engaging story without a complete Canvas is not an Operator Economy episode. It did not fully define how to handle a strong story created before E3 or distinguish a safe test question from a lock-blocking unknown.

That ambiguity could encourage a team to keep polishing the story while assuming the business decisions would be solved later.

## Proposed v0.4 amendments

- Added a decision-completeness standard before narrative approval.
- Required a first paid transaction, buyer acceptance event, completion boundary, safe ownership model, and reconciled delivery capacity at E3.
- Required every unknown to be classified as a safe test question, later-stage blocker, or current E3 blocker.
- Added a formal premature-story quarantine: preserve and hash the idea, but do not approve, outline, script, scene-plan, or polish it while E3 is failed.
- Clarified that a later Canvas pass never approves the early story retroactively.
- Added an E3 readiness checklist to the Operator Canvas template.
