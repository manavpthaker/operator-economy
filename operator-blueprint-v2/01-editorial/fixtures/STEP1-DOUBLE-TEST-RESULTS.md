# Step 1 double-test results

Status: calibration complete; proposed v0.2 amendments applied; Step 1 not authoritative

Test date: 2026-08-21

Episode workspaces created: zero

Narration authorized: no

## Plain-English verdict

The editorial workflow worked on two meaningfully different ideas.

- GEO began as a new business assembled from valid parallels. The workflow kept it hypothetical and turned it into a decision-grade diagnostic instead of a ranking promise.
- “Boring automation” began as an existing service category with dangerous legacy claims. The workflow stripped those claims out and turned it into a reliability service that can honestly recommend not building.

Both first drafts were complete on paper and still not good enough. The independent reviews forced real changes. Both revised scripts passed as test candidates. Both remained blocked from production because neither Step 0 fixture was promoted and neither had owner script or Content OS public-claim approval.

| Workflow-reliability service | Existing category; offer and economics modeled | Script sold implementation before diagnosis and lost the client inside a process lecture | Paid diagnostic, real no-build result, one acceptance measure, recurring signed-client thread, separated economics | pass after revision | blocked at E1 and E6 |

## What discriminated correctly

The same framework did not force the two candidates into the same episode.

- GEO's central decision is which answer-and-source gap deserves action.
- Workflow reliability's central decision is whether the handoff deserves automation at all.
- GEO uses adjacent components without claiming an established GEO-agency market.
- Workflow reliability acknowledges a real category without using partner programs as proof that a new entrant will win.
- Both models expose price, hours, cost, and capacity as assumptions, not promises.
- The automation test preserved safety, access, exception, recovery, and client-ownership boundaries that do not belong in the GEO model.

## Weaknesses the tests exposed

1. The documentation required a protagonist but did not force the protagonist or buyer decision to survive past the opening.
2. It did not explicitly label the evidentiary status of a recurring illustrative thread.
3. It could let diagnosis and implementation collapse into one inevitable commercial package.
4. It required review findings but had no reusable disposition artifact.
5. It requested a word count without requiring an exact match to the clean performance read-through.
6. It did not formally define how an eligible fixture may simulate the workflow while still failing production gates.

## Proposed v0.2 amendments made from the evidence

- Added fixture-mode rules that separate simulated and production decisions.
- Added a recurring human situation, transaction, or buyer decision to Gate E4 and the narrative templates.
- Required hypothetical or composite threads to identify themselves and prohibited invented proof.
- Required a primary buyer acceptance measure unless one would be misleading.
- Separated diagnostic, build decision, and implementation when diagnosis can responsibly end in no build.
- Added `REVIEW-DISPOSITION.template.md` and completed disposition logs for both tests.
- Required the exact script word count to match the clean read-through.

## What remains unproven

These are two positive-path tests. They do not prove rejection behavior for a flat narrative, incomplete Canvas, unsupported script claim, irreconcilable economics, or a Step 2 word-change request. They also do not exercise a genuinely promoted Step 0 package or real owner lock.

Therefore the correct status is: proposed Step 1 v0.2 is better calibrated, but not yet locked as V2 authority.
