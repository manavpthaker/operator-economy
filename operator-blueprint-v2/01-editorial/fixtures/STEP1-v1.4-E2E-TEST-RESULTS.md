## What the test caught

### 1. An incomplete review loop

The first Workflow verdict advanced v0.2 after a claims retest and one combined E5V review. Its seven separate role reviews still pointed to v0.1. `TEAM-WORKFLOW.md` requires affected reviews to run against the integrated script hash and keeps first-listen, voice, conviction and comedy, and performance separate.

The corrected current-hash review round changed the Workflow verdict from ready to blocked.

### 2. A commercially undefined first purchase

Workflow Operations says the buyer first purchases a paid diagnostic, but the model prices and capacity-tests only the later sprint. A blueprint cannot tell the viewer what to sell first while testing the economics of a different transaction.

The repair requires an explicit choice among a standalone diagnostic, a diagnostic credited toward the sprint, or one combined staged engagement. This decision returns the package to Gate E3 and invalidates its Investment Thesis and downstream script artifacts.

### 3. A claim-boundary upgrade

The sources establish a hands-on automation-service market. They do not establish `Workflow Operations` as an externally recognized category. The v0.2 phrases `the category is real enough` and `the category exists` blur those two statements and must be rewritten.

### 4. Unsupported frequency language

The Workflow script uses `rarely`, `a lot`, `often`, `usually`, and `almost never` without a prevalence locator. Operating intuition may motivate a hypothesis, but it cannot create a magnitude claim.

### 5. Spoken clarity that mechanics alone missed

The current Workflow math requires the listener to divide the two-sprint contribution before understanding why six sprints are required. Two additional phrases sound like framework labels or leave account-control safety ambiguous. These correctly fail first-listen and performance even though punctuation, banned-word, placeholder, and hash checks pass.

### 6. Historical false voice passes

The frozen v0.5 reviews reported zero banned terms. A current parse of the live Content OS voice list finds:

- Historical AI Visibility: `easy` twice and `simple` once.
- Historical Workflow control: `easy` once.
- Both historical scripts also open with `Today, we're`.

Both new final narrations contain zero current banned terms. The regression shows that future voice checks must parse the live authority instead of copying an older result.

### 7. A reviewer pass cannot overrule the owner

AI Visibility v0.2 was mechanically clean and had seven reviewer recommendations, but the owner still heard school-report attribution, an uncertain company premise, generic market advice, repetitive mannerisms, and weak narrative handoffs. That `REVISE` decision invalidated the earlier simulated lock and narration handoff.

The proposed v1.5 controls now require:

- conversational and audio-honest evidence in voiceover;
- exact source metadata in an on-screen receipt;
- full citations and provenance in show notes;
- operator-market selection through experience, relationships, access, buyer understanding, and reachable decision-makers;
- exact last-two-lines to first-two-lines causal seam tests;
- zero repeated discourse-marker scene openings; and
- one continuous owner cold read that no score or agent consensus may override.

The frozen v0.7 regression passed these then-proposed controls, but the owner still rejected its opening and ending. V0.8 repaired those surfaces. The later voice audit then proved that the control also needed positive evidence that the hosted lexical surface sounded like Manav, not merely evidence that the script was clean and readable. V1.0 passed that reviewer-level regression. The later horizontal v1.1 rewrite and owner decision completed the accepted v1.5 control.

### 8. A late owner correction must reopen the exact hash

AI Visibility v0.7 passed all seven independent review lanes, but the owner still heard a weak cold open, an unresolved pre-sting payoff, the wrong order in the show string, and an incomplete ending. That decision froze v0.7 as rejected history and created a new v0.8 script/read-through pair rather than rewriting an approved artifact in place.

The v0.8 control now:

- establishes the customer problem inside the first five seconds;
- completes the specific business payoff at spoken word 37;
- hands into the silent sting with `Here's the blueprint`;
- uses the fixed sentence `This is The Operator Economy, where we show you how to use AI to build, own, and operate a sustainable business of one.` exactly once;
- returns to the green report before a stronger BUILD close; and
- ends with one benefit-led like-and-subscribe request.

Seven independent roles passed the same exact v0.8 hashes. That remains preserved evidence for the framing repair, not a positive-voice pass or lock.

### 9. Negative voice hygiene is not positive identity

The v0.8 reviewers correctly found no word-level blocker within their assigned scopes, but the owner still did not hear the intended voice profile. A later audit found that the reviews had over-weighted banned-word checks, clean seams, and sentence-level readability.

The proposed repair now separates:

- first-listen clarity;
- lexical performability; and
- positive hosted-voice identity across the opening, opportunity, operator build, economics, and ending.

V0.9 proved the new control can reject a script that uses the right mannerisms in the wrong way. It increased direct address and host stance, but repeated scene-opening markers, stacked profile techniques, report cadence, awkward phrases, and a 19.0 percent very-short-sentence rate made the voice feel assembled.

V1.0 froze every structural and factual input, returned very-short sentences to 11.8 percent, preserved the stronger host relationship, and passed independent claims-and-structure, positive-voice, and lexical-performance reviews. V1.1 then reopened the opportunity case and Canvas, preserved the resulting exact lexical pair, and received the decisive owner approval recorded in `132-owner-approval-v1.1.md`.
