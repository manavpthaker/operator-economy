## Audible target

The target is Manav talking one viewer through a business he has just finished working out, not an institutional narrator reading the finished memo.

That means the voice must do positive human work:

- notice what is odd before naming the framework;
- reason from a familiar object to the business consequence;
- let a fair objection interrupt the easy answer;
- use ordinary detail to make an abstraction concrete;
- distinguish what the evidence says from what Manav thinks should be built;
- show a reaction when the plan becomes absurd, evasive, or operationally dishonest;
- land a decision with conviction after the reasoning earns it;
- speak directly to one operator who may have experience, need income, or be looking for a credible path to build.

The observed speech profile is a source, not a bag of mannerisms. Do not assign phrases, count signature moves, rotate sentence starters, manufacture asides, or force contractions onto prose after the fact. The eventual script should inherit language from a real talk-through of these scene jobs.

## Governing performance logic

### Narrator stance

Manav is a practical career strategist and operator. He is generous with the viewer and skeptical of systems that confuse activity with value. He is not neutral about the company: the recommendation is BUILD. He is equally clear that the paid test, not the narration, decides whether the model survives.

### Analogy logic

The two-doorway analogy remains the governing analogy. Begin with literal behavior: a customer can research through conventional search and through an AI answer while the old report measures only one path. Let the doorway language return only when it clarifies a new consequence: measurement, ownership, or the ending. Do not keep repainting the same metaphor.

Ordinary detail must come from objects already in the frozen episode: the green dashboard, the clicked page, the answer and its sources, the buyer's approved questions, the evidence record, the operator's calendar, the invoice, the prospect list, and the four decisions. These details make the reasoning feel lived-in without adding a fact or an invented anecdote.

### Comedy logic

Humor is a reaction to a recognizable business absurdity, not a separate entertainment layer. The episode's richest comic pressure comes from a sales document that always recommends more work, a spreadsheet that behaves better than client work, and one report quietly absorbing an entire team. Preserve the failure or contradiction; preserve a punchline only if it sounds natural in the owner talk-through.

The target of contempt is the broken incentive, fake certainty, hidden scope, or software activity masquerading as a result. The buyer, worker, customer, source, and uncertain operator are protected. A scene may be completely straight if the human reaction is already audible. No joke is owed.

### Cadence logic

Use thought-shaped rhythm rather than uniform short declarations. A scene can begin with a concrete picture, stretch into one worked-through causal thought, pause on the complication, and then land a short ruling. Another scene can use a genuine question and answer, or a concession followed by a decision. The form follows the reasoning; it does not alternate by template.

First person may express present reasoning or reaction only when it comes from the owner talk-through and adds no factual claim. It may not create biography, client history, credentials, or an experience story. Self-repair may survive only when it occurs naturally and preserves every fact and number.

## Scene-by-scene voice plan

### S00: the blind spot

- **Voice function:** Invite the viewer to notice the contradiction before the narrator explains it. Make the business opening feel discovered from an ordinary customer moment, not announced as a thesis.
- **Narrator reaction:** Mild disbelief at the coexistence of the stale or missing answer and the green dashboard, followed by quiet recognition that the missing view can become paid work.
- **Analogy or ordinary detail:** Stay literal: customer question, hotel result, unrecognized description, green dashboard. The doorway name can wait; the objects already carry the tension.
- **Cadence:** Begin cleanly and visually. Let the contradiction breathe. Move from observation to the operator action in one connected thought, then leave a clear handoff into silence. Avoid a stack of trailer taglines.
- **Objection to honor:** The dashboard may be accurate for what it measures. Do not imply negligence, fraud, or a broken search report.
- **Transition intent:** The paid-diagnostic possibility must be alive before S01. The sting punctuates a promised blueprint; it does not interrupt an unanswered generic mystery.
- **Guardrail:** Illustration only. No invented hotel, metric, quote, buyer reaction, or evidence claim.

### S01: silent identity sting

- **Voice function:** Absence of voice is the function. Give the opening claim enough space to register and reset the viewer into the show identity.
- **Narrator reaction:** None spoken.
- **Analogy or ordinary detail:** None added.
- **Cadence:** Three to six seconds of distinct silence.
- **Objection to honor:** Do not use the sting to over-explain the premise or repeat the payoff.
- **Transition intent:** Preserve the blind spot across the pause so S02 feels like the answer to what the viewer is watching.
- **Guardrail:** No narration, ad-lib, logo read, second promise, or production copy.

### S02: show and episode promise

- **Voice function:** Re-enter as a host with a clear promise: name the show, name the company, explain its job, and tell the operator what they will be able to do by the end.
- **Narrator reaction:** Confident and energized by the build, without becoming an announcer or claiming the model is proven.
- **Analogy or ordinary detail:** No new metaphor is needed. The company name and plain job should make the cold-open opportunity legible.
- **Cadence:** Deliver the fixed brand string as one settled identity thought. Give the episode-specific promise its own breath and make its three benefits sound like one useful commitment, not a marketing list.
- **Objection to honor:** None introduced here; uncertainty belongs inside the evidence and test, not as a hedge in the show identity.
- **Transition intent:** The cold open named a missing view. S03 now follows the customer through the two paths so the company responsibility can be defined.
- **Guardrail:** The fixed brand string cannot change. Do not add a `today` opener, internal category name, throat clearing, or hypothetical retreat.

### S03: the second doorway

- **Voice function:** Start with something the viewer already understands, then notice the added path with them. Defend the old system before explaining what it misses.
- **Narrator reaction:** Familiarity with query-to-click analytics, then a measured shift in attention when the customer's research no longer stays inside that trail.
- **Analogy or ordinary detail:** Query, page, click, visit, and dashboard first. Introduce the second doorway only after those literal mechanics are clear. The different source mix is what the viewer sees through it.
- **Cadence:** Let the conventional chain move conversationally. State the McKinsey finding in ordinary language, give its population limit without bibliography cadence, and then interpret it. Use varied sentence length so the source does not sound like a report being read aloud.
- **Objection to honor:** Regular search remains useful and did not disappear. The survey is bounded, and different sources do not prove demand for the company.
- **Transition intent:** End by converting the missing view into a specific ownership question. S04 must inherit the observable evidence and ask where the existing components stop.
- **Guardrail:** No second formal citation introduction, death-of-search claim, universal customer statement, or claim that an opening is already a company.

### S04: from evidence to responsibility

- **Voice function:** Sort the evidence with the viewer. Show that collection, measurement, and a familiar audit form are real, then distinguish each from the commercial responsibility the company must own.
- **Narrator reaction:** Fair-minded interest in what the tools can already do, followed by mild bemusement that more outputs still leave the buyer holding the actual decision.
- **Analogy or ordinary detail:** Put prompts, mentions, citations, the benchmark, and the neighboring audit on the buyer's desk. The ordinary picture is several useful outputs answering different questions, not an abstract `measurement layer`.
- **Cadence:** Treat each evidence family as a compact thought: what it establishes, then where it stops. Pause before returning to the marketing lead. Let the buyer's situation accumulate naturally rather than reciting a product inventory.
- **Objection to honor:** Existing tools or SEO services may already cover enough of the work for some buyers. Collection is useful. Adjacent services are real. None of that proves demand or price for this exact company.
- **Transition intent:** The scene must end on the human responsibility left after the evidence arrives. S05 inherits that responsibility and names the complete company around it.
- **Guardrail:** Do not restart the company definition here, overstate the benchmark, or add humor that competes with the evidence limits.

### S05: the company before the offer

- **Voice function:** Turn the unowned responsibility into a mature company. Explain what the operator keeps, judges, recommends, and refuses before discussing the smallest invoice.
- **Narrator reaction:** Decisive. The investigation is over; this is where Manav rules on what the company is and what it is not.
- **Analogy or ordinary detail:** Agreed questions, recorded answers and sources, client-approved facts, and the four decisions. Develop the doorway analogy only to clarify that the operator records and interprets the view rather than controlling it.
- **Cadence:** Use one worked-through explanation of the continuing responsibility, then short landings for the value, the no-action possibility, and human accountability. Avoid a chain of polished negate-and-replace slogans.
- **Objection to honor:** A weekly score, screenshot pile, or calendar-based monitoring cycle can be useful only if it serves another real decision. The operator does not control an answer and does not impersonate a specialist.
- **Transition intent:** The company has become a judgment function. S06 inherits that dependency and asks what kind of experience lets one operator own it responsibly.
- **Guardrail:** Do not reduce the company to the diagnostic, repeat S04's component inventory, or claim categorical AI incapacity.

### S06: why the operator's experience matters

- **Voice function:** Help the viewer recognize transferable judgment in work they may already know. Turn prior context or access into a practical advantage without pretending every professional qualifies.
- **Narrator reaction:** Respect for experienced judgment, with impatience toward the idea that buying software creates expertise.
- **Analogy or ordinary detail:** A result can be interesting but useless; a source can look strong while answering the wrong question; a buyer still needs a decision. Use those existing distinctions rather than inventing a personal client story.
- **Cadence:** Speak directly to the viewer. Let a practical question lead into the answer, or let the thought work itself out from evidence to judgment. Any first-person reaction must come from the owner talk-through and introduce no biography.
- **Objection to honor:** Software can collect much of the material. Experience alone is not enough without an understood buyer decision and a credible path to the buyer.
- **Transition intent:** Experience is valuable but invisible. S07 inherits it by packaging the smallest complete piece a first buyer can inspect and purchase.
- **Guardrail:** No invented credential, personal history, client anecdote, universal statement about professionals, or travel prescription.

### S07: the first purchase

- **Voice function:** Bring the large company down to one inspectable transaction. Walk the viewer through what the buyer approves, receives, decides, and does not accidentally buy.
- **Narrator reaction:** Protective skepticism on behalf of the first buyer and firm conviction that `leave it alone` must be a valid outcome.
- **Analogy or ordinary detail:** One brand, one market, ten approved questions, two surfaces, a dated record, the four decisions, and the first invoice boundary. The offer should feel like work someone can point to, not a service-category diagram.
- **Cadence:** Move through the job in causal order, not checklist cadence: decision before collection, agreed questions before evidence, evidence before classification, classification before completion. Let the no-action ruling land before any comic reaction.
- **Objection to honor:** A strange answer is not automatically a paid problem. The buyer should not hand over an entire function at the first purchase. Implementation and monitoring remain separate.
- **Transition intent:** Once the offer is bounded, the practical missing piece is a reachable buyer. S08 inherits the offer and asks where the operator has earned a right to make the ask.
- **Guardrail:** Keep the test qualifier before every count. Do not imply buyer behavior, demand, a guaranteed action, or hidden expansion.

### S08: choose the market you can enter

- **Voice function:** Give the viewer a reusable selection method and push back on choosing a market because AI makes it look fashionable.
- **Narrator reaction:** Practical impatience with abstract market enthusiasm, followed by grounded encouragement to use real experience, adjacent work, or relationships.
- **Analogy or ordinary detail:** The person the operator can actually call or email, the language they already understand, a named list of prospects, and a three-question public preview. Travel appears only after the reusable method is clear.
- **Cadence:** Give direct advice, then instantiate it with the travel example. Keep the outreach sequence conversational: preview, decision question, owner, paid ask. Do not make the numbered plan audible as a deck.
- **Objection to honor:** Access is not demand. A busy category, product launch, or interesting AI trend does not substitute for a buyer with a consequential decision.
- **Transition intent:** The operator now has a plausible route to a buyer. S09 inherits the route and tests whether one owner can carry the price, hours, selling, support, and reach.
- **Guardrail:** Travel remains an example and arithmetic carrier. Do not tell every viewer to enter travel or treat the 25-brand list as market size.

### S09: make the numbers work aloud

- **Voice function:** Work the model out beside the listener, showing why each layer is being calculated and where a tidy answer still fails to prove a company.
- **Narrator reaction:** Transparent interest in whether the arithmetic fits, then healthy skepticism toward the cooperative spreadsheet when acquisition, support, exceptions, and actual delivery remain unknown.
- **Analogy or ordinary detail:** Use the owner's invoice and calendar as the physical anchors. Money is allocated, hours are consumed, and the remaining calendar has to hold everything the delivery line omitted.
- **Cadence:** This scene needs a real talk-through rather than a rewritten table. Group the validation case, working case, annual requirement, capacity, and reach into distinct decisions. Within each, explain the purpose, give the numbers, and land the consequence. Use casual money language only where it preserves the exact amount and model boundary. Give every remainder and exclusion room to be heard.
- **Objection to honor:** The model is not a forecast. Price, demand, acquisition, delivery time, support, tax, unlisted costs, specialist work, and reachability remain unknown. A calendar that fits arithmetically may still fail operationally.
- **Transition intent:** End on the gap between arithmetic and reality. S10 inherits the named unknowns and turns them into concrete failure and refusal conditions.
- **Guardrail:** Preserve every E001-E011 value and relationship. Do not round, improvise, self-correct a number, call a case typical or realistic, or let humor obscure an exclusion.

### S10: know what breaks

- **Voice function:** Give the viewer permission to refuse a bad version of the business. Convert the unresolved assumptions into observable reasons to narrow, reprice, bring in a specialist, or stop.
- **Narrator reaction:** This is the clearest place for dry contempt: not for people, but for outcome promises, activity fees, and a supposedly solo report that quietly contains an agency.
- **Analogy or ordinary detail:** Let the report visibly accrete research, content, public relations, legal review, and software support. The hidden team and overloaded invoice make scope drift tangible.
- **Cadence:** Accumulate the scope one recognizable responsibility at a time, pause when the business-of-one boundary breaks, then land the refusal rule. Keep the other failure modes direct and unsensational.
- **Objection to honor:** Some buyers may correctly see ordinary SEO, some tools may supply enough of the decision, samples may be unstable, and specialists may be necessary. Those are valid reasons to narrow or stop, not objections to wave away.
- **Transition intent:** Every named failure becomes a test requirement. S11 inherits those requirements and turns them into assets, conversations, measured work, and the continuation gate.
- **Guardrail:** No contempt toward the buyer, worker, tool vendor, or specialist. Do not preserve a polished wisecrack if it overwhelms the stop rule.

### S11: the first 30 and 90 days

- **Voice function:** Change the episode from advice into construction. Tell the operator what to make, whom to speak with, what to ask for, and what must be observed before continuing.
- **Narrator reaction:** Pragmatic momentum. The uncertainty is no longer a reason to hedge; it is the reason to run a bounded test.
- **Analogy or ordinary detail:** Question template, evidence record, firm scope, public preview, prospect list, paid ask, time log, cost record, buyer response. These are the pieces the operator can build and hold.
- **Cadence:** Move through action and consequence rather than reading a policy. Separate the continuation conditions so each can be understood, but make clear that all remain required together. Let the paid-ask sequence sound operationally unambiguous.
- **Objection to honor:** Buyer interest without payment is not paid demand. A budget-backed condition is still conditional. Manual delivery must remain responsible and repeatable inside the 24-hour boundary.
- **Transition intent:** The operator has now replaced abstract risk with a test and a decision gate. Return through the explicit green-report callback into S12.
- **Guardrail:** Preserve the exact gate, count status, `ask first, deliver second` sequence, and funded-work boundary. Do not turn actions into expected results.

### S12: the report can stay green

- **Voice function:** Resolve the opening image, make the BUILD recommendation feel earned, and leave the viewer with the first investable action rather than a disclaimer or abstraction.
- **Narrator reaction:** Calm conviction. The narrator is satisfied that the old report and new company can coexist, while remaining clear that the buyer and paid work decide whether the model earns expansion.
- **Analogy or ordinary detail:** Return to the same green report and two doorways. Then move to one paid diagnostic, one reachable market, and the buyer's real decision. Do not introduce a new image at the finish.
- **Cadence:** Callback first, company value second, first action third, broader business-of-one principle fourth, exact CTA last. Give each landing space. Avoid a pile of polished closing slogans or a last-minute caution block.
- **Objection to honor:** BUILD authorizes construction, not guaranteed demand, sustainability, income, or outcome. The company continues only when buyers pay for judgment and the work repeats responsibly.
- **Transition intent:** Complete the episode. The CTA follows the delivered value; it does not tease an unlocked topic or reopen the case.
- **Guardrail:** Preserve the closing payload and exact CTA. No new evidence, joke, question, hypothetical retreat, or earnings language.

## Seam performance map

The scene labels must disappear in audio. Each seam has one conversational inheritance:

| Seam | Spoken inheritance |
|---|---|
| S00 to S02 across S01 | The blind spot has become a paid-diagnostic possibility; the show promise names the company that will explain it. |
| S02 to S03 | To define what the promised company owns, follow the customer through the changed research path. |
| S03 to S04 | The second doorway is observable; determine where existing collection and measurement stop. |
| S04 to S05 | The components reach evidence but not the decision; name the company around that missing responsibility. |
| S05 to S06 | The company depends on judgment; explain where one operator earns it. |
| S06 to S07 | Experience is not inspectable by itself; package its smallest complete buyer-facing unit. |
| S07 to S08 | The offer exists; find a market and buyer the operator can credibly reach. |
| S08 to S09 | Access creates a route, not a sustainable operating model; test the math and capacity. |
| S09 to S10 | The arithmetic fits while reality remains unknown; expose the failure modes. |
| S10 to S11 | The failures define the test; turn each one into construction and evidence. |
| S11 to S12 | The operator has a bounded test and stop rules; return to the opening report and rule BUILD. |

A seam fails when the next scene could begin unchanged after any unrelated scene. A discourse marker, recap, rhetorical question, or chapter announcement is not a transition unless it carries the specific object or consequence above.

## Drafting and performance method

1. Talk through the scene map from the immutable job and claim rail, not from v0.8 sentences. Record the session.
2. Transcribe the talk-through without cleaning its reasoning rhythm. Mark the language that sounds natural, the places where Manav genuinely reacts, and the places where he reaches for an ordinary example.
3. Conform those source lines to the frozen evidence and structure. Remove accidental new facts; do not replace the natural rhythm with report prose.
4. Read the whole narration continuously with headings removed. A technically correct scene still fails when the narrator disappears.
5. Compare S04, S07, S09, and S12 in isolation because they expose the main failure modes: memo voice, checklist voice, arithmetic voice, and polished-closing voice.
6. Compare the full v0.9 audio against v0.8 without identifying which is newer. The pass question is whether one version contains a recognizable person reasoning, reacting, and ruling while preserving the same case.

## Voice-review questions

- Can a listener hear why Manav cares about the distinction, or only understand the distinction?
- Does the narrator work through at least some consequences with the viewer, or announce every conclusion after the thinking has already happened?
- Does specificity come from recognizable work and objects as well as model numbers?
- Does the fair objection alter the reasoning before the ruling, or sit in a disclaimer sentence?
- Does humor reveal a broken incentive or hidden scope, or merely decorate the paragraph?
- Do sentence lengths and landings follow the thought, or repeat one polished pattern?
- Do transitions inherit causal material, or simply restart the presentation?
- Is every moment of certainty earned by the frozen evidence or explicitly identified as Operator Economy's BUILD recommendation?
- Could any first-person wording be misheard as a biographical or client claim? If so, remove it.
- Would the script still sound like Manav with every branded catchphrase removed? If not, the voice is not yet present.

## Disposition rule

This map can support a v0.9 draft. It cannot approve that draft.
