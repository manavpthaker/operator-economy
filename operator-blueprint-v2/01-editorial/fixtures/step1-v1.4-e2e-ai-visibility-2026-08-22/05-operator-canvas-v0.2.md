## 1. Operator

Who could run it: an experienced search, content, digital-strategy, or competitive-intelligence professional who can design a sample, validate sources, communicate uncertainty, and work with a marketing buyer. `PARALLEL`, Step 0 candidate and analogy map.

The operator personally owns discovery, question design, fact and source judgment, delivery, buyer communication, and scope decisions. They do not need to train a model or pretend access to a tool is the advantage.

## 2. Buyer and beneficiary

Economic buyer: a marketing, content, or organic-growth leader responsible for digital discovery at a consumer-facing brand. `PARALLEL`, CLM-001 through CLM-007.

First test segment: consumer-travel brands with an existing search or content responsibility. `MODELED` test bench, not a proven winning vertical.

Beneficiary: the consumer using AI-assisted research to compare options.

Purchase trigger: the buyer can name a planning, factual, content, reputation, or measurement decision the diagnostic may change. `MODELED`.

Not a fit: a buyer demanding guaranteed placement, traffic, leads, or revenue.

## 3. Costly problem

The normal search report may remain accurate while an agreed AI-answer sample describes, omits, or compares the brand using another mix of sources. The buyer lacks a repeatable baseline and a responsible way to decide whether a factual or source gap deserves action. `OBSERVED/PARALLEL`, CLM-001 through CLM-006.

The financial consequence is `UNKNOWN`. The company cannot claim a recoverable revenue loss.

## 4. Offer and result

Mature company: a focused outsourced AI visibility-intelligence function for a small portfolio of brands. `SYNTHESIS`.

Short public category: AI Visibility.

Short spoken name: AI visibility company.

Plain definition: Helps brands understand how they appear in AI answers and decide what deserves action.

Entry wedge: a fixed-scope diagnostic for one brand, one market, ten buyer-approved questions, and two answer surfaces. `MODELED`.

Included: scoped question set, recorded sample, mention and description log, source and fact review, controllability classification, prioritized recommendation, and repeat-measurement plan.

Excluded: guaranteed placement, content production, public-relations execution, recurring monitoring, regulated-category advice, and implementation unless a later buyer decision and evidence justify separate scope.

Primary acceptance event: the buyer accepts that the evidence record is complete for the agreed sample and that each material item is classified as act, investigate, monitor, or leave alone.

Completion boundary: deliver the agreed evidence register, decision briefing, and repeat-measurement protocol. Future action is a separate decision.

## 5. Delivery system

1. Confirm buyer, decision, market, approved facts, permissions, access, and exclusions.
2. Agree on ten questions and two answer surfaces before collection.
3. Capture platform, date, answer, brand presence, material claims, competitors, and sources where available.
4. Compare material statements with buyer-approved facts.
5. Classify gaps as controlled, influenceable, outside current control, or too uncertain to act on.
6. Recommend action, further investigation, monitoring, or no action.
7. Deliver the evidence registry and decision briefing.
8. Repeat only if another buyer decision justifies it.

Human review: required for every material fact, source interpretation, controllability ruling, and recommendation.

Failure route: record unstable or missing evidence, narrow the conclusion, and stop the affected recommendation.

Recovery: rerun only the approved sample when the issue is collection failure. Do not rewrite an unfavorable result.

Revocation: buyer may revoke private-data access. The initial test defaults to public information.

Manual fallback: spreadsheet or database evidence registry and direct answer capture.

## 6. Capabilities and business-of-one split

| Capability | Software or AI job | Human responsibility | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Answer sampling | Capture and organize an agreed question set | Define sample and record limits | observed component |
| Source comparison | Extract and compare possible source patterns | Validate what matters and what is uncertain | observed component plus modeled work |
| Evidence registry | Preserve question, answer, source, date, confidence | Maintain provenance and completion | parallel |
| Brand truth set | Store client-approved facts | Buyer approves authority and operator checks use | modeled client input |
| Recommendation | Draft classifications and summaries | Own action, no-action, and refusal decisions | modeled service work |

AI compresses collection, organization, comparison, and draft summaries. It does not own permissions, factual authority, source judgment, exception handling, buyer context, or the final recommendation.

Specialist help is allowed for regulated, legal, technical, or category-specific review. Work is refused or separately scoped when that help would turn the entry offer into a hidden team.

## 8. Economics and capacity

Every forward-looking figure below is a fixture scenario inherited from Step 0, not market pricing, expected performance, or an income forecast.

Validation case:

```text
1 diagnostic x $2,000 = $2,000 modeled gross revenue
$960 labor allowance + $99 software + $300 listed acquisition and overhead
= $1,359 modeled listed cost
$2,000 - $1,359 = $641 before tax and unlisted costs
```

Working case:

```text
2 diagnostics x $3,000 = $6,000 modeled gross revenue
48 total delivery hours with $2,880 labor allowance
+ $198 software + $600 listed acquisition and overhead
= $3,678 modeled listed cost
$6,000 - $3,678 = $2,322 before tax and unlisted costs
```

Capacity boundary: the current entry model plans no more than two diagnostics per month at 24 delivery hours each, or 24 diagnostics and 576 delivery hours per modeled year.

For this fixture only, the operator declares 960 annual working hours, modeled as 20 hours per week across 48 weeks. At the required 19 engagements calculated below, delivery uses 456 hours and leaves 504 hours for acquisition, administration, support, exceptions, and specialist coordination. Whether that remainder is enough is unproven and must be time-tracked.

Recurring monitoring capacity: not included. It may be modeled only after one repeated sample changes a buyer decision and actual support time is measured.

Fixture-only owner-support target: $48,000 per modeled year before tax and unlisted operating costs. This is a declared planning target for testing the gate. It is not market evidence, expected income, a recommendation, or a public claim.

Available owner support per working-case engagement before tax and unlisted costs:

```text
$3,000 modeled price
- $99 modeled software allocation
- $300 modeled acquisition and listed overhead
= $2,601 available for owner labor and owner support
```

Required annual engagement count:

```text
$48,000 fixture planning target / $2,601 per engagement
= 18.45, rounded up to 19 paid diagnostics per modeled year
```

Capacity comparison:

```text
19 required engagements x 24 delivery hours = 456 delivery hours
24-engagement modeled ceiling x 24 delivery hours = 576 delivery hours
19 required engagements fit the delivery-only ceiling
remaining non-delivery capacity = 960 declared owner hours - 456 delivery hours = 504 hours
```

Defined reachable buyer set: 250 named consumer-travel brands that the operator could identify and contact across ten bounded prospecting lists of 25. This is a fixture-only reachability model, not a claim that all 250 are qualified, reachable in practice, or willing to buy.

Implied required share:

```text
19 annual paid diagnostics / 250 modeled reachable brands = 7.6 percent
```

The 7.6 percent is a planning hurdle, not a conversion forecast. It is high enough that acquisition and repeat purchase are load-bearing. The first test must show whether the operator can reach the buyer, earn a paid decision, and preserve the hours before any sustainability language is allowed.

Sustainability status: `PLAUSIBLE TO TEST, UNPROVEN`. The arithmetic fits the declared delivery and owner-hour ceilings, but no demand, conversion, repeat purchase, non-delivery workload, tax, unlisted cost, or income outcome is established.

Most sensitive assumption: a buyer pays enough for responsible judgment without requiring a promised outcome.

## 9. Risks, stop rules, and conditional expansion

| Risk | Early warning | Mitigation | Stop condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| No incremental buyer value | Buyer names no changed decision | Do not sell beyond the screen | fold into existing SEO or stop |
| Unstable sample | Repeat cannot support comparison | narrow method and conclusion | stop if no responsible baseline exists |
| Dashboard substitution | Tool supplies the full decision | sell no extra report | stop the standalone offer |
| Outcome pressure | Buyer demands placement or revenue | refuse the promise | walk away |
| Capacity drift | Actual hours exceed modeled scope | narrow, reprice, or refuse | stop expansion |
| Specialist dependency | Every case needs a large expert bench | narrow segment or scope | model is no longer a business of one |

Expansion ladder:

1. Paid fixed diagnostic.
2. One repeated sample only when another buyer decision exists.
3. Monitoring only when the repeat changes a decision and fits capacity.
4. Focused portfolio function only after buyer, delivery, support, and economics evidence agree.

The scope invariant is decision-grade evidence with explicit limits. Expansion is never automatic.

## 10. First 30 and 90 days

First 30 days: build the question-set template, evidence registry, public screen, delivery boundary, and prospect list. Conduct five buyer conversations. Run one consenting manual baseline. Track every hour and make one paid diagnostic ask.

Continue only if buyers independently identify consequential decisions, at least one accepts paid work or gives a specific budget-backed condition, and the scope can repeat responsibly inside the 24-hour delivery boundary.

Days 31 through 90: deliver funded work if the first gate passes. Record actual acquisition, delivery, software, specialist, support, and communication load. Repeat only for a live buyer decision. Add no recurring service until that repeat creates useful evidence and fits capacity.

Maximum risk: the bounded time and cash recorded in the approved test. Do not build a platform, broad agency, or recurring product first.
