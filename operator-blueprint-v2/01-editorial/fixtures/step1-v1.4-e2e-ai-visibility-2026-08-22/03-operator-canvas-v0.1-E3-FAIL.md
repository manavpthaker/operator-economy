## Complete-company hypothesis

Operator: experienced search, content, digital-strategy, or competitive-intelligence professional. `PARALLEL`

Buyer: marketing, content, or organic-growth leader at a consumer-facing brand. `PARALLEL`

Problem: conventional search reporting may not show how an agreed set of AI answers describes the brand or which sources appear in those answers. `OBSERVED/PARALLEL`

Mature company: a focused outsourced AI visibility-intelligence function. `SYNTHESIS`

Entry wedge: one fixed-scope diagnostic for one brand, one market, ten agreed questions, and two answer surfaces. `MODELED`

Result: a decision-grade baseline, source and fact review, controllability classification, prioritized recommendation, and repeat-measurement plan. `SYNTHESIS/MODELED`

## Delivery model

1. Confirm the buyer decision, scope, facts, access, and permissions.
2. Agree on the question set and answer surfaces.
3. Capture and preserve the sample.
4. Trace important sources and compare material statements with approved facts.
5. Classify what the buyer controls, may influence, or should only observe.
6. Recommend action, monitoring, or no action.
7. Repeat only when another buyer decision justifies it.

AI may organize and compare material. The operator retains question design, evidence judgment, permissions, exceptions, recovery, and recommendation.

## Deliberately irreconcilable economics and capacity attempt

- four diagnostics per month at 24 delivery hours each;
- a future monitoring portfolio, which this deliberately faulty Canvas assigns 12 clients at five support hours each per month;
- 40 monthly hours for acquisition, administration, and unplanned support; and
- 120 monthly owner hours available for all delivery and operating work.

The Canvas still claims one operator can sell and deliver all of it and that monitoring is the natural recurring layer.

These injected monitoring, administration, and availability figures are test variables only. They are not Step 0 evidence, script claims, public assumptions, or proposed production inputs.

## Failure analysis

- Price, labor, tools, acquisition, support, and capacity do not reconcile.
- The mature company and entry wedge are mixed into one simultaneous package.
- Monitoring is presented as earned before a repeated sample changes a buyer decision.
- The claimed business of one hides an additional operator's worth of labor.
- The model has no valid completion boundary or refusal rule for support expansion.
- The story cannot proceed because downstream narrative polish would conceal the contradiction.
