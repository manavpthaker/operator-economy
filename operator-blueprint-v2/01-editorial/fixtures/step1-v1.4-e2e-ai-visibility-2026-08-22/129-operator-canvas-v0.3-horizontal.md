# Operator Canvas v0.3: AI Visibility

Status: fixture candidate for owner review. Supersedes Canvas v0.2 for the v1.1 horizontal-pitch test. Not production-locked.

Evidence class: adjacent synthesis

## 1. Operator

Who could run it: an experienced search, content, brand, public-relations, product-marketing, strategy, research, or competitive-intelligence professional. `PARALLEL`

Earned advantage: understanding a high-consideration purchase, speaking the buyer's language, reaching the accountable owner, designing consequential questions, and judging whether evidence deserves action. Tool access alone is not an advantage. `SYNTHESIS`

The operator initially owns qualification, question design, sampling boundaries, source and fact judgment, recommendations, buyer communication, and scope decisions. They do not need to train a model, guarantee placement, or be expert in every industry.

## 2. Buyer and beneficiary

Economic buyer: the person accountable for search, content, brand, reputation, public relations, acquisition, or an adjacent discoverability responsibility inside a high-consideration business. `PARALLEL`, claims C001 through C010.

Day-to-day user: that accountable buyer or a small team supporting the responsibility.

Ultimate beneficiary: a prospective customer using AI to discover, compare, shortlist, qualify, or verify providers.

Purchase trigger: the buyer can name a consequential decision that an AI-answer sample might change. `MODELED`

Qualified buyer: meaningful purchase, researched customer journey, existing discoverability responsibility, accountable owner, and a decision the evidence may change.

Not a fit: impulse or foot-traffic-led business, no accountable owner, no consequential decision, or a demand for guaranteed placement, traffic, leads, or revenue.

## 3. Costly problem and why now

AI has added another doorway to customer research. Existing search and brand reports can remain accurate while customers form an opinion from a different mix of answers and sources. `OBSERVED/PARALLEL`

The buyer lacks a repeatable decision record showing what appeared, where it came from, whether material claims match approved facts, and what deserves action. `SYNTHESIS`

Potential costs include exclusion from a shortlist, weak qualification, stale positioning, wasted response work, and decisions made from incomplete evidence. Their frequency and financial value remain `UNKNOWN`.

Current alternatives include search reporting, brand tracking, public relations, reputation work, competitive research, AI-visibility software, agencies, and internal manual checks. Their limit is not necessarily poor execution. The missing responsibility is interpretation across those pieces.

## 4. Company, offer, and result

Short public title: AI Visibility

Spoken company name: AI Visibility company

Plain definition: a business that helps companies understand how they appear when customers use AI to discover, compare, shortlist, or verify providers, then helps the responsible buyer decide what deserves action.

Precise internal description: an operator-owned decision service that maintains a bounded evidence record across approved customer questions, answer surfaces, sources, client-approved facts, controllability, and action decisions.

Mature company: a focused outsourced AI-visibility decision function for a small portfolio. Recurring work is earned by another meaningful buyer decision, not the calendar.

Entry wedge: one paid fixed-scope diagnostic for one qualified company, one market, ten buyer-approved questions, and two answer surfaces. `MODELED`

Included: question set, dated answer sample, material claims, competitors and sources where available, comparison with client-approved facts, controllability classification, and act/investigate/monitor/leave-alone recommendation.

Excluded: guaranteed placement or outcomes, content or public-relations execution, regulated advice, implementation, software build, and recurring monitoring.

Acceptance event: the buyer accepts the evidence record as complete for the agreed sample and can evaluate every material item through one of the four decision states.

Completion boundary: delivery of the record, briefing, and repeat-measurement protocol. Implementation or another measurement is a separate purchase decision.

## 5. Delivery system

1. Qualify the buyer, purchase, responsible owner, and decision.
2. Approve the market, ten questions, two answer surfaces, client facts, permissions, and exclusions.
3. Capture dated answers, material claims, competitors, and available sources.
4. Compare important statements with buyer-approved facts.
5. Classify each material issue as controlled, influenceable, outside current control, or too uncertain.
6. Recommend act, investigate, monitor, or leave alone.
7. Deliver the evidence record and decision briefing.
8. Repeat only when another buyer decision justifies it.

Human review is required for question relevance, factual authority, source meaning, uncertainty, controllability, recommendation, and refusal.

Failure route: record unstable or missing evidence, narrow the conclusion, and stop the affected recommendation. Never rewrite an unfavorable result.

Access boundary: the initial diagnostic defaults to public information plus client-approved facts. Private access is revocable and must be separately authorized.

Manual fallback: a spreadsheet or database evidence register with direct answer capture.

AI compresses collection, organization, comparison, and drafting. The operator retains permissions, buyer context, factual authority, judgment, accountability, exceptions, and the recommendation.

Bounded specialist help is allowed for legal, regulated, technical, or category-specific review. If most engagements require a bench of specialists, the segment or offer fails the business-of-one boundary.

## 7. Go-to-market path

First segment: one high-consideration market the operator understands and can reach. No fixture industry is the universal recommendation. `MODELED`

First prospect set: 25 named companies meeting the buyer rule, drawn from the operator's experience, relationships, adjacent work, or credible access. Qualification is required before counting a company as reachable.

Buyer signal: accountable discoverability owner plus a customer question that could change a shortlist, qualification, recommendation, or verification decision.

Credibility artifact: a three-question public-information preview labeled as a sample, not a diagnosis.

First conversation: ask which customer decision matters, who owns it, and what the evidence could change.

First paid ask: the ten-question diagnostic across two answer surfaces after the buyer names a consequential decision.

Expansion: another diagnostic, implementation, or monitoring only after the prior work produces a separate funded decision and fits capacity.

Acquisition time, acceptance rate, sales cycle, and repeat purchase remain `UNKNOWN`.

## 8. Market and opportunity argument

Observed or adjacent operating layers:

1. Companies already fund search, content, brand, reputation, public relations, competitive research, and acquisition responsibilities.
2. A major consultancy is staffing related AI-search client work.
3. An established visibility platform reports emerging paid AI-product activity.
4. Specialist platforms report funding and organizational adoption.
5. Agencies package and deliver related services.

OE synthesis: a smaller operator can own the decision record between measurement and buyer action.

What this proves: the underlying responsibility has budgets, multiple operating layers exist, and organizations are paying for adjacent products and services.

What it does not prove: demand for this diagnostic, accepted price, representative outcome, recurring purchase, required prospect pool, sustainable economics, or income.

## 10. Risks and stop rules

| Risk | Early warning | Response | Stop condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| no incremental value | buyer names no changed decision | do not sell beyond preview | fold into existing work or stop |
| unstable evidence | repeat cannot support comparison | narrow method and conclusion | no responsible baseline |
| software substitution | tool provides sufficient interpretation | sell no redundant report | no standalone operator value |
| outcome pressure | buyer demands placement, leads, or revenue | refuse promise | walk away |
| scope expansion | work absorbs content, PR, legal, implementation, and support | separate or refuse scope | hidden team required |
| capacity drift | actual hours exceed model | narrow, reprice, or stop | business-of-one boundary fails |
| weak acquisition | qualified conversations do not become paid decisions | change segment or offer | continuation gate fails |
| false recurring model | another sample changes no decision | do not sell calendar monitoring | no repeated responsibility |

## 11. First 30 and 90 days

First 30 days: build the question template, evidence record, scope, three-question preview, and qualified prospect list. Hold five qualified buyer conversations. Ask what decision the evidence could change and who owns it. Make the paid ask before delivery.

Continue only when:

1. at least three of five buyers independently describe the problem as consequential;
2. at least one accepts paid work or names a specific budget-backed approval condition; and
3. manual delivery creates repeatable evidence within 24 hours.

If the gate fails, revise the segment or offer, or stop.

Days 31 through 90: perform funded work only, record acquisition and delivery time, costs, support, specialist help, and buyer decisions, then repeat for another qualified buyer. Add monitoring only when another measurement changes a decision.
