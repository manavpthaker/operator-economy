## Positive findings that are not blockers

### Opening and opportunity

The move from `Your hotel is missing` to the green dashboard is concrete and direct. `The dashboard isn't broken` preserves the fair incumbent case. S03 explains the ordinary search chain before the source change and keeps the McKinsey population bounded. The two-doorway analogy remains episode-specific and returns with more meaning.

### Operator build

S06, S08, S10, and S11 now sound like advice to a capable professional rather than instructions in a business memo. `If your judgment adds nothing, stop` and `Don't start by proving you can build a nice website` are clear commercial rulings. Travel remains the illustration rather than the prescribed market.

### Comedy and contempt

The humor remains restrained and points at an incentive or operating absurdity:

- S07's report that always becomes `the next sales call` exposes compromised judgment.
- S10's `team wearing one invoice` makes hidden scope memorable.
- S11's website line rejects performative setup in favor of buyer work.

`A team wearing one invoice` is visibly authored, but it earns its place by compressing the exact scope failure. It is not a blocker. No joke targets the viewer, buyer, worker, customer, source, or under-resourced operator.

### Biography and first person

No narrator biography, client history, credential, or personal result is invented. `This is why I wouldn't tell you...`, `this is where I want to be clear`, and `If I were starting it...` express present reasoning or hypothetical advice. They do not claim past experience.

## Word-changing blockers

### 1. Repeated `Okay` scene resets

S05 opens:

> Okay, so now we can name the company.

S09 opens:

> Okay. Now put money around the test and remember that you're the one doing the work.

The proposed control explicitly fails reuse of the same discourse marker as a scene-opening device. S09 is also the weaker causal seam: S08 ends on whether the work fits inside one person's week, but `Okay. Now put money around the test` sounds like a chapter announcement. Reword the S09 opening so it directly inherits the one-person capacity consequence. Do not merely replace `Okay` with another decorative filler.

### 2. S04 stacks speech-profile moves until the technique becomes audible

The following sequence appears in one short passage:

> That's useful. It just isn't the business.
>
> So yes, you can study the pattern. No, you still can't predict what will happen for a client.
>
> You can collect a sample. You can compare it. You can watch it change.
>
> But here's the thing.

Negate-and-replace, yes/no contrast, anaphora, and a profile-listed pivot all arrive together. Each move is individually plausible. In combination they sound assembled from the profile rather than produced by one unfolding thought. Reflow the passage around the single reasoning job: components reach evidence but stop before the buyer decision. Preserve the benchmark/domain/no-client-result boundary.

The report-shaped sentence in the same scene also needs a conversational conform:

> One research benchmark showed that AI visibility can be measured, and the results changed from one domain to another.

Its meaning is valid, but `One research benchmark showed` still sounds like a source summary being read. Keep the benchmark status, domain variation, and no-client-prediction limit while making the narrator work through the finding naturally.

### 3. S06 contains an unnatural host instruction

> Think about the judgment you've already earned in your buyer's situation.

`Earned in your buyer's situation` is not natural spoken idiom and makes the viewer-buyer relationship momentarily unclear. Preserve the idea that prior work creates relevant judgment, but rewrite the sentence in words a colleague would say without repair.

### 4. Two S09 lines show the conform rather than the person

> Set aside 960 dollars for your time, 99 for software, and 300 for finding the client and the other overhead counted here.

`Finding the client and the other overhead counted here` is a claims-safe construction, but it is not a natural spoken object. Preserve the exact $300 modeled acquisition-and-listed-overhead meaning and incomplete-cost boundary while removing the mouth-level knot.

> So the requirement is 19 paid diagnostics. Not a forecast. The requirement.

The last two fragments are a conspicuous double-tap applied to a technical distinction. The distinction is essential; the phrasing sounds authorial. State the 19-job requirement and non-forecast boundary once in a natural ruling.
