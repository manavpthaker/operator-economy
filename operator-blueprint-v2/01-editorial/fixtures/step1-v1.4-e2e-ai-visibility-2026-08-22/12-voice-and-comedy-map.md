## Company language lock

Public title: AI Visibility

Spoken name: AI visibility company

Plain definition: Helps brands understand how they appear in AI answers and decide what deserves action.

Internal description: AI visibility-intelligence function

Category status: proposed editorial label

First spoken use: `We're building an AI visibility company. It helps brands understand how they appear in AI answers and decide what deserves action.`

Prohibited first-listen substitutions: AI visibility-intelligence company, discovery intelligence agency, generative search optimization function, or any stack of abstract labels.

## Base relationship

Viewer: experienced search, content, digital-strategy, or intelligence professional.

Why the narrator can advise: the episode gives away the buyer decision, evidence boundary, delivery loop, economics, refusal rules, and construction test instead of selling a tool list.

What remains unknown: demand, accepted price, delivery time, conversion, repeat value, sustainable economics, and income.

Opening temperature: curious and mildly bemused.

Ending temperature: direct, commercially cautious, and decisive.

## Opening voice map

Cold-open details: steady traffic, green report, customer asks AI, company missing or stale, report still green.

Notice without explaining: two views of the same customer decision can disagree without either one lying.

Question: which view is missing?

Verdict withheld: no category, company thesis, or operator instruction before the identity break.

Identity and promise: exact brand string, short name, plain definition, and first-proof promise. Do not use `Today, we're...`.

Context runway: explain the old search chain in ordinary language, defend it, add bounded survey evidence, then source variation.

Earned reveal: products can gather evidence, but the decision about what deserves action still needs an owner.

## Reporting and operator plan

Carrier: marketing lead and green report.

Business mechanism: question set, answer sample, source and fact review, controllability classification, recommendation, and optional repeat.

Hidden consequence: a dashboard can record activity without giving the buyer the decision they need.

Portable rule: collection is not ownership. The company begins where someone becomes accountable for the decision.

Operator blueprint: sell the fixed diagnostic, accept no-action as valid, measure every hour, refuse outcome promises, and add monitoring only for a continuing decision duty.

## Thesis language

Opportunity thesis: `The second doorway can be measured. The decision behind it still needs an owner.`

Operator thesis: `The advantage is not the software account. It is knowing which question matters, which source deserves trust, and when the evidence is too weak to act on.`

Entry-wedge thesis: `The first purchase is one evidence-and-decision diagnostic, not a promise to improve the answer.`

BUILD verdict: `Build the AI visibility company. Start with one paid diagnostic. Earn every responsibility after it.`

What remains unproven: all commercial and sustainability results.

Opportunity-to-build transition: `So who should own that judgment? This is where your experience starts to matter.`

Mature-company-to-wedge transition: `Built out, the company owns the ongoing decision function. But you do not ask the first buyer to trust all of that. You sell the first part they can inspect.`

## Governing analogy

Family: two doorways.

Literal explanation first: conventional search reports one path while an agreed AI-answer sample may show another.

| Mapping | Analogy | Business meaning |
|---|---|---|
| What moves | customer through a doorway | research behavior and evidence |
| What blocks view | report facing one door | measurement boundary |
| Who owns consequence | marketing lead inside the room | buyer decision |
| What changes | view of the second door | evidence plus accountable recommendation |

First use: after the conventional search chain and source variation are clear.

Development: the diagnostic does not build or control the second doorway. It gives the buyer a recorded view through it.

Ending: the green report keeps watching the first door while the new company helps the buyer decide what the second deserves.

Excluded metaphors: radar, plumbing, flight deck, map, immune system, or any competing analogy family.

## Understatement and humor

Default: quiet recognition, not outrage.

Systemic target: dashboards or reports that turn collection activity into a claimed result without owning the decision.

Fair explanation first: current products and search reports perform useful jobs inside their scope.

Planned decodes:

1. After explaining no-action: if every diagnostic recommends more work, the operator has made a sales pitch with a clipboard.
2. After the economics: revenue on a slide does not deliver the client work.
3. At the capacity boundary: when one report quietly becomes research, content, public relations, legal review, and software support, that is not a company of one. It is a team wearing one invoice.

Cut any line that does not improve the mechanism or memory.

## Manav language map

Direct address: speak to one operator as `you`.

Ask and answer: `So what is the buyer paying for? The decision.`

Negate and replace: after the wrong frame is understood, `You are not selling control of the answer. You are selling a better decision about it.`

Conversational pivot: `Okay, now we have to make the model carry its own weight.`

Long thought: allow the economics and responsibility explanations to run with natural connectors.

Short ruling: `If the evidence changes nothing, do not sell the report.`

Aside: at most one mild self-correction if it occurs naturally. Do not insert one to satisfy a profile.

Markers to avoid as quotas: okay, right, here's the thing, double-tap landings, and stacked one-line verdicts.

## Evidence language

| Class | First boundary | Later treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Survey | attribute McKinsey, date, sample, and reported behavior | do not repeat sample unless population changes |
| Source analysis | say mix can vary by question, category, and platform | use `source mix` plainly |
| Adjacent service | neighboring SEO buyers understand diagnostic work | never transfer demand or price |
| Component | products can collect named evidence | never call collection buyer value |
| Model | `Here is the fixture model.` | numbers remain scenarios and non-public |
| Unknown | name buyer demand, total work, and reachability | convert to paid test or stop rule |
| BUILD | editorial recommendation | never use as validation evidence |

Disclaimer spiral to avoid: repeating `this is hypothetical`, `this does not prove`, or `this is not real` after the boundary has already been established.

## Cadence plan

Opening: medium sentences with one short unresolved question.

Context: patient explanation, then a breath after the survey.

Opportunity: alternate evidence, plain implication, and the recurring buyer.

Turn: one practical question and answer.

Build: longer causal explanations broken by earned refusal rules.

Economics: one case at a time, with a plain restatement after each.

Ending: return to the opening and land three direct construction sentences without turning the whole script into fragments.

Formal language to rewrite: represents an expanding divergence, decision-intelligence layer, commercially actionable evidence architecture, and any sentence that could live in a consulting report.

## Regression against v0.5

The historical complete script is a negative input for current hygiene, not current authority. It contains the banned terms `easy` twice and `simple` once, plus a `Today, we're...` episode opener. The fresh narration must contain zero occurrences of those terms, zero em dashes, zero semicolons, and zero unresolved report language.
