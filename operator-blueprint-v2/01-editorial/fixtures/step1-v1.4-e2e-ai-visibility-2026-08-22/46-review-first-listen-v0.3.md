## First-listen result in plain language

The episode is easy to enter and the main business is finally clear. A listener can repeat the central idea after one pass: the company does not control AI answers; it helps a buyer decide what the evidence deserves. The experience-led market rule, paid diagnostic, valid no-action result, refusal boundaries, and final BUILD instruction are also understandable.

The listen breaks in two places and drags in one:

1. S04 defines the company, then S05 opens by saying it will define the full company and explains much of it again.
2. S09 asks the listener to hold too many modeled numbers and three closely related cases before explaining which figures actually govern the business decision.
3. S11 says to deliver a diagnostic and then make a paid ask `for the same work`, leaving the buyer and timing of that ask unclear.

Each problem lives in the exact words. None can be repaired with delivery, graphics, or Step 2 direction.

## Blocking finding FL-001: repeated company definition

Exact S04 ending:

> The company records what appeared, checks the important facts and sources, separates what the client controls from what it can only watch, and tells the buyer what deserves action.
>
> You aren't selling control of the answer. You're selling a better decision about it.

Exact S05 opening:

> That missing responsibility tells us what the full company has to look like. Not the first report. The thing you're actually trying to build.
>
> The AI visibility company works with a small group of brands. It keeps track of the questions that matter, records how the agreed answers and sources change, and checks important statements against facts the client has approved. Then it connects the evidence to a content, reputation, planning, or measurement decision.

First-listen effect: the listener reasonably believes S04 has already answered what the company does. S05 announces the same answer as a new section. This creates the exact pitch-deck reset prohibited by the repair controls.

Required disposition: script revision. Give the mature company definition one clear home and make the adjacent scene advance rather than restate it.

## Blocking finding FL-002: S09 arithmetic overload

The scene presents, in order:

1. A $2,000, 16-hour validation case with four cost or remainder figures.
2. A $3,000, 24-hour one-project working case with three further figures.
3. A two-project $6,000 aggregate that doubles the working case and introduces four further figures.
4. A $48,000 annual owner-support target and $2,601 per-project amount.
5. A 19-project annual requirement.
6. A 456-hour requirement inside 960 owner hours, a 24-project and 576-delivery-hour ceiling, and 504 remaining hours.
7. A 250-brand travel set, 7.6 percent hurdle, and three unresolved assumptions.

The exact arithmetic may be valid; that is not this review's question. On a first listen, the listener cannot reliably tell why both the one-project working case and its two-project aggregate must be spoken, which remainder is used in the annual formula, or which two or three numbers should remain in memory.

The upstream load rule required one scenario at a time with a plain restatement after each. The first three arithmetic paragraphs arrive back to back before the first plain-language interpretation.

Required disposition: script revision. Establish a clear spoken hierarchy and move non-decision-critical precision out of the listener's working memory. The exact numbers and claims remain governed by the claims review.

## Blocking finding FL-003: paid-ask chronology

Exact line:

> With one consenting brand, run the ten-question diagnostic across two answer surfaces. Track every hour, deliver the record and decisions, then make one paid ask for the same work.

First-listen effect: `the same work` appears to have been completed and delivered before the paid ask. The listener cannot tell whether the ask goes to the consenting brand for a future repeat, to another prospect using the sample as credibility evidence, or retroactively to the brand that already received the diagnostic.

This ambiguity affects the episode's central practical action, not a minor production detail.

Required disposition: script revision. Identify the buyer and timing of the paid ask in the spoken words.
