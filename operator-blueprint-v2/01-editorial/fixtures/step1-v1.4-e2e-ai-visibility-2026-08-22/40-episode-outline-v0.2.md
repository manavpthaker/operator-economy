## Sequence detail

### S00: the report is green

Viewer enters through one travel-marketing lead. Search traffic is steady, then the buyer sees an AI answer that omits the company or relies on stale information. The report remains green.

Do not ask three broad mystery questions. Focus the tension: the customer used a research path the report did not see.

Exit condition: one view of the customer decision is missing.

### S01: silent identity sting

No narration. Do not use the sting to erase the opening question.

### S02: show and company promise

Use the exact brand string. Name the AI visibility company and explain its job in one plain sentence. Promise that the viewer will understand what the operator owns, how to choose a first market, and what the first paid version has to prove.

Incoming inheritance: the green report cannot see the second research path.

Exit condition: before defining the company, follow the customer through that path.

### S03: the second doorway

Explain query, page, click, analytics. Defend the conventional report's usefulness.

Then introduce the behavior conversationally:

> McKinsey found that roughly half the people it surveyed were deliberately using AI-assisted search.

The next thought must make the limit natural: regular search did not vanish; customers added another research doorway.

The on-screen evidence receipt carries `McKinsey`, the report title, August 2025 survey date, U.S. population, n=1,927, exact 50 percent, locator, and C001. The description or show notes carry the full citation and link.

Explain that the source mix can change by question, category, and platform. If this follows the survey in the same passage, do not make the narrator introduce McKinsey a second time like another bibliography entry. Use the C002 screen receipt.

Use the doorway analogy only after the literal reporting difference is clear.

Exit condition: the second doorway is the opening. An opening is not a company until someone owns the decision it creates.

Prohibited ending: asking whether there is enough here to build a company.

### S04: components are not the company

Open from the preceding consequence: before naming the company, identify where the existing components stop.

Show what vendors can collect, what a benchmark can measure, and what neighboring SEO services establish. State each transfer limit once. Products prove collection. The benchmark proves bounded measurability. SEO proves a familiar purchase shape. None proves this exact buyer, demand, price, or result.

Keep evidence, interpretation, analogy, and humor from competing in the same paragraph.

Exit condition: every component reaches the evidence, but the buyer's recommendation and refusal boundary remain unowned.

### S05: name the complete company

Open by naming that missing owner. The company maintains the agreed questions, records the sample, reviews facts and sources, explains what is controllable, and connects each material finding to the buyer's next decision.

Name the AI visibility company in plain language. Describe the mature portfolio function before any diagnostic scope.

Do not repeat the component inventory from S04.

Exit condition: this is a judgment company. That makes the operator's prior experience more important than the software account.

### S06: why your experience matters

Ask what software cannot decide, then answer in familiar language: which question matters, which source deserves trust, which company fact is authoritative, what the buyer can control, and when the honest recommendation is to leave it alone.

Teach the viewer to begin in a field where they have earned context or access. That may come from direct industry experience, adjacent client work, or a network that can open real buyer conversations.

Travel remains the episode's example. It is not the instruction.

Exit condition: experience is the advantage, but the first buyer needs a small piece they can inspect before trusting the whole function.

### S07: first paid wedge and delivery

Bring the company down to one brand, one market, ten questions, and two surfaces. Define the evidence registry, classifications, buyer acceptance, completion, and a valid no-action result.

The no-action result proves that the operator sells judgment rather than a prewritten recommendation. Use the `sales pitch with a clipboard` decode only after that mechanism is clear.

Walk through the AI, software, buyer, operator, and specialist split without turning it into five job descriptions read from a chart.

Exit condition: the offer is bounded and buyable. It still needs a buyer the operator can credibly reach.

### S08: choose the first market and buyer

Lead with the reusable selection rule:

1. Start with experience, adjacent work, or an actual network.
2. Name the buyer decision in that field.
3. Name a narrow set of companies and a credible path to the responsible person.
4. Reject a field chosen only because AI makes it look fashionable.

Then instantiate the rule with the modeled consumer-travel example. Build the bounded 25-brand prospect list and three-question public screen. Ask what decision the evidence changes. Offer the complete diagnostic only after the buyer can answer.

The 250-brand travel set remains the fixture's later arithmetic carrier. It is not an addressable-market claim or viewer prescription.

Exit condition: the operator can reach a first buyer, but access does not show that one person can deliver the work sustainably.

### S09: make the model carry its own weight

Open from that delivery question, not with `Okay` or a chapter reset.

Introduce `the planning model we are testing` once. Preserve the validation case, working case, 24-hour boundary, 19-engagement requirement, 960-hour model, 250-brand travel example, and 7.6 percent hurdle exactly as approved.

Every displayed model number is labeled `MODELED`. Remainders and owner-support figures retain the audible `before tax and unlisted costs` boundary. Neither scenario is called typical, viable, conservative, realistic, or a livelihood.

Exit condition: the arithmetic can fit on a page while acquisition, support, exceptions, specialist work, and actual delivery time can still break it.

### S10: what breaks

Open by testing the specific unknowns the spreadsheet could not answer.

Cover buyer substitution, unstable samples, outcome pressure, specialist dependency, and capacity drift. Tie every risk to narrowing, refusal, or a stop decision. Monitoring is not earned merely because another month appears on the calendar.

The hidden-team decode belongs here after scope drift is concrete: when one report becomes research, content, public relations, legal review, and software support, the operator has a team wearing one invoice.

Exit condition: every failure has created a question the first 30 and 90 days must answer.

### S11: construct and test

Open by turning those failure conditions into work.

First 30 days: build the evidence registry, question template, delivery boundary, public screen, and prospect set. Conduct five buyer conversations, run one consenting baseline, track every hour, and make one paid diagnostic ask.

Days 31 through 90: deliver funded work only if the gate passes, replace assumptions with observed records, and repeat only for another buyer decision.

Do not present counts as expected results. They are actions and test boundaries.

Exit condition: the operator now has the assets, buyer path, paid ask, evidence standard, math, and stop rules needed to return to the opening.

### S12: green report callback

Return to the original buyer. The green report can remain useful while another doorway remains unmeasured. The company does not control that doorway. It gives the buyer an accountable view and a decision about what deserves action.

Land BUILD as construction, not proof.

Final direction:

> Build the AI visibility company. Start with one paid diagnostic in a market you understand and can reach. Earn every larger responsibility after it.

No new evidence, disclaimer stack, hypothetical retreat, or closing question.
