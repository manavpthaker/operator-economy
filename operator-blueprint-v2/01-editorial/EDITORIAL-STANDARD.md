# Editorial standard

Status: approved V2 Step 1 v1.4 authority. V1.4 incorporates the tested v1.3 opening and voice controls and adds the Episode Investment Thesis, opportunity-to-build architecture, build-only editorial rule, and short public naming standard.

## The job of Step 1

Step 1 answers one question:

> What company is worth building, why should this operator believe in the opportunity, and what is the clearest, most credible blueprint for beginning to build it?

The episode is not a company profile, a trend report, a software tutorial, or an earnings pitch. It is an experienced operator walking another capable professional through a business opportunity: why it exists, how it works, what would have to be true, how it could be delivered, and what to test first.

## The brand promise

Operator Economy exists to help a capable professional build, own, and operate a sustainable business of one using AI.

The core viewer may be employed and looking for an independent path, between jobs and rebuilding income, or already building and looking for a better operating model. The episode does not speak to all three as separate personas every time. It chooses one clear operator while preserving the same underlying promise: your experience and judgment can become an owned business, and AI can reduce the amount of labor and infrastructure required to run it.

The economic thesis is not venture scale:

> You do not need a huge share of the market. You need a reachable share of a problem large and costly enough to support one operator.

“Sustainable” is a design test, not an income promise. The model must count owner labor, tools, acquisition, support, delivery capacity, risk, and the number of customers required to support the operator. The episode must show how to test those assumptions. It may not tell the viewer that a livelihood is guaranteed, typical, easy, or immediate.

A business of one means one accountable owner can sell and deliver the initial model with AI, software, and bounded specialist help. It does not mean the operator must do every task manually, can never use a contractor, or may never grow. It means the entry model does not depend on raising capital, hiring a large team, or winning a large market share before it can become useful.

## What changes from Step 0

Step 0 decides whether an opportunity deserves editorial development. Its Canvas gate asks whether a complete blueprint appears feasible.

Step 1 authors that blueprint. It turns the approved parallels, evidence, assumptions, and caveats into an internally consistent Operator Canvas and a useful viewer journey. It may clarify and synthesize the approved material. It may not upgrade a modeled assumption into an observed fact or invent missing evidence.

## The Operator Canvas is the content model

The Operator Canvas is not a lead magnet assembled after the episode. It is the underlying model from which the outline and script are written. The video walks the viewer through the Canvas in a compelling order; the public Canvas later gives the viewer a concise way to revisit it.

Every Canvas must make the following legible:

1. Who operates the business.
2. Who buys and who ultimately benefits.
3. Which costly problem creates urgency.
4. What the operator sells.
5. What measurable result the buyer is purchasing.
6. How delivery works in practice.
7. Which tools and capabilities are required.
8. How the operator reaches and closes the first customers.
9. How pricing, costs, capacity, and contribution could work.
10. Which assumptions, risks, and failure modes matter.
11. What the operator can test in the first 30 days.
12. How the first entry wedge relates to the aspirational business and which proof would justify expansion.
13. Which work AI compresses or enables, which judgment remains human, and why that combination is viable for one accountable operator.
14. How many buyers or transactions the modeled livelihood case requires, whether that share is plausibly reachable, and which assumption must be tested first.

Each material Canvas statement must be classified as observed, synthesized from parallels, modeled, or unknown. Completeness is not certainty; uncertainty must remain visible.

## Decision completeness comes before narrative approval

A promising story, analogy, demo, character, or visual concept may reveal editorial potential. It cannot decide the business model.

Before Gate E3 passes, the Canvas must make the safe first transaction explainable:

- Who pays first and what triggers the purchase.
- What the buyer purchases first.
- What determines build, no build, acceptance, and completion.
- How delivery, access, judgment, exceptions, recovery, and ownership work.
- How the operator reaches a bounded first buyer set.
- How price, labor, tools, acquisition, support, and capacity reconcile.
- Which proof is required before the business expands.

Unknowns may remain when the first test can resolve them safely. An unknown is blocking when it prevents the buyer, offer, delivery boundary, acceptance event, economic model, or safe test from being defined.

Early narrative exploration must be labeled and quarantined. Preserve useful story material, but do not let it fill missing Canvas fields, become approved language, or pull the workflow past E3. A later Canvas pass never approves the early story retroactively.

## Pitch-deck logic, not a slide-deck script

The familiar pitch-deck questions are a completeness test, not a mandatory sequence and not a visual instruction. Every episode should address the problem, why now, solution, buyer, market proof, delivery, stack, go-to-market path, economics, risks, and first validation test. The narrative may reorder or combine them.

A strong story has:

- A protagonist: the potential operator or the buyer they serve.
- A desired change: the result someone is trying to produce.
- A constraint: why the result is difficult today.
- An opening: what changed enough to make a new operating model possible.
- A mechanism: how the proposed business produces value.
- Proof: what the approved evidence and parallels establish.
- A decision: what the operator must choose, trade off, or test.
- A resolution: a practical first move, not a promise of success.

The mechanism needs a human carrier. Use one recurring operator decision, buyer consequence, customer journey, transaction, or other concrete situation long enough for the viewer to see cause and effect. It may be observed, composite, or an archetypal illustration. Never invent an identity, metric, quote, fact, outcome, or proof for an illustrative thread. Require spoken disclosure only when a reasonable viewer could mistake the scene for a documented or measured case; routine labeling may live in metadata, a source card, or a visual label.

If an outline merely lists Canvas blocks, it is complete but not yet a story. If it tells an engaging story but cannot populate the Canvas, it is interesting but not yet an Operator Economy episode.

## The Episode Investment Thesis

After the Operator Canvas locks and before narrative development begins, Step 1 must approve an Episode Investment Thesis. The Canvas proves that the operating model is complete enough to examine. The Investment Thesis decides what company the episode recommends and why the viewer should commit attention, judgment, and a bounded first build to it.

The artifact keeps five layers distinct:

1. The show thesis: an experienced professional can use AI to build, own, and operate a sustainable business of one around a reachable share of a large, costly problem.
2. The opportunity thesis: what changed, why the problem is valuable, which market or delivery evidence matters, and what responsibility remains insufficiently owned.
3. The operator thesis: why this viewer's experience, judgment, access, or relationships create an advantage that tool access alone does not.
4. The entry-wedge thesis: the first bounded purchase that lets the buyer inspect and trust part of the larger company.
5. The affirmative build verdict: what mature company to build, where to enter, what the first paid work must prove, and when to stop.

The mature company must be named before the first offer becomes the center of attention. A diagnostic, audit, sprint, or installation may be the right entrance. It may not silently become the entire ambition merely because it is easiest to describe.

### Opportunity half and build half

The episode should spend roughly half its narrative energy making the opportunity worth believing and roughly half showing the operator how to build it. This is a pacing target, not a mechanical timer.

The opportunity half establishes the normal system, market change, costly problem, fair incumbent case, high-end or established proof, evidence limits, missing ownership, and complete company. The build half establishes operator advantage, mature operating model, entry wedge, delivery, go to market, economics, required share, expansion conditions, hard part, refusal boundaries, first 30 and 90 days, and final verdict.

Removing the first half should leave a recipe without a reason to invest. Removing the second should leave a market explainer rather than an Operator Blueprint.

### Build-only editorial rule

A published Operator Blueprint requires an honest `BUILD` verdict at the company level. If Step 1 cannot recommend bounded construction after the evidence, risks, and uncertainty are stated, return the candidate to the Canvas, request a bounded Step 0 amendment, or park it before production. Do not produce a blueprint whose final advice is to keep considering the idea.

`BUILD` is an editorial recommendation to begin disciplined construction. It is not proof of demand, accepted pricing, recurring purchase, sustainable economics, or income. A diagnostic inside the company may still produce a responsible no-build, refuse, narrow, defer, or stop decision for a particular buyer.

### Short public name, precise internal definition

The public and spoken company name should usually use two or three familiar words that a listener can repeat after hearing once. The next plain sentence explains the buyer and job. A longer internal operating description may preserve technical precision inside the Canvas and Investment Thesis.

Do not force the name itself to explain the buyer, mechanism, result, and business model. Reject stacks of abstract nouns, technical labels that arrive before a familiar picture, and names that reduce the mature company to its first offer. An owner-approved editorial label does not prove that the market already recognizes or buys that exact category.

## The report must orient before it rules

Pitch-deck completeness does not determine the listener's first two minutes. The episode needs a reported opening that lets a new viewer understand the case before hearing the main conclusion.

Every episode begins with these distinct functions:

```text
concrete cold open
→ silent identity sting
→ fixed Operator Economy brand string
→ episode-specific promise
→ context runway
→ central question
→ earned thesis
```

The cold open is one recognizable person, transaction, workflow, customer consequence, or strange result. It ends with something unresolved. It is not the central thesis, a market definition, a statistic without context, or a compressed agenda.

The context runway explains how the normal system works, why reasonable people use it, what changed, who feels the consequence, and why it matters. The main thesis arrives only after the viewer understands the nouns and has enough evidence to evaluate the ruling.

The fixed brand string is:

> This is The Operator Economy, where we show you how to build, own, and operate a sustainable business of one using AI.

The next sentence is episode-specific. It names the business being examined and the useful result the viewer will receive.

The required `EPISODE-BEAT-SHEET` separates these opening functions before the sequence outline is approved. It also checks first-listen load, the recurring human or operating carrier, fair context, the earned reveal, and the ending callback. This is editorial structure, not visual direction.

## Earn the next commercial step

The business model must preserve the decisions a responsible operator would actually make. Do not let the desire for a complete offer force a sale before the evidence supports it.

When discovery or diagnosis determines whether implementation should happen:

1. Define what the diagnostic buys.
2. State the pass, narrow, defer, and no-build outcomes.
3. Name the decision and acceptance measure that authorizes implementation.
4. Price or scope implementation separately.
5. Add recurring service only when a real continuing duty exists.

This is conditional, not a mandatory two-offer formula. The requirement is that the commercial sequence match the operating logic.

## The entry wedge is not the business ceiling

Editorial specificity narrows the route through the episode; it does not automatically narrow the approved business ambition.

When the potential company is broader than its first offer, the Canvas, Investment Thesis, and narrative must distinguish:

1. The aspirational business: what the operator may ultimately build.
2. The entry wedge: the first buyer, problem, transaction, or offer that can be tested responsibly.
3. The scope invariant: the operating promise that remains true as the business expands.
4. The expansion ladder: the next possible offers or capabilities.
5. The proof required before each expansion step.

The episode should not give every future offer equal weight merely to preserve ambition. It should name the complete company after the opportunity case is earned, describe the mature operating model before narrowing to the wedge, then make the first offer concrete. Expansion remains a destination that must be earned, not a simultaneous package or inevitable roadmap.

Narrowing the entry point is a sequencing decision. Removing an approved destination, pretending the wedge is the permanent company, or presenting future stages as inevitable are separate editorial errors.

## Viewer transformation

Every episode begins with a defined starting state and ending state.

The viewer should move from:

```text
"I see a problem or trend"
```

to:

```text
"I understand the company worth building, why the opportunity matters, the
operating model, and the first responsible part I can begin building."
```

The episode is successful when a skeptical professional can explain the mature company to someone else, identify its weakest assumption, and understand the first bounded construction step and its stop conditions.

## Editorial voice is part of the exact script

Step 1 owns both what the episode means and the exact language used to convey it. The script must satisfy the universal truth and language rules in `content-os/voice.md`, the V2 long-form application in `VOICE-ARCHITECTURE.md`, and the observed Manav spoken-language patterns in `studio/config/speech-profile.md` before it can lock.

These sources do different jobs:

- `voice.md` governs universal truth hygiene, banned language, rhythm principles, and the underlying craft vocabulary.
- `SCRIPT-BEAT-RESEARCH.md` records the craft sample behind the V2 opening, orientation, reveal, and wording rules.
- `VOICE-ARCHITECTURE.md` applies those findings to V2 long-form: reporting spine, plainspoken surface, business context, restrained reveal, operator judgment, governing analogy, brand identity, and Manav cadence.
- `speech-profile.md` governs the observed sentence mechanics, vocabulary, conversational moves, and phrasing that make the words plausible in Manav's mouth.
- Step 2 governs narrator identity, vocal performance, capture, and audio. It cannot add personality by changing locked words.

The dedicated editorial-voice gate requires evidence from the exact script, an approved beat sheet and voice-and-comedy map, a clean first-listen read, and a non-imitation judgment. A script does not pass because it has many short sentences, polished verdicts, or good cadence metrics. A cautious script does not pass merely because every disclaimer is accurate. A script stuffed with signature phrases does not pass merely because it resembles the profile superficially.

The full contract lives in `EDITORIAL-VOICE-STANDARD.md`.

## Evidence boundary

Step 1 receives evidence; it does not independently certify it. The claims map must preserve:

- The approved source or Step 0 artifact locator.
- The evidence class.
- Confidence and provenance.
- Required spoken qualification.
- Allowed and prohibited wording.
- Any refresh or permission constraint.

New facts, numbers, companies, market claims, or legal assertions return to Step 0 for an amendment. Editorial convenience never overrides evidence integrity.

A script claim is new when it adds or changes a population, magnitude, prevalence, comparison, causality, typicality, demand signal, price interpretation, outcome, or legal meaning, even when the general topic already exists in the approved package. Plausibility and softer certainty do not create provenance.

When E5 finds an unsupported claim, remove it if the argument works without it. If it is necessary, issue a bounded Step 0 amendment for the exact proposed wording and missing evidence. Do not reopen broad research merely to rescue an attractive line.

Illustrative stories do not need independent research when they introduce no public fact. They may not receive fabricated evidence. Their status must be recorded in the editorial package, but spoken labeling is reserved for a material risk that the viewer could mistake the scene for measured evidence.

## Working promise versus publishing package

Step 1 defines one honest working promise that the cold open and script must deliver. It may record working title language for internal clarity, but it does not produce or approve the final YouTube title or thumbnail. Final packaging belongs to Step 7 and must remain faithful to the locked editorial promise.

## Definition of done

Step 1 is done only when:

- The Canvas is complete enough to teach and transparent about uncertainty.
- The Episode Investment Thesis separates the show, opportunity, operator, entry-wedge, and BUILD theses.
- The short public and spoken company name passes a first-listen test, while the precise operating definition remains available internally.
- The opportunity case earns belief in the mature company before the first offer becomes the center of the episode.
- The narrative gives material weight to both the opportunity case and the operator blueprint.
- The narrative has causal movement, not just section coverage.
- A concrete cold open, silent identity break, fixed brand string, episode promise, context runway, central question, and earned thesis are separately approved in the beat sheet.
- A new viewer can explain the subject, normal system, change, affected person, stakes, and episode question before the script reaches its main verdict.
- The script is natural when read aloud.
- The exact words pass editorial-voice conformity against the reviewed `voice.md` and observed speech-profile identities.
- The exact words also pass the V2 voice architecture for opening orientation, first-listen clarity, reporting structure, business context, conviction, analogy, understated humor, cadence, ending payoff, and non-imitation.
- Every load-bearing public claim maps to approved evidence.
- Observed facts and modeled possibilities are clearly distinguished.
- No unresolved placeholder, citation task, owner decision, or research gap remains.
- Every review finding has an explicit disposition and every accepted change appears in the integrated revision.
- The exact spoken-word count matches the clean performance read-through.
- The owner approves the exact script revision and its hash.
- The final script reaches an affirmative, evidence-safe BUILD verdict or the candidate is returned before production.
- Step 2 can perform the script without deciding what the episode means.
- The narration handoff is created after the editorial lock and records that lock's hash without creating a circular dependency.
