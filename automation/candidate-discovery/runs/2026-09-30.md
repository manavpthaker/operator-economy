## New lead: `DISC-2026-09-30-016` — AI-leveraged special-education (IEP) advocacy for parents

- **Change (dated):** the Education Department signed interagency agreements on 2026-06-15, announced
  2026-06-16, moving IDEA "grant administration, enforcement, compliance and monitoring" to HHS and civil
  rights referrals to DOJ (K-12 Dive, read). This follows the October 2025 reductions that cut roughly
  89–95% of OSERS/OSEP staff (K-12 Dive, CAP, secondary). A claim that OCR resolved 1% of pending
  disability cases in 2025 appears only on a Substack (not verified; not used as evidence). Effect for
  parents: federal oversight is thinner, so the local IEP meeting and the state complaint are what's left.
- **Audience/question signals (independent):** DCUM threads on whether an advocate is needed and what
  it costs; Quora titles asking what advocates charge; Trends 5-year shows "IEP advocate" and "special
  education advocate" seasonal (school-year peaks) with 2025–26 peaks at or above earlier years —
  relative direction only.
- **Problem-value signals:**
  - `residual` (buyer accounts, DCUM 2021-01/02, read): a parent paid "$2,000" for an advocate who
    "interviewed us, the kid, read the reports, attended two meetings", then $500/yr for edits (worth
    it); another paid "$3,500" and the advocate "basically rewrote the entire IEP" (worth it); one
    "$3,500+" not worth it ("just do it yourself instead"). Posts are 2021, older than ideal.
  - `problem cost`: DCUM 2023-09 thread notes one child got "60 minutes of reading intervention a week"
    where a similar child got "150 minutes" — services depend on who pushes.
  - Context only (directory seller copy): independent advocates $80–$250/hr, 10–15 hours per case,
    packages ~$950 (records review + meeting prep) to $2,500–$5,000.
- **Payment evidence:** found — `residual`, buyer accounts (dated 2021).
- **AI-change test: plausibly passes, to verify.** AI reads the evaluations, prior IEPs and progress
  reports against the child's data and state rules and drafts the parent's concerns letter — the
  records-review hours that make a case cost $1,500–$3,500. Before, that reading was billed at
  $150/hr; with it collapsed, one advocate could serve several times more families at a fixed fee
  near $400–$600 per IEP cycle. To verify: share of advocate hours spent on records review vs meetings.
- **Delivery boundary: passes.** IDEA lets parents bring "other individuals who have knowledge or
  special expertise regarding the child" to IEP meetings at their discretion (34 CFR 300.321(a)(6));
  non-attorney advocates practise widely. Due process hearings and litigation stay with counsel (most
  states bar lay representation; lay advocates' fees are not recoverable). Residual in one sentence: an
  unlicensed advocate requests and reviews the child's records, prepares the parent's written requests,
  and attends IEP meetings with the family; disputes past the IEP table go to counsel.
- **Substitute:** parent-facing AI tools — IEP Advocate.ai ($30/mo; explicitly no human, no school
  contact), ProIEP (free tools); free parent training centres (PTIs); My IEP Hero, an AI plus
  certified-advocate marketplace charging advocates a 4% fee (a sales channel for the operator more
  than a substitute). The residual — an accountable person at the table and on the follow-up emails —
  is not what the software outputs.
- **Switching question:** why would a parent pay an operator ~$500 rather than use a $30/mo tool
  alone, or pay a traditional advocate $2,000+? The DCUM accounts suggest the value is the person in
  the room; the $30 tool addresses only the prep.
- **Strongest existing answer / gap:** plenty of parent explainers, advocate directories, and AI-tool
  marketing. Nothing observed on the economics of a small AI-leveraged advocate practice.
- **Possible OE point of view:** the district writes the plan and holds the records, and federal
  enforcement just moved out of the building; the parent's check is reading 80 pages before a
  45-minute meeting. AI makes the reading cheap, but somebody accountable still has to sit at the
  table. Showable with a synthetic evaluation report and draft IEP; no guest needed.
- **Strongest invalidating question:** will parents with a $30/mo AI tool go alone, and do districts
  dig in against advocates enough that outcomes don't justify even a low fee?
- **Evidence still needed:** a 2025–26 buyer account of what an advocate cost and changed; hours
  split prep vs meeting; state rules on lay advocates (some states license or certify); whether My IEP
  Hero advocates attend meetings and at what rates.
- **Semantic deduplication:** no match. "IEP" appears in the repo only as TEFA funding context in
  `candidate-2026-09-28-private-school-enrollment-analysis-research.md` (CLM-001). Different from held
  `DISC-2026-09-28-015` (private-school admissions help): buyer (public-school special-ed families),
  counterparty (the district, with legal duties), and buyer accounts (mostly "worth it" vs mostly
  "waste of money").
- **Disposition: shortlisted, priority 2.** Decisive reason: a dated federal operating change plus

  boundary, and AI cutting the costly part. Recheck 2026-10-28; expires 2027-06-30.
