- Spend already happening (`residual`: towns pay independent codifiers for exactly this work; all

    (<https://www.alexandriava.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/072%20Cost%20of%20codification.pdf>).
  - Novi MI 2022: $36,650 CivicPlus full recodification, legal review then republish.
  - North Adams MA: $19,540 over two or more fiscal years; vendor says average recodification takes
    about 24 months (iBerkshires).
  - East Troy WI: General Code, $18,835, 12–18 months (mywalworthcounty.com).
  - Richland County WI 2021: Municode base $16,750 for an 814-page code, 10–12 months.
  - Greenbelt MD 2015: Municode not to exceed $13,530; last comprehensive update 1984.
  - Bancroft WV 2022: American Legal quoted $12,000 base, plus hosting and copies.
  - Sundance WY 2022: Municode basic package $10,800, 12 months.
  - Low end: Monticello IA (Simmering-Cory, $5,000 vs an $8,000 alternative, 2017); Louisville CO
    (Sterling Codifiers $4,850, undated).
  - Supplements: $21.50/page (Santa Ana), $23.00/page (Siskiyou County 2020–23), $18.00/page
    (Jefferson County MO 2009 bid), plus hosting (~$500/yr, Santa Ana).
- Buyer actions (procurement, not conversation): Isle of Palms SC RFP 2026-07 for recodification
  (issued 2026-06-11), objectives include removing obsolete, duplicative and conflicting provisions;
  Salt Lake City RFI SLCI27019 for codification services, posted 2026-08-03, responses due
  2026-08-27, and it **asks vendors about generative-AI capabilities** (Starbridge, SAMsearch
  mirrors; Bonfire portal not read); Shepherdstown WV 2025 RFP requires references from similar
  municipalities.
- `problem cost`: codes left decades without comprehensive review (Greenbelt 1984; Westfield IN
  "rendered insufficient" by later ordinances); vendor timelines of 10–24 months.
- Operating change: ICC merged General Code and American Legal Publishing under one brand, ICC Code
  Solutions, serving more than 7,000 communities (release dated 2026-07-16, read). With CivicPlus
  Municode (4,000+ reported by a third-party page), two companies now hold most of the national
  market. The release names no AI product and no pricing; no evidence found yet that the merger
  raised prices or cut service to small towns.
- AI-change test: **plausibly passes.** Before AI, recodification needed a firm of editors and
  attorneys for 10–24 months to read every ordinance since the last code, find what is obsolete,
  duplicative or superseded, renumber, cross-reference and index. AI can read the whole ordinance
  book and draft the reorganized code and the issue list in days, so one operator could deliver what
  needed a firm. To verify: hours per 100 pages with and without AI on a public ordinance set.
- Delivery boundary: **passes on paper.** Codifiers are publishers, not law firms; the town attorney
  decides legal conflicts and the council adopts the code by ordinance. Residual: an unlicensed
  operator delivers a reorganized, renumbered, indexed draft code and an issue memo flagging
  obsolete, duplicative and possibly state-conflicting provisions for the town attorney to decide,
  then keeps the code supplemented. Some incumbents bundle "legal review" by in-house attorneys;
  whether a non-attorney's conflict memo is acceptable to buyers is unconfirmed.
- Substitutes: ICC Code Solutions (General Code + American Legal); CivicPlus Municode (full-service
  and self-publishing); regional codifiers (Sterling, Simmering-Cory/Iowa Codification, Code
  Publishing Co.); enCodePlus (self-publishing platform, markets AI for planning work, unverified).
  No AI-native codifier found in two searches; not proof of absence.
- Coverage gap: no AI codification product from the incumbents in search results; the closest AI
  example is San Francisco's STARA project with Stanford RegLab (Daily Journal), a government project
  that cut reporting requirements, not a vendor.
