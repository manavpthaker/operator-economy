### `DISC-2026-09-28-013` — CAM reconciliation review for small commercial tenants — **shortlisted, priority 1**

- Question: small retail, restaurant and office tenants on triple-net leases get a year-end CAM
  (common-area maintenance) true-up bill, often much larger than estimated, with 60–90 days to use
  their audit rights. Audits have only been worth doing for large tenants. Can one AI-equipped operator
  make them worth doing for a $10k–$50k CAM bill?
- Sources (observed 2026-09-28):
  - Quora titles (SERP only, answers not read): "My landlord sold the property and the new owner
    doubled my CAM charge. What can I do?"
    <https://www.quora.com/My-landlord-sold-the-property-and-the-new-owner-doubled-my-CAM-charge-What-can-I-do>;
    "If I was notified February 16th of a CAM increase … can my landlord back-charge me …"
    <https://www.quora.com/If-I-was-notified-February-16th-of-a-CAM-increase-commercial-lease-can-my-landlord-back-charge-me-for-the-previous-December-January-February-and-March-in-Utah>;
    a third on a new landlord raising CAM without a new lease.
  - BiggerPockets, "CAM reconciliation: the charges tenants push back on most often", about six months
    old (≈2026-03), property manager in Calabasas, 8 replies, no product pitch. Tenants dispute admin and
    management fees, capital repairs billed as maintenance, unitemised lines, legal fees; a commenter
    shows gross-up math overcharging tenants; "tenant auditors" find these quickly:
    <https://www.biggerpockets.com/forums/32/topics/1281823-cam-reconciliation-the-charges-tenants-push-back-on-most-often>
  - Google Trends, US, 5 years, 2026-09-28: "CAM charges" climbs from ~20–30 (2021–22) to its 5-year
    peak (100) in mid-2026, ~30–40 now; "CAM reconciliation" and "I-9 audit" are sporadic, more frequent
    in 2025–26. Relative index only.
  - CAMAudit (camaudit.io, read 2026-09-28): an AI "CAM audit service engine" sold to CPAs, tenant reps,
    lease-abstraction firms and attorneys — "runs 20 checks, builds the report, and drafts the dispute
    letter", white-label, "You price the audit, not any recovery". Also offers an outsourced audit
    service. Price not collected.
  - LeaseParse (leaseparse.com): AI lease abstraction aimed at landlords and asset managers; free
    abstraction tier; prices not published.
  - Context only (seller copy): contingency lease-audit firms take 30–50% of recoveries or $2,000–5,000
    plus 25–33%; boutique minimums about $2,500–5,000 per property (camaudit.io and springbord.com
    guides); vendor claim of 11.4% average CAM overcharge in a 212-lease NYC sample (Stratafolio).
- Buyer / costly problem: owner-operators with one to five leased locations (restaurants, retail,
  clinics, franchisees) receiving a reconciliation they cannot check, with a short dispute window.
- Offer / outcome: fixed-fee CAM review per location. Outcome: each disputed line tied to a lease clause
  and a statement line, dispute letter sent inside the window, dollars credited or refunded.
- Mechanism: AI abstracts the lease's expense definitions, caps, exclusions, gross-up and audit clauses
  and checks each reconciliation line; operator requests the landlord's ledger and invoices under the
  lease, reviews findings, drafts the letter; the tenant sends it and decides whether to escalate.
- Why now: AI abstraction makes a small-lease review minutes, not auditor-days; insurance and tax
  pass-throughs pushed reconciliations up (search direction peaked 2026); a vendor now sells the engine
  to small firms.
- **AI-change test: passes.** AI reads the lease and the reconciliation line by line against it; before
  AI, a lease auditor's hours made audits uneconomic below large CAM bills, so small tenants paid or
  argued blind. To verify: minutes per location with and without AI.
- **Delivery boundary: passes, with a lease condition.** Lease auditing and dispute letters are done
  today by unlicensed auditors, lease administrators and CPAs; litigation stays with counsel. Some leases
  restrict audits to CPAs, bar contingency auditors, or make the tenant pay if the overcharge is under
  3–5% (Ruberto Israel & Weiner 2024 on audit restrictions). Residual: an unlicensed operator reviews a
  small tenant's CAM reconciliation against its lease, requests backup, and prepares the documented
  dispute for the tenant to send.
- **Substitute:** CAMAudit (sold to firms, so a tenant cannot self-serve the engine; its outsourced
  service is a direct substitute — price unknown); contingency firms with minimums; the tenant reading
  the lease itself. Residual is more than software output: records request, window timing, landlord
  follow-up.
- **Problem-value signals:** `problem cost` — tenants' own accounts of doubled CAM (Quora titles) and a
  property manager confirming tenants find gross-up and admin overcharges (BiggerPockets); `demand
  signal` — recurring "what can I do / can they back-charge" questions on Quora plus rising Trends
  direction. Context only: contingency pricing and overcharge statistics (seller copy).
- **Payment evidence: unknown — viewer's first test.** No small tenant observed paying for a review.
- Strongest invalidating question: do small tenants' leases give usable audit rights (window, auditor
  restrictions, cost shifting), and is the typical recovery on a $10k–$50k CAM bill big enough to pay a
  fixed fee?
- Evidence still needed: sample small-shop lease audit clauses; a disclosed small-tenant recovery with
  amounts; CAMAudit's outsourced price; state rules on who may audit.
- OE point of view: the landlord's accountant writes the bill and the tenant's lease is the only check;
  AI makes that check cheap enough to be worth running for the small shop. Showable with a synthetic
  lease and reconciliation, and public SEC-filed lease examples. No guest needed.

### `DISC-2026-09-28-014` — I-9 self-audit and correction for small employers after ICE's 2026 reclassification — **held**

- Change: on 2026-03-16 ICE updated its fact sheet "Form I-9 Inspection Under INA § 274A" without notice,
  moving more than ten common errors (missing date of birth, incomplete Section 2 document data even
  when copies are kept, missing first day of work, remote-verification checkbox errors) from technical
  (10-day cure) to substantive, fined $288–$2,861 per form with no cure. Sources: Morgan Lewis
  2026-04 <https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/04/ice-rewrites-the-rules-on-form-i-9-violations>;
  Holland & Knight 2026-04; Ogletree; Kutak Rock 2026-05; CUPA-HR (titles and summaries). Restaurants,
  construction and agriculture are about half of worksite audits (vendor summary, context).
- Offer idea: scan a small employer's paper I-9s, AI-check every field, produce a correction list the
  employer fixes under counsel's guidance before any Notice of Inspection.
- Problem value: passes, `problem cost` (per-form fines with no cure; Morgan Lewis warns an audit that
  finds errors and does not fix them can leave the employer worse off).
- AI-change: AI reads handwritten forms and checks fields at scale — but that is what the software does.
- **Hold reasons:** (1) substitute — Equifax I-9 Inspect (scans converted paper forms against 300+
  rules with remediation workflows), Mitratech Tracker (paper scanning service), EMPTrust (legacy-form
  audit) sell directly to employers; the residual is substantially their output. (2) delivery boundary
  unresolved — law firms advise audits under immigration counsel; correction choices touch
  anti-discrimination rules.
- Reopen when: small employers (under ~50 staff, paper forms) are shown to be priced out of or unserved
  by those tools, **and** the residual beyond software output is stated in one sentence that survives
  the counsel boundary. Recheck 2026-10-28.
