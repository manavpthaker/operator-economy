  29,185,448 signals and 46,959,744 concepts; signals sorted into opportunities (4.47M), enablers

  (`https://idealab.ai/how-it-works`, read 2026-10-20).
- Visitors (no account) get 50 searches and 10 opened briefs a day; the advanced explorer
  (`advanced.idealab.ai`, sources and lineage per idea) needs an account and was not used.
- Search filters: industry, detailed industry, capital to start, vehicle (`studio_build` "Highly

  (software, human-delivered service, physical ops, financial product, hardware, lab), team size
  (solo to workforce), time to market, market size, geography, signal type, and **evidence type**
  (consumer complaint, forum post, news, filing, patent, paper, standard, recall, web page).
- A brief carries: pitch, imagined buyer and who pays, why now, first build, 90-day plan, start
  capital, revenue at success, year-one headcount, team size, TAM and its derivation, nine
  self-scores (clarity, whitespace, differentiation, automability, severity, why-now strength,
  protectability, market, conviction), a model web check of the field, source publishers and
  evidence types, competitors with URLs, and risks. Briefs name the writing model (`gpt-5.6-sol`)
  and the checking model (`gpt-5.6-luna`).
- Its "elements of business" page lists 118 business functions in 12 families, marking physical
  operations "robotics-gated" and governance and liability "human signature required"
  (`https://idealab.ai/elements-of-business`, read 2026-10-20). That second family (tax filing,
  regulatory filings, insurance and claims, KYC) matches this scout's delivery-boundary test.

Findings:

1. **A venture, not an operator.** No brief assumed one person; all assumed $12M–$30M a year at
   success. With `teamSize: solo` and complaint or forum evidence, 8 of 12 thesis queries returned 0
   to 2 ideas; with `solo, team_2_5` and human-delivered service under $100,000, they returned 4 to 33.
2. **Software first, institution-paid.** FairBill sells to employers and universities, not patients;
   the IEP idea sells to districts, not parents. The Operator Economy buyer is usually the person
   with the problem.
3. **Thin, often foreign evidence recast as US problems.** Most briefs cite 1 to 4 sources. The
   photo-ledger idea rests on Japanese public-works photo ledgers; FairBill on Slovak news.
4. **The field check is useful.** Every opened brief was `contested`, `crowded` or `license_play`,
   and the competitors named were real and current when checked (below).

Conclusion: idealab ideas are search prompts and substitute checklists, not buyer evidence. Rules
recorded in the owner decision and added to `automation/prompts/oe-candidate-scout.md` and
`automation/prompts/oe-weekly-research.md`; helper at `automation/tools/idealab.py`.

## Ideas screened (idealab prompt → Operator Economy restatement)

### 1. Small medical-bill disputes for patients (from 1699455 FairBill)

- idealab assumed: a 2–5 person software company selling dispute packets to employers and
  universities, $180,000 to start, because "the amounts rarely justify hiring help".
- Restated: one operator with AI disputes small medical bills (unauthorized lab fees, telehealth
  no-show fees, small balances in collections) for patients, flat fee or share of savings.
- Test 4 (substitutes), from search summaries of seller pages, 2026: Goodbill takes 20% of savings,
  capped at $1,000, **no minimum bill size** (financeaidaily.com, 2026-07-27; coveredusa.org,
  2026-05-28); mediloop publishes a **$129 flat** fee per bill and a $69 EOB review, while Resolve
  takes bills of $5,000+ with a $249–$499 deposit (mediloop.ai, 2026-10-01, a competitor's
  comparison); MediBill Saver sells a $19.97 flat audit (medibillsaver.com, May 2026); Dollar For
  and the CoveredUSA analyzer are free. The idealab premise that small bills go unserved does not
  hold in the US.
- Disposition: **rejected** on test 4(a) (done-for-you at $69–$129 flat or 20% with no minimum) and
  test 1 (no buyer-side observation). Related: patient insurance appeals rejected 2026-10-01.
- Reopen only with buyer-side evidence that patients with bills under $1,000 are refused by or fail
  with these services.

### 2. Insurance claims-history (CLUE) error correction (from 853461 and 869492)

- idealab assumed: a 2–5 person dispute platform for motorists ($120,000) or homeowners ($75,000)
  paid from a diagnostic fee or a share of premium savings.
- Restated: one operator with AI finds and disputes errors in a driver's or homeowner's claims
  history (LexisNexis C.L.U.E.) that raised a premium.
- Test 4: the dispute with LexisNexis costs the consumer nothing (888-497-0011 or mail; 30-day
  investigation), and consumer-law firms advertise C.L.U.E. dispute representation with no fee
  unless they win (consumerattorneys.com, search summary; FCRA fees are paid by the losing side).
  Test 3 (unverified, needs counsel): a paid service to improve a consumer report may fall under the
  Credit Repair Organizations Act's advance-fee ban. Test 1: one news report (KSL, search summary)
  and one old forum thread title (AnandTech, SERP); no buyer-side observation read.
- Disposition: **rejected**: free dispute route plus free-to-consumer counsel, small one-off value.
- Reopen with buyer-side evidence that people fail C.L.U.E. disputes in volume and are turned away
  by FCRA counsel.

### 3. Construction photo ledgers and weekly site records (from 1494777)

- idealab assumed: a managed service at $400–$800 per project a month for regional general
  contractors, licensed to Procore in the end; its only source is one Japanese news story.
- Restated: one operator with AI turns site photos into sign-ready weekly records for small
  contractors.
- Test 1: no US buyer-side observation in 3 queries (two Exa, one WebSearch returned a Procore-hosted
  company SOP on daily logs). Japanese public works require photo ledgers; the US record that is
  required weekly and signed is unclear. Test 4 context (idealab's list, unpriced): Procore photos,
  Punch, Houzz Pro daily logs, Sub360, ConstruLog.
- Disposition: **rejected** as evidence-free for the US.
- Reopen with a US buyer-side observation and a US record that must be produced and signed on a
  schedule. Search prompt for a later run: weekly stormwater (SWPPP) inspection reports for small
  builders (idealab returns 425 matches, all permitting software for engineers).

### 4. Household bill audits and fixed-cost cutting (from 994767 and 1336358)

- idealab assumed: paid audits and memberships, $80,000–$90,000 to start; its own check marked the
  fixed-cost idea `crowded`.
- Disposition: **rejected** on test 4(a): bill-negotiation services that work for a share of
  savings (Rocket Money, Billshark, BetterBill, named in the brief; prices not rechecked this pass)
  already serve households, and the small-business utility audit was rejected 2026-09-28.
- Reopen with buyer-side evidence that households with billing errors are refused by these
  services.

### 5. Supplier refund recovery for travel agencies (from 1743672)

- idealab assumed: UK travel agencies, batched claims, ATOL context; sources Turkish and Portuguese
  news plus one CFPB complaint.
- Disposition: **rejected** as outside the evidence: no US buyer-side observation, and the brief's
  setting is UK.
- Reopen with US agency buyer-side observations of supplier refunds trapped beyond normal
  chargeback routes.
