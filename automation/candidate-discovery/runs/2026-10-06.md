## Ideas screened

1. **Continuing-disclosure (EMMA) filing for small municipal bond issuers (lanes 2 + 3) — rejected.**
   - Old-way spend found: dissemination-agent agreements in public board packets charge $1,250–$1,500
     per security class per year plus about $250 per event notice (Centerville OH, New Hampton IA /
     Piper Sandler, Sauk County WI / PMA; SERP renderings of the PDFs). EMMA submission itself is
     free (MSRB).
   - Why now: the Financial Data Transparency Act will require machine-readable muni disclosures, but
     no compliance date exists; final muni standards are expected late 2026 with effective dates
     "2027 or after" (Bond Buyer, GFOA FDTA page, Forvis Mazars; SERP). MSRB is itself adding AI to
     EMMA (Bond Buyer, SERP).
   - Reason: fails the AI-change test today. The job is a cheap, ministerial upload bundled by the
     issuer's municipal advisor or underwriter, and it runs the same without AI. No dated operating
     change. Reopen when an FDTA final rule sets a compliance date for small issuers' data tagging,
     and only if the tagging residual is not bundled free by advisors or by EMMA's own tooling.
   - Side signal kept for the next run (not a lead yet): small towns that miss filings usually do so
     because their audits are late after finance-staff turnover (Town of Gila Bend AZ document
     center; Welaka FL news report on missing audits and an outside firm; SERP only). Catch-up
     governmental bookkeeping to make a small town audit-ready is a separate job from the filing
     and was not screened this run. Next run: check the delivery boundary (audit is CPA attest
     work; reconciliation is not), state delinquent-audit lists, and AI-bookkeeping substitutes.

2. **TSCA 8(a)(7) PFAS reporting for small manufacturers and importers (lane 2) — rejected.**
   - EPA's final rule of 2026-04-13 moved the submission window to the earlier of 60 days after a
     future revised rule or 2027-01-31; the 2025-11-13 proposal would exempt imported articles,
     PFAS at 0.1% or lower, byproducts, impurities, R&D chemicals, and non-isolated intermediates
     (J. J. Keller, BCLP, Faegre Drinker, ALL4; SERP).
   - Reason: no live dated obligation, and the proposed exemptions would remove most small article
     importers — the buyer the idea depended on. Supplier-survey platforms (Assent, Source
     Intelligence, 3E) already sell the document-collection job. Reopen when the revised rule is
     final and still covers small importers or formulators, with a submission window set.

3. **School-based Medicaid billing for small school districts (lane 3) — rejected.**
   - Old-way spend found: Pasadena USD contracted Paradigm Healthcare Services for Medi-Cal LEA
     billing for 2026-27 at a contingency fee capped at 7% of approved claims (OUSD/PUSD board
     agenda, SERP); Frontline Education describes contingency and SaaS models as standard.
   - Reason: the job is already sold to small districts on contingency by established vendors and
     SaaS, and AI is not the work — claims come from service logs therapists already enter. Fails
     the AI-change test and the substitute check. Reopen only with evidence that small districts
     are refused by contingency vendors and that the unserved part is reading unstructured
     IEP/therapy records into claims.
