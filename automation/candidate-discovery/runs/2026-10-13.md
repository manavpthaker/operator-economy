## Ideas screened

### Cost-plus distributor invoice audits for independent restaurant groups (lane 3) — rejected

- Question: independent groups on cost-plus or contract pricing with broadline distributors get
  invoices that drift from contract (late-loaded contracts, data-entry errors, unpassed rebates,
  fuel and delivery surcharges). Can one AI-equipped operator check every invoice line against the
  contract for a small group?
- `problem cost` (context, trade press and seller analyses): Consolidated Concepts found at least
  one overcharge on about 35% of 11,000 invoices from 400 restaurants, averaging about 1.5% of the
  invoice and above 10% in some subsets (FSR Magazine
  <https://www.fsrmagazine.com/finance/overcharges-continue-show-restaurant-invoices>; Food Service
  Director); the US Foodservice cost-plus class action settled for $297M (alleged conduct
  1998–2005). All 2015–2018 or older; interest-bearing sources.
- Substitute: Buyers Edge / Dining Alliance GPO joins independents with no membership fee and is
  paid from manufacturer rebates; MarginEdge and similar invoice tools set per-item price alerts
  (MarginEdge help centre); contingency audit firms (Cost Reduction Management, sponsored NRN
  content); public how-to guides for auditing invoices with a general AI assistant (Vellum blog).
- Buyer voice: none found from 2025–26; no restaurant-owner account of catching or paying to catch a
  cost-plus overcharge.
- Delivery boundary: passes (no licence). Not the reason.
- Why rejected: the residual (line-by-line contract check) is what invoice software plus rebate-funded
  GPOs and contingency auditors already offer the buyer, and no current buyer voice exists. Fails the
  substitute check; problem-value evidence is dated and seller-sourced.
- Reopening condition: a dated 2025–26 buyer account of material cost-plus overcharges missed by
  invoice software, with independents paying (or refused by) contingency auditors.

### Medicaid work-requirement compliance and exemption help (lane 2) — rejected

- Change: states must start Medicaid community-engagement requirements for expansion adults by
  2027-01-01 (extensions to 2028-12-31 possible); CMS interim final rule 2026-06-01 narrows the
  medically frail exemption to conditions that significantly impair work (KFF
  <https://www.kff.org/medicaid/the-medical-frailty-exemption-from-medicaid-work-requirements-key-takeaways-from-the-cms-interim-final-rule/>;
  Foley Hoag 2026-06; AMA patient guide). Washington HCA provider memo dated 2026-08-21. ACP expects
  more documentation requests to physicians.
- Who pays: the enrollee (low-income, cannot pay); states (verification systems, ~$15M per state,
  CMS arranging discounted vendor services; six states already using AI — AR, CA, MD, MO, NM, OK);
  health centers and enrollment assisters (grant-funded).
- Delivery boundary: the exemption evidence that matters is clinical documentation written by
  licensed clinicians; states must verify from existing data (payroll, claims) before asking the
  enrollee. Residual for an unlicensed operator is enrollment assistance, which is grant-funded and
  free to the enrollee.
- Why rejected: no private buyer, the core deliverable is licensed or state-run, and AI is being
  deployed on the state side. Fails the delivery-boundary and problem-value (buyer) tests.
- Reopening condition: an FQHC, hospital or managed-care plan paying an outside, non-clinical
  provider per member for work-requirement compliance support.
