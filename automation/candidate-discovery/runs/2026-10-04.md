## Ideas screened

1. **E-rate filing help for small schools and libraries (lane 2 + lane 3) — added as held,
   `DISC-2026-10-04-018`.**
   - Change: the FCC adopted an E-Rate NPRM/FNPRM on 2026-06-25 (WC Docket 26-133 et al.), published
     2026-08-14; comments due 2026-10-13, replies 2026-11-12. It proposes a consultant definition,
     a new annual Form 5654 consultant certification, a consultant registration database, and a ban on
     fees set as a percentage of E-Rate funding (Cooley 2026-07-02, read; CRS IF13289 2026-08-17,
     SERP). It is also a "top-to-bottom review" of program scope, including screen-time and CIPA
     questions (CRS, SERP).
   - Problem-value: `problem cost` — small applicants (1–5 sites) filing alone had a 52.3% commitment
     rate against 75.4% with a consultant, yet only 27.5% of small entities use one, against 65.9% of
     large applicants (Funds for Learning, 2025-05-21, read). `old-way spend` — consultants are
     already named on FY2027 Form 470s of small private schools and libraries (USAC public data,
     SERP); fee levels not found. Payment evidence: unknown — viewer's first test.
   - AI-change test: plausibly passes. AI drafts the Form 470 scope, builds the bid-evaluation
     matrix, reads vendor bids and contracts, and answers PIA review questions; before, this was a
     consultant paid a share of funding, which is uneconomic for a one-building library.
   - Delivery boundary: no licence; if the rule is adopted, consultants must register and certify.
     Residual: preparing and tracking a small applicant's filings and bid documents for a flat fee;
     the applicant certifies.
   - Substitute: Funds for Learning's Form 471 Wizard and E-rate Manager (software, long-standing);
     established consultants.
   - Hold reasons: (1) the program's scope and funding are themselves under review, so the why-now
     could flip to "less to file for"; (2) buyers are mostly public (libraries, districts), against
     the owner's preference for private buyers — private schools are eligible but a minority; (3) no
     buyer voice on the application burden (Light Reading quotes are about losing funding, not
     filing).
   - Reopen when: the FCC acts on the NPRM (after reply comments 2026-11-12) without cutting small
     applicants' eligibility, and one small private school or library is found asking for, or paying
     a flat fee for, filing help. Recheck 2026-11-22; expires 2027-03-31.
