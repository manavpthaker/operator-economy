## Ideas screened

### Claims audit for small self-funded employer health plans (lane 3) — rejected

- Question: small self-funded or level-funded employers pay claims through a TPA or carrier and have
  ERISA fiduciary duties; the CAA 2021 gag-clause ban says plan contracts may not block access to
  de-identified claims data. Could one AI-equipped operator audit every claim for a 50–500 life plan?
- `problem cost` (search summaries, not read): employer suits over withheld claims data and
  overpayments (Owens & Minor v. Anthem; Kraft Heinz v. Aetna, 2023); Tiara Yachts v. BCBS Michigan,
  Sixth Circuit reversed dismissal 2025-05, docket active to 2026-07 (Georgetown litigation tracker).
  All large plans. Nothing specific to small plans found.
- Substitute: AI vendors already sell 100% claim review to self-funded employers — Bluespine
  (contingency on recoveries; $72M raised, MobiHealthNews), Avelis Health (YC; claims 3–10% savings,
  vendor claims), ClaimInformatics (flat per-claim fee). Prices not published.
- Why rejected: the residual is what these vendors output, and for small plans the binding
  constraint is getting the claims file from the carrier or TPA, which AI does not change. Fails
  the substitute check and the AI-change test for a small operator.
- Reopening condition: evidence that the AI vendors refuse plans under ~500 lives while those plans
  can obtain claims files, plus a small employer paying an outside auditor.

### Contractor prequalification (ISNetworld / Avetta RAVS) for small trade subcontractors (lane 3) — rejected

- Question: refineries, utilities and plants require subcontractors to hold a passing ISNetworld or
  Avetta grade, which means writing 30+ client-scored safety programs and answering the RAVS
  questionnaire. Small subs hire consultants. Can AI let one operator do it cheaply?
- `old-way spend`: forum accounts (rpls.com, 2017, read) of a four-person survey firm that "spent
  $$$$" and hired a safety consultant, and ISN-linked help offered "for thousands of dollars"; an
  advisory firm quotes after an assessment (price not published); PrequalPilot frames the alternative
  as a "$5K consultant" (seller framing, context only). ISNetworld subscription from $875/yr
  (Capterra, context). `problem cost`: maintaining a grade takes "hours"; some firms dropped
  clients rather than comply (2017).
- Substitute: PrequalPilot sells AI-drafted RAVS answers, a safety-program generator, COI parsing
  and expiry alerts direct to subcontractors at $299/yr founding price (Product Hunt, read; private
  beta). Avetta embeds a generative AI assistant (AskAva, 2024) for hazards and controls. Many
  consultancies sell template program bundles.
- Delivery boundary: passes (no licence to write a company safety program). Not the reason.
- Why rejected: the residual — tailoring AI-drafted programs to the sub's operations — is what the
  $299/yr tool plus the owner already does, and existing consultants will adopt the same drafting.
  Buyer voice found is from 2017. Fails the substitute check.
- Reopening condition: a dated 2025–26 buyer account of AI-drafted programs failing RAVS review
  while subs pay a hybrid provider between the tool price and the consultant price.
