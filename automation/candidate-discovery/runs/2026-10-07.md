## Idea screened

**`DISC-2026-10-07-020` — catch-up reconciliation to audit-ready for small towns and special
districts (lanes 2 + 3) — held.**

- Operating changes (dated, primary or near-primary):
  - NY Office of the State Comptroller town fiscal-transparency report, September 2026 (FingerLakes1
    2026-10-02, read): of 933 towns, 159 had not filed 2025 annual reports by June 30 and 181 filed
    late; 17 missed every year 2022–2025; in an 18-town review, 8 had incomplete or outdated records
    and 15 had no evidence of a 2024 audit. Causes officials gave: incomplete records, vacancies and
    turnover, need for training or technical help.
  - Tennessee comptroller declined to approve the City of Jackson's FY27 budget over a delinquent
    FY25 audit (WBBJ 2026-09-01, SERP); without an approved budget the city may not issue debt.
  - Oregon Secretary of State delinquent-filer list: 238 entities, 374 delinquent reports (December
    2025; office cites staffing and resources). South Carolina treasurer withholds state money from
    delinquent municipalities; Colorado withholds property-tax revenue from ~25 municipalities that
    did not file 2024 audits (CML). All SERP renderings.
- Problem-value signals:
  - `residual` — buyers already pay outside firms for exactly this job: La Marque TX, not to exceed
    $146,250 at $175–$295/hr to reconcile accounts and finish the 2025 audit (Galveston Daily News,
    SERP); Scottsdale AZ interim accounting at $210/hr, up to $75,000/yr, for treasurer vacancies
    (council packet 2026-02-24, SERP); New Hartford NY raised its accounting-firm fee from $4,750 to
    $9,200 a month while about two years behind (Rome Sentinel 2026-01-07, SERP); Watervliet NY
    one-year contract for bank reconciliations and year-end close (minutes 2026-02-11, SERP);
    Tiburon CA up to $250,000 to clear a three-year audit backlog (The Ark 2026-08-12, read —
    note the outside firm there finishes audits and fixes controls; scope is wider than
    reconciliation).
  - `problem cost` — withheld state revenue (SC, CO), rejected budget and frozen borrowing (Jackson
    TN), possible dissolution of special districts with three years of missed filings (Oregon,
    ORS 198.345), potential overpayments found where records lapsed (NY, $21,143 across 5 towns).
  - Buyer voice in a public deliberation: Hollow Rock TN board, 2026-08-18 (read) — chose 2–4 hours
    of recorder overtime over an outside firm's quoted $1,000–$2,000/month ("$10,000 a year") to
    catch up bank reconciliations that predate the current recorder.
- Payment evidence: found — `residual` (council contracts above).
- AI-change test: **plausibly passes, to verify.** AI reads years of bank statements, check images,
  deposit records, and the fund-ledger export, proposes matches and fund-level corrections, and
  drafts the adjustment schedule; before, the same reconstruction was CPA-firm hours at
  $175–$295/hr, scheduled around audit busy season (September–December, per Tiburon). Before AI the
  business looked like an accounting firm's outsourced-accounting line (Baker Tilly, CLA, Regional
  Government Services). To verify: hours to reconcile one month of a messy small-town account with
  and without AI.
- Delivery boundary: **passes on paper.** Bookkeeping and bank reconciliation need no licence; the
  audit, review, or compilation is CPA attest work; and Yellow Book independence rules make the
  town's own auditor a poor provider of the bookkeeping (2018 revision treats preparing records for
  an audit client as an independence threat needing safeguards; some services prohibited — not
  verified against GAO-18-568G this run). Residual: an unlicensed operator reconstructs and
  reconciles a small town's cash and fund records month by month to the point its independent
  auditor can start, and documents each adjustment for board approval. Caveat: some procurements
  ask for CPA firms (White City UT 2026 RFP; Westerly RI hired CPAs).
- Substitute: CPA firms' outsourced accounting at $175–$375/hr; Texas education service centres
  sell bank reconciliation to districts (public cooperative); the town's fund-accounting software
  vendor (Hollow Rock contacted Local Government Corp.); state comptroller technical assistance
  (NY). Small-business AI bookkeeping (Xero JAX auto-reconciles >80% of feed lines; HelloBooks,
  Pilot) is built for for-profit, cash-basis books, not fund accounting or multi-year
  reconstruction from paper — not a direct substitute, but it caps what a buyer will pay for the
  matching step.
- Switching question: why would a town move this spend to a solo operator rather than to its
  auditor's affiliated firm, its software vendor, or overtime for its own clerk (Hollow Rock chose
  overtime)?

  strong spend, but no independent audience/question signal — no Quora titles, no buyer-phrased
  Trends movement, no clerk or treasurer forum thread was reachable. Board decisions reported in
  local news are buyer spend, not a question surface. Also a public-sector buyer, against the
  lane-3 preference for private buyers.
- Strongest invalidating question: will a small town's auditor and procurement accept records
  rebuilt by a non-CPA solo operator, or does the backlog really wait on the auditor's calendar and
  the clerk's turnover rather than on reconciliation labor?
- Reopening condition: one independent audience/question signal from the buyer side (a clerk,
  treasurer, or finance-officer forum or listserv thread asking who to hire or how to catch up, a
  `site:quora.com` title, or buyer-phrased Trends movement). Evidence still needed after that: one

  current Yellow Book prohibited-bookkeeping list; hours per reconciled month with AI.
- Possible OE point of view: the town's books are a public trust run by one part-time clerk; when
