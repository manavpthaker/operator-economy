## Ideas screened

1. **Ownership screening for small US exporters under the BIS Affiliates Rule (lane 2 + lane 3) —
   added as held, `DISC-2026-10-05-019`.**
   - Change: BIS adopted the Affiliates Rule on 2025-09-29: any foreign entity 50% or more owned,
     directly or indirectly, alone or in aggregate, by Entity List or MEU List parties inherits their
     restrictions. Its implementation was suspended for one year from 2025-11-10 as part of the
     US–China trade deal; the suspension ends 2026-11-09 "absent further action" and further delay
     "is not guaranteed" (Willkie, 2026-09-21, read). Exporters become "affirmatively responsible"
     for determining foreign counterparties' ownership (Willkie; MoFo, Latham, Parker Poe 2026-08,
     SERP).
   - Buyer / problem: small manufacturers and distributors that ship to foreign customers and
     distributors (especially China, Hong Kong, Russia-adjacent markets) without a trade-compliance
     department; name-only screening on the free Consolidated Screening List does not reveal
     ownership chains.
   - Problem-value: `problem cost` — administrative penalties up to about $374,000 per violation or
     twice the transaction value; criminal penalties up to $1M and 20 years (statutory figures as
     restated in Beancount.io, 2026-07-20, vendor content marketing; context). Willkie notes BIS is
     "aggressively flexing its enforcement authorities". `old-way spend` — trade counsel retainers
     and enterprise screening subscriptions are the existing ways this is done (law-firm alerts and
     Beancount.io, SERP/read); no rate or small-buyer engagement found. Payment evidence: unknown —
     viewer's first test. Switching question: why would a small exporter pay an operator per
     customer file rather than buy a screening subscription or rely on customer ownership
     certifications in its contracts (the cheap step every law-firm alert recommends)?
   - AI-change test: plausibly passes. AI reads foreign-language corporate registries, filings, and
     shareholder disclosures to trace ownership chains and aggregate stakes against the listed
     parties; before, this was a law-firm or enterprise-data job (Kharon, Descartes, Castellum,
     Moody's Orbis) priced for large exporters, so small exporters screened names only.
   - Delivery boundary: no licence is needed to research ownership or to run export-compliance
     screening; the licence determination and any voluntary self-disclosure stay with the exporter
     and its counsel. Residual: a sourced ownership-chain file per foreign counterparty, with any
     listed owner and aggregate stake flagged and gaps documented, for the exporter's own decision.
   - Substitute: Descartes (ownership screening marketed for the rule; landing pages June 2026),
     Kharon via e2open (majority-owned-subsidiary dataset), Castellum.AI (share-based lists from 50%
     down to 10%). None publishes a price; all are sold through demos to enterprises. If any ships a
     small-business tier that outputs the same flag, the residual becomes a software output.
   - Audience / question signal: none found on independent buyer surfaces (no Quora or Stack
     Exchange titles; zero HN stories). Supply-side coverage is heavy (law-firm alerts, vendor
     landing pages, one small-business explainer). Qualification rule 1 is therefore not met: the
     operating change is material and dated, but there is no independent audience signal.
   - Hold reasons: (1) no buyer voice from small exporters; (2) substitute pricing unknown —
     the residual may already be what enterprise screening vendors sell; (3) the why-now could
     reverse if the suspension is extended in US–China talks before 2026-11-09.
   - Reopen when: the rule takes effect on 2026-11-10 (no further suspension), **and** one small
     exporter, trade-association forum, or question surface asks how to check customer ownership or
     who to hire, **and** the screening vendors' small-business pricing is found to leave small
     exporters out. Recheck 2026-11-12; expires 2027-05-31.

2. **SBA 7(a) loan packaging for small-business borrowers (lane 3) — rejected.** The lender
   packages the loan and may charge a capped packaging fee ($2,500 up to $350k, $5,000 above; some
   banks charge $1,250–$1,750 or waive it — SERP via Starfield & Smith 2025-12 and lender pages).
   AI origination platforms already sell the job to lenders: Casca (Live Oak, Huntington, Celtic
   Bank), StackAI, Biz2X (SERP, 2026). The borrower-side residual is small and the AI gain goes to the
   lender. Reopen only with borrowers shown paying an outside packager after lenders refuse to package
   small loans.

3. **EcoVadis and supplier sustainability questionnaires for small suppliers (lane 3) — rejected.**

   pattern as outsourced security questionnaires (rejected 2026-10-04). Reopen only with small
   suppliers shown unserved by both the AI tools and accredited consultants.
