## Ideas screened

### Outsourced warranty-claim administration for small powersports, marine, and equipment dealers (lane 3) — rejected

- Spend already happening (`old-way spend` / `residual`):
  - In-house warranty clerks and administrators are widely posted: Vern Eide Motoplex powersports;
    Mitchell Equipment; Redhead Equipment part-time; a $16/hr staffing listing; and a Nashua NH
    Volvo listing at $50,000–$65,000 (SERP).
  - A DealersEdge small-store thread says an outside company "is here twice a week" and does all
    warranty paperwork (SERP).
  - Outsourcing fees cited at roughly 2%–4% of claims (DealersEdge 2009, SERP).
  - MarineLab Warranty Processing, sold through MRAA to marine retailers, bills a percentage of paid
    claims (MRAA, Boating Industry, Trade Only Today; SERP).
  - Warranty Consulting Services sells hands-on warranty processing to farm and heavy-equipment
    dealers; no price published (Farm Equipment 2025-04-03, read).
- `problem cost`:
  - One multi-store equipment dealer recovers 85%–90% of claim value across brands, about 60% for
    some brands, and disputes flat-rate labor times (Farm Equipment 2014, read).
  - OEM audit chargebacks are statutory issues: Colorado C.R.S. 44-20-427 limits audits of
    powersports claims to nine months.
- Operating change: Maine watercraft warranty-reimbursement law (LD 1296). It requires the retail
  labor rate, actual time, and parts markup. The Maine Senate reports it was signed 2025-06-12; a
  tracker lists chapter 284 with action dated 2026-05-01. The discrepancy is unresolved. Either
  way, the law raises claim value but does not create a new job.
- AI-change test: **fails as a new business.** AI can read repair orders and technician notes, write
  the complaint/cause/correction story, and code labor operations. But this is a decades-old human
  outsourcing business already priced at a few percent of claims. FrogData has sold
  "WarrantyMind AI", described as an end-to-end remote warranty administration service that pairs
  AI with experts, to dealerships since 2023-08-22 (read). AI makes the existing service cheaper;
  it does not change who can deliver it or open a job that used to need a firm. This matches the
  2026-10-02 SSDI rejection pattern: the business exists unchanged, and AI tools are sold to its
  incumbents.
- Delivery boundary: passes, since no licence is needed. Not decisive.
- Substitutes: FrogData WarrantyMind AI (auto); MarineLab (marine, contingency); Warranty Consulting
  Services (ag/heavy equipment); OEM portals moving to AI adjudication (Tavant Warranty.AI with
  AGCO; ServiceCPQ dealer portal pre-fills parts and labor).
- Coverage gap noted but not established: no AI-backed remote service specific to powersports or
  outdoor power equipment was found. One search is not proof of absence.
- Reopening condition: a dealer-side source showing small multi-line powersports or outdoor power
  equipment dealers are refused or unserved by FrogData-type and contingency processors, **and** an
  independent dealer question signal (a service-manager forum asking who to hire for warranty).

### Lease and rent-roll audit for small multifamily buyers (lane 3) — rejected

- Signal: BiggerPockets guides tell small-multifamily buyers to reconcile leases and rent rolls
  against trailing financials themselves. The only per-unit spend found ($25–$50 per unit) is for
  physical due-diligence inspections, which is a different job (BiggerPockets blog, SERP).
- No sourced `old-way spend`, `residual`, or `problem cost` for the lease audit itself. For small
  deals, lenders require only an inspection and appraisal, and buyers do the lease review in-house
  or with their accountant.
- Reason: no problem-value signal, so evidence-free at the scout stage.
- Reopening condition: a small-multifamily buyer (under 50 units) paying a third party for a
  lease/rent-roll audit, or a documented loss from an unaudited rent roll at that scale.
