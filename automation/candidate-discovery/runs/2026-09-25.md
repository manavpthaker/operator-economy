### 1. `DISC-2026-09-23-008` — shortlisted lead, invalidating question worked

The open question was whether small systems buy inventory field work only as engineering services.

- **Arlington, Vermont Water Department RFP**, posted 2023-04-17, proposals due 2023-05-26
  (<https://arlingtonvermont.org/rfp-for-service-line-inventory-arlington-water-department/>, read
  2026-09-25). Scope: review system records, survey customers, inspect service lines at the building
  inlet, enter data on the state template, and draft a replacement plan if needed. It asks the firm to
  explain how it is qualified and to list past inventory projects; **no engineering licence or
  credential is required on the page.** Priced per project or per service line. Funded by a 0% DWSRF
  loan. This is a small system buying the residual through an open RFP, not qualifications-based
  engineering selection. It is 2023 (the first LCRR inventory), not an LCRI-era award, and the winner
  and price are unknown.
- **Village of Romeo, Michigan**, "Water Service Line Verification Program", about 200 services,
  posted 2024-11-12, due 2024-12-03 (Starbridge aggregator listing:
  <https://starbridge.ai/rfp/water-service-line-verification-program>). Method, qualifications, and
  award not shown.
- **Kingsport, Tennessee**, LCRI inventory-update request for **qualifications**, due 2026-07-30
  (<https://www.kingsporttn.gov/Uploads/LCRI%20RFQ%20Combined.pdf>, search snippet only). A
  counter-example: a larger city using the qualifications route.
- **Litigation:** AWWA v. EPA oral argument confirmed for 2026-09-30 (NRDC case page, secondary).
  Recheck 2026-10-07 stands.
- **Effect:** the invalidating question is partly answered: at least one small system bought the
  residual through an open RFP with no licence requirement, while larger systems may use
  qualifications-based selection. Every observed payee is still a firm; a payment to a non-engineering
  provider has not been observed. Stays `shortlisted`, same rank.

### 3. EU PPWR obligations for non-EU small sellers — rejected

- From 2026-08-12 the PPWR applies directly; non-EU producers selling packaged goods to EU end users
  must appoint an authorised representative for EPR in each member state reached. The Commission's
  December 2025 Omnibus proposal to suspend the obligation covers EU-established producers only, and
  secondary sources report the Council did not advance it in June 2026 (Coolset, ppc.land, EUROPEN;
  vendor and trade content, not the Official Journal).
- Rejected: the authorised representative must be an EU-established party, so the core role is not
  open to the operator; the remaining packaging-data work (materials and weights per SKU, per-country
  reporting) is what the EPR representative services already bundle, and many small non-EU sellers
  can simply stop shipping to the EU. No buyer-side payment signal for the residual was sought or seen.

## Findings

1. **Substitute test: largely fails as first stated.** The main platform already bundles AI conversion
   plus human review of complex pages for a subscription. "The documents automation can't fix" is not a
   gap the buyer sees.
2. **Willingness to pay: not met.** Small entities observed buy the AI tool and do the rest with staff.
   Outside-service RFPs seen come from large cities, counties and states.
3. **AI-change test: passes, but the gain goes to the vendor.** Bellingham's $1,100 vs $134 comparison
   is the clearest AI cost collapse in the pool so far; it goes to a software subscription, not a small
   operator.
4. **Two cracks worth keeping open:**
   - Fillable forms. A read-only HTML transcript is not a usable form. Converting PDF forms into
     accessible web forms is a narrower job the transcript approach does not appear to cover, and AI
     could plausibly do most of the build.
   - The alternate-version question. If DOJ or an enforcement action says transcripts do not satisfy
     the rule, the residual reopens at scale. This is a legal question; the scout records it and does
     not answer it.
