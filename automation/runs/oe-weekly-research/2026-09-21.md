## Evidence pass

### Fact

- Nacha Phase 2 is current. All non-consumer Originators, Third-Party Senders, and relevant
  Third-Party Service Providers are subject to a risk-based process-and-procedure requirement with
  annual review, regardless of volume.
- Nacha names controls around vendor and payroll payment changes as one possible implementation.
- Nacha does not prescribe this service, a callback, every-entry screening, or a specific technology.
- Nacha allows internal, bank-provided, and third-party controls, making those parties substitutes as
  well as possible channels.
- AFP's 2025 survey of 521 corporate practitioners reports material payments-fraud, BEC, and
  vendor-imposter incidence, but it is not small-business-specific.
- FBI, FTC, an insurer form, and seller case studies support the failure mode and recognizable
  controls. They do not establish target-buyer purchases.
- Adjacent sellers and software products publicly package AP controls, assessments, toolkits, and
  vendor-bank-change workflows. Seller prices are asking prices, not transactions.

### Observed model

- AP/P2P consulting, cyber incident response plus payment-process hardening, and packaged vendor-
  control assessments exist.
- The exact preventive, small-business, fixed-scope install remains an adjacent synthesis rather
  than an independently proven model.

### Model

- A $1,500-$3,000 fixed fee, 16-32 delivery hours, two monthly clients, and $75 loaded hourly labor
  cost were used only as boundary cases. The low case loses money before overhead; the high case
  leaves $3,600 before acquisition, insurance, tools, and overhead.

### Inference

- Some covered small businesses may need implementation help.
- A tested control and evidence record may be more valuable than a policy document.
- Existing bookkeeping, controller, accounting, banking, broker, or insurer relationships may be the
  plausible acquisition path.

### Hypothesis

- Buyers will understand and buy a “vendor-payment verification install.”
- A qualified operator can stay outside payment authority, cybersecurity assurance, and legal or
  compliance certification.
- A later annual review could support repeat project work without continuous monitoring.

### Unknown

- Target-buyer paid demand, price, acquisition cost, sales cycle, delivery hours, gross margin,
  qualifications, insurance, liability, sensitive-data requirements, and substitute coverage.

### Source limits

- One direct small-business conversation is anonymous and cannot prove demand.
- The FBI AI-related BEC figure is an AI-referenced subset, not total BEC or small-business market size.
- The AFP survey population spans corporate organizations and was underwritten by Truist.
- The Chubb form is Australian and cannot be generalized to all insurers.
- Inlight IT and Kroll case studies are seller-authored and broader than the proposed service.
- A J.P. Morgan toolkit surfaced in search but returned HTTP 403 on direct access and was not used as
  load-bearing evidence.

## Adversarial editorial pass

- Strongest alternative explanation: this is not a durable independent business. Banks, bookkeepers,
  accountants, MSPs, insurers, AP consultants, and low-cost software may deliver the control with
  more trust and lower acquisition cost.
- Strongest causal weakness: a current obligation creates work but does not prove a new provider
  category or willingness to pay.
- Strongest editorial risk: converting fraud statistics and a 2026 rule into a false market-demand
  claim.
- Hidden labor: process mapping, exception design, training, evidence capture, follow-up, insurance,
  and acquisition could overwhelm a small fixed fee.

  loses urgency and remains commercially unproven.
