# Presenter references and accepted delivery

Before producing or replacing a presenter take, find the latest explicit delivery lock as well as
the current episode edit. These are separate records: accepting a new performance recipe does not
mean its test clip contains every line in the edit. A later owner request to update the avatars
can reopen the presenter sources while preserving the accepted cuts, narration and other scenes.

## Give each reference a job

Use an identity/look source for the person, wardrobe, room, lighting and framing. Use behavioral
video for specific delivery qualities. Distinguish relaxed conversational settling from deliberate
public-facing articulation. When these require different source excerpts, assign their roles
explicitly and ask for one coherent performance. The number of references is not a quality rule.

Behavioral references must not transfer their room, clothing, devices, unrelated words, mouth
timing or screen-call gaze into the new take. Muted references still contain mouth movement;
the approved current audio governs speech. Preserve the real narration's cadence and pauses.

Natural articulation includes changing mouth shapes and brief tooth visibility. Avoid a fixed
teeth display, broad held smile or a stretched jaw. Brief head/brow emphasis should return to rest;
do not assign the same gesture to every sentence. Inspect across the episode for repeated patterns.

## Integration and review

For every replacement, bind the exact master/review/source interval and preserve the editorial
job of the old slot. Check restored audio's placement in the generated container before aligning
muted picture to the existing master. An audio insertion offset is not a perceptual lip-sync
measurement. Quantize picture selection deliberately and review the seam with the original audio.

When a reviewer cannot distinguish lip mismatch from choppy playback, separate the hypotheses.
Check source offsets, timestamp continuity and unexpected repeated frames, then provide a small
local video with the same picture selection and original audio. State the actual audio codec and
whether its decoded samples are preserved. A clean waveform/timestamp check cannot certify mouth
sync or blame the connection; compare at normal speed before applying a global frame shift.

Before reporting a batch as integrated, enumerate the active presenter video elements in the
actual preview, including nested compositions, and bind each to its replacement file. Label a
partial preview explicitly. A recipe lock or prepared request does not establish that later
footage has changed. Keep one continuous video element across adjacent crop changes when the
performance is continuous; inspect its own source placement and coverage before replacing it.

For a new take, keep the accepted reference roles and adapt performance to the passage. A quiet
explanation, a viewing promise and a challenging question need different emphasis. Preserve the
identity/look, then inspect connected articulation, phrase/rest transitions, naturalness, likeness,
source continuity and surrounding shots. One selected test is evidence for the recipe, not a
guarantee that every stochastic output will pass.

Record a new episode decision and result; preserve the earlier lock and rejection history. Reuse
provider receipts as provenance only. Cost or upload authority for a new job must come from the
current authorized task and applicable provider workflow, never from an old submission receipt.
