# Context to form, motion and cut

## The decision unit

Work at the smallest unit with a coherent viewer change. It may be a shot, a narrated passage or a
transition across media. Do not force one sentence to equal one scene. Establish:

1. **Situation:** who is acting, what is known, what is at stake or deliberately not at stake.
2. **Viewer movement:** what they understand/expect before and what they should infer afterward.
3. **Narrative job:** establish, question, expose, compare, qualify, explain, react, land or hand off.
4. **Visible delta:** what relation, condition, choice or consequence picture can make perceptible.
5. **Limits:** what picture could wrongly imply; what remains unknown; what the source actually proves.

The proposed treatment must improve that particular movement. Repeating nouns from the voiceover,
adding a larger diagram, using a polished animation, or showing a face does not demonstrate value.
If a plain film hold or narration carries the beat better, select it and record why.

Added value can be comprehension, not a new factual claim. A literal process diagram can earn its
time when unfamiliar order, responsibility or handoffs are hard to follow by ear. State which
confusion its spatial arrangement resolves; do not reject it merely because narration names the
same steps. Likewise, a purposeful hold can build anticipation without adding a new fact.

## Select a medium by its contribution

| Need in this passage | Useful candidate | When it is the wrong choice |
|---|---|---|
| Establish people, relationship, action and physical context | Film with legible geography and relevant interaction | Hands or props hide characters the audience has not met; invented tension supplies a false story |
| A caused reaction changes the meaning of what was said | Character coverage, with the reaction allowed to finish | Generic worried face, silent missing dialogue, or an unrelated reaction from another narrative moment |
| Address the viewer, frame a question, make a promise or interpret evidence | Presenter with exact approved speech | Merely breaking up animation, a redundant back-to-back presenter insert, or a take against different words |
| Let a question or identity land distinctly | Purposeful question/title/sting | A duplicate caption over a fully clear line, or a heading used in place of a meaningful reaction |
| Show an otherwise invisible operating or economic relationship | Working Model | A diagram has no state change, its only change repeats narration, or available duration cannot establish its objects |
| Establish a factual number, source, caveat or comparison | Attributable evidence with a legible explanation | Generated proof, a precise-looking model for uncertain evidence, or losing the caveat while the number persists |

Mixed forms need a handoff job. Do not rotate film/presenter/animation at a fixed interval. Rhythm
comes from changes in knowledge, performance and density; also review accumulated repetition
across adjacent scenes and reused takes.

## Choose animation form from the relationship

These are conditional candidates, not an exhaustive taxonomy or a template to fill.

| Underlying relation | Candidate visual grammar | Necessary clarity and common false inference |
|---|---|---|
| Many operating functions depend on one person/decision | Converging routes or a bottleneck | Establish distinct functions and where decisions occur; a funnel must not imply only orders or a measured loss rate |
| Work precedes a conditional payment | Persistent actors, forward work path and a closing-triggered fee return | Show who carries risk and when money returns; do not condemn a fee model that also solves a real problem |
| A needed role is absent from an otherwise working structure | Negative space between named responsibilities, then a traced boundary | Show exactly what is unassigned; do not imply the business fails or the work never exists anywhere |
| A buyer compares the same business with alternatives | Pull back while retaining the original business as a recognizable anchor | Other boxes/people are illustrative unless counts are evidenced; do not replace the original object silently |
| The same input behaves differently under a changed condition | Counterfactual branch or paired state change | Both states must have a materially different supported result; leave unevidenced customer response unknown |
| Passage argues that entry and completion differ | Distinct stages or an evidence-based cohort | Bind numerator/denominator, sampling and caveat; never convert an uncertain directional claim into measured outcomes |
| The visual only needs to identify or qualify something | A label, pin or still | Motion adds no value if there is no operation to show |

First choose the mechanism, then fit the available time. A full business bottleneck may be useful
later and still be redundant in a four-second opening. If establishment + causal action + reading
hold cannot fit, simplify, relocate, replace or remove; do not accelerate until comprehension fails.

Use the current Boundary Ledger mark language: authored overlapping thin strokes, broken outlines,
open corners and controlled roughness where that binding applies. Keep the mechanism readable.
Style repair and meaning repair are separate decisions. Do not make an arbitrary grid, equation or
polished geometry the default because it is easy to implement.

## Choreograph the inference

Give the viewer enough continuity to recognize what changes. Establish only the objects needed for
the next inference. Let a question or missing role remain unresolved until its narrated answer if
that suspense serves comprehension. Expose the relevant connection or absence at its exact cue,
then settle long enough to read it. Do not reveal the punchline early, animate everything at once,
or keep moving after the relationship is understood.

A film-to-model transition can locate the meeting within the illustrated business. Preserve
people/table/room anchors during the handoff; use a spatial match only when geometry supports it.
In the EP007 case, a photo inset made the model feel like a slide containing footage. The selected
solution replaced corresponding contours during one pullback, leaving a wholly illustrated
business. This is a reusable technique for that problem, not a requirement to erase every photo
from every diagram. Do not use movement that implies an unsupported physical connection.

## Cut with a reason and a protected hold

For each meaningful cut, specify:

- the outgoing and incoming functions, not just their asset filenames;
- the word ID/phrase, relation to it (before, after, during a pause), and exact available timing;
- the source and review clocks, frame rate and rounding rule;
- the action, expression, word or breath that must not be interrupted;
- what the viewer gains on entry and what completes the shot's purpose on exit;
- the media and visibility dependencies that change with the cut.

Word alignment proposes a cut; the original waveform checks whether it is actually clean. At an
excerpt end, inspect the neighboring breath or word onset and choose a source-silent frame when
available. Preserve the prior proposal and record any measured correction. EP007 S06's proposed
203.2917-second master outpoint included the next breath; 203.125 seconds was digitally silent.
That timestamp is episode-specific, and silence alone does not certify the full spoken delivery.

An emphasis punch-in may arrive just after a conjunction, before the stressed words, while audio
and source performance remain uninterrupted. Moving the cut toward the stressed syllable can
make it feel clipped. That does not establish a universal conjunction rule: listen to this voice,
breath and phrase. Prefer a crop change on a continuous correct take when it solves emphasis;
do not cut/loop speech to manufacture the effect.

Reaction coverage can bridge a line such as "that pause" to its human consequence. If the next beat
already returns to the presenter, another presenter insert may add repetition. Choose the buyer,
owner, object, heading or hold from the current narrative job, not from the word "pause" alone.

Duration is not an obligation to consume every frame of a source. Trim before a gaze shift or a
person leaving the frame changes the implied relationship. Recalculate the underlying scene's
return and every dependent cue. The R29 42-frame blank was a technical dependency error, not a
creative experiment or a reason to keep an unsuitable expression longer.

## Performance, voice and identity

Subtle articulation, facial emotion, head/body variation and lip synchronization are separate
controls. Record which dimension is being changed. "More emotive" need not mean more head motion,
a wider mouth or raised eyebrows. Match neighboring takes and the accepted likeness before
optimizing an isolated shot. A global emotion preset cannot guarantee continuity.

Narrated dramatization can show illustrative conversation without exact character words; presenter
address and synchronized dialogue require the approved exact speech. Follow `oe-film-direction`
for the mode contract. Do not infer that every visible mouth must track the narrator.

Plan presenter performance variety across a session: distinguish takes/source ranges and intended
gesture roles so short overlapping excerpts do not create recurrent head movements. If the owner
defers variety until the next recording, preserve the selected current cut and carry that trigger
forward. Provider presets remain bounded empirical recipes, not proof of a general best model.

## Critique before handoff

Check the strongest alternative explanation of the image: could concern look like hostility,
comparison look like a measured count, a missing paid role look like a failed business, or a zoom
look like a photograph pasted into a slide? State the material risk and how the selected form
resolves it. Where evidence cannot resolve it, show a narrow test rather than inventing certainty.

Compare to the simpler alternative and prior rejected variant. Review at normal speed with audio,
at practical display size, and across both boundaries. Technical tests support this review; they
cannot determine that the audience learned the intended thing. Record actual owner outcomes
separately from the director's predicted effect.

When a scene claims to show a causal mechanism, temporarily hide its explanatory headings and
ask what action makes the dependency observable. Moving an object along a path does not establish
why it can or cannot proceed. Establish the enabling action, retain unaffected people/objects,
and make the absence or changed condition cause a readable consequence. For acted examples,
test whether the same behavior could instead imply incompetence, refusal, or simple delay.
Use a short movement study when that distinction is unresolved before rebuilding the full scene.
This is a diagnostic for claimed visual explanation, not a universal text-free rule: conditions,
evidence labels and unfamiliar relationships can require words.
