# R40 direction proposal — make the dependency observable

Status: response to owner revision of R39 animation; not built or owner accepted.

## What failed

R39 has six explanatory headings, numerous object labels and a worker who never performs a task.
The tickets move, but the picture does not establish why one finishes or another stops. Removing
the headings exposes the weak causal action. Staged object entry improved order, not explanatory
value. Fewer labels or cleaner strokes alone would leave this failure intact.

## Proposed treatment

Concentrate the animation on the conditional owner-dependence passage, approximately master
279.32–289.48 / review276.32–286.48. Start with one fixed workshop view, large enough to read the
people's actions. No simultaneous miniature workshop, owner icon, receipt stack, alternate titles,
moving routine book and tray route.

1. An employee reaches a decision in a partly completed task and offers two options to the owner.
2. The owner selects an option; the employee acts and completes the task.
3. The owner leaves. The employee and equipment stay in their established positions.
4. A different task reaches another choice. The employee turns toward the owner's empty place,
   waits for the missing answer, and leaves the task unfinished.
5. Hold long enough for the viewer to see what remains and what is missing. No sentence-length
   heading or red failure annotation supplies the conclusion.

The second task must visibly require a fresh choice. An identical repeat would suggest that the
employee forgot a step just demonstrated, making incompetence a competing explanation. The action
shows one owner-dependent decision as a conditional example; it does not establish that this
seller's whole business actually fails or that all dependency is a production problem.

The viewer should infer: the assets and employee are still here, but the capability needed for
the next piece of work left with her. This supports the spoken conditional rather than adding an
invented outcome.

Build only the short owner-dependence action as a rough movement study before another full review.
Temporarily hide explanatory headings and mute narration. An independent viewer should identify
the owner's enabling decision, the later absence, the employee's continued presence, and the
unfinished task. If the viewer only sees a person leaving and an object stopping, the cause still
is not staged clearly. Restore necessary condition/source context for the narrated episode.

This diagnostic applies to this claimed visual mechanism. It is not a ban on titles, necessary
labels, evidence captions, or graphics that legitimately require text.

work insert with narration. If the animation cannot make the dependency readable, do not retain it
