## The open decision
**Resolution.** The episode is natively 1280x720 end to end. 83 percent of the picture is
HyperFrames scenes that can be re-rendered at 4K and downsampled to genuinely sharp 1080p; the
other 17 percent is generated presenter and film plates that can only be upscaled.

Three paths, costed:
1. **Upscale the 720p master to 1080p.** Ten minutes, no new detail, but YouTube gives 1080p uploads
   a better bitrate ladder than 720p.
2. **Full 1080p rebuild.** Several hours. r11 is an overlay graph over r10 over r9 over a frozen r3;
   there is no builder past `build_r4.py --select`. Needs 720p parameterised out of `build_r3.py`
   (hardcoded in four places plus source validators), 21 scenes re-rendered, 24 plates upscaled to
   pass those validators, the chain re-run, every hash binding regenerated, and the result verified
   against the accepted cut.
3. **Ship native 720p.**

Proven this session: a scene re-renders at 4K in 41s for 19.5s of video (~36 min for all 21), with
`--low-memory-mode` because 4K disk capture wants 15.5 GB of scratch and only 9.5 GB is free. The
re-render matches the approved scene frame for frame; the 2.8 percent pixel difference is
antialiasing from the higher DPR, which is the point.

**The better fix is upstream.** Generate presenter plates at 1080p and set the pipeline to 1080p
from the start, so No. 003 is 100 percent native rather than retrofitting 83 percent of this one.
The 720p ruling was a cost decision on the Shorts and is worth revisiting with the numbers.
