# EP007 Boundary Ledger callback-arc frame

## Format

- Canvas: 1920×1080, 16:9, 24 fps.
- Picture: 1,765 frames / 73.541667 seconds.
- Audio: three exact excerpts from locked narration; no retiming and no generated audio.
- Image register: generated live-action-style `humanContext`; no evidence claim attached.

## Visual law

- The exact same capable owner remains screen-left and the same buyer remains screen-right.
- The same workshop, clothing, daylight direction, and owner–buyer axis persist across all callbacks.
- Cameras are locked or genuinely stabilized. Native speed only. Hard cuts only.
- Performance and changed operating state carry the scene. No post pan, zoom, slow motion, focus flourish,
  generated graphic overlay, caption, claim ID, or explanatory annotation.
- The cold open must show the action in the narration: the owner visibly begins a brief silent answer,
  stops before continuing, and calculates. This is not a closed-mouth reaction portrait.
- The ending must change one load-bearing object: an open, inspectable operating binder. It may contain
  abstract line groups and diagrams but no readable synthetic text or numbers.
- Competence stays constant. The owner is not embarrassed in the opening or triumphant in the ending;
  the answer moving from her memory onto paper is the payoff.

## Boundary Ledger binding

Warm human context stays photographic. `constraint` is expressed through work routing back to the owner.
`counter` is expressed through the binder carrying knowledge the buyer can inspect. Boundary Ledger
supplies semantic meaning, not a teal/orange grade over the people.

## Failure conditions

Identity, wardrobe, environment, or screen-direction drift; statue-like faces; missing answer-and-stop
action; page morphing; unreadable hands; melodrama; buyer verdict; sale cue; documentary implication;
or a graphic/text layer that explains what the physical scene should show.
