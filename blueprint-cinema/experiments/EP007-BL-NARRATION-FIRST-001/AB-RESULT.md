# EP007 narration-first A/B result

Status: **Variant B wins the directional test. Review Cut 007 is now the strongest performed
execution candidate, awaiting owner comparison.**

## Result

The narration-first method produced the stronger story decision. It removed the default emotional
close-up and used gaze plus focus to move scrutiny from the capable owner to the operating system.
That better serves the narration than Variant A's cleaner but more literal three-shot edit.

The result now separates five decisions:

1. **Keep B's directing logic:** relationship master, cut after “looks,” owner-present reverse,
   scrutiny resolves into the workplace.
2. **Reject B1–B4 as executions:** they introduce speech, owner eyeline breaks, reaction, mistimed
   action, or unstable blocking. Seedance B5 returned no reviewable clip because the provider's
   safety system blocked the synthetic human reference.
3. **Review B6 rather than silently passing it:** it fixes the fake dialogue, owner movement,
   camera drift, and action timing. It clearly turns scrutiny from the owner into the workshop.
   But it uses a visible 30–40° head turn and a blink instead of the specified eyes-first 3–5°
   adjustment. It succeeds editorially where it fails the literal action contract.
4. **Keep Shot 2 as a candidate, not a lock:** the rack technically lands on the operating space,
   but one blind reviewer read the owner softness as synthetic degradation. Its exit geometry also
   does not yet make the promised graphic match into the rough pencil baseline.
5. **Review Cut 007 tests the missing dramatic cause:** competence is performed at the bench, an
   ordinary staff question exposes the dependency, and a close-up lets the absence test register.
   This is a new three-shot build made after the A/B result, so it can replace B6 as an execution
   candidate but cannot retroactively become part of the blind comparison.

## What this test establishes

It supports testing narration-first shot design as the provisional default for the next generated
human-context passages: define the dramatic question, each shot's job, screen geography, one
action, word-locked edit, and excluded coverage before generation. Existing footage can still fill
gaps, but should not decide what the passage means.

It also exposes a practical limit: the tested generators can produce a stable semantic head turn,
but did not reliably execute phrase-locked facial microblocking. That is a model-and-staging
finding, not evidence that the narration-first method failed.

It does not establish a universal two-shot template, approve this generated cast or take, approve
V4, authorize Step 4, or approve production or publication.

## Decision after viewing

Watch Review Cut 007 in the ten-second context at normal speed. Judge one thing: does the staff
interruption make the owner's final look feel earned? If yes, keep the three-shot causal structure
and treat the broader final turn as the selected action. If the final look feels staged or the
dependency remains unclear, do not make another prompt-only facial pass; replace the middle beat
with real filmed/licensed operational coverage or a clearer faceless process action.
