# Direction record — The Unfinished Answer

## What the scene is doing

The opening is not a conversation scene. It is a narrated observation of a business review whose meaning changes when one physical question cannot be completed on paper.

The buyer’s question is carried by the narrator. The owner’s reaction is carried by the pencil stopping. The model carries the structural explanation. This division prevents the generated human coverage from imitating dialogue while the audience hears a different voice.

## Performance direction

### Owner

- Woman, approximately 55+, visibly experienced and at home in the business.
- Baseline state: competent task focus, not anxiety.
- She reviews the material without demonstrative pointing or explanatory pantomime.
- Her only authored emotional turn is physical: she begins to map the answer, recognizes the dependency, and stops the pencil.
- The face may register a small, caused recalculation after the pencil stops. No generic pensive stare, random look-away, aimless scanning, embarrassment, or defeat.
- No visible speech in generated plates.

### Buyer

- Attentive inspecting party, not antagonist.
- Baseline state: procedural task focus.
- He moves the finished binder aside to make room for the operating answer, then later places the unfinished sheet over the binder.
- He does not mouth the question, lean in theatrically, exchange reaction shots, or signal judgment through exaggerated facial expression.
- No visible speech in generated plates.

## Future moving-plate specification

The still animatic is a timing and composition test. Final human plates should be filmed or generated as four separate, short, one-action setups matching R1–R4.

- Lock the camera in each setup. Do not animate a still with a slow zoom or pan.
- Give each clip a short pre-action and post-action handle for editorial timing.
- Preserve one room, one work surface, one owner, one buyer, one binder, one sheet, one pencil, and one key set.
- Generate no spoken performance, lip motion, reciprocal eyeline exchange, background chatter, or uncaused facial emotion.
- Do not ask a generation model to invent the notebook, ledger, or binder contents. Frame or focus so the contents are irrelevant and unreadable.
- Direct one verb per setup: **review**, **shift**, **mark-and-stop**, **cover**.
- Reject a plate if hands, props, screen direction, wardrobe, or room geometry change enough to break continuity.

## Edit law

- Use cuts between setup changes. Do not hide weak causality with transitions.
- A cut is justified only when it reveals a different action, object relation, or explanatory state.
- No cutaway to a face merely to add emotion. A face appears only when an observable event causes the reaction.
- No close-up of fabricated page contents.
- No repeated filler cuts of the same action.
- Do not use camera motion to compensate for a still image. Replace the placeholder with a moving plate when motion is required.
- The unfinished pencil line is the only cross-medium carrier: paper mark → model relation → oxide practice path.

## Sound law

- The locked narration is the sole language carrier until a later, separately approved presenter or source-sound segment.
- Do not add dialogue, room chatter, generic office tone, or dramatic scoring to manufacture emotion.
- Event-specific foley may be considered only after final moving plates exist and the visible causes are clear: binder movement, pencil contact, pencil stop, paper placement.
- Preserve the narration break from `00:44.50–00:48.52`. The OE sting remains absent unless a specific master is selected and approved.
