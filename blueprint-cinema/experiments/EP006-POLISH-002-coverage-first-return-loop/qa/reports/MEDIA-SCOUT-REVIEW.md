## Review method and set-level finding

I inspected the local 1280x720, 25 fps MP4 sources and their 1 fps temporal contact sheets. The proposed ranges below preserve real source-native hand, body, object, or camera movement; no still-image movement is being counted. All 13 files are silent, so none supplies production ambience or usable sync foley. The abbreviated hashes are identification aids only; the integration owner must record full SHA-256 values in the select manifest.

The set has strong same-production continuity: the same reception desk, pendant lights, wall clock array, black service bell, masked beige-hat guest, and masked burgundy-jacket receptionist recur. It does **not** match several requested continuity targets: the guest wears beige rather than dark olive; the operator wears burgundy rather than a cream shirt/deep-navy apron; the lobby reads as a modern generic reception rather than a modest wood-and-linen inn; and no worn brass key tag is physically present. The visible word `RECEPTION`, masks, televisions, and glossy desk make the production period and stock-hotel origin conspicuous.

Most importantly, this candidate family cannot by itself establish the required persistent key tag. A graphic key tag added over unrelated desk actions would clarify a concept but would not turn those actions into filmed possession, handoff, or return. The footage can support a technically coherent reception sequence, but the lead should not infer that it satisfies the full continuity or documentary-first creative gate.
