## Direction judgments and causal limits

1. **The prior interrogation diagnosis was too narrow.**003 attributed that reading partly to isolated faces and inactive paperwork. The preferred earlier R8 film already used close-ups. That is a counterexample to treating close-ups as the decisive problem. Cast, setting, blocking, source acting, shot duration and model also changed.
2. **003 removed part of the enacted conversation while trying to soften it.** Its question insert hides the questioner; its wider owner shot reduces facial emphasis. The original [R8 direction](/Users/brownmanbrain/GitHub/operator-economy/blueprint-cinema/experiments/EP007-NET-NEW-UNFINISHED-ANSWER-003/direction/r8-context-coverage/DIRECTION.md) intentionally preserved reciprocal attention and a caused close-up, and explicitly declined a hands-only insert. Returning to that interaction is a direction recommendation, not a claim that paperwork shots are inherently wrong.
3. **The owner timing instruction became ambiguous.**003 request204 says she speaks around 1–3 seconds and stops around 5.5 seconds. It starts her attention at the folder rather than holding confident eye contact. The resulting early stop is consistent with this ambiguity; the evidence cannot assign causation between prompt and model.
4. **Model selection was not a quality comparison.**003 reused Wan because it produced the liked avatar baseline. That does not establish the best film model or the best synchronized-avatar route. Price, release recency and resolution alone do not establish performance quality.

The scratch-test request authorized a fresh visual interpretation; rebuilding the world was not itself unauthorized. The mistake is treating that rebuild as evidence of provider superiority or inferiority. Technical checks establish valid files, timing and preserved audio, not convincing acting. The user has not named an exact earlier R-number, so “the earlier Kling film” is the supported comparison label.

## Proposed compact film prompt

Proposed only; this audit does not amend any submitted request. If used, record it as a prompt change rather than implying exact replay of the original Kling request.

> One continuous nine-second locked-camera close-up using this exact reference: same woman, buyer's blue shoulder, workshop, clothes, daylight and framing. She maintains eye contact screen-right. For the first second she is comfortably ready to answer. She then begins an ordinary confident answer, with natural conversational mouth movement and a small gesture only if her hand enters frame. Keep her gaze raised and her answer continuing until about5.5 seconds. Between5.5 and6.5 seconds she interrupts herself: lips close, gesture settles, gaze lowers slightly. Hold her quiet consideration through the end. The buyer remains patiently seated and still. Illustrative silent conversation under narration; no audible dialogue or literal words. No second answer, early gaze drop, dramatic reaction, added objects, camera movement or cut.

This prompt preserves the dramatic turn while reducing competing directions. It is a testable proposal, not a guarantee of naturalness or exact timing.
