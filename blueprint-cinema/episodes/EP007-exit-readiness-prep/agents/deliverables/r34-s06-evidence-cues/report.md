## Supported visual distinction

Showing listing as entry into a process and closing as a separate outcome adds a useful relationship. The important state change is that a business acquires an “on the market” status without acquiring a completed-sale status. A marker or document can change; the business itself need not move or vanish. Hold the unresolved separation long enough for the audience to notice what has not happened.

An approximate “3 in 10” source mark can support this, provided it remains a sourced estimate rather than a count of pictured businesses. Keep “roughly” or an approximation sign with the number, identify the population as listed businesses, and attach the Exit Planning Institute attribution. A readable “directional” qualification should remain with the mark, particularly while narration describes source interests. EPI trains and certifies exit planners; the visual must not imply neutral or audited measurement.

No ten-business cohort is needed. Avoid animating exactly three businesses through a closing gate and dropping seven: that creates a countable pseudo-sample and implies known individual outcomes. Do not convert this number into an exact 30.0% dashboard, a universal prediction, or a precisely scaled time-to-sale chart. An approximate quantitative mark is permissible; exact stage losses or invented cohort destinations are not.
