# R38 S08: build the buyer's test in front of the viewer

Proposed direction, not an owner acceptance or a rendered result.

The current graphic arrives almost finished. The roof, four named functions, arrows, owner and revenue page compete before the buyer's distinction has landed. Later motion mostly replaces the center label. The next version should let the audience first distinguish a business from the owner's work, then discover the connection that has to survive her absence.

Use one authored operating bench that grows from the small workshop drawing. Avoid another factory outline with a labeled box inside it. The decisive image is the work continuing with other people after she leaves; in the alternative, the critical connection leaves with her while everything else remains.

## Illustration form

An open workshop workbench is more specific and legible than a generic factory/funnel. Use a worn wooden surface, trays, an operating book, two deliberately distinct people, modest tools and a physically readable joining mechanism. The apparatus represents coordinated business work; it must not read as a literal automated factory. The second worker makes that difference visible.

Authored short overlapping strokes, retraced edges, open joins and selective cross-hatching belong in the drawing. Keep perspective modest and consistent. Institutional headings remain typeset. Do not use a uniform wobble filter, perfect outlined pictograms, decorative gears or permanent animation. The receiving tray and finished-work tray are supporting forms; the retained versus departing connection is the main action.

The same current work object, operating book, workshop, owner and past receipts should survive the relevant beats. Object continuity earns the later absence test. Introducing six distinct function icons would compete with that test and reproduce R37's noun inventory.

## What animation adds

Narration can state that a buyer needs a business that survives its owner. The animation can let the viewer watch the same operation twice with one dependency changed. The building and historical revenue remain equal. The critical knowledge either stays with the other worker or leaves with the owner. The audience sees why a valuable past record does not settle transferability.

If the second operation, preserved objects and departing connection are removed and comprehension is unchanged, the animation has failed its reason for existing.

## Material review checks

- The first graphic frame contains only the business/job heading; no prebuilt model leaks through.
- The owner leaves before the positive work item finishes; another person still does the work. Otherwise the image implies full automation or removes the actual test.
- “What the buyer needs” precedes the positive test. “If it all depends on her…” precedes the alternative. Otherwise the sequence falsely claims the seller passed and then deteriorated.
- The operating book/linkage stays with the business in the first condition and visibly leaves with her in the second. If that difference is unreadable, more labels will not repair the mechanism.
- Past revenue remains after the negative test. Erasing it would falsely suggest that owner absence rewrites historical performance.
- The stopped task is an illustrative consequence of the stated condition, not measured lost revenue or a definitive finding about this seller. No numerical throughput, transaction outcome or universal claim that an owner-dependent business has no assets is implied.

The next scene names relationships, customer concentration and operations. Do not pre-explain all three here. S08 establishes the one dependency test that makes those later variations intelligible.
