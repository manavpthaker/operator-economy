# Context-sensitive coverage and editing

## Read the scene before choosing tools

Read the approved passage and adjacent beats, not just a sentence or asset noun. Establish:

- what is happening and what the viewer already knows;
- who wants what, the relationship and stakes, if present;
- what changes in knowledge, confidence, pressure, or control;
- whether this is observed action, narrated dramatization, heard dialogue, presenter address,
  evidence, explanation, or a deliberate progression between them;
- what the picture contributes and which sounds the audience actually hears.

Use the simplest scene form that carries the intended inference. Do not manufacture tension for a
neutral process, or suppress interaction when the story is an exchange. Preserve uncertainty:
“meeting again,” friendship, a hostile buyer, or a successful outcome needs upstream support.

## Select by need, not by quota

These are options, not required setups or a fixed sequence:

| Scene need | Useful option | Avoid when |
|---|---|---|
| Establish relationship or shared space | master / two-shot with readable orientation and behavior | geography is already clear and the shot adds nothing |
| Follow a question, answer, challenge, or listening beat | medium, OTS, or reverse with coherent eyelines | it implies exact unheard words essential to understanding |
| Let the viewer detect a confidence break or decision | closer face view entered before the change; hold through it | the face has no motivated change or task |
| Show the effect on another person | caused reaction / listening coverage | it fabricates a response or cuts away from the more important event |
| Establish a decisive action or object state | insert / detail, cut on action | the object is decorative or cannot carry the claimed meaning |
| Carry place or time across a boundary | brief establishing action, motivated ellipsis, sound bridge | it invents chronology or requires rewriting locked narration |
| Explain an invisible mechanism | hand off to a bounded graphic or Working Model | an unrelated sketch, equation, or visual metaphor obscures the mechanism |
| Let evidence be read | stable source view with exact locator and reading hold | a reaction montage or camera move competes with proof |
| Mark a personal judgment or structural turn | presenter return with exact synchronized audio | periodic avatar appearances merely interrupt the argument |

Master-derived coverage preserves continuity, but the audience need not see the master first if
another opening is clearer. Generate only necessary setups; five available angles do not imply
five required cuts. A digital punch-in is not automatically an independently captured close-up:
check resolution, perspective, and whether it supplies the needed performance.

## Shape time around the turning point

For each shot, state the attention owner or subject, why this framing matters now, the incoming and
outgoing trigger, and what must remain uninterrupted. Triggers may be an exact word, an action, a
look, new information, or completion of a reading hold. Place the frame early enough to see the
change rather than cutting in after it happened. Protect the crucial pause when its duration is the
story. More pressure may call for fewer cuts, not more.

Use a hard cut unless another transition clarifies a specific relationship. Cut on action only
with compatible action phases. Match an object only when it is genuinely the same carrier or a
declared transformation. J/L cuts need an actual approved incoming/outgoing sound event or voice
source; continuous narration over picture edits alone is not a new sound bridge. Do not invent
character dialogue, add a musical hit, or remove a VO pause to make an edit work.

## Phase 2 to Phase 3 handoff

Before authoring generation prompts, translate approved context into:

1. **Scene choice:** situation, form and reason, stakes, emotional progression.
2. **Coverage choice:** selected technique(s), what this shot adds, entry/exit trigger, protected
   hold, and a simpler option considered and rejected (or selected).
3. **Performance:** observable start, one primary change, consequence and final image; mode-specific
   speech permission; continuity anchors and setup reference.
4. **Review:** a scene-specific question tested against the exact soundtrack and chosen source
   window, plus continuity, sync, and disclosure checks.

The director chooses; validation checks completeness and consistency; the generator and builder
implement. Do not substitute a keyword classifier, fixed cutting rhythm, or aesthetic adjectives
for the decision. Preserve the authored rationale in downstream packets so later agents do not
revert to a global default.

## Review the intended inference

Mute viewing exposes blocking, geography, and performance. With sound restored, test whether the
scene remains clear without private production notes. In narrated dramatization, a visible
conversation can be intentional; fail it if essential meaning still depends on unavailable words,
not merely because a mouth moves. Also check that the cut has not manufactured a reaction, made
illustration look evidentiary, or hidden the turning point. Record unreviewed footage as unreviewed;
a planned mute-test result is not a playback verdict.

Use different test cases when changing this skill: an interpersonal turn, a neutral process, a
dense evidence passage, and a presenter/film transition. Success is different useful direction for
different needs, not repeated use of a favored technique.
