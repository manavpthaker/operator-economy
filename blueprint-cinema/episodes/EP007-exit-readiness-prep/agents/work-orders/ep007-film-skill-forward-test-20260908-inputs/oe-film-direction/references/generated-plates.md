# Generated-plate direction

Generated footage is a directed illustration of the episode's situation. It is not evidence and it
does not get to invent story logic.

## Build a coherent world before close coverage

Create or approve a master that fixes:

- the room and work zones;
- each person's position and orientation;
- the action line and screen direction;
- wardrobe, age, physical features, and role;
- the recurring object or work surface;
- time of day, motivated light sources, and material palette.

Derive closer setup frames from the approved world. A close-up should preserve enough spatial or
action continuity to belong to the same scene. A new angle is not a license to swap roles, sides,
wardrobe, props, or emotional state.

## Prompt observable direction

Separate immutable continuity from the single changing beat. Describe:

- who is where;
- one primary action;
- the exact object acted on;
- a physical expression or micro-action only when something causes it;
- camera position, framing, and motivated movement;
- what must remain still;
- the final image and hold;
- negative constraints, especially no speaking in `narrated_observation`.

Compile these from the scene context and editorial choices, not from narration keywords. In
`narrated_dramatization`, preserve the requested rapport, reciprocal eyelines, and caused answer or
listening beat; do not append observation's no-conversation negatives. State whether this particular
shot permits illustrative speech. Keep the exact narration in the timing/edit brief, not as words
the generator should make the characters say. Give the generated performance usable pre-action and
post-action handles where the edit needs them.

Prefer “she stops writing and keeps one finger on the unresolved line” to “she looks concerned.”
Prefer “the buyer's eyes move from the record to the owner, then hold” to “a tense cinematic
moment.” Do not prescribe visible micro-expression merely to decorate a face.

## One clip, one beat

Ask a generated clip to perform one legible change. Generate separate clips when the scene needs a
new action, camera setup, or emotional consequence. Select the usable sub-window; do not keep the
whole clip because it was paid for.

Cut before actions decay into idle pointing, aimless looking, repeated gestures, or model drift.
Hold only when stillness itself communicates thought, load, refusal, or completion.

## Narrated observation negatives

Include only negatives relevant to the shot, such as:

- no speech, lip movement, conversational gesturing, or answer beat;
- no random looking away or unfocused head turns;
- no slow motion unless the physical event actually requires temporal emphasis;
- no unmotivated pan, orbit, push-in, rack focus, or handheld drift;
- no legible invented page, screen, ledger, price, or document contents;
- no object teleportation, side switching, wardrobe change, or role reversal;
- no exaggerated performance, stock-business pantomime, or aimless background action.

## Edit before polish

Assemble the chosen actions against the locked narration. Confirm that each cut adds meaning,
preserves the important turn or hold, and follows the selected mode's speech relationship. In a
dramatization, the narrator must supply the essential meaning of the interaction. Only then normalize color, sound,
transitions, or motion treatment. The default transition is a cut; use continuity seams only when a
real carrier crosses the edit.
