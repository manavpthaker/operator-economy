# EP007 directing decisions

The selected baseline is **R29 after the blank-screen repair**, the private 0–157.5 second prefix.
Its [acceptance manifest](../../../../experiments/EP007-NET-NEW-UNFINISHED-ANSWER-003/hyperframes/reviews/r29-neutral-seller/OWNER-ACCEPTANCE.json)
binds 30 runtime files. Older accepted stages remain part of the history, not competing current cuts.

This retrospective covers **31 documented states, 27 decision cases and 77 source messages**:
base revision C, R3–R29, and the documented R27/R29 intermediate states. The review reconstructs
intent, implementation, owner response, checks and outcomes from preserved files and thread
messages. It does not claim a fresh full audiovisual review of every old version.

The cases distinguish an owner's stated preference, an assistant's declared intent, an observed
technical result and the retrospective author's interpretation. A rationale is not evidence that
the treatment worked. The original case packets retain the fine detail behind the compact catalog.

## What the revisions taught us

| Decision | What changed and why | Reuse boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Drawing language | Thin overlapping pencil construction replaced polished hard lines; mark opacity and uneven pressure were preserved. | Apply the approved Working Model binding. It does not fix a weak explanation. |
| Metaphor and referent | Page transforms, equations and empty-chair studies did not make the intended opportunity clear. “Business hiding inside it” needed the correct referent. | Resolve meaning and scope before selecting a shape. |
| Establishment and rapport | Meet the people before using a hand or page as a carrier. Explicit narrated dramatization allowed illustrative conversation where rapport required it. | Select picture/audio mode from the situation; narration alone does not require silent observation. |
| Opening animation | Order scope expanded to customers, decisions and operations, but the 117-frame funnel still failed to add enough and was removed. | Whole-business scope was retained; that funnel and that placement were not locked. |
| Cuts and holds | Seller attempt, buyer wait and presenter interpretation carry different parts of the argument. “That pause” belongs with its recipient before returning to the presenter. | Cuts transfer responsibility or reveal something; no fixed alternation or shot count. |
| Identity | Show wording and Build. Own. Operate. stay in the accepted opening; verified silent identity waiting was shortened. | Preserve exact accepted wording and intentional thinking pauses. Do not treat every silence alike. |
| “One number” | The close-up was liked. Decoder handoff, phrase-boundary placement and lip articulation were separate fixes. The cut moved to just after “and.” | Exact timestamps belong to the source take; use current word timing and uninterrupted performance. |
| Avatar performance | Exaggerated movement was rejected despite technical checks. Restrained articulation was selected. | More emotion does not mean larger head/mouth motion. Match adjacent takes. |
| Deferred variety | Current take was accepted; broader head-movement variation was deferred to the next avatar recording. | Carry an explicit trigger forward rather than reopening accepted footage. |
| Broker model | The stronger direction located one business within a wider market and made the compensation structure visible. R26 had already intended causal meaning but did not satisfy the owner. | Test the relationship viewers can actually perceive; declared intent is insufficient. Literal process diagrams can be useful for unfamiliar handoffs. |
| Film-to-model seam | The meeting becomes the illustrated business through a spatially matched pullback. A screenshot sitting inside the illustration was rejected. | Preserve geography for this handoff; a photo inset may serve another purpose elsewhere. |
| Presenter question and gap | Presenter asks through “make it closeable”; the model returns at the answer. Negative space identifies the missing paid role; the fee returns to sale professionals. | Do not reveal the answer early or imply the business has failed. |
| Owner expression | The fee explanation needed neutral/warm conversation, not the earlier serious realization reaction. The chosen source ends before the buyer gets up. | Select the action and emotional arc for the current narration, not simply the same person's face. |
| Blank-screen repair | Trimming the seller without moving the underlying layer's return left 42 blank frames. A generic check had passed. | One cut must govern every affected timing dependency; inspect actual rendered coverage. This is a technical control, not a style preference. |

Exact word cues, master/review/source clocks, frame rounding, holds, source ranges, performance
settings and rejected alternatives are preserved in the referenced cases. They are not replaced
with a generic “good animation” rule.

## How this continues

Before each meaningful new scene or revision, the director reads the current script context and
retrieves relevant cases. It records the desired viewer change, chosen form, strongest alternative,
reason, cues, continuity needs and material uncertainty. After implementation it appends the actual
checks and feedback against that exact decision revision. New choices supersede old ones without
erasing their rationale or outcome.

The new skill and log are reusable now. They do not automatically make R29 a compiled production
pipeline: shared cue generation, rendered continuity checks and the canonical HyperFrames/Resolve
integration remain separate implementation work. Technical validation cannot certify engagement.

## Known gaps

R1/R2 are not identifiable in the reviewed files. R6 has no issued REVIEW.md. Some rejected
intermediate runtimes were overwritten, including the initial R27 photo inset; its rejection is
supported by feedback and revision records. The remembered earlier preferred illustration is not
uniquely identified. These limits remain explicit rather than reconstructing nonexistent certainty.
