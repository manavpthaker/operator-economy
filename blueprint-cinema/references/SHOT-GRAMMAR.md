# OE shot grammar

## Purpose

This catalog defines reusable explanatory shot structures for Blueprint Cinema. A grammar entry is a causal shape, not a finished composition or style template. It tells a director how information changes over time while leaving episode-specific objects, framing, and art direction to the direction bible and shot board.

Use a grammar when it makes the business relationship easier to understand. Use `compose` when no grammar honestly fits. Never force narration into a grammar because a reusable component exists.

## Shot roles

Every shot names one primary role:

- `establish_reality`: establish person, place, object, or consequence;
- `orient_system`: establish the minimum operating geography needed now;
- `introduce_object`: give a persistent object identity and meaning;
- `operate`: show a workflow, route, decision, or handoff;
- `compare`: hold two states, paths, or assumptions in readable relation;
- `prove`: present and attach evidence;
- `parameterize`: insert a sourced value and change system behavior;
- `fail`: show a working connection, route, or assumption break;
- `reroute`: reveal an alternative path after failure or qualification;
- `human_judgment`: interrupt automation with a visible person or review gate;
- `stress_test`: change volume, cost, time, conversion, capacity, or exception load;
- `resolve`: complete the operating or human outcome;
- `breathe`: hold a meaningful resolved state;
- `identify`: show identity or title without disconnecting the film;
- `reset`: change explanatory altitude while preserving necessary accumulated state.

A shot may support secondary roles, but one role determines its construction and review.

## Grammar 1: reality to system

### Use when

A physical action has an invisible operating mechanism the viewer needs to understand.

### Structure

1. Establish a real person, place, or object.
2. Let one physical action become the visual anchor.
3. Reduce or resolve that anchor into system geometry.
4. Reveal only the relationships necessary to explain the action.
5. Preserve a recognizable identity marker into the system view.

### Signature move

The physical object's contour, position, movement, or texture becomes its system representation without a full visual reset.

### Reject when

- the mechanism does not actually correspond to the observed action;
- the reality footage is generic wallpaper;
- the transformation erases the person or physical consequence;
- the full system map appears before the immediate relationship is understood.

## Grammar 2: system to reality

### Use when

An abstract mechanism must return to the person, work, cost, or outcome it affects.

### Structure

1. Hold the relevant system state.
2. Isolate the output object or consequence.
3. Carry it through the transition.
4. Resolve into a real action, object, environment, or human response.
5. Preserve the measurable state where appropriate.

### Signature move

The system outcome becomes a physical or human consequence rather than disappearing behind B-roll.

## Grammar 3: relationship route

### Use when

The thesis depends on who owns, receives, pays for, controls, or loses a relationship.

### Structure

1. Establish the subject token.
2. Establish the origin and destination needed for this passage.
3. Draw or reveal a route only as the relationship becomes valid.
4. Move the subject or value along that route.
5. show the handoff, toll, interruption, or destination state.

### Signature move

One persistent token carries identity across the route while the relationship around it changes.

### Reject when

- the token could represent any generic customer;
- arrows appear without a real direction or ownership claim;
- several paths animate simultaneously;
- route motion is used as decoration between topics.

## Grammar 4: leak and recovery

### Use when

Owned value, information, attention, money, or relationship repeatedly escapes and a counter-system can recover it.

### Structure

1. Show the healthy or intended connection.
2. Perform the action that breaks, fragments, diverts, or leaks it.
3. Make the consequence visible.
4. Introduce the recovery mechanism only after the leak is understood.
5. Run the recovered value through the guarded route.
6. resolve into a measurable outcome.

### Signature move

The same object or relationship is visibly restored, not replaced by a success title.

## Grammar 5: evidence extraction

### Use when

A source validates a claim, value, assumption, or system parameter.

### Structure

1. Source appears with enough context to identify it.
2. Relevant passage, field, row, or region is highlighted.
3. The exact claim or value is extracted.
4. The extracted element moves to the system node it informs.
5. System behavior changes.
6. Source remains pinned or retrievable as context.

### Signature move

The extracted evidence visibly changes the model.

### Reject when

- the citation card flashes and disappears;
- the source is reconstructed without a label;
- the system changes before the evidence arrives;
- the source does not directly support the narrated claim;
- a synthetic image functions as proof.

## Grammar 6: cost accumulation

### Use when

Repeated operations increase cost, time, delay, workload, risk, or complexity.

### Structure

1. Establish the unit operation.
2. Attach one visible cost object or meter.
3. Repeat the same operation with the same object identity.
4. Accumulate the consequence rather than replacing it.
5. Hold the total or range.
6. Compare with a changed route or assumption only after the accumulation is understood.

### Signature move

The economic or operational consequence physically accumulates across repetitions.

## Grammar 7: before-and-after path

### Use when

The same customer, job, or value takes two meaningfully different routes.

### Structure

1. Establish the shared input.
2. Run the current path to its consequence.
3. Preserve the starting token and key parameters.
4. Reset only the path, not the comparison basis.
5. Run the counter-system.
6. Compare results at the same destination and scale.

### Signature move

One shared token runs both systems, eliminating a dishonest apples-to-oranges comparison.

## Grammar 8: decision gate

### Use when

A workflow qualifies, approves, rejects, suppresses, escalates, or waits.

### Structure

1. Establish the case and required information.
2. Move the case to the gate.
3. Reveal the decision criterion.
4. visibly select one branch.
5. Keep other branches legible but inactive.
6. Show the consequence of the selected branch.

### Signature move

The case cannot pass until the criterion becomes visible and resolves.

## Grammar 9: exception and human intervention

### Use when

Automation must stop for ambiguity, sensitivity, risk, consent, quality, or judgment.

### Structure

1. Run the normal automated path.
2. Introduce the exceptional case.
3. show the condition that makes automation unsafe or incomplete.
4. Stop the route.
5. Hand the case to a visible human role.
6. show approve, revise, suppress, or escalate.
7. Return to the system only when the decision permits it.

### Signature move

Automation visibly relinquishes control; the human action changes the permitted path.

## Grammar 10: capacity stress

### Use when

The operating truth depends on volume, throughput, delay, queue, utilization, or bottleneck.

### Structure

1. Run the system at a readable baseline.
2. Increase one input at a time.
3. show where inventory, cases, tasks, or time accumulates.
4. reveal the bottleneck or failure threshold.
5. change one approved parameter or resource.
6. rerun at the same scale.

### Signature move

The bottleneck emerges from the system's behavior rather than from a label announcing it.

## Grammar 11: parameter stress test

### Use when

Economics or performance depends on an assumption or range.

### Structure

1. Establish the base model and sourced or labeled estimate.
2. Identify one variable.
3. change the variable through a bounded range.
4. keep other variables visibly constant.
5. show outcome movement.
6. identify the fragile threshold or range.

### Signature move

One control and its consequence change in the same beat.

## Grammar 12: outcome confirmation

### Use when

The counter-system produces a human and measurable result.

### Structure

1. Carry the persistent subject or outcome object into the final operation.
2. Complete the operation.
3. show the confirmation or result.
4. attach the agreed measurement.
5. return briefly to reality or the operator.
6. hold long enough to understand what changed.

### Signature move

The result resolves the episode's recurring object and its measurable state at once.

## Grammar 13: blueprint pullback

### Use when

The viewer has learned the system in parts and can now understand the accumulated whole.

### Structure

1. Begin on the last resolved object or result.
2. pull back gradually.
3. reveal previously learned components in their current states.
4. keep irrelevant or unintroduced parts absent.
5. highlight the first actionable entry point.
6. hold the completed blueprint.

### Signature move

The whole system is earned through accumulated knowledge; it is not shown as orientation before the parts are understood.

## Grammar 14: identity over continuing action

### Use when

Show identity or episode identity must appear without abandoning the story.

### Structure

1. Continue or complete the opening action.
2. reduce secondary detail.
3. place identity on the existing visual world or on a deliberately brief hold.
4. preserve a recurring object or motion into the episode premise.
5. return directly to the operating question.

### Signature move

Identity feels like authorship of the film already underway, not a separate promotional bumper.

## Grammar 15: chapter reset with continuity

### Use when

The episode changes question, explanatory altitude, or operational phase and the viewer needs orientation.

### Structure

1. settle the preceding state;
2. preserve one accumulated object, value, or route;
3. cut or reframe to the new explanatory altitude;
4. introduce the next question or state;
5. avoid rebuilding the episode from empty space.

### Signature move

One load-bearing element survives the reset and proves the chapter belongs to the same operating world.

## Composition rules

- Begin with the minimum state required for comprehension.
- Use one primary subject and one primary action.
- Supporting elements remain subordinate until their cue.
- Design the eye path from subject to action to consequence.
- Place real evidence at a scale that can be read.
- Use text as subject only for a genuine thesis, reversal, warning, identity, or critical number.
- Establish the static resolved frame before designing motion.
- Allocate explicit reading time after the last meaningful reveal.
- Keep caption, source, and delivery-safe areas visible during direction.
- Vary framing because the story changes, not to satisfy a novelty quota.

## Motion rules

- Reveal information when the VO reaches it; do not front-load the complete canvas.
- Use anticipation only when it makes an operation easier to perceive.
- Let action cause consequence in that order.
- Stop motion after the consequence resolves unless another operation remains active.
- A hold is a directed state, not a missing animation.
- Do not animate every layer independently.
- Do not move the camera and the primary subject in conflicting directions unless the conflict is meaningful.
- Do not use bounce, overshoot, blur, glow, parallax, or 3D merely as a quality signal.
- Name the business or editorial purpose of every meaningful movement.

## Cut and transition rules

Use a cut when:

- the next shot answers a new question;
- no object or property needs to remain continuous;
- speed, clarity, or contrast is more important than transformation;
- a designed transition would advertise itself.

Use a match or continuity transition when:

- a persistent object crosses the boundary;
- evidence becomes a parameter;
- a physical action becomes a system action;
- the camera follows a real handoff;
- a route visibly fails or changes;
- a pullback reveals accumulated state.

Every designed transition specifies:

- outgoing anchor;
- incoming anchor;
- shared property;
- direction and speed when relevant;
- duration;
- audio bridge;
- why the relationship is real.

## Anti-amateur review

Reject a shot or sequence when:

- every noun has an icon;
- every sentence creates a new composition;
- cards enter from arbitrary directions;
- the same layout is reused with new nouns;
- everything moves because nothing has priority;
- the camera floats after the scene has resolved;
- the effect is more memorable than the operating idea;
- text repeats the VO;
- evidence appears as decoration;
- B-roll matches a keyword but not a story job;
- AI footage supplies generic mood instead of a necessary world;
- a transition destroys object identity;
- the viewer cannot state what changed before the next sentence begins.
